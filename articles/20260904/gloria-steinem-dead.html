美國女權運動標誌性人物斯泰納姆去世，享年92歲

Gloria Steinem, Personification of the Women’s Movement, Dies at 92

KATHARINE Q. SEELYE • 2026-09-04
Gloria Steinem, the feminist crusader who galvanized the modern women’s movement, helping millions of women develop a sense of self-worth and gain a measure of equality, died Wednesday at her home in New York City. She was 92.
推動現代女性運動蓬勃發展的女權主義鬥士格洛麗亞·斯泰納姆於週三在紐約市的家中去世，享年92歲。她幫助數以百萬計的女性建立自我價值感，並在爭取性別平等方面取得了一定程度的進展。
For more than half a century, Steinem personified the women’s movement and became its most enduring symbol; her tinted aviator glasses and curtain of streaked-blond hair parted in the center made her one of the most recognizable women in the world.
半個多世紀以來，斯泰納姆一直是女性運動的化身，並成為其最持久的象徵；茶色飛行員眼鏡和中分挑染金色長髮使她成為世界上最具辨識度的女性之一。
She was an unlikely revolutionary. Growing up a transient in the Midwest, she was 10 when her parents divorced and she was left to care for her mentally anguished mother. She had scant formal education until sixth grade.
她並不是那種人們想像中的革命者。小時候，她在美國中西部過著四處遷徙、居無定所的生活，10歲時父母離異，照料精神備受折磨的母親成了她的責任。直到六年級之前，她幾乎沒有接受過正規教育。
But she read voraciously, graduated from college with honors and believed fiercely that women should be able to live whatever life they chose.
但她博覽群書，以優異成績從大學畢業，並堅信女性應該有權選擇自己想要的生活。
廣告
Her own feminist awakening didn’t come until her mid-30s, when, as a journalist in 1969, she attended a “speak out” of women discussing their illegal abortions.
她自己直到35歲左右才迎來女權意識的覺醒——那是1969年，作為一名記者，她參加了一場由女性公開談論自己非法墮胎經歷的「發聲會」。
She led protests and consciousness-raising sessions, wrote essays and delivered speeches, delved into political organizing, helped establish rape crisis centers and shelters for battered women, founded numerous groups that promoted women and raised money for their causes.
她領導抗議活動，主持提高女性自我意識的討論會，撰寫文章、發表演講，投身政治組織工作；她幫助建立為強姦受害者提供援助的危機中心和為遭受家庭暴力的女性提供庇護的收容所；她還創立了許多倡導女性權益並為相關事業籌措資金的團體。
And she and a cadre of feminists had the audacity to start a publication that flew in the face of traditional women’s magazines. The first glossy national periodical to be created, owned and run entirely by women, Ms. magazine, founded in 1971, sought to channel a rising sense of female empowerment and eradicate sexist assumptions that permeated the culture.
她和一群女性主義者甚至大膽創辦了一本與傳統女性雜誌背道而馳的刊物。1971年創立的《女士》(Ms.)雜誌是全美第一本由女性創辦、擁有和運營的銅版紙雜誌，旨在引導日益高漲的女性賦權意識，消除滲透在文化中的性別歧視觀念。
“When we started, there was not even a term for sexual harassment,” Steinem often said. “It was just called life.”
「我們剛開始的時候，甚至還沒有『性騷擾』這個詞，」斯泰納姆常說，「那時候它就被叫作生活。」
Indefatigable, she wrote essays and lectured into her 90s. She was the author of nearly a dozen major nonfiction books; her latest, “An Unexpected Life,” is scheduled for publication this month.
她精力過人，年過九旬仍在撰寫文章和發表演講。她著有十幾部重要的非虛構作品；最新一部《意想不到的人生》(An Unexpected Life)定於本月出版。
Steinem defied the stereotype of “women’s libbers” as sexless, humorless and dateless. She led a glittering social life and enjoyed relationships with many high-voltage men, including director Mike Nichols and real estate and publishing mogul Mort Zuckerman. She called Franklin A. Thomas, the pathbreaking president of the Ford Foundation, the love of her life.
斯泰納姆打破了人們對「婦女解放運動支持者」的刻板印象——認為她們缺乏性感、沒有幽默感、沒有約會對象。她擁有光彩奪目的社交生活，與許多魅力四射的男性有過戀情，其中包括導演邁克·尼科爾斯和房地產及出版業大亨莫特·祖克曼。她稱福特基金會具有開創性的主席富蘭克林·托馬斯是「一生的摯愛」。
But she spurned marriage (mostly) and remained child free, a choice she said she never regretted.
但她拒絕婚姻（大部分時間裡），並且沒有子女，她說自己從未後悔這一選擇。
廣告
Her lifestyle and unapologetic beliefs made her a lightning rod, adored (though not always) by liberals, demonized (always) by conservatives.
她的生活方式和毫不妥協的信念使她備受爭議，自由派對她愛戴有加（儘管並非總是如此），保守派（始終如一）對其妖魔化。
While the male-dominated news media all but anointed Steinem as the feminist movement’s spokeswoman (to the annoyance of its many other leaders and of the decidedly anti-hierarchical Steinem herself), it simultaneously patronized and marginalized her as a rebel with a pretty face.
雖然由男性主導的新聞界幾乎一致把斯泰納姆奉為女性主義運動的發言人（這既讓運動中的其他許多領袖感到不滿，也讓極力反對等級制度的斯泰納姆本人頗為惱火），與此同時，媒體又以一種居高臨下的態度將其邊緣化，把她視為有著一張漂亮面孔的叛逆者。
Steinem, who grew up in the Midwest, began her career in New York as a freelance writer in 1960. When she was first job-hunting in New York, an editor at Life turned her away. “We need a reporter,” he told her, “not a pretty girl.”
在中西部長大的斯泰納姆於1960年在紐約開始了自由撰稿人的職業生涯。當她最初在紐約找工作時，《生活》(Life)雜誌的一位編輯拒絕了她。「我們需要的是記者，"他告訴她，"不是漂亮姑娘。」
She broke through with a witty exposé, “A Bunny’s Tale,” for Show magazine in 1963. Squeezing into a too-small corset, she worked undercover as a cotton-tailed server at the Playboy Club in New York. Her article was praised for revealing the degrading work conditions in that male-fantasy cocoon. But she regretted writing it, for a time anyway, because some used it to dismiss her as frivolous.
1963年，她憑藉為《秀》(Show)雜誌撰寫的一篇機智詼諧的調查報導《兔女郎的故事》(A Bunny’s Tale)打響了名聲。她穿上過小的緊身胸衣，在紐約的花花公子俱樂部卧底當兔女郎服務員。她的文章因揭露那個男性幻想繭房中令人屈辱的工作條件而受到讚譽。但她一度後悔寫了這篇文章，因為有人藉此將她貶低為輕浮膚淺之人。
By 1968, she had become a co-founder of New York magazine. Writing a monthly political column, “The City Politic,” she found her voice.
到1968年，她已成為《紐約》(New York)雜誌的聯合創始人。通過撰寫每月一期政治專欄《城市政治》(The City Politic)，她找到了自己的聲音。
Her column led to political advocacy. She marched with Cesar Chavez, the head of the United Farm Workers, who was revered until reports emerged this year that he had sexually abused girls and women. She served as treasurer of the legal defense fund for Angela Davis, the revolutionary Black feminist who was in prison awaiting trial for murder, and was later acquitted. Steinem supported Norman Mailer in his losing campaign for mayor of New York in 1969.
她的專欄最終引領她走上政治倡導之路。她曾與美國農場工人聯合會領袖塞薩爾·查韋斯一起參加遊行。查韋斯曾備受尊敬，但今年有報導曝出他曾性侵女性。她還曾擔任安吉拉·戴維斯法律辯護基金的財務主管。戴維斯是一位激進的黑人女性主義者，當時因謀殺罪被關押候審，後來被判無罪。1969年，斯泰納姆支持諾曼·梅勒競選紐約市市長，但他最終落敗。
She was jolted into feminism that year when she reported on a group of women in Greenwich Village discussing their illegal abortions. Angered that they had been made to feel like criminals, Steinem, who had had a secret abortion in England in 1957, wrote her first openly feminist essay, titled “After Black Power, Women’s Liberation.”
也是在那一年，當她報導格林威治村一群女性討論她們的非法墮胎經歷時，她深受觸動，從而投身女權主義。她對這些女性被當作罪犯對待感到憤怒。斯泰納姆本人1957年曾在英國祕密接受墮胎手術，此後她寫下了第一篇公開表達女性主義立場的文章，題為《繼黑人權力之後：婦女解放》(After Black Power, Women’s Liberation)。
Her essay appeared just as the women’s movement, sparked by Betty Friedan’s “The Feminine Mystique” (1963), was gaining steam.
她的文章發表之時，由貝蒂·弗裡丹的《女性的奧秘》（The Feminine Mystique，1963年）所引發的女性運動正聲勢日盛。
廣告
In 1971, a powerhouse group of feminists — Friedan, Steinem, Reps. Bella Abzug and Shirley Chisholm, both Democrats from New York, and others, including Jill Ruckelshaus, a prominent Republican activist — founded the National Women’s Political Caucus to advance the Equal Rights Amendment and elect women to public office.
1971年，一個強大的女性主義團體——包括弗裡丹、斯泰納姆、紐約州民主黨眾議員貝拉·阿布祖格和雪莉·奇澤姆，以及著名共和黨活動家吉爾·拉克爾紹斯等人——創立了全國女性政治核心小組，旨在推動平等權利修正案，並推選女性擔任公職。
The first issue of Ms. was published in December 1971 and ran as an insert in the year-end issue of New York, thanks to editor Clay Felker, who financed and promoted it. The first full stand-alone issue came out in January 1972, again with Felker’s backing.
1971年12月，《女士》雜誌創刊，作為《紐約》雜誌年終刊的插頁發行，這要感謝編輯克萊·費爾克的資助和推廣。1972年1月，在費爾克的再次支持下，首個獨立發行的完整版面世。
The magazine attracted prominent writers, including Alice Walker, Adrienne Rich, Toni Morrison and Vivian Gornick. It covered a range of topics, including sisterhood, gender-neutral child rearing, battered wives and maternity leave. It rated political candidates by their stands on issues that were important to women.
這本雜誌吸引了許多知名作家，包括艾麗斯·沃克、艾德里安娜·里奇、托妮·莫里森和薇薇安·戈尼克。它關注的議題十分廣泛，包括女性之間的姐妹情誼、性別中立的育兒方式、遭受家庭暴力的妻子以及產假等。它還根據政治候選人在女性關心的問題上的立場對他們進行評級。
For a time, Ms. thrived. But the expanding women’s movement splintered into factions and faced backlashes. Schisms divided the magazine, too. More radical women and some women of color found the magazine wishy-washy and not inclusive enough.
有一段時間，《Ms.》發展得相當不錯。但不斷壯大的女性運動逐漸分裂成不同派別，也遭遇了來自社會的反彈。雜誌內部同樣出現分歧。一些更加激進的女性主義者以及一些有色人種女性認為這本雜誌立場過於溫吞，而且包容性不夠。
Gloria Marie Steinem was born in Toledo, Ohio, on March 25, 1934, the daughter of Leo and Ruth (Nuneviller) Steinem. Her father, whom she adored, was a peripatetic antiques dealer, a big dreamer with no money.
格洛麗亞·瑪麗·斯泰納姆於1934年3月25日出生在俄亥俄州托萊多，是利奧·斯泰納姆和露絲·努內維勒的女兒。她的父親（她深愛著他）是個四處奔波的古董商，是個充滿夢想卻身無分文的人。
For years, the family spent summers in rural southern Michigan. When fall came, they traveled to warmer climes in a house trailer. Steinem grew up an itinerant with no full year of formal schooling until sixth grade. She said she learned to read from road signs along the highway and buried herself in books.
多年來，一家人每年夏天都住在密西根州南部的鄉間。到了秋天，他們便拖著房車一路前往氣候更溫暖的地方。斯泰納姆從小過著漂泊不定的生活，直到六年級才第一次完整地上滿一整年的正規學校教育。她說，自己是通過公路上的路牌學會閱讀的，後來一頭扎進了書堆裡。
廣告
Her mother had been one of the first female reporters and editors at The Blade of Toledo. She had a nervous breakdown, became addicted to a sedative and spent two years in a sanitarium, all before Gloria was born.
她的母親曾是《托萊多刀鋒報》(The Blade of Toledo)最早的一批女記者和編輯之一。她曾精神崩潰，後來對一種鎮靜劑產生依賴，並在療養院住了兩年，這些事情都發生在格洛麗亞出生前。
When Gloria was about 10, her parents divorced, and her sister, Susanne, left for Smith College in Massachusetts. Gloria and her mother moved into a condemned farmhouse in East Toledo where Ruth had once lived. Gloria’s life was consumed by caring for her mother.
格洛麗亞大約10歲時，父母離異，姐姐蘇珊娜前往麻薩諸塞州的史密斯學院上學。格洛麗亞和母親搬進了東托萊多一棟廢棄的農舍——母親早年曾在那裡住過。格洛麗亞的生活從此被照料母親所佔據。
Gloria tended to Ruth for seven years, until her senior year of high school, when they sold the house, saving the money for Gloria’s college tuition. Gloria spent her senior year living with her sister in Washington, D.C., while Ruth went to California, where she was cared for by her ex-husband.
她照料露絲長達七年，直到高中畢業班那一年。那時，她們賣掉了房子，把錢留下來作為格洛麗亞上大學的學費。格洛麗亞最後一年高中是在華盛頓特區與姐姐一起度過的，而露絲則去了加利福尼亞，由前夫照料。
Gloria followed in her sister’s footsteps to Smith, where she majored in government. She graduated magna cum laude and Phi Beta Kappa in 1956.
格洛麗亞追隨姐姐的腳步進入史密斯學院，主修政治學。1956年，她以優等成績畢業，並成為斐陶斐榮譽學會成員。
During her senior year of college, Steinem planned to marry her boyfriend, but then decided against it. She left her engagement ring under her fiance’s pillow and “fled,” in her words, to India in early 1957.
大四那年，斯泰納姆計劃與男友結婚，但後來改變了主意。她把訂婚戒指留在未婚夫的枕頭下，用她的話說「逃跑」了，於1957年初前往印度。
On her way, she stayed in London while awaiting her visa and discovered that she was pregnant. She managed to get an abortion.
途中，她在倫敦等待簽證時發現自己懷孕了。她設法把孩子打掉了。
She was drawn to India, just a decade into its independence, by the legacy of Mahatma Gandhi. She had won a Chester Bowles Asian fellowship to study there, but she quickly abandoned her classes. Wearing a sari, she walked through southern India with followers of Gandhi, absorbing lessons about “talking circles” and grassroots organizing.
當時印度獨立還不到十年，斯泰納姆被聖雄甘地的政治遺產所吸引，因此前往印度。她獲得了切斯特·鮑爾斯亞洲獎學金前往那裡學習，但她很快便放棄了課程。她身著紗麗，與甘地的追隨者一起徒步穿越印度南部，在此過程中習得了"談話圈"和基層組織動員的經驗。
After returning to the United States, she spent a year in Cambridge, Massachusetts, as a director of the Independent Research Service, a nonprofit educational foundation that recruited and provided government support for American students to attend communist-run youth festivals in Europe. The magazine Ramparts subsequently reported that the foundation had been partly funded by the CIA, and Steinem came under criticism from the left. She dismissed the entire venture as harmless, telling The New York Times in 2016 that no one in the CIA had ever told her what to do or say and that she had never reported to the agency.
回到美國後，她在麻薩諸塞州劍橋待了一年，擔任獨立研究服務機構的負責人。這是一家非營利教育基金會，負責招募美國學生並提供政府資助，使其得以赴歐洲參加由共產黨主導的青年節活動。後來，《壁壘》(Ramparts)雜誌報導說，該基金會部分由中央情報局資助，斯泰納姆因此受到左翼人士批評。她認為整件事沒有什麼大不了。2016年她告訴《紐約時報》，中央情報局從未告訴她該做什麼或說什麼，她也從未向該機構彙報工作。
She moved to New York in 1960 and began working as a freelance journalist. At the time, women were not taken seriously as writers; she wanted to cover politics but was stuck with celebrity profiles and fashion.
她於1960年搬到紐約，開始擔任自由撰稿記者。當時，女性作家不受重視；她想報導政治，卻只能接到名人採訪和時尚方面的稿件。
廣告
Still, she was prolific. Her articles appeared in Vogue, Cosmopolitan, Esquire, Family Circle and Life, among other magazines. She was a contributing editor at Glamour and New York.
儘管如此，她的產量非常高。她的文章發表在《時尚》(Vogue)、《大都會》(Cosmopolitan)、《君子》(Esquire)、《家庭圈》(Family Circle)和《生活》等眾多雜誌上。她還擔任過《魅力》(Glamour)和《紐約》雜誌的特約編輯。
At the same time, Steinem moved comfortably in the sophisticated milieu of New York’s intelligentsia and dated powerful men.
與此同時，斯泰納姆在紐約知識精英雲集的高雅社交圈中如魚得水，並與一些有權勢的男人約會。
In 2000, Steinem overlooked her antipathy toward marriage and wed David Bale, a social justice activist born in South Africa (and the father of actor Christian Bale). She was 66. She initially denied rumors that she had married him because he needed a green card, but years later conceded that this had been the case. The marriage lasted three years before he died in 2003, at 62, from brain lymphoma.
2000年，斯泰納姆暫時放下自己對婚姻的厭惡，嫁給了戴維·貝爾。貝爾是一名出生於南非的社會正義活動人士，也是演員克里斯蒂安·貝爾的父親。當時斯泰納姆66歲。起初，她否認自己嫁給貝爾是因為他需要綠卡的傳言，但多年後承認確實如此。這段婚姻維持了三年，直到貝爾於2003年因腦淋巴瘤去世，終年62歲。
Steinem had faced her own recurring battle with cancer (breast), starting in 1986, after which she suffered from total exhaustion and depression and underwent psychoanalysis. She then spent several years writing and rewriting “Revolution From Within: A Book of Self Esteem.”
斯泰納姆自己也曾反覆與癌症（乳腺癌）抗爭。1986年，她第一次患病，此後長期陷入極度疲憊和抑鬱，並接受精神分析治療。隨後她花了數年撰寫和修改《內在革命：一本關於自尊的書》(Revolution From Within: A Book of Self Esteem)。
It was her first long public look at herself, and it disappointed some feminists, who saw her introspection as a retreat from much-needed social activism.
這是她第一次如此公開而深入地審視自己，但卻讓一些女性主義者感到失望。在她們看來，這種自我反思讓斯泰納姆從迫切需要的社會活動中退縮了。
“Revolution From Within” wasn’t the only time that she disappointed her natural allies. Over the years, her defense of Bill and Hillary Clinton landed her in political hot water. In an opinion column in the Times in 1998, she defended President Bill Clinton against numerous sexual allegations, saying that his advances toward various women had not been unwelcome or coercive.
《內在革命》並非她唯一一次讓自己的天然盟友失望。多年來，她為比爾·柯林頓和希拉蕊·柯林頓辯護，使她陷入了政治困境。1998年，她在《紐約時報》的觀點專欄中為比爾·柯林頓總統辯護，反駁眾多性指控，稱他對各種女性的追求並非不受歡迎或具有強迫性。
She enraged many again during the 2016 Democratic presidential primaries, when she said in a television interview that young women were supporting Sen. Bernie Sanders of Vermont over Hillary Clinton because “when you’re young, you’re thinking, ‘Where are the boys? The boys are with Bernie.’” She eventually apologized and said she had misspoken.
2016年民主黨總統初選期間，她再次激怒了許多人。當時她在一次電視採訪中說，年輕女性支持佛蒙特州參議員伯尼·桑德斯而不是希拉蕊·柯林頓，是因為「年輕的時候，你會想：『男生都在哪兒？男生們都支持伯尼呢。’」她後來為這番話道歉，表示自己言語失當。
Steinem devoted much of her life to writing. One of her bestsellers was “Outrageous Acts and Everyday Rebellions” (1983), her first collection of essays. Other books included “Marilyn: Norma Jean” (1986), a sympathetic portrait of Marilyn Monroe, and “Doing Sixty and Seventy” (2006), a reflection on women and aging.
斯泰納姆一生大部分時間都致力於寫作。她的暢銷書之一是1983年的《越軌之舉與日常反抗》(Outrageous Acts and Everyday Rebellions)，這是她的第一部文集。其他著作包括1986年出版的《瑪麗蓮：諾瑪·簡》(Marilyn: Norma Jean)，一本對瑪麗蓮·夢露充滿同情的傳記式作品，以及2006年關於女性與衰老的思考《六十歲與七十歲》(Doing Sixty and Seventy)。
Steinem is survived by what she called her “chosen family” — her close friends. Her mother died in 1981, and her sister, Susanne Steinem Patch, in 2007.
斯泰納姆仍在世的親人是她所謂「選擇來的家人」——那些親密的朋友。她的母親於1981年去世，她的姐姐蘇珊娜·斯泰納姆·帕奇於2007年去世。
Ms. magazine reached its peak circulation in 1989, with 540,000 subscribers, but its finances were constantly in peril. In less than a decade, its circulation fell to 200,000, and the owner threatened to close it.
《女士》雜誌的發行量於1989年達到頂峰，擁有54萬訂戶，但其財務狀況始終岌岌可危。不到十年，其發行量降至20萬，雜誌所有者甚至威脅要關掉它。
Cobbling together a group of venture capitalists and entrepreneurs, Steinem formed Liberty Media for Women, which acquired Ms. for $3 million. In 2001, the Feminist Majority Foundation took over and kept the magazine alive. The magazine now publishes quarterly.
斯泰納姆召集了一批風險投資家和企業家成立了婦女自由媒體公司，以300萬美元收購了《女士》雜誌。2001年，女性多數基金會接收，使這本雜誌得以繼續出版發行。該雜誌現為季刊。
