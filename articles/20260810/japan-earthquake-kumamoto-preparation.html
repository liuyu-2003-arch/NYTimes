走訪震後熊本：為何經歷強震但死傷較少？

How Japan Reduced Deaths From a Major Quake

RIVER AKIRA DAVIS, HISAKO UENO • 2026-08-10
When a 6.8-magnitude earthquake jolted southwestern Japan last week, its force mirrored the kind of seismic shocks capable of flattening cities and claiming hundreds or even thousands of lives.
7月底，一場6.8級地震襲擊了日本西南部，其威力堪比那種能夠夷平城市、奪走數百甚至數千人生命的強烈地震。
Yet across the island of Kyushu, the violent shaking destroyed or heavily damaged just a few hundred buildings. One week after the quake, the death toll was 38.
但在九州島各地，劇烈搖晃僅造成幾百棟建築物倒塌或嚴重受損。地震發生一周後，死亡人數為38人。
In Kumamoto, the prefecture near the epicenter, seismologists and local officials credit painful lessons learned a decade ago, when twin earthquakes of similar scale ravaged the region, toppling more than 8,000 buildings and ultimately causing more than 270 deaths.
在震中附近的熊本縣，地震學家和當地官員認為，這得益於十年前慘痛教訓所帶來的改變。當時，兩場規模相當的地震重創當地，導致8000多棟建築倒塌，最終造成270多人死亡。
Experts say the recent Kumamoto earthquake shared significant similarities — including its ground-shaking intensity, shallow depth and proximity to heavily populated areas — with the June tremors in Venezuela, which leveled coastal areas, killing more than 6,000 people.
專家指出，此次熊本地震與今年6月委內瑞拉地震在多個關鍵指標上頗為相似——地面震動烈度、震源較淺以及靠近人口密集區。委內瑞拉地震將沿海地區夷為廢墟，造成逾6000人遇難。
“But we see that there are huge differences in the number of casualties,” said Katsuichiro Goda, a professor of earthquake engineering at Western University in Canada. Mr. Goda, who conducted field reconnaissance in Kumamoto after the 2016 quakes, credits much of that difference to how the prefecture has rebuilt over the past decade, including spending tens of billions of dollars on earthquake proofing.
「但我們看到，兩者在傷亡人數上存在巨大差異，」加拿大西安大略大學地震工程學教授五田勝一郎（音）說。2016年熊本地震後，合田曾前往當地開展實地考察。他認為，這種差異很大程度上歸功於熊本過去十年來的重建工作，包括投入數百億日元用於抗震加固。
In the days following last week’s disaster, two colleagues and I traveled throughout Kumamoto. Damage was scattered across the region: Wooden homes had collapsed, roads were warped, and a shopping center gutted by a post-quake gas explosion stood in ruins.
7月的地震發生後數日，我與兩位同事走訪熊本各地。整個地區到處都是受損的地方：木結構民宅倒塌、道路扭曲變形、一座購物中心因震後燃氣爆炸被燒得只剩廢墟。

But from earthquake-proofed city centers, bridges and homes to communities trained in search and rescue, there were also clear signs of a region well prepared for the worst.
但從經過抗震加固的城市中心、橋樑和住宅到具備搜救訓練基礎的社區，人們也能清楚地看到，這裡已經為最壞的情況做好了準備。
Vital Infrastructure
至關重要的基礎設施
Perhaps the most striking example of this readiness was in Uto, a city of 35,000 situated near the quake’s epicenter. In 2016, the town’s five-story City Hall warped and partially collapsed, crippling local officials’ ability to coordinate disaster response during those critical first days.
這種防災能力最鮮明的例子也許就是位於震中附近、人口3.5萬的宇土市。2016年，當地五層高的市政大樓發生傾斜並部分坍塌，導致地方官員在災後最關鍵的幾天裡幾乎無法協調救災工作。
Municipal leaders designed a new City Hall, which opened in 2023, specifically engineered to remain standing through severe earthquakes. Even with last week’s epicenter less than five miles away, the concrete structure and its long stretches of glass windows stood unscathed.
地方當局吸取教訓，按照強震標準設計了一座新的市政大樓，於2023年竣工啟用。即使距離此次地震震中不到八公里，這棟混凝土結構建築和大片玻璃窗依然完好無損。
Beneath the building was one of the secrets to its survival: a seismic isolation structure. In a dimly lit subterranean passage between the foundation and the building above, a web of rubber and steel absorbed and dissipated seismic energy, allowing the earth to shift below while the building above remained relatively still.
大樓得以安然矗立的祕密之一就藏在它的地下——一套隔震結構。在地基與上層建築之間那條昏暗的地下通道裡，橡膠和鋼材組成的網狀結構吸收並消散了地震能量，下方土地發生位移時，上方建築卻保持相對靜止。
Shogo Mitsui, the mayor of Uto, explained that the new complex embodied a lesson from a decade ago: Critical city infrastructure must be focused on during earthquake-proofing so that essential services never lapse during a crisis.
宇土市市長光井正吾解釋說，這座新建綜合體體現了十年前汲取的一個教訓：城市關鍵基礎設施必須成為抗震加固的重點，以確保危機期間基本服務永不中斷。
“We have already been damaged once,” Mr. Mitsui, clad in blue and orange emergency response coveralls, said in an interview. “Because of that, the new building was designed with great consideration.” In the aftermath of the recent quake, Uto residents “have a base to rely on,” he said. “It leads to a sense of security.”
「我們已經遭受過一次損失，」身著藍橙兩色應急救援制服的光井正吾在接受採訪時說。「正因如此，新大樓在設計時經過了精心考量。」他表示，面對這次地震，宇土市民「有了一個可以依靠的地方」，「這會讓人心裡踏實」。
The municipal center was buzzing with activity last Friday. With much of Uto cut off from running water, residents drew water from a well outside, while a local basketball team passed out bottled drinks to people waiting in line. Inside, displaced residents took shelter in the air-conditioned atrium.
上上週五，這座市政中心裡一片忙碌。由於宇土市大部分地區停水，居民們在大樓外的一口水井取水；當地一支籃球隊向排隊等候的人們發放瓶裝飲料。大樓內部，失去住所的居民臨時安置在帶空調的中庭裡。

Community Training
社區培訓
Across the towns and hamlets of Kumamoto, community leaders said that after the 2016 quake they had strengthened local response networks designed to act in the hours before emergency teams can arrive.
社區負責人表示，2016年地震後，他們進一步強化了本地應急響應網路，以便在專業救援隊伍抵達之前的關鍵數小時內迅速啟動自救。
In Yatsushiro, one of the cities hit hardest by last week’s quake, two nursery school teachers overseeing an evacuation center described a well-planned disaster response. Designated emergency roles each year, they reported to a local school to launch operations even before city officials called.
在此次受災最嚴重的城市之一八代市，兩名負責管理避難所的託兒所教師描述了一套規劃周密的應急方案。由於每年都有預先指定的應急分工，甚至在市政官員發出指令之前，她們便已趕赴當地一所學校，啟動應急工作。
“Taking into account what happened 10 years ago, these were plans we all took seriously,” said Ayumi Miyazaki, one of the teachers.
「考慮到10年前發生的事情，我們所有人都非常認真地對待這些預案。」其中一名教師宮崎步（音）說。
In central-eastern Kumamoto, the village of Nishihara — nestled at the end of a road winding through mountains and rice paddies — conducts rigorous biennial disaster drills for all of its residents. Locals said they grew up being told that because they lived directly on top of active fault lines, a major earthquake would inevitably come.
在熊本縣中東部，西原村坐落在一條蜿蜒穿過群山和稻田的道路盡頭。這裡每兩年都會組織一次嚴格的全民防災演練，所有居民都要參加。當地人從小就被告知，他們的家園正好位於活躍斷層之上，因此遲早會遭遇一次大地震。
That reality struck in 2016, when Nishihara sat at the epicenter of the region’s largest quake. In the hamlet of Oogirihata, virtually every home collapsed, trapping nine residents. Tetsuya Sakata, a former community leader, recalled the neighborhood looking “as if bombs had dropped and flattened everything.”
這一預言在2016年成真——西原村恰好處於該地區最強地震的震中位置。在大切畑這個居民點，幾乎所有的房屋都倒塌了，九名居民被困。原社區負責人坂田哲也回憶，當時的村莊「就像被炸過一樣，一切都夷平了」。
Just a year before that disaster, a village drill had focused on sawing through mock rooftops to extract trapped victims. When the real earthquake struck, local residents sprinted from house to house, peeling back ceramic tiles and carving entry points for rescuers to crawl through. Every resident was pulled out alive, earning Nishihara the moniker “the miracle village.”
就在那場災難發生前一年，村裡進行了一次演習，專門演練了用鋸子鋸開模擬屋頂救出被困者的技能。當真正的地震降臨，村民們從一家奔向另一家，掀開屋瓦，鑿開入口，讓救援人員得以爬入施救。所有被困居民均被活著救出，西原村由此贏得了「奇蹟村」的美譽。
Earthquakes come with the risk of blocking roads and cutting off the hamlet from emergency workers in Kumamoto’s capital. In 2016 and in last week’s quake, “we had to do what we could on our own,” Mr. Sakata said. “If we hadn’t conducted those thorough drills, lives could have been lost.”
地震往往會引發道路阻斷，讓這片村落與熊本市的救援力量完全隔絕。2016年和此次地震中，「我們不得不自己想辦法，」坂田說，「如果沒有進行那些紮實的演練，可能會有生命損失。」

Housing Codes
住宅建築規範
Kumamoto’s resilience also owes much to the unseen engineering in its homes.
熊本的抗災能力也在很大程度上得益於住宅中那些肉眼看不見的工程設計。
Driving for four days along the roads closest to the epicenter, the stark divide was visible: Virtually every home that collapsed appeared to be an older, traditional timber-frame house. Modern homes and recently built structures seemed almost entirely unscathed.
在震中附近的道路上驅車行駛了四天，一個鮮明的對比顯而易見：幾乎所有倒塌的房屋，都是年代較久的傳統木結構建築。現代住宅和近年竣工的建築則幾乎完好無損。
Nationally, Japan enforces some of the world’s most stringent seismic codes. With steel-anchored concrete foundations and heavy-duty metal connectors that lock building frames together, modern structures are engineered to withstand violent shaking.
在日本全國範圍內，政府執行著世界上最嚴格的抗震建築規範之一。現代建築採用鋼材錨固的混凝土地基，以及能夠將建築框架牢牢連接在一起的高強度金屬連接件，從而能夠承受劇烈的搖晃。
Since the 2016 disaster, prefectural and local governments in Kumamoto have offered subsidies for retrofitting costs to help homeowners reinforce frail wooden supports, and replace heavy ceramic tiles with lighter materials.
2016年的地震災害發生後，熊本縣政府和地方政府開始為住宅抗震改造提供補貼，幫助房主加固脆弱的木質支撐結構，並將沉重的陶瓦屋頂更換為更輕便的材料。
By 2024, roughly 90 percent of buildings in Kumamoto Prefecture met official national earthquake-resistance standards. Earthquake experts said this allowed the vast majority of homes to survive the 2026 quake even in areas built on unstable soil, “the consistency of tofu,” as one worker at a temple near Nishihara described it.
到2024年，熊本縣約90%的建築已達到國家抗震標準。地震專家指出，這使得絕大多數住宅即便位於地質條件不穩定的地區——西原村附近一座寺廟的工作人員形容那裡的土地「像豆腐一般」——也能夠經受此次地震的考驗。
“If houses were constructed well, following the code in general, they behaved well,” said Mr. Goda, the earthquake expert. The survival of so many homes after last week’s tremor demonstrates that “improvements have been made.”
「如果房屋建造得足夠規範，大體上按照建築規範來施工，它們就不會有問題。」地震專家五田勝一郎說。此次地震之後有如此多的房屋安然無恙，說明「這些方面已經取得了改善」。

Challenges Ahead
未來的挑戰
Even if the immediate destruction from the Kumamoto earthquake was lighter than feared, challenges remain.
即使熊本地震造成的直接破壞比預想中要輕，但挑戰依然存在。
In 2016, a substantial portion of the overall death toll came not from initial building collapses, but from secondary health complications as evacuees succumbed to severe stress and prolonged stays in cramped shelters and cars.
2016年，總死亡人數中有很大一部分並非來自最初的建築倒塌，而是來自次生健康併發症——避難者在狹小的避難所和汽車中長期滯留，承受著巨大的心理壓力，最終不治。
As in 2016, evacuation centers lack basics. These include air conditioning amid blistering heat. Many facilities were also missing bedding, leaving many to sleep on hard gymnasium floors.
與2016年一樣，避難所依然缺乏基本物資，包括酷熱天氣裡的空調。許多設施也缺少寢具，許多人不得不睡在堅硬的體育館地板上。
At an evacuation shelter in Yatsushiro, 69-year-old Eiko Ishibashi said that only two sets of bedding were brought to a facility serving roughly 280 households. “An old couple came and slept here overnight,” she said. “When they got up in the morning, they went back home saying they couldn’t sleep on the floor.”
在八代市的一處避難所，69歲的石橋榮子（音）說，在一處服務大約280戶家庭的避難設施，只送來了兩套寢具。「有一對老夫婦過來，在這裡住了一晚。」她說，「第二天早晨，他們起床後就回家了，說他們睡不了地板。」
Yet amid the gaps, a deeply ingrained culture of mutual aid has stepped in. At the Yatsushiro shelter, middle school volunteers staffed the front tables to check in arrivals. Outside, aid groups distributed bento boxes.
但儘管有種種不足，一種根深蒂固的互助文化仍發揮了作用。在八代市的避難中心，中學生志願者在入口處值守，逐一為到來的居民登記。外面，援助團體則忙著發放便當。
In a nearby neighborhood, Masae Taniguchi, 73, was out gathering water from a store across the street from her home when an older woman in a sun hat approached, asking Ms. Taniguchi how she was faring. Learning that Ms. Taniguchi had lost access to water, the neighbor offered her access to her home’s well.
在附近的一個社區，73歲的谷口正惠（音）到家對面的商店取水時，一位戴遮陽帽的老婦人走過來詢問她的情況。得知谷口家斷水後，鄰居主動提出可以到她家的水井取水。
Mr. Mitsui, the mayor of Uto, said he believes a culture of helping others was common across Japan, but perhaps even more profound in communities that have experienced disasters. When the earthquake struck last week, “there was immediately a feeling of ‘Oh no, not again,’ but because we experienced this once, we also know how to support each other,” he said.
宇土市市長光井正吾表示，他認為互相幫助的文化在整個日本都很普遍，但在那些經歷過災難的社區，這種文化或許更加深厚。上週地震發生時，「大家馬上就有一種『糟糕，又來了』的感覺，但因為我們經歷過一次，所以我們也知道應該如何互相幫助。」他說。

“Instead of relying on us or city hall staff, the local residents help each other out — delivering water, delivering food,” Mr. Mitsui said. “I don’t know if that directly correlates to the number of casualties, but I do think that aspect is big.”
「當地居民不是等著我們或市政府工作人員去幫忙，而是彼此伸出援手——送水、送食物，」光井正吾說。「我不知道這是否能直接與傷亡人數聯繫起來，但我確實認為，這一方面發揮了很大的作用。」
