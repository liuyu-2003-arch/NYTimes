一文讀懂美國期中選舉：最後關頭兩黨選情如何？

Just Tuning Into the Midterms? Here’s What to Know About the Fight for Congress.

TIM BALK • 2026-11-03
The United States has reached the midterm elections, and voters will have an opportunity to deliver a judgment on the country’s direction two years into President Trump’s second term.
美國已進入期中選舉，在川普總統第二任期開始兩年之際，選民將有機會對國家的未來方向作出評判。
Mr. Trump enters the midterms on Tuesday with his approval rating near its lowest level ever, dragged down by the unpopular war in Iran and rising consumer prices that have outpaced wages and left many Americans struggling to cover their basic needs.
川普週二迎來期中選舉時，其支持率接近歷史最低水平，這主要是因為不受歡迎的伊朗戰爭以及不斷上漲的消費價格。物價漲幅超過工資增長，使許多美國人難以負擔基本生活需求。
The president’s diminished standing and the country’s gloomy mood have given Democrats hope that voters will hand them majorities in Congress, which is controlled by Republicans.
總統聲望下滑和全國低迷的情緒讓民主黨人燃起希望，認為選民可能會將目前由共和黨控制的國會多數席位交給他們。
More often than not, the party that controls the White House loses seats in the midterms. From 1934 to 2022, the president’s party bled an average of 27 seats in the House and three in the Senate, according to the American Presidency Project at the University of California, Santa Barbara.
通常情況下，控制白宮的政黨會在期中選舉中失去席位。根據加州大學聖巴巴拉分校的「美國總統項目」統計，從1934年到2022年，總統所屬政黨平均在眾議院失去27個席位，在參議院失去三個席位。
廣告
This year, strategists in both parties say it is likely that Democrats will wrest back the 435-seat House, offering the party a check on the Trump administration. But the picture in the 100-seat Senate is more complicated.
今年，兩黨策略師都說，民主黨人很可能奪回擁有435個席位的眾議院，從而對川普政府形成制衡。但擁有100個席位的參議院情況更為複雜。
Here’s what to know.
以下是需要了解的情況。
What is the state of play in the Senate?
參議院選情如何？
Both parties see the battle for the Senate as a tossup, and recent polling has encouraged Democrats.
兩黨都認為參議院之爭勝負難料，而近期民調令民主黨人受到鼓舞。
In this chamber, where lawmakers serve six-years terms and only 35 of 100 seats are on the ballot, Democrats have a more challenging path than they do in the House.
在參議院，議員任期六年，今年100個席位中只有35個需要改選，因此與眾議院相比，民主黨面臨的挑戰更為艱巨。
Democrats’ route to a Senate majority goes through a number of states that are traditionally Republican strongholds.
民主黨若想在參議院獲得多數席位，必須在多個傳統上屬於共和黨大本營的州取得勝利。
Democrats hold 47 Senate seats. To take back the chamber, they must flip at least four seats held by Republicans. At the same time, Democrats are trying to defend seats in two states Mr. Trump won in 2024. And five of the seven most competitive Senate races are in states that Mr. Trump carried by double digits in 2024.
民主黨人目前擁有47個參議院席位。要奪回參議院，他們必須從共和黨手中奪取至少四個席位。與此同時，民主黨人還要在川普2024年贏下的兩個州努力守住席位。而在競爭最激烈的七場參議院競選中，有五場位於川普在2024年以兩位數優勢獲勝的州。
The seven most competitive races are in Alaska, Iowa, Kansas, Maine, Michigan, Ohio and Texas.
競爭最激烈的七場選戰分別在阿拉斯加、愛荷華、堪薩斯、緬因、密西根、俄亥俄和得克薩斯。
Early this year, even the most optimistic Democrats viewed their chances of winning back the Senate as low, given the daunting map. But then Mr. Trump took the country to war, and the conflict clogged a vital oil-shipping corridor, sending the price of gasoline and diesel soaring. Rising shipping costs made myriad goods more expensive, upending Mr. Trump’s vows to make America “affordable again.”
今年早些時候，鑒於選情嚴峻，即使最樂觀的民主黨人也認為奪回參議院的機會很低。但隨後川普帶領國家捲入戰爭，衝突堵塞了一條重要的石油運輸通道，導致汽油和柴油價格飆升。運輸成本上升使大量商品價格上漲，顛覆了川普讓美國「重新負擔得起」的誓言。
廣告
Democrats have campaigned on promises to curb inflation and end the war. Although the party is still viewed skeptically by much of the public, according to opinion polls, the message has appeared to resonate.
民主黨人以遏制通膨和結束戰爭作為競選承諾。儘管民調顯示公眾仍對該黨持懷疑態度，但這一信息似乎引起了共鳴。
Now polls suggest Democrats are ahead in almost all of the key Senate battlegrounds. Still, polls are imperfect, and some Democrats have sought to manage expectations, cognizant that they must overcome a challenging partisan tilt in many of those states.
如今民調顯示，民主黨人在幾乎所有關鍵參議院戰場都領先。不過，民調並不完美，一些民主黨人試圖管理外界預期，因為他們知道，必須在其中許多州克服嚴峻的黨派傾向。
What is the state of play in the House?
眾議院選情如何？
More than in the Senate, races in the House tend to closely follow the national mood. Americans often vote for parties rather than specific candidates when they elect representatives, who serve two-year terms and are on the ballot every election cycle.
與參議院相比，眾議院選戰往往更緊密地跟隨全國情緒。在選舉任期兩年、每個選舉週期都要改選的眾議員時，美國人往往會投票給政黨，而非特定候選人。
This year, the powerful political headwinds facing Republicans have left them with long odds of keeping the chamber. At the moment, Republicans have a razor-thin advantage in the House: 218 seats to 214 seats.
今年，共和黨面臨強大的政治逆風，使它們保住眾議院的機會十分渺茫。目前，共和黨在眾議院僅有218席對214席的微弱優勢。
The Cook Political Report, a nonpartisan election forecaster, projects that Democrats will flip between 12 and 25 seats and finish with up to 240 seats.
無黨派選舉預測機構庫克政治報告預計，民主黨將奪回12到25個席位，最終席位總數可能達到240席。
廣告
The vast majority of the most competitive House races are playing out in districts held by Republicans.
絕大多數競爭最激烈的眾議院選戰發生在共和黨持有的選區。
The national House battle was reshaped earlier this year, after Republicans, urged on by Mr. Trump, redrew congressional district lines to advantage their party, starting in Texas.
今年早些時候，在川普敦促下，共和黨人從得克薩斯開始重劃國會選區，以便令本黨獲利，這重新塑造了全國眾議院選戰的格局。
Democrats fought back, redrawing districts in blue states such as California, but in some cases they were foiled by courts. In the end, Republicans emerged from the redistricting war with a structural advantage, aided by a Supreme Court decision that weakened the Voting Rights Act and allowed them to redraw more districts.
民主黨人進行了反擊，在加利福尼亞等藍州重劃選區，但在一些情況下被法院阻止。最終，共和黨人在重劃選區戰中取得結構性優勢，這得益於最高法院一項削弱《投票權法》效力的裁決，允許共和黨重新劃分更多選區。
Still, that edge is seen as unlikely to be enough for Republicans to keep the House in a difficult political climate.
不過，在當前艱難的政治氣候下，這一優勢被認為不足以讓共和黨保住眾議院控制權。
What are the parties saying?
兩黨各持何種立場？
Mr. Trump has spent weeks crisscrossing the country, delivering an unusual midterm pitch.
川普數週來在全國各地奔波，發表不同尋常的期中選舉拉票講話。
廣告
“Pretend I’m on the ballot, because essentially I am on the ballot,” Mr. Trump pleaded at a rally Sunday in Iowa, a Senate battleground. The line invariably appears in his long, winding and often unscripted speeches.
「假裝我也在選票上，因為我實際就在選票上，」川普週日在參議院戰場州愛荷華的集會上懇求道。這句話總會在他那些冗長、迂迴且往往脫稿的演講中出現。
It’s the same message Democrats are relaying to voters.
這也是民主黨人向選民傳遞的信息。
Unpopular presidents don’t typically try to make midterms referendums of their presidencies. But Mr. Trump and his advisers are hoping to juice turnout among Americans who don’t always vote but turned out to elect him to the White House in 2016 and 2024.
不受歡迎的總統通常不會試圖把期中選舉變成對自己總統任期的公投。但川普及其顧問希望激發那些平時不常投票、但在2016年和2024年投票把他送入白宮的美國人的投票熱情。
Democrats have prosecuted a case that Mr. Trump has made America unaffordable and lost touch with the needs of voters. They have accused his administration of corruption and described the Iran war as ill-planned and unnecessary. They have pilloried the president’s efforts to make over Washington and renovate the White House, saying that they are gaudy vanity projects.
民主黨人則主張，川普讓美國人難以負擔生活費用，並脫離了選民需求。他們指責其政府腐敗，稱伊朗戰爭計劃不周且毫無必要。他們抨擊總統改造華盛頓和翻修白宮的努力，稱這些都是浮誇的自我炫耀工程。
“He should be on HGTV,” former President Barack Obama said at a rally in Ohio in October, mocking Mr. Trump. “He’s got the White House looking like Saddam Hussein opened a Cheesecake Factory. Just gold everywhere.”
「他應該上『美國家園』頻道，」前總統歐巴馬10月在俄亥俄州一場集會上嘲諷川普說。「他讓白宮看起來像薩達姆·侯賽因開了一家芝樂坊。到處都是金色。」
Republicans have also sought to persuade voters that Democratic candidates are unacceptable options, casting them as politically extreme — and sometimes worse. The strategy has produced one of the darkest elections in modern American history, as Republicans have attacked Democrats with false and incendiary claims.
共和黨人也試圖說服選民，民主黨候選人不可接受，他們把民主黨人描繪成政治極端分子——有時甚至更糟。隨著共和黨人使用虛假和煽動性說法攻擊民主黨人，這一策略造就了美國現代史上最黑暗的選舉之一。
