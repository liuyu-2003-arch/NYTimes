窮人交社保、富人補稅：中國「盯上」所有人的錢包

From Gig Workers to Tycoons, China Is Reaching Into Every Pocket

袁莉 • 2026-11-23
A new joke has been making the rounds on the Chinese internet.
中國網路上最近流傳著一個新笑話。
Economists have long said that China’s growth has three engines: investment, consumption and exports. The new engines, the joke goes, are raiding officials’ homes, making the rich pay back taxes and collecting social insurance payments from the poor.
經濟學家長期以來一直說，中國經濟增長有三大引擎：投資、消費和出口。這個笑話說，新引擎是查官員的家、讓富人補繳稅款，以及向窮人徵收社保。
The government needs money to support its rapidly aging population and for costly bets on artificial intelligence. But the economy is in a slump. The result: Chinese people, rich and poor, are feeling the squeeze as Beijing reaches deeper into their pockets to cover its bills.
政府需要資金來支持其迅速老齡化的人口，並投入巨資發展人工智慧。但經濟正處於低迷狀態。結果是：隨著北京為了支付各項開支進一步向民眾的錢包伸手，無論貧富，中國民眾都感到生活壓力增加。
Hudson Zhou, a 27-year-old freelance illustrator and graphic designer in the southwestern province of Guizhou, saw work dry up as artificial intelligence replaced human-created designs. Zhou, who uses they/them pronouns, had earned only about $4,500 for the year by the end of October — about half as much as in 2025 — but they owe more this year toward the government pension program.
27歲的自由插畫師和平面設計師哈德森·周（音）居住在西南部的貴州省，隨著人工智慧取代了人工設計，其工作逐漸減少。周（使用代詞they/them）截至10月底今年的收入僅約3萬元，大約是2025年的一半，但今年需向政府養老金計劃繳納的金額卻更多。
廣告
Their pension bill — $1,500 — came out higher than last year because the province raised its minimum contribution for gig workers like Zhou.
哈德森·周的養老金帳單——約1萬元——比去年更高，因為該省提高了像周這樣的零工經濟從業者的最低繳費標準。
China’s economy is in the doldrums, and a property bust has gutted local governments’ revenue from land sales, deepening local government debt.
中國經濟陷入低迷，房地產市場崩盤導致地方政府土地出讓收入銳減，加深了地方政府債務問題。
Workers and employers pay into China’s pension funds, but local governments run them and share some of the cost. Strapped for cash, some have taken money from the funds. In a report released in June 2025, the National Audit Office said 13 provinces had diverted about $6 billion from pension funds to cover government wages and repay debt.
勞動者和僱主向中國的養老基金繳費，但這些基金由地方政府管理，且地方政府分擔部分費用。由於資金緊張，一些地方政府從養老基金中挪用了資金。在2025年6月發布的一份報告中，國家審計署表示，13個省份挪用了約406億元的養老基金，用於支付政府工資和償還債務。
At the same time, the pension system is under strain because of an aging population. Last year only 7.9 million newborns were born in China, roughly half of the average in the 2000s and 2010s.
與此同時，由於人口老齡化，養老金體系正承受壓力。去年中國僅有790萬新生兒出生，大約是2000年代和2010年代平均水平的一半。
Beijing also has present-day reasons to encourage people to pay more into the national pension system. If people believe they will have something to rely on in retirement, they might save less and spend their money now, boosting the sluggish economy.
北京目前也有理由鼓勵人們向國家養老金體系繳納更多資金。如果人們相信他們在退休後能夠有所依靠，他們可能會減少儲蓄，將錢花在當下，從而提振疲軟的經濟。
Xu Chenggang, an economist at Stanford, calls the government’s all-out push to collect more social insurance self-defeating. “The economy’s original problem is insufficient domestic demand,” he said. “Now the government squeezes ordinary people to plug its fiscal holes, which makes demand even weaker.”
史丹佛大學經濟學家許成鋼認為，政府全力以赴收取更多社會保險的做法適得其反。「經濟的根本問題是內需不足，」他說。「現在政府卻擠壓普通人來填補其財政缺口，這反而使得需求更加疲軟。」
Last year China’s Supreme People’s Court made it mandatory for employers to pay for employees’ social benefits, which include pensions, health care and unemployment. Businesses have long been required to pay into the system and automatically deduct payments from their employees’ paychecks, just as in the United States, but the law was not always enforced. The court’s decision was met with resistance from small business owners who say they can’t afford the costs and skepticism from workers who fear they will never collect.
去年，中國最高人民法院規定，僱主必須為員工繳納社會保險費，其中包括養老金、醫療保健和失業保險。長期以來，企業一直被要求向該系統繳費，並像在美國一樣從員工工資中自動扣除相關款項，但這項法律並非總能得到執行。最高人民法院的這一決定遭到了小企業主的抵制，他們表示自己負擔不起這些費用，同時也遭到了員工的懷疑，他們擔心自己永遠也領不到這筆錢。
廣告
This year the Chinese government turned its attention to gig workers, who are estimated by one research center to number some 320 million, or more than 40 percent of the country’s labor force. Only about 70 million of them were enrolled in the basic pension at the end of 2024, according to a government report.
今年，中國政府將注意力轉向了零工經濟從業者，據一家研究中心估計，零工人數約為3.2億，佔全國勞動力的40%以上。根據一份政府報告，截至2024年底，他們當中只有約7000萬人參加了基本養老保險。
People in the gig economy, which is called flexible employment in China, pay their own social security. The government wants them to pay 20 percent on a base income tied to the provincial average wage. That wage is often far above what gig workers earn, and it rises every year.
零工經濟在中國被稱為靈活就業，從業者需自行繳納社會保障。政府要求他們按與省級平均工資掛鈎的基數收入繳納20%的社保。該平均工資通常遠高於零工從業者的實際收入，而且每年都在上漲。
To get people to pay, local officials have tried cajoling and coercion. In Yichang, in central Hubei Province, the authorities threw a street festival in October with dancing robots, costumed historical figures and social security planners in matching vests. Elsewhere, the state is steering people toward taking on debt to pay for their retirement. Banks in a few provinces, working with local social security bureaus, offer loans so gig workers can keep paying into the pension fund, with the debt repaid from their future pensions. Some cities also let residents pay with Huabei, a consumer credit platform.
為了讓人們繳費參保，地方官員們既嘗試過勸說，也採取過強制手段。在湖北省中部的宜昌市，當局在10月舉辦了一場街頭節慶活動，現場有跳舞的機器人、身著戲服的歷史人物，以及穿著相應馬甲的社會保障規劃人員。在有些地方，政府引導人們通過舉債來參與養老金保險。幾個省份的銀行與當地社保局合作，為零工提供貸款，以便他們能繼續向養老金基金繳費，而債務將從其未來的養老金中扣除。一些城市還允許居民使用消費者信貸平台花唄進行支付。
More ominously, the government has also pledged to mine its data to track down people who aren’t contributing. Beijing’s slogan, “leave no one uninsured,” echoes that of the Zero Covid era, “leave no one unquarantined.”
更為令人不安的是，政府還承諾將利用數據挖掘來追蹤未繳費人員。北京提出的參保「不漏一人」的口號，與新冠清零時期的隔離「不漏一人」如出一轍。
“Where exactly are these people?” Li Zhong, a vice minister of human resources and social security, asked at a news conference last month, referring to people who aren’t contributing. “We have to find them.”
「這些人到底在哪兒？」人力資源和社會保障部副部長李忠上個月在一次新聞發布會上問道，他指的是那些沒有繳費的人。「還是要通過各種各樣的方式把人找到。」
The efforts have drawn widespread criticism on social media. A hashtag on why young people opt out of social insurance drew at least 48 million views on the social media platform Weibo before the topic was blocked in searches.
這些做法在社群媒體上引發了廣泛批評。一個關於年輕人為何放棄參保的話題標籤，在社群媒體平台微博上獲得了至少4800萬次瀏覽量，隨後該話題在搜索中被屏蔽。
廣告
The Chinese government urgently needs to replenish its pension fund, which experts have said may run out by 2035. It has been raising the retirement age and contribution levels but moving slowly to fix inequalities: Retired government workers receive nearly twice the average pension of private sector retirees and more than 30 times what rural pensioners make.
中國政府迫切需要補充其養老基金，專家表示該基金可能會在2035年耗盡。政府一直在提高退休年齡和繳費水平，但在解決不平等方面進展緩慢：退休政府工作人員領取的養老金幾乎是私營部門退休人員平均水平的兩倍，是農村養老金領取者收入的30多倍。
For Zhou, the visual artist, payments to the pension fund and for medical insurance would amount to $206 a month, or 46 percent of their $450-a-month salary.
對於視覺藝術家哈德森·周來說，向養老基金和醫療保險的繳費將達到每月約1400元，占其每月約3000元工資的46%。
Zhou decided to join a growing number of Chinese who have stopped paying into the pension system, which means they won’t get money in retirement.
哈德森·周決定和越來越多的中國人一樣，不再向養老金系統繳費，這意味著退休後將無法領取養老金。
More concerning for Zhou now is the neighborhood committee that serves as the government’s eyes and ears at the local level.
現在讓哈德森·周更擔心的是居委會，這個在基層充當政府耳目的機構。
In 2022 the neighborhood committee tagged Zhou in WeChat groups repeatedly, calling on them and other residents to enroll in medical insurance. Its workers called Zhou’s mother to urge Zhou to enroll. China Mobile, the state-owned wireless carrier, sent automated reminders.
2022年，居委會在微信群裡反覆@哈德森·周，呼籲哈德森·周和其他居民參加醫療保險。居委會的工作人員打電話給哈德森·周的母親，敦促哈德森·周參保。國有無線運營商中國移動也發送了自動提醒。
The Chinese government has other ways to compel payment. In some of the biggest cities, residents without local registration can’t buy homes or cars or enroll their children in public schools without contribution records.
中國政府還有其他方式強制繳費。在一些最大的城市，沒有當地戶口的居民如果沒有繳費記錄，就不能買房買車，其子女也不能進入公立學校就讀。
廣告
Wang, a 24-year-old trainee lawyer in Sichuan Province, has no choice to opt out. (She asked to be identified by only her family name, for fear of government retribution.)
在四川省工作的24歲實習律師王女士（音）卻沒有退出的選擇。（由於害怕政府的報復，她要求只透露自己的姓氏。）
She earns roughly $450 a month and said she had to show proof that her firm pays social insurance for her to obtain a necessary trainee certificate. The social insurance siphons off one-sixth of her pay. She’d rather not pay, she said, partly because it’s too expensive and partly because she questions whether the pension system will still exist by the time she’s old.
她每月的收入約為3000元，並表示為了獲得必要的見習證書，必須出示律所為她繳納社會保險的證明。社會保險抽走了她六分之一的工資。她自己寧願不繳費，部分原因是費用太高，部分原因是她懷疑等她老了的時候，這個養老金體系是否還會存在。
Some wealthy Chinese entrepreneurs say they feel like fatty meat the government takes a bite out of when it gets hungry. In recent years, even local police have preyed on businesses, sometimes with trumped-up charges, and extracted payment for debt-plagued local governments.
一些富有的中國企業家表示，他們感覺自己就像一塊肥肉，政府餓了就會來啃上一口。近年來，甚至地方警察也對企業下手，有時甚至捏造罪名，以此為債務纏身的地方政府榨取資金。
In July Beijing issued a new rule that demands wealthy Chinese to pay taxes on assets they hold in offshore trusts. Bill, an entrepreneur I’ve known for more than a decade, expects to owe tens of millions of dollars.
今年7月，北京發布了一項新規定，要求富有的中國人對其在離岸信託中持有的資產納稅。比爾是一位我認識十多年的企業家，他預計自己需繳納數千萬美元的稅款。
Over breakfast overlooking the Imperial Palace in central Tokyo, Bill, who asked to be identified by his English first name because he feared government repercussions, described his confusion and resignation.
在東京市中心一家可俯瞰皇居的餐廳吃早餐時，比爾（因擔心政府報復，他要求僅使用英文名字）描述了自己的困惑和無奈。
He has lived in Hong Kong for more than a decade, holds a Hong Kong passport and says he has paid taxes there for years. Under the new rule, he said, none of that may matter.
他在香港生活了十多年，持有香港護照，並表示他已經在那裡納稅多年。他說，根據新規定，這些可能都不重要了。
廣告
He fears making the payment would amount to accepting Chinese tax residency, leaving his income and investments outside China open to taxation later.
他擔心，一旦繳納稅款，就等於承認了中國的稅務居民身份，從而使他在中國境外的收入和投資日後也可能被徵稅。
“Being born in China has become original sin,” he said.
「生在中國已經成了原罪，」他說。

袁莉為《紐約時報》撰寫「新新世界」專欄，專注中國及亞洲科技、商業和政治交叉議題。
