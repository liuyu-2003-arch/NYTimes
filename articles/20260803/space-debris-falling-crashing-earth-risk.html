越來越多太空垃圾墜落地球：它們有多危險？

A Ton of Space Junk Tumbles Unpredictably to Earth Every Week

SELAM GEBREKIDAN • 2026-08-03
One December afternoon, the sky tore open and a fiery hunk of metal, wider than a school bus, came hurtling down.
去年12月的一個下午，天空撕開了一道口子，一塊比校車還寬的熾熱金屬從天而降。
It was louder than a thunderclap when it plunged into a patch of farmland, startling everyone in the village of Mukuku, about three hours south of Nairobi, Kenya.
它墜入肯亞首都奈洛比以南約三小時車程的穆庫庫村一片農田，發出了比雷霆還響的轟鳴聲，驚嚇到了村裡的每一個人。
“People were thinking this is the end of the world,” said John Mukunuu, who lives nearby.
「人們還以為這是世界末日到了，」住在附近的約翰·穆庫努說道。
When the villagers arrived, they found a giant ring, taller than a man and heavier than a horse, lodged into the earth. They watched it cool from a glowing ember to a dull gray. They guarded it, taking the occasional selfie, until officials from the capital swarmed the village.
村民們趕到現場，發現了一個巨大的金屬環，一人多高，比馬還重，深塞在泥土中。他們看著它從一塊耀眼的餘燼冷卻成暗灰色。他們守著它，不時和它自拍，直到來自首都的官員蜂擁至這個村莊。
The ring had once connected parts of a rocket. It was supposed to have burned up on its return to Earth.
這個金屬環曾用於一個火箭連接部件。它原本應該在返回地球時燒毀。
“This is an isolated event,” the Kenya Space Agency said in early 2025.
「這是一個孤立事件，」肯亞航天局在2025年初表示。
Not exactly.
事實並非如此。
廣告
More space debris is raining down on Earth than ever before — one effect of the frenetic race among governments and companies like SpaceX to reign over the skies.
墜落地球的空間碎片比以往任何時候都要多——這是各國政府以及像SpaceX這樣的公司為了掌控天空而展開的狂熱競賽所帶來的影響之一。
Last year, the U.S. Space Force issued alerts for nearly 820 objects entering the Earth’s atmosphere. The government issued alerts for 110 objects a decade ago.
去年，美國太空軍針對進入地球大氣層的近820個物體發布了預警。而十年前政府發布的預警僅為110個。
“The re-entries happen each and every day,” said Mariusz Slonina, who heads space situational awareness operations for the Polish Space Agency. Huge objects weighing a metric ton or more arrive about once a week, he said.
「再入大氣層的事件每天都在發生，」波蘭航天局空間態勢感知行動負責人馬利烏什·斯洛尼納表示。他指出，重達一噸或更重的巨型物體大約每週就會降落一次。
Much of the debris incinerates in the atmosphere or splashes into the Pacific, but when it does not, it plummets to the ground, uncontrolled and unpredictable.
絕大多數碎片在大氣層中燒毀或落入太平洋，但未能完全燒毀時，它們就會不受控制且不可預測地墜落到地面。
The more objects humans launch into orbit, the more debris they will have to dodge on Earth. More than 300 rockets launched last year, nearly four times as many as a decade ago. That means many more bits and spent parts are expected to drop back.
人類向軌道發射的物體越多，在地球上不得不躲避的碎片也就越多。去年發射的火箭超過300枚，幾乎是十年前的四倍。這意味著預計將有更多殘骸和廢棄部件掉落回來。
Dying satellites are another common detritus of this space age. A good number of the roughly 16,000 satellites in orbit will ultimately come back to Earth.
報廢的衛星是這個太空時代另一種常見的殘骸。在軌道上的大約1.6萬顆衛星中，相當一部分最終都將返回地球。
In 2024, discarded hardware from the International Space Station crashed into a Florida home. Early last year, part of a SpaceX rocket landed near a busy shopping mall in Poznan, Poland. Another piece was found near the city’s airport, according to the Polish Space Agency.
2024年，國際太空站廢棄的硬件砸中了佛羅里達州的一戶民宅。去年年初，SpaceX火箭的一部分掉落在波蘭波茲南一家繁忙的購物中心附近。據波蘭航天局稱，另一塊碎片在該市的機場附近被發現。
廣告
Months later, a defunct Chinese satellite barreled down over Tenerife, one of the Canary Islands of Spain. The three-metric-ton satellite sent shock waves across the region before fragmenting and burning in the air.
幾個月後，一顆失效的中國衛星在西班牙加那利群島之一的特內裡費島上空急速墜落，令該地區陷入一片恐慌，最終這顆重達三噸的衛星在空中解體燒毀。
In October, miners discovered burning debris on a desert road in Western Australia. Recently, giant “space balls” washed ashore on an Australian beach.
10月，礦工們在西澳洲一條沙漠公路邊發現了燃燒的碎片。最近，巨型「太空球」被衝上了澳洲的海灘。
The patchwork of regulation governing rocket launches and orbital junk is no match for the pace of space development.
針對火箭發射和軌道垃圾的監管只是一些東拼西湊的規章，根本趕不上太空發展的步伐。
Elon Musk’s SpaceX, which has more than 10,000 satellites in orbit, has applied to launch a million more. Jeff Bezos’ Blue Origins has similar ambitions and Chinese “megaconstellations” are expected to have hundreds of thousands of satellites.
伊隆·馬斯克的SpaceX公司在軌道上擁有超過1萬顆衛星，並已申請再發射100萬顆。傑夫·貝索斯的藍色起源也有類似的野心；中國的「巨型星座」預計將擁有數十萬顆衛星。
For now, people are more likely to be struck by lightning than by falling space junk. The only documented case is of an Oklahoma woman who was hit by debris in the 1990s. She survived relatively unharmed.
目前而言，人們被雷劈中的概率要高於被墜落的太空垃圾擊中。唯一有記錄的案例是1990年代一位奧克拉荷馬州婦女被碎片擊中，她倖存了下來，幾乎沒有受傷。
But those odds are shifting. A study for the U.S. Federal Aviation Administration concluded that by 2035, debris from SpaceX satellites alone could kill or injure someone every two years.
但這種概率正在改變。美國聯邦航空管理局的一項研究得出結論，到2035年，僅來自SpaceX衛星的碎片就可能每兩年造成一人死亡或受傷。
廣告
The risk diminishes if satellites burn up in the atmosphere, the study concluded. For a while, SpaceX said that all its returning satellites incinerated. Then, in 2024, a chunk of SpaceX metal was found on a Canadian farm. SpaceX, which previously disagreed with the F.A.A. report, did not respond to questions. The company now says that about 5 percent of the mass of some satellites may not disintegrate.
該研究總結道，如果衛星在大氣層中燒毀，風險就會降低。有一段時間，SpaceX表示其所有返回的衛星都會燒毀。然而在2024年，一座加拿大農場上發現了更大塊的SpaceX金屬。曾對管理局報告持不同意見的SpaceX未回應提問。該公司現在表示，某些衛星約5%的質量可能不會分解。
Sean O’Keefe, a Syracuse University professor and former NASA administrator, said: “Humans have a lot of failure of imagination. We just can’t imagine that it will happen to us.”
雪城大學教授、前美國國家航空航天局局長肖恩·奧基夫表示：「人類的想像力時常跟不上。我們就是無法想像這種事會發生在自己身上。」
“All it’s got to do is show up in a major metropolitan area,” he added.
「它只需要掉落在一個主要大都會地區就行了，」他補充道。
Mr. O’Keefe and other experts say that governments and companies should be compelled to use materials that will fully disintegrate.
奧基夫和其他專家表示，應強制要求政府和公司使用能夠完全分解的材料。
No such regulation exists. In the United States, the Federal Communications Commission requires companies to report on the risk of casualties from falling satellites, but the agency mostly focuses on regulating radio frequencies.
目前尚不存在此類法規。在美國，聯邦通信委員會要求公司報告墜落衛星造成人員傷亡的風險，但該機構主要關注於監管無線電頻率。
The F.A.A., which oversees launches, proposed a rule to regulate the disposal of orbital debris but it withdrew the idea in January after the space industry pushed back.
負責監管發射的航空管理局曾提出一項規範軌道垃圾處置的規則，但在航天工業界的反對下，該機構於1月撤回了這一提議。
廣告
United Nations treaties govern liability for space debris that hurts people or damages property, but those are Cold War relics with limited scope. The U.N. issued guidelines for space debris in 2007, when there were far fewer satellites. Those guidelines are nonbinding.
監管對傷人或損壞財產的太空碎片責任的是聯合國條約，但這些都是冷戰時期的產物，適用範圍有限。聯合國在2007年衛星數量少得多的時候發布了太空碎片指南，而這些指南並不具備約束力。
If objects are in free fall, even the best rocket scientists cannot accurately predict where they will land or whether they will fully incinerate. A one-minute error in predicting re-entry changes the landing zone by nearly 300 miles, according to the Aerospace Corporation, a nonprofit research group.
如果物體處於自由落體狀態，即便是最頂尖的火箭科學家也無法準確預測它們會落在哪裡或是否會完全燒毀。據非營利研究機構航天公司稱，預測再入大氣層的時間哪怕出現一分鐘的誤差，落地區域就會相差近480公里。
In January, for example, a fireball pierced the atmosphere over Europe. It was the upper stage of a Zhuque-3 rocket, out of control after a Chinese firm, Landspace, launched it the previous month.
例如，在今年1月，一個火球劃破了歐洲上空的大氣層。這是朱雀三號火箭的上面級，由中國公司藍箭航天於前一個月發射，隨後失控。
Space agencies across the world monitored the object for four days. It was so heavy — about 11 metric tons — that they did not expect it to incinerate.
世界各地的航天機構對該物體監測了四天。它太重了——大約11噸——以至於他們預計它不會完全燒毀。
Governments feared that it could plunge into heavily populated areas. British officials told mobile networks to test the emergency alert system.
各國政府擔心它可能會墜入人口稠密的地區。英國官員要求移動網路測試緊急警報系統。
“The last hours, last orbits were over Europe, and it was really dangerous,” said Mr. Slonina of the Polish Space Agency.
「最後的幾小時、最後的軌道是在歐洲上空，這真的很危險，」波蘭航天局的斯洛尼納表示。
廣告
In the end, the debris splashed down somewhere in the Indian or Pacific Oceans.
最終，碎片墜落在了印度洋或太平洋的某個地方。
Rocket debris is stoking geopolitical tensions.
火箭殘骸正在激化地緣政治緊張局勢。
China’s newest launchpad is in Hainan, an island off the country’s southern coast.
中國最新的發射場位於海南，這是中國南部沿海的一個島嶼。
Many of the surrounding drop zones, where debris is likely to fall, are in contested waters in the South China Sea.
周圍許多可能落入碎片的落區，都位於南海存在爭議的水域。
Filipino fishermen have for years faced confrontations with the Chinese Coast Guard in these waters. These days, they also have to watch for rocket debris.
多年來，菲律賓漁民在這些水域一直面臨著與中國海警的對峙。如今，他們還必須時刻提防火箭殘骸。
The Philippine authorities advise against going to sea whenever China notifies pilots or mariners about a launch from Hainan, said Gay Jane P. Perez, director general of the Philippine Space Agency.
菲律賓航天局局長蓋伊·簡·P·佩雷斯表示，每當中國通知飛行員或船員有關海南發射的消息時，菲律賓當局都會建議不要出海。
Thirteen rocket pieces, some the size of small boats, have washed ashore in the Philippines in the past four years. Most had markings with Chinese characters or parts of the Chinese flag, the space agency said.
過去四年裡，有13塊火箭碎片（有些如小船般大小）被衝上了菲律賓海岸。該航天局表示，大多數碎片上都有中文文字或中國國旗圖案的標識。
“Before, we were looking at access to the sea. Now we’re even getting rocket debris,” Dr. Perez said.
「以前，我們關注的是出海通道。現在我們甚至還要面對火箭殘骸，」佩雷斯說。
Who pays if space junk hits you or your home?
如果太空垃圾擊中你或你的房屋，誰負責？
If the debris belongs to a company or an agency in your country, the local legal system settles the liability questions.
如果碎片屬於你所在國的公司或機構，當地的法律體系將解決責任問題。
If not, your country will likely rely on U.N. treaties to recoup costs.
如果不是，你所在的國家可能會依賴聯合國條約來追回損失。
The liability convention, for one, gives countries the right to compensation if another state’s space object hurts people or damages property. Another treaty, called the Rescue and Return Agreement, allows governments to be paid for the cost of returning space objects.
例如，責任公約賦予相關國家在另一國的太空物體造成人員傷亡或財產損失時獲得賠償的權利。另一項名為《救助和歸還協定》的條約允許政府獲得歸還太空物體的費用補償。
But the treaties were written in the early space age, when countries were more concerned about astronauts becoming prisoners of war or technology being stolen, said Rosanna Deim-Hoffmann, who works on law and policy issues at the U.N. Office for Outer Space Affairs.
但聯合國外層空間事務廳法律和政策官員羅珊娜·戴姆-霍夫曼表示，這些條約起草於航天時代的早期，當時各國更關心的是宇航員成為戰俘或技術被盜。
The terms are now being applied in a more commercial and complex space environment, she added.
她補充說，這些條款現在正被應用於一個更加商業化和複雜的太空環境中。
“The way the treaty was drafted is not the way the treaty is used nowadays,” she said.
「條約起草的方式並非如今使用該條約的方式，」她說。
The first step, then, is confirming where the debris came from. That means everyday people are at the mercy of their governments.
因此，第一步是確認碎片來自何處。這意味著普通老百姓只能依靠政府。
In Kenya, the authorities took months to confirm that the ring was a part of a French rocket that had launched an American television satellite. The debris orbited Earth for more than 16 years before crashing, according to the French space agency.
在肯亞，當局花了數月時間才確認該金屬環是發射美國電視衛星的一枚法國火箭的一部分。據法國航天局稱，該殘骸在墜毀前繞地球軌道運行了超過16年。
Kenya’s government has not formally requested compensation from France, the French Foreign Ministry said. It also has not shared this information with the village residents, who said that the impact cracked nearby homes, according to Daniel Maanzo, a senator who represents the area.
法國外交部表示，肯亞政府尚未正式向法國提出賠償要求。據代表該地區的參議員丹尼爾·馬安佐稱，肯亞政府也沒有向村民透露這一信息，而村民們表示撞擊震裂了附近的房屋。
“The government says they have first to identify the owner of the object, because that’s the one who is the source of negligence,” Mr. Maanzo said.
「政府說他們必須首先確定物體的所有者，因為那才是疏忽的源頭，」馬安佐說。
“That didn’t happen,” he said. “There was no compensation.”
「但那並沒有發生，」他說。「沒有任何賠償。」
