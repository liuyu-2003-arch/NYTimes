戀愛結婚效率為上？日本單身年輕人走進婚介所

For Japan’s Young Singles, Romance Gets a Corporate Middleman

RIVER AKIRA DAVIS, KIUKO NOTOYA • 2026-09-30
True love may be priceless. But in Japan, 20-somethings are flocking to marriage agencies that promise a fast track to the altar — and charge a premium for it.
真愛可能是無價的。但在日本，二十多歲的年輕人正湧向婚姻介紹所，這些機構承諾提供通往婚姻殿堂的捷徑，並為此收取高額費用。
For hundreds of years, through the post-World War II era, Japanese marriages were brokered through “omiai,” a practice in which matchmakers — usually relatives, family friends or bosses — linked singles in their orbit. But the custom fell out of fashion, giving way to organic dating and, eventually, a wave of casual-dating apps.
直至二戰後時期，數百年來日本的婚姻都是通過「相親」來撮合的，根據這種習俗，媒人——通常是親戚、世交或老闆——為身邊的單身人士牽線搭橋。但這種習俗後來不再流行，取而代之的是順其自然的約會，最終被休閑約會應用的浪潮取代。
Now, young adults are enrolling at record rates in marriage agencies, where counselors serve as professional matchmakers. Across Tokyo, advertisements line metro cars, urging commuters to “leave life’s fateful encounters to the pros.” Another declares: “Better one omiai than 100 swipes.”
如今，年輕人正以創紀錄的比例在婚姻介紹所註冊，那裡的顧問擔任著專業媒人的角色。在整個東京，地鐵車廂裡布滿了廣告，敦促通勤者「把人生中註定的相遇交給專業人士」。另一則廣告宣稱：「一次相親勝過滑動螢幕一百次。」
Experts say the shift is being driven by a backlash against frustrations familiar to modern daters globally. With social circles shrinking, young Japanese — tired of the ghosting, catfishing and noncommittal matches rampant on dating apps — are turning to hands-on human guidance for greater efficiency and assurance.
專家表示，種轉變源於全球現代約會者對常見挫折感的一種反噬。隨著社交圈的縮小，日本年輕人厭倦了在約會應用上泛濫的「玩失蹤」、網路身份造假和不願承諾的配對——正轉向需要親力親為的人工指導，以獲得更高的效率和保障。
廣告
Among the new recruits was Taizen Yasuda. A soft-spoken 28-year-old, he said he had run out of patience for algorithm-driven romance.
安田泰然（音）就是這些新註冊者中的一員。這位說話輕聲細語的28歲年輕人表示，他對算法驅動的戀愛已經失去了耐心。
“The process of endless matching, messaging and finally meeting on apps was exhausting,” Mr. Yasuda said. Matchmaking services, he thought, “would be the quickest way to find someone with the exact same goal — marriage.”
「在交友軟體上無休止地匹配、發信息，最後見面，這個過程令人筋疲力盡，」安田泰然說。他認為，相親服務「將是找到有著結婚這個相同目標者的最快方式」。
The practice is resurging as Japan grapples with a shrinking population and fewer young families. Because childbearing in Japan remains overwhelmingly tied to marriage, encouraging more people to wed has become part of the country’s broader push to boost its birthrate.
隨著日本努力應對人口萎縮和年輕家庭減少的問題，這種做法正在復甦。由於在日本，生育仍然絕大程度上與婚姻掛鈎，因此鼓勵更多人結婚已成為該國提高出生率的更廣泛舉措的一部分。
One company has positioned itself at the center of the matchmaking trend: IBJ, a Tokyo-based giant with a nationwide network. Promising to take clients from first meeting to proposal in three months, the company has seen membership and profits soar. It now claims a hand in roughly one of every 25 marriages in Japan.
一家公司將自己定位在這一相親趨勢的中心：總部位於東京、擁有全國性網路的巨頭IBJ。這家公司承諾在三個月內讓客戶從初次見面走向求婚，其會員數量和利潤都在飆升。該公司目前聲稱，日本大約每25場婚姻中就有一場是他們撮合的。
Mr. Yasuda is part of a wave of young singles who have joined IBJ. On a recent Sunday, he sat across from his counselor in a quiet consultation room, clad in a stiffly pressed suit, with a sweep of wavy hair combed to the side. A little over a month after joining the matchmaking service, Mr. Yasuda had already narrowed his prospects to two women. He had just taken one on a weekend drive to see a field of yellow canola blooms.
安田泰然是加入IBJ的年輕單身人士中的一員。在最近的一個週日，他坐在安靜的諮詢室裡，坐在顧問的對面，穿著筆挺的西裝，梳著偏分的捲髮。在加入這項相親服務一個多月後，安田泰然已經將潛在對象縮小到兩名女性。他剛剛帶了其中一位在週末自駕出遊，欣賞一片盛開的黃色油菜花田。
Every member is assigned a consultant who micromanages the courtship process. The consultants advise on everything from grooming and messaging etiquette to date itineraries.
每位會員都會被分配一位顧問，負責細緻管理整個求偶過程。顧問會就各方面提供建議，從儀容打扮、發信息禮儀到約會行程。
廣告
When two members match, their consultants work in tandem behind the scenes, doing what they call “Cupid work” — exchanging and filtering postdate feedback and helping orchestrate every milestone, down to an eventual proposal.
當兩名成員匹配成功時，他們的顧問會在幕後協同工作，做他們所說的「丘比特工作」——交流和篩選約會後的反饋，協助安排每一個重要節點，直至最終的求婚。
“We are providing the kind of support only humans can offer to help people get married as quickly as possible,” said Shigeru Ishizaka, IBJ’s chief executive.
「我們提供只有人類才能提供的那種支持，幫助人們儘快結婚，」IBJ首席執行官石坂茂表示。
In an increasingly digitized society, “there is a deep desire to have a person guiding you through the process of dating,” said Junko Yamada, a lecturer at Tokai University in Tokyo who studies the psychology of romance. Echoing the reassurance of traditional omiai, she said having an expert curate matches offered psychological safety.
在一個日益數位化的社會中，「人們內心深處渴望有人能在約會過程中提供指導，」在東京東海大學研究戀愛心理學的講師山田順子說。她表示，與傳統相親帶來的安心感相應，由專家來精心挑選匹配對象能提供心理安全感。
Ms. Yamada said that today’s youths seemed fixated on maximizing efficiency in every aspect of their lives, and that these agencies appealed to that mind-set by filtering out people who weren’t serious about marriage.
山田順子說，如今的年輕人似乎追求在生活的方方面面實現效率最大化，而這些機構通過過濾掉那些對婚姻不認真的對象，迎合了這種心態。
IBJ’s network of affiliated marriage agencies has varying costs and eligibility rules. Members typically pay about $30 to $100 a month, an enrollment fee of $650 to $1,300 and an additional $1,300 “success fee” if a couple get engaged. It also charges a $1,300 fee if couples violate its rules against “banned activities,” including premarital sex and cohabitation. IBJ said its counselors tracked the progression of their clients’ relationships.
IBJ附屬婚姻介紹所網路有著不同的收費標準和資格規定。會員通常每月支付約30至100美元，註冊費為650至1300美元，如果兩人訂婚，還需額外支付1300美元的「成功費」。如果情侶違反該機構關於「禁止活動」（包括婚前性行為和同居）的規定，該機構還會收取1300美元的罰金。IBJ表示，其顧問會跟蹤客戶關係的發展。
Enrollment is generally limited to applicants with steady incomes. IBJ’s largest directly operated agency accepts only women ages 20 to 39 and men ages 25 to 49.
註冊通常僅限於有穩定收入的申請人。IBJ最大的直營機構只接受20至39歲的女性和25至49歲的男性。
Critics argue that corporate services like IBJ, a company listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange with annual revenue exceeding $130 million, strip the soul from traditional matchmaking, impose rigid timelines on anxious young adults and turn dating into a moneymaking endeavor.
批評人士認為，像IBJ這樣的企業化婚介服務剝奪了傳統相親的靈魂，給焦慮的年輕人強加了死板的時間表，將約會變成了一項賺錢的生意。IBJ已在東京證券交易所上市，年收入超過1.3億美元。
Among the skeptics is Ikuko Kato, a veteran matchmaker who operates from her lace-curtained home just outside Tokyo, filled with hundreds of smiling pictures of couples she has matched. Dressed in pearls and a pink knit suit, Ms. Kato said she had matched more than 1,300 couples over 44 years and claimed that not a single pair had divorced.
加藤郁子（音）就是這些懷疑者之一。這位資深媒人經營著一家家庭式婚介所，它位於東京郊外、掛著蕾絲窗簾，裡面擺滿了數百張她撮合的情侶的照片，每個人都笑容滿面。加藤郁子戴著珍珠項鏈，穿著粉色針織套裝，她說自己在44年裡撮合了1300多對情侶，而且沒有一對離婚。
“They’re too focused on business,” said Ms. Kato, who also charges clients a monthly fee as well as a fee if her matchmaking results in marriage. “My parents were neighborhood matchmakers. It’s in my blood. Modern corporate agents cannot do what I do.”
「它們太注重商業了，」加藤郁子說，她也向客戶收取月費，如果相親成功結婚也會收取一筆費用。「我的父母就是街坊鄰裡間的媒人。這融入了我的血液。現代的企業婚介機構做不了我做的事情。」
廣告
Unlike neighborhood matchmakers, IBJ moves clients through a lucrative, end-to-end program. Beyond matchmaking, its group companies offer wedding planning, financial consulting and housing services, along with referrals to affiliated businesses like eyebrow-grooming salons.
與鄰裡間的媒人不一樣，IBJ為客戶提供一條高利潤的端到端服務流程。除了相親，其集團公司還提供婚禮策劃、財務諮詢和住房服務，還會將客戶推薦給修眉沙龍等合作商家。
Membership has grown about 50 percent over the past five years, and IBJ reported a 44 percent jump in first-half revenue this year, to about $91 million.
過去五年裡，其會員人數增長了約50%，IBJ報告稱，今年上半年的收入激增了44%，達到約9100萬美元。
Mr. Ishizaka, the chief executive, said he viewed IBJ’s mission as more than just money. When he left banking and entered the matchmaking industry in 2000, he said, he hoped to reverse Japan’s severe demographic decline.
首席執行官石坂茂表示，他認為IBJ的使命不僅僅是為了賺錢。他說，當他在2000年離開銀行業並進入婚介行業時，他希望能扭轉日本嚴重的人口下降趨勢。
“Since Japanese society rarely conceives children outside of wedlock, I realized a matching service specifically focused on marriage would have a vital future,” he said.
「由於日本社會很少在婚外生育，我意識到，專門針對婚姻的匹配服務將擁有至關重要的未來，」他說。
Recently, that mission got a dose of encouraging news. In the first six months of 2026, births in Japan rose 0.8 percent, the first increase in 11 years for that period, according to the Ministry of Health, Labor and Welfare. The modest uptick followed two straight years of increased marriages.
最近，這一使命迎來了一個令人鼓舞的好消息。據日本厚生勞動省的數據，2026年前六個月裡，日本的出生人數增長了0.8%，這是11年來該時期內首次出現增長。在這一溫和回升之前，日本的結婚人數已連續兩年實現增長。
Mr. Yasuda, the IBJ client, said he hoped to contribute to those numbers. Part of his desire to marry quickly, he said, comes from wanting to start early enough to raise three or four children.
IBJ客戶安田泰然表示，他希望能為這些數字做出貢獻。他說，他渴望儘快結婚，部分原因是想儘早開始生育，以便有時間養育三四個孩子。
廣告
Mr. Ishizaka sees clients like Mr. Yasuda as evidence that a generation frequently portrayed as retreating from marriage may instead be looking for a more efficient route toward it.
石坂茂將像安田泰然這樣的客戶視為一種證明，這一代人雖然常被描繪為對婚姻退縮，但實際上，他們可能正在尋找一條更高效的通往婚姻之路。
“Surveys consistently show that over 80 percent of young people say they want to marry if they find the right partner,” he said. “I am certain that every married couple we create contributes directly to reversing the birthrate decline.”
「調查始終顯示，超過80%的年輕人表示，如果找到合適的伴侶，他們是想結婚的，」他說。「我確信，我們撮合的每一對已婚夫婦都在為扭轉出生率下降做出直接貢獻。」
