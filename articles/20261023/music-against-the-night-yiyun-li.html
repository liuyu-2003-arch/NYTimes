李翊雲新作：「小有名氣」者的宿命與悲歡

A Major Novel About Minor Fame

RADHIKA JONES • 2026-10-23
MUSIC AGAINST THE NIGHT, by Yiyun Li
《抗衡黑夜的音樂》(MUSIC AGAINST THE NIGHT)，李翊雲著
Is it worse to come within grasping distance of a dream than never to have gotten close? In “Music Against the Night,” her new novel about the intertwined lives of two real-life classical musicians at the turn of the 19th century, Yiyun Li traces the arc of that dubious honor called minor fame. “All people are protagonists only in their own lives,” she writes. “Very few people are granted a line in a masterpiece or a footnote in history.” It’s success in a way, but only the kind you settle for.
夢想已經觸手可及但未能實現，是不是還不如從未接近過？在《抗衡黑夜的音樂》這部以19世紀之交兩位真實古典音樂家交織的命運為主題的新作中，李翊雲勾勒出那種被稱為「小有名氣」的可疑榮譽。她寫道：「所有人唯有在自己的生活裡才是主角。有極少數人能在傑作中留下一句台詞，或在歷史中成為一個腳註。」這在某種程度上固然算是一種成功，但也只是那種人們不得不妥協接受的成功。
Certainly John Field, the composer and pianist born in Dublin in 1782, wants to be more than a footnote. His violinist father and organist grandfather call him a genius, and train him up to be the next Mozart. But as a prodigy, he’s always a few years behind Salzburg’s prize pupil. Belatedness hangs over him as a perpetual condition. He isn’t even the first John in his family. His older brother, of the same name, died before he was born.
毫無疑問，1782年出生於都柏林的作曲家兼鋼琴家約翰·菲爾德渴望獲得的絕不僅僅是一個腳註。他的小提琴手父親和管風琴手祖父稱他為天才，將他往下一個莫扎特的方向培養。但作為神童，他跟薩爾茨堡的天之驕子相比總是滯後個幾年。「遲滯」如同一道永久的陰影籠罩著他。他甚至不是家族裡第一個叫約翰的人——在他出生前，同名的大哥就已經夭折了。
His eventual wife and narrative counterpoint, Adelaide Percheron, known by the nickname Percherette, is an orphan. Born to a mixed-race mother and a French father in Pondicherry, the French colony on the southeast coast of India, she pursues music not as an accomplishment in the Jane Austen sense — a drawing-room pastime for refined ladies — but with secret passion, as a way to express herself beyond her disadvantaged state.
他後來的妻子、與他形成鮮明對照的阿德萊德·佩什隆（暱稱「佩什蕾特」）是一名孤兒。她出生於印度東南海岸的法國殖民地本地治裡，母親是混血兒，父親是法國人。她追求音樂並非出於簡·奧斯汀筆下那種才藝——即精美客廳裡供優雅淑女消遣的玩物，而是懷揣著隱秘的熱情，將其作為突破自身卑微境遇、傾聽與表達自我的途徑。

As Li moves between their stories, similarities emerge. John and Percherette are outsiders on the European continent. Both shake off their pasts to advance their ambition. Music isolates them from their families, sometimes heartbreakingly; it motivates them, guides their movements and brings them inexorably toward each other.
隨著李翊雲在兩條故事線之間穿梭，相似之處顯現出來。約翰和佩什蕾特都是歐洲大陸上的外來者。兩人都試圖掙脫過去以實現野心。音樂將他們與家庭隔離開來，過程有時令人心碎；音樂也驅動著他們，指引著他們的軌跡，並不可避免地將他們推向彼此。
Keeping time is a natural theme for the novel, and Li bends it to her will, often flashing forward to reveal events in her characters’ futures. John’s maestro in London, Signor Clementi, waves off the concept of fate so as not to foreclose on possibility, but fate is built into the structure of “Music Against the Night.” The novel’s first sentence concerns John’s last words. Adelaide, receiving the news of her estranged husband’s death, wastes no time in broaching a frequent preoccupation of Li’s and of literature’s: “What did it signify, to be remembered? When a life ends, night falls. All the daybreaks hence belong to others. Men die, nearly always to be forgotten.” We are acutely aware throughout the novel of the gulf between what these characters desire and what tragedies, major and minor, await them.
保持時間標度是這部小說天然的主題，李翊雲對其運用自如，經常通過預敘揭示角色未來的命運。約翰在倫敦的導師克萊門蒂先生對命運這個概念充滿不屑，認為那會斷送可能性，但命運本身已被嵌入《抗》的結構之中。小說的第一句話談的就是約翰的臨終遺言。阿德萊德在收到分居丈夫的死訊時，毫不停留地觸及了李翊雲乃至整個文學界經常關注的核心議題：「被記住，究竟意味著什麼？當生命終結，夜幕便已降臨。此後所有的黎明都已屬於他人。人死之後，幾乎無一例外會被遺忘。」在整部小說中，我們能敏銳地察覺到這些角色所渴望的一切與等待著他們的悲劇（無論大小）之間存在著巨大鴻溝。
That absence of chronological suspense keeps us firmly rooted in the moment. Li focuses on John’s and Percherette’s comings of age, vividly portraying the sensory and emotional experience of two young people forged in a crucible of precocity. The child John — small for his age, burdened with a stutter and shouldering his family’s expectations of greatness — cuts an especially vulnerable figure.
由於沒有按時間順序的懸念延伸，我們得以緊緊紮根於當下。李翊雲聚焦於約翰和佩什蕾特的成長曆程，生動地呈現了兩個在早熟熔爐中淬鍊而出的年輕人的感官與情感體驗。童年時期的約翰身材矮小、口吃，同時背負著家族對功成名就的期待，展現出一個格外脆弱的形象。
It doesn’t help that as early as 7 he is treated to such truisms as, “What makes one musician different from another is not the notes he can play, but how to turn music into a commodity.” (In fairness, his grandfather has his best interests at heart.) Percherette learns charm and cynicism at the feet of her worldly-wise grandmother, Leonora, and adopts a protective shell so strong that it kept even this sympathetic reader at a distance, until a devastating loss cracks her open.
更無奈的是，早在七歲時他就被迫接受了這樣的至理名言：「區分音樂家的不是他能彈奏什麼音符，而是如何將音樂轉化為商品。」（公平地說，他的祖父確實是出於好意。）佩什蕾特在她精明世故的祖母萊奧諾拉身邊學會了迎合與嘲諷，並披上了一層堅硬的外殼，以至於連抱有同情心的讀者都被拒之門外，直到一場毀滅性的喪失才將她破開。
“Music Against the Night” dives freely into the discourse of art and commerce. In this novel, classical music is not a highbrow calling that floats above market forces; it’s a cutthroat vocation, governed by strategy, rivalry, scandal and survival.
《抗》坦率地深入探討了藝術與商業的議題。在這部小說中，古典音樂絕非凌駕於市場力量之上的高雅天職，而是一門極其殘酷的職業，充斥著策略、競爭、醜聞與生存。
Characters sound off on the virtue of originality (“When people dance, they don’t want to think. You must write music that feels familiar to them”); the balance between inspiration and ownership (John’s first composition owes a debt to Dublin’s chimney sweeps); the anxiety of influence (“If you want to be the next Mozart,” his father tells him, “you’ll have to be at least twice as good as he is. Why? Because all those positions on the ladder — you know the ladder I’m talking about — those rungs are occupied”); the importance of self-promotion (people “spend more to hear us because they’ve heard our names, and they hope it will reflect well on them”); and the technology of the instrument as a vehicle for technique (“Pianofortes are young — they still have a lot of growing to do”). I will be pondering for years to come the riddle Clementi poses to John: “Which came first, an organ or an organist, a harpsichord or a harpsichordist, a pianoforte or a pianist?”
角色們紛紛對原創性的價值發表見解（「人們跳舞時並不想思考，你必須寫出讓他們感到熟悉的音樂」）；探討靈感與所有權之間的平衡（約翰的首部作品受惠於都柏林掃煙囪人）；表達對影響力的焦慮（父親告訴他：「如果你想成為下一個莫扎特，就必須至少比他優秀兩倍。為什麼？因為階梯上的那些位置——你知道我指的是哪座階梯——那些梯級都已經有人佔了」）；強調自我營銷的重要性（人們「願意花更多錢聽我們演奏，是因為聽說過我們的名字，並希望這能為他們增光」）；以及將樂器技術視為演奏技巧的載體（「鋼琴還很年輕，還有很大的發展空間」）。在未來的歲月裡，我都會反覆琢磨克萊門蒂向約翰提出的那個謎題：「先有管風琴還是先有管風琴家？先有羽管鍵琴還是先有羽管鍵琴家？先有鋼琴還是先有鋼琴家？」

In a quietly revelatory moment, Li deploys the title language of her Pulitzer Prize-winning memoir, “Things in Nature Merely Grow,” about the death of her younger son by suicide, six years after her older son died the same way. There, the phrase asserts that, clichés to the contrary, growth occurs unfreighted with meaning or intention, and Li’s garden “is not a metaphor for hope or regeneration.”
在一個看似平靜卻極具啟示性的時刻，李翊雲借用了她那部獲普立茲獎的回憶錄《自然萬物只是生長》(Things in Nature Merely Grow)的標題——該書記錄了其幼子繼長子走後六年同樣自殺離世。與人們常掛在嘴邊的陳詞濫調相反，在該書中，「自然萬物只是生長」強調的是生命生長並不承載任何意義或意圖，而李翊雲的花園也「並非希望或重生的隱喻」。
In the novel, the neutrality of growth applies to John’s future:
在小說中，這種關於發芽生長的中立性同樣適用於約翰的未來：
Things in nature merely grow: roses and hemlocks, beloved puppies and feral cats, prodigies and chimney sweeps, all expanding within their capacities, until they reach that turning point where the world is no longer new. Living then becomes a chore of marking time: hours, days, seasons.
自然界的事物僅僅是生長：玫瑰與毒堇，受寵的小狗與野貓，神童與掃煙囪人，都在其能力範圍內舒展，直到抵達那個世界不再新鮮的轉折點。此後的生存便成了打發時間的苦差事：計算著小時、日子與季節。
Many children die young in “Music Against the Night,” upending our understanding of the order of things: “A child’s death is a negation of time, which no longer has now and then, now and again, now and later, only now and now and now and now.”
在《抗》中，許多孩子過早夭折，顛覆了我們對生命秩序的認知：「孩子的死亡是對時間的否定，時間不再有此時與那時、此時與再次、此時與隨後，只剩下此時、此時、此時與此時。」
The sequence of “now”’s picks up directly from Li’s nonfiction, as if she were passing herself a baton — still carrying these themes, still rigorously thinking them through. We are lucky to be the beneficiaries of her process. I left this novel — her ninth work of fiction — keenly aware that I’d been in the presence of a world-class artist, and I am not talking about John Field.
這種由「此時」構成的序列直接承接了李翊雲的非虛構寫作，彷彿她正在向自己傳遞接力棒——依然承載著這些主題，依然在嚴謹地審視著它們。我們很幸運能成為她這一思考過程的受益者。讀完這部小說——她的第九部虛構作品——我敏銳地意識到自己剛剛領略了一位世界級藝術家的風采，而我說的並不是約翰·菲爾德。
The idea of negating time lands differently in the final scene, though. It’s a coda that doubles as a prelude, as the chapter title puts it, and it carries the surprise and optimism of a fresh start. In fiction, Li can reverse course, resuscitate voices and phrases from the past, and grant them a second chance to be heard.
然而，否定時間的觀念在尾聲階段帶來了截然不同的落腳點。正如章節標題所寫，這是一個兼作序曲的尾聲，蘊含著全新開始的驚喜與樂觀。在虛構世界裡，李翊雲可以逆轉時光，復活來自過去的的聲音與詞句，賦予它們第二次被傾聽的機會。
廣告
“There would never be a novel about Adelaide Percheron,” Li writes some hundred pages in, ventriloquizing her heroine’s youthful frustration with her insignificance. Now there is one. And while I wrote about it, I listened for the first time to a recording of nocturnes by Field. I hadn’t known he invented the form; I only knew that Frédéric Chopin made it famous.
「永遠不會有一部關於阿德萊德·佩什隆的小說，」幾百頁的文字過後，她寫道，這是在借女主角之口宣洩她年輕時因自己的微不足道而產生的沮喪。而如今，這樣的小說誕生了。在我撰寫這篇書評時，我第一次聆聽了菲爾德的夜曲。我此前竟不知道是他創造了夜曲這種體裁，只知道是弗雷德里克·肖邦將其發揚光大。
