真的有人能活到200歲嗎？

Will Anyone Actually Ever Live to 200?

CHRISTIE ASCHWANDEN • 2026-10-16
This article is part of our Longevity special section on living longer and healthier.
本文是我們關於如何活得更長久、更健康的「長壽」專題的一部分。
In 2000, Steven Austad, a biologist, made a provocative claim: “The first 150-year-old person is probably alive right now.”
2000年，生物學家史蒂芬·奧斯塔德提出了一個頗具爭議的說法：「世界上第一個將活到150歲的人現在很可能已經出生了。」
It was a bold proclamation, but Dr. Austad, who studies aging at the University of Alabama, Birmingham, was (and remains) optimistic that science will find new ways to extend life. It’s a confidence shared by a seemingly endless stream of influencers and futurists, some of whom make even wilder predictions. Aubrey de Grey, a biomedical gerontologist and the founder of the SENS Research Foundation, claims that there’s a greater than 50 percent chance that a 40-year-old living today will never die of old age.
這一預言可謂大膽，但在阿拉巴馬大學伯明翰分校研究衰老的奧斯塔德博士當時樂觀地認為，科學將找到延長壽命的新方法，而且他至今仍持這一看法。似乎數不勝數的網紅和未來學家也抱有同樣的信心，其中一些人甚至做出了更為驚人的預測。生物醫學老年學家、SENS研究基金會創始人奧布裡·德格雷聲稱，如今40歲的人有超過50%的可能性永遠不會因衰老而死亡。
But how realistic are these tales of impending longevity? How far can we really extend the human life span?
但是，這些關於人類即將實現長壽的說法究竟有多少可信度？我們究竟能將人類的壽命延長到什麼多少？
Why do people age?
人為什麼會衰老？
Throughout our lives, our bodies are continually healing themselves. What we perceive as aging is really the inflection point at which we’re no longer able to repair damage as fast as it accumulates, said Jamie Justice, an adjunct professor at Wake Forest University School of Medicine and the executive director of XPRIZE Healthspan.
維克森林大學醫學院兼職教授、XPRIZE Healthspan執行主任傑米·賈斯蒂斯說，在我們的一生中，身體一直在不斷自我修復。我們所感知的衰老其實是身體修復損傷的速度開始趕不上損傷累積速度的那個轉折點。
廣告
Over the last few decades, scientists have identified about a dozen hallmarks of aging — biological changes that befall the human body as it ages. The simplest of these are akin to wear and tear. For instance, chromosomes, after a lifetime of dividing every time a cell splits, deteriorate as the protective caps at their ends, called telomeres, get shorter and shorter. When telomeres become too short, chromosomes stop dividing properly.
過去幾十年，科學家已經確定了大約十幾項衰老的標誌性特徵，也就是人體隨著年齡增長而發生的生物學變化。其中最簡單的一些類似於磨損。例如，細胞每次分裂時，染色體都要複製並分離。經過一生中無數次這樣的過程，染色體末端被稱為端粒的保護結構會越來越短，染色體也隨之逐漸退化。當端粒變得過短時，染色體就無法正常分離。
Similarly, mitochondria, which produce energy inside cells, become less efficient over time. These changes can mean oxygen is used less efficiently in your muscles.
同樣，細胞內負責產生能量的線粒體效率也會隨著時間推移而下降。這些變化可能意味著你的肌肉利用氧氣的效率降低。
Proteins, which are crucial to almost every process in the human body, can become damaged or misfolded with age, increasing the risk of diseases like Alzheimer’s. Breakdowns in DNA-repair mechanisms can allow cancers to form.
蛋白質對人體幾乎所有生理過程都至關重要，但隨著年齡增長，蛋白質可能受損或發生錯誤摺疊，從而增加阿爾茨海默病等疾病的風險。DNA修復機制出現故障則可能使癌症得以發生。
Sometimes cells don’t die but stop working as they should, and when this happens they’re said to be senescent, said Stephen Kritchevsky, a professor of gerontology and geriatrics at Wake Forest University School of Medicine. Senescent cells exist in a sort of zombie state: They use energy but they aren’t performing their original function. Some of these cells may secrete chemicals that promote inflammation and tissue dysfunction, which contributes to aging-related diseases like osteoarthritis, atherosclerosis and cancer.
維克森林大學醫學院老年學和老年醫學教授斯蒂芬·凱瑞切夫斯基說，有時細胞並不會死亡，而是停止正常發揮作用，這種情況被稱為細胞衰老。衰老細胞處於一種類似「殭屍」的狀態：它們消耗能量，卻不再履行原有功能。其中一些細胞還可能分泌促進炎症和組織功能障礙的化學物質，從而促成骨關節炎、動脈粥樣硬化和癌症等與衰老相關的疾病。
And then there are stem cells, which are critical for tissue repair, and can become depleted over time, reducing the body’s ability to perform regular maintenance and repair, Dr. Justice said.
賈斯蒂斯博士說，還有對組織修復至關重要的幹細胞。隨著時間推移，幹細胞可能逐漸耗竭，削弱身體日常維護和自我修復的能力。
Can these processes be reversed?
這些過程能夠逆轉嗎？
Researchers understand a lot about how these hallmarks work, which is allowing them to develop ways to slow some parts of the aging process in lab animals, said Dr. John Newman, an associate professor at the Buck Institute for Research on Aging. “We can identify them. We can study them. In the laboratory, we can manipulate them,” he said.
巴克衰老研究所副教授約翰·紐曼博士說，研究人員已經相當了解這些衰老特徵的作用機制，因此得以開發出一些方法，在實驗動物身上減緩某些衰老過程。「我們能夠識別它們，研究它們。在實驗室裡，我們還能對它們進行干預，」他說。
廣告
For instance, there’s a lot of excitement about a class of experimental, anti-aging drugs called senolytics, which aim to clear senescent cells from the body or dilute them to limit their damage.
例如，一類名為「衰老細胞清除劑」的實驗性抗衰老藥物引起了廣泛關注。這類藥物旨在清除體內的衰老細胞，或減少其數量，以限制它們造成的損害。
Another approach focuses on epigenetics, in which scientists examine the biochemical markers that attach to DNA and regulate gene expression. Reprogramming these systems is an attempt to make your cells believe they’re younger than they are, Dr. Kritchevsky said. At least two biotech firms are testing drugs that target this process.
另一種方法著眼於表觀遺傳學，也就是說，科學家研究附著在DNA上、調控基因表達的生化標記。凱瑞切夫斯基博士說，對這些系統重新編程就是試圖讓細胞認為自己比實際年齡更年輕。目前至少有兩家生物科技公司正在測試針對這一過程的藥物。
Several drugs that are already approved by the Food and Drug Administration have also shown hints they might slow aging-related processes, like inflammation. Studies are underway to test three diabetes drugs for longevity benefits — metformin, SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1s.
幾種已經獲得美國食品藥品監督管理局批准的藥物也顯示出可能減緩炎症等衰老相關過程的跡象。目前，研究人員正在測試三類糖尿病藥物是否具有延長壽命的益處，包括二甲雙胍、SGLT2抑制劑和GLP-1類藥物。
XPRIZE Healthspan is also funding work on drugs that affect metabolism, inflammation, tissue repair and neural protection. The Advanced Research Projects Agency for Health (ARPA-H) is similarly backing research on biomarkers and protocols for testing anti-aging therapies.
XPRIZE Healthspan也在資助影響新陳代謝、炎症、組織修復和神經保護的藥物研究。美國衛生高級研究計劃局也在支持有關生物標誌物和抗衰老療法測試方案的研究。
So will we ever halt aging?
那麼，我們最終能夠阻止衰老嗎？
Exciting as this is, much of this longevity work focuses on health span — helping people to live more healthily into their 80s, 90s and even 100s — rather than extending the human life span. Researchers continue to debate how possible that will be in the future. The problem is that their answers are often hypothetical and impossible to prove.
儘管這些研究令人興奮，但許多長壽研究關注的是健康壽命，也就是幫助人們在80歲、90歲甚至100歲時仍然保持健康，而不是延長人類的總體壽命。研究人員對於未來究竟能在多大程度上延長壽命仍爭論不休。問題在於，他們的答案往往只是假設，而且無法得到驗證。
廣告
“If somebody says they have something that’s going to make you live 50 years longer, then you have to wait 50 years to find out whether or not it’s actually true,” said S. Jay Olshansky, a professor of epidemiology and biostatistics at the University of Illinois Chicago.
「如果有人說他們有一種東西能讓你多活50年，那你就得等50年才能知道它是否真的有效，」伊利諾大學芝加哥分校流行病學和生物統計學教授S·傑伊·奧爾尚斯基說。
In the end, aging is unlikely to be halted through any single breakthrough.
歸根結底，單靠某一項突破性成果恐怕無法阻止衰老。
“There’s not just one way that people age, it’s a bunch of systems all stacking on top of each other,” said Coleen Murphy, director of the Lewis-Sigler Institute for Integrative Genomics at Princeton University. “If you cure one, there’s always going to be some next one,” she said.
「人並不是只通過一種方式衰老，而是許多系統的問題相互疊加，」普林斯頓大學劉易斯—西格勒綜合基因組學研究所所長科琳·墨菲說。「即使你解決了一個問題，總還會有下一個問題，」她說。
Kidney, heart and knee replacements have become routine, for example, and it’s possible we’ll get better at swapping out other aging human parts, like bones or muscles. But there are limits to what cells and organs we can keep repairing.
例如，腎臟、心臟和膝關節置換已經成為常規醫療手段，未來我們或許會更擅長替換骨骼或肌肉等其他老化的人體部位。但細胞和器官能夠被不斷修復的程度終究存在極限。
“Some cells can’t replace themselves,” Dr. Murphy said, giving as examples most muscle and brain cells.
「有些細胞無法自我更新，」墨菲博士說，並以大多數肌肉細胞和腦細胞為例。
The brain poses a particular challenge, Dr. Austad said. Most people don’t want to live beyond 100 if their minds aren’t intact, he said, and even if someone could get a new brain, are they still the same person?
奧斯塔德博士說，大腦帶來了尤其棘手的挑戰。大多數人都不希望在心智已經不健全的情況下活到100多。他說，即使一個人能夠換上新的大腦，那還是原來的那個人嗎？
廣告
After Dr. Austad made his proclamation about the world’s first 150-year-old, Dr. Olshansky bet him $150 that he was wrong. The bet stipulated that the person must also be of sound mind for Dr. Austad to win. The money is currently held in an investment fund, to be paid out to the winner (or his descendants) in 2150.
奧斯塔德博士提出世界上將出現第一個150歲壽星的預言後，奧爾尚斯基博士拿出150美元與他打賭，認為他的預測是錯的。賭約規定，要讓奧斯塔德獲勝，那位150歲的老人還必須神志清醒。這筆錢目前存放在一個投資基金中，將於2150年支付給獲勝者（或其後代）。
But for now, the strongest evidence that we cannot live that long might be the simple fact that nobody on record has. The age of the oldest documented living person has not risen since 1997, when a Frenchwoman, Jeanne Calment, died at the purported age of 122. (There’s some debate about her true age.)
但就目前而言，我們無法活那麼久的最有力證據，可能就是一個簡單的事實：有記錄以來，還沒有人活到150歲。自1997年以來，有記錄可查的最長壽命紀錄一直沒有被打破。那一年，法國女性讓娜·卡爾芒去世，據稱享年122歲。（她的真實年齡存在一些爭議。）
“Nobody has lived even 120 years since then,” Dr. Austad said.
「自那以後，甚至沒有人活到120歲，」奧斯塔德博士說。
