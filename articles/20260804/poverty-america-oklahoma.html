那些走投無路的美國窮人

Meet the Americans Who Can’t Afford Haircuts or Toilet Paper

紀思道 • 2026-08-04
After a 1,000-mile road trip across a swath of America, I can’t get one person out of my mind: Trinity Goodman, a cheerful, chatty woman in Oklahoma City who had a bandage on her arm.
在一次穿越美國廣袤土地、行程約1600公里的公路之旅後，有一個身影始終縈繞在我腦海——住在奧克拉荷馬城的特里尼蒂·古德曼，她是一個性格開朗、話匣子打開了就停不下來的女人，手臂上纏著一塊繃帶。
The reason for the bandage? Goodman, 44, survives by selling blood plasma twice a week. She had $3.78 in her checking account, one cent in her savings account to keep it open, and six pennies in her piggy bank.
為什麼要纏繃帶？44歲的古德曼靠每週賣兩次血漿維持生計。她的支票帳戶裡只有3.78美元；儲蓄帳戶裡只有一美分——只是為了讓帳戶不被關掉；存錢罐裡還有六枚一分硬幣。
“That’s my full assets,” she said, smiling in mockery of her own poverty.
「這就是我的全部家當，」她說，臉上帶著一絲自嘲的笑。
“Barely making it,” she added. “It’s really hard and it’s miserable.”
「勉強維生，」她又說，「真的很難，太慘了。」
廣告
She dwells in an America that many of us might have trouble imagining. For five years she staved off homelessness only by staying in the home of a man who was not a romantic partner but who took advantage of her vulnerability, she said, by regularly demanding sex. A heroic local nonprofit, Hope Community Services, helped her get low-income housing for $50 a month, she said; otherwise she would still have to choose between unwanted sex and homelessness.
她生活在一個許多人或許難以想像的美國。她說，五年來，為了免於流落街頭，她只能住在一個男人家裡——那人並非她的伴侶，不過是利用她的脆弱定期索要性關係。她說，一家名為「希望社區服務」的當地非營利機構雪中送炭，幫她申請到了低收入住房，每月只需支付50美元；否則，她將仍然不得不在違背意願的性關係和無家可歸之間做出選擇。
Food stamps, $298 a month, sustain her, but they can’t be used for toilet paper or feminine hygiene products. So she searches for them at church-run pantries — lifelines in much of America.
她靠每月298美元的食品券維持生計，但食品券不能用於購買衛生紙或女性衛生用品。所以她只能靠教堂開設的救濟站來獲取這些東西，這些救濟站是美國許多地方貧困人口賴以生存的生命線。
Haircuts are likewise unaffordable. “I’ve never been to a barber or any of those places,” she said. “Never. I don’t have that luxury.”
剪頭髮同樣是她負擔不起的奢侈。「我從來沒進過理髮店之類的地方，」她說，「從來沒有。對我來說太過奢侈。」
Many sectors of America — and many Americans — are thriving. Yet my sense is that many of us are blind to how many people have been left far behind. So far behind that they scarcely inhabit the same country as the rest of us. And this pain across America is now magnified by President Trump’s cuts in food stamps, Medicaid, mental health funding for children, pregnancy prevention initiatives and other social programs, as well as by rising gas and food prices. Republicans are also imposing tougher work requirements for benefits; this is reasonable in theory, but in practice such requirements have proved costly to administer and end up mostly just driving people deeper into poverty.
美國的許多行業欣欣向榮，許多美國人也過得很滋潤。但我的感覺是，許多人對於有多少人已被遠遠拋在身後渾然不覺。他們落後得如此之遠，以至於他們所生活的美國幾乎和我們其他人生活的美國不是同一個國家。而如今，川普總統削減食品券、醫療補助、兒童心理健康資金、預防懷孕項目和其他社會福利項目，再加上汽油和食品價格上漲，這份苦痛在全美各地愈發沉重。共和黨人還對救濟福利的申領設置了更為嚴格的工作要求；從理論上說，這種要求似乎合理，但實踐證明，這類要求實施起來成本高昂，而且最終往往只是把人們進一步推入貧困。
On this trip, I talked to so many people who are losing food stamps and other benefits and increasingly desperate — and resentful, understandably, of elites who seem indifferent to their struggles. A recent Washington Post-Ipsos poll found that two-thirds of Americans consider groceries unaffordable.
這次旅途中，我遇到了太多正在失去食品券和其他福利的人，他們越來越走投無路——他們對那些似乎對自己的困境漠不關心的精英階層感到憤怒，這完全可以理解。《華盛頓郵報》與益普索聯合開展的最新民調顯示，三分之二的美國人認為食物和日常生活用品的價格已高得難以承受。
Goodman is among those feeling the squeeze, and there appears to be some risk that she will lose her food stamps — and in that case she doesn’t know how she would get by.
古德曼就是感受到這種壓力的其中一員，而且她似乎面臨失去食品券的風險——如果真的發生了，她不知道自己還能靠什麼生活下去。
廣告
I’m on my annual win-a-trip journey, which I started 20 years ago to take a university student on a reporting trip to cover neglected issues. Usually we travel abroad, often in Africa, but this time we explored poverty, addiction and human trafficking in Oklahoma, Arkansas and Kentucky.
我每年都會舉辦「贏得一次報導之旅」活動，這個項目已堅持了20年，每次我會帶一名大學生一同外出採訪，報導那些被忽視的議題。通常我們去海外，往往是非洲，但這一次，我們深入奧克拉荷馬、阿肯色和肯塔基，探訪貧困、成癮和人口販賣問題。
My student winner this year is Brunella Tipismana, who grew up in a working-class neighborhood in Lima, Peru, and just graduated from Yale University. One thing we saw repeatedly on this road trip is that poverty in America is not just about a lack of money, but is often multigenerational and self-replicating.
今年獲獎的學生是布魯內拉·蒂皮斯馬納，她在秘魯利馬的一個工薪階層社區長大，剛從耶魯大學畢業。這趟公路旅行中，我們反覆看到的一點是：美國的貧困並不只是缺錢的問題，它往往還具有代際延續性，會不斷自我複製。
Goodman, for example, says she grew up steeped in trauma, in a toxic family that often abused drugs. She told us that she was raped and sexually abused beginning at the age of 6 or 7. She was 9 when her mother killed herself; afterward, Goodman was raised by other family members, she said. All the trauma left a mark: She says she was clinically diagnosed with complex post-traumatic stress disorder that has left her unable to work. She has applied for disability benefits, and says she was denied, but is now appealing.
就拿古德曼為例，她說自己在創傷中長大的，生活在一個經常濫用毒品、充滿毒性的家庭裡。她告訴我們，她從六七歲起就遭到強姦和性虐待。她九歲那年，母親自殺身亡；之後她由其他親屬撫養長大。所有這些創傷都在她身上留下了印記：她說自己後來被臨床診斷為複雜性創傷後應激障礙，導致她無法工作。她申請過殘疾福利，並稱自己遭到拒絕，目前正在提出申訴。
I asked Goodman what message she would have for Trump as the cuts and work requirements he ordered ripple across the land.
我問古德曼，隨著川普下令的福利削減和工作要求在全國鋪開，她有什麼話想對川普說。
“You do not know what you’re doing to people who are struggling to live,” she said. “You got money in the bank. You don’t know how it feels to struggle. Live in my shoes for one day. You wouldn’t be able to handle it.”
「你根本不知道你對那些掙扎求生的人做了什麼，」她說，「你銀行裡有的是錢。你不知道掙扎是什麼感覺。來過一天我的生活。你根本撐不下去。」
In Pine Bluff, Ark., a largely Black city that is struggling economically, racked by crime and losing population, I visited the home of Latoya Foster, 31, a single mother with six children who has lost food stamps and as a result is behind in rent.
在阿肯色州派恩布拉夫——一個以黑人為主、經濟凋敝、犯罪猖獗、人口流失的城市，我走訪了31歲的拉托亞·福士特的家。她是一個獨自撫養六個孩子的單親母親，如今已失去食品券，房租也因此拖欠。
廣告
Foster works as a shift manager at Taco Bell, earning $13.40 an hour. She relied on $1,172 in food stamps per month — but they were abruptly cut off in May, apparently because of suspicions that two of her children were collecting improper benefits in other states. She denies that and says there was some kind of mistake in the system.
福士特在快餐連鎖塔可鍾擔任值班經理，時薪13.40美元。她過去每月依靠1172美元的食品券生活——但5月這些福利突然停止，原因似乎是有關部門懷疑她的兩個孩子在其他州領取了不應領取的福利。她否認這一點，並說系統裡可能出了什麼差錯。
Nationwide, some 1.5 million children have lost food stamps under Trump’s crackdown over the last year, according to the Center on Budget and Policy Priorities. It notes, and superb reporting by my Times colleague Jason DeParle underscores, that pressure on states to cut costs and reduce error rates may lead to wrongful denials of benefits. In addition, some three million Americans have lost Obamacare health insurance since the beginning of this year, after Republicans cut subsidies to help pay for insurance.
根據預算與政策優先中心的數據，在川普過去一年的打擊行動下，全美約有150萬名兒童失去了食品券。該中心指出——並且我的《紐約時報》同事傑森·德帕爾萊的出色報導也強調，各州在削減成本和降低錯誤率方面的壓力可能導致不當拒絕福利。此外，自今年年初以來，在共和黨削減保險補貼後，約有300萬美國人失去了歐巴馬醫改的健康保險。
Without food stamps, Foster now is behind in her rent — $650 a month for a three-bedroom home in a crime-ridden neighborhood — and accumulating a $25-a-day late fee on top of the rent owed.
失去食品券後，福士特開始拖欠房租——她住在一個犯罪高發社區，三居室房子每月650美元——而且在欠款之外，每天還要增加25美元的滯納金。
“I hit rock bottom daily,” she told me. “The more I work, the harder stuff gets.”
「我每天都在跌到谷底，」她告訴我，「我越努力工作，日子越艱難。」
And then she broke down and wept.
說著，她就情緒崩潰，泣不成聲。
The push to cut benefits for struggling Americans is partly rooted in a desire to trim federal spending, but that’s hard to square with the expense of the Iran war, as well as subsidies for private planes and tax cuts for the wealthiest. It’s also based on a perception that the “undeserving poor” are exploiting the system, but I think we exaggerate the degree to which it is the poor rather than the rich who get away with manipulations. As of 2019, according to ProPublica, the place in America with the highest percentage of tax audits was not a haven of billionaires but rather Humphreys County, Miss., with a majority Black population and a median household income then of roughly $26,000.
推動削減美國困難人群福利的部分動因是壓縮聯邦開支，但這跟伊朗戰爭的巨額支出、對私人飛機的補貼以及給最富有人群的減稅放在一起，實在難以自圓其說。還有一個原因，是人們覺得那些「不值得幫助的窮人」在鑽系統的空子。但我認為，我們把窮人（而非富人）能夠鑽空子得手的程度誇大了。根據ProPublica的數據，截至2019年，美國稅務審計比例最高的地方不是億萬富翁的天堂，而是密西西比州漢弗萊斯縣——那裡黑人占多數，當時家庭收入中位數約為2.6萬美元。
廣告
In any case, the age group most likely to live in poverty — by far — is children, particularly children 2 years old and under, and they can’t be accused of fraud, laziness or personal irresponsibility. Yes, many end up making bad choices, but we fail them before they fail us.
無論如何，最有可能陷入貧困的年齡段（而且遠遠高於其他年齡段）是兒童，尤其是兩歲及以下的兒童。他們無論如何都不應被扣上欺詐、懶惰或個人不負責任的帽子。是的，許多人最終會做出糟糕的人生選擇，但往往是我們先辜負了他們，而不是他們先辜負了我們。
Trump initially proposed eliminating Head Start, the federal program with bipartisan support that serves those vulnerable children in poverty. He later backed off that idea, but congressional investigators found that his administration illegally withheld $12 billion from the program for several months, causing great hardship.
川普最初曾提議取消「啟蒙計劃」，這是一個得到兩黨支持、專門幫助貧困弱勢兒童的聯邦項目。他後來放棄了這一想法，但國會調查人員發現，川普政府曾非法扣留該項目120億美元的資金長達數月，給項目帶來巨大困難。
But while it’s easy to focus on Trump and Republicans cutting Medicaid, food stamps and other benefits, it’s not just the Trump administration that seems to have a myopia about Americans falling behind.
然而，雖然我們很容易把矛頭指向川普和共和黨削減醫療補助、食品券和其他福利，但似乎不僅川普政府對那些被甩在後面的美國人視而不見。
Democrats talk a good game about housing being a human right, but the three states with the highest rates of unsheltered homelessness right now are blue ones: California, Oregon and Hawaii. Liberals rightly protest Trump’s plans to abolish the Department of Education, but it is a handful of red states — especially Alabama, Louisiana and Mississippi — that have shown astonishing progress and now may represent the best hope for American education.
民主黨人經常高談闊論，稱住房是一項人權，但目前無家可歸者中露宿街頭比例最高的三個州恰恰都是民主黨佔優勢的州：加利福尼亞州、奧勒岡州和夏威夷州。自由派人士抗議川普廢除教育部的計劃是有道理的，但恰恰是少數紅州——尤其是阿拉巴馬州、路易斯安那州和密西西比州——取得了驚人的進步，如今或許代表著美國教育最大的希望所在。
A Black fourth-grader in Mississippi is now two and a half times as likely to test proficient in reading as a Black fourth-grader in California. Democrats have every right to call out Trump, but they should also look in the mirror at their own failures to help those left behind.
密西西比州的黑人四年級學生閱讀達標率現在是加利福尼亞州同年級黑人學生的2.5倍。民主黨人完全有權利批評川普，但他們也應該照照鏡子，看看自己在幫助落後人群方面的失敗。
When the problems are structural, we tend to ignore them. Palo Alto, Calif., is full of well-meaning liberals — four-fifths of voters supported Kamala Harris over Donald Trump in 2024 — and it makes its outstanding public schools available to children of all colors and classes … as long as their families can afford an average home price of $3.6 million.
面對結構性問題，我們往往視而不見。加利福尼亞州帕洛阿爾托住著許多善意滿滿的自由派人士——2024年有五分之四的選民支持卡瑪拉·哈利斯而不是唐納德·川普——這裡的優秀公立學校向所有膚色、所有階層的孩子開放……但前提是他們的家庭負擔得起360萬美元的平均房價。
廣告
It’s understandable that many working-class Americans feel betrayed. As I see it, politicians of all parties have been far too complacent about Americans being left behind, even as 1.3 million Americans have died of overdoses since 2000. That’s roughly the number of American service members who have died from all the wars in American history.
許多美國工薪階層感覺遭到背叛，這是可以理解的。在我看來，各黨派的政治人物對美國人被拋在後面都顯得太過麻木不仁，儘管自2000年以來，已有130萬美國人死於藥物過量。這大致相當於美國歷史上所有戰爭中陣亡的美軍人數。
One reason for the complacency is that the problems, at least in their complexity, are often invisible. Americans may see a homeless person shooting drugs, but they won’t see the arc of hopelessness that began with, say, foster care — which at its worst is a government system of child abuse.
這種麻木不仁的原因之一是，這些問題往往是隱形的，至少在其複雜性方面是如此。美國人可能看到一個無家可歸的人在注射毒品，但他們看不到那條始於寄養系統的絕望軌跡——在最糟糕的情況下，寄養系統堪稱一個政府主導的虐待兒童體系。
Courtney Williams, 31, endured that broken foster care system. I met her in Oklahoma City, where she is caring for a new baby and hoping her $298 a month in food stamps won’t be taken away.
31歲的考特尼·威廉斯曾保受那個破碎的寄養系統之苦。我在奧克拉荷馬城遇到了她，她正在照顧一個新生的嬰兒，希望自己每月298美元的食品券不會被取消。
We taxpayers paid for Williams to be incarcerated — three times — but we never provided her the protection and opportunities she needed, especially when she was young and most needed help. And when we don’t invest in the front end, we end up paying at the back end.
我們納稅人曾經三次為威廉斯的入獄埋單——但我們從未給予她所需的保護和機會，尤其是在她年幼、最需要幫助的時候。如果我們不在前端投資，最終只能在後端付出代價。
Some of Williams’s earliest memories are of men sexually assaulting her, perhaps when she was 3 or 4. Her mother was a prostitute with addictions, she said, and at age 6 she saw her younger sister killed and was then put in foster care.
威廉斯最早的記憶之一就是男人對她實施性侵，那時她可能只有三四歲。她說，她的母親是吸毒的妓女，六歲那年她親眼目睹妹妹被殺，隨後她被送進寄養家庭。
From there she bounced around foster homes in seven states, carrying her life belongings with her in a trash bag. When she was 11, one foster home put padlocks on the refrigerator, freezer and food cabinets, keeping her and other foster children hungry, she said.
從那以後，她在七個州的寄養家庭間輾轉，全部家當裝在一個垃圾袋裡。她說，她11歲那年的寄養家庭在冰箱、冰櫃和食品柜上都上了掛鎖，讓她和其他寄養兒童挨餓。
Williams first tried meth at age 12 and at 14 became pregnant by a man in his late 20s who, she later found out, was a convicted sex offender. She put the baby up for adoption and dropped out of school in 10th grade.
威廉斯12歲第一次嘗試冰毒，14歲時懷上了一個20多歲男子的孩子——她後來才知道那人是有前科的性犯罪者。她把孩子送人收養，10年級時輟學了。
Not every foster family was abusive or hosting her for the money, she said; she recalls that one was wonderful and wanted to adopt her. But Williams, by now deeply suspicious of human kindness and full of self-doubt, asked for a different placement.
她說，並非每個寄養家庭都虐待她或是為了錢才收留她。她記得有一個家庭非常好，還想收養她。但威廉斯當時已對人間的善意充滿懷疑，而且極度缺乏自信，她要求換一個寄養家庭。
When Williams was 17, her foster mother stopped getting checks for her — she’s not sure why, but perhaps some snafu in the system — and kicked her out. After that, Williams spent a decade largely homeless, wrestling with drugs, washing up in Dunkin’ Donuts restrooms and periodically getting locked up. Finally a new boyfriend, now husband, who didn’t use drugs helped her rebuild her life, and she says she hasn’t used drugs since last September, when she discovered that she was pregnant.
17歲那年，她的寄養母親不再收到為她發放的補助支票，她不清楚原因，也許是系統出了差錯。於是她被趕了出去。之後，威廉斯有近十年基本無家可歸，與毒品作鬥爭，在唐恩都樂店的洗手間洗漱，時不時被關進監獄。最終，一個不吸毒的新男友（她現在的丈夫）幫她重建了生活。她說自去年9月發現自己懷孕以來就再也沒碰過毒品。
Conservatives often emphasize the importance of “personal responsibility,” and they have a point. Self-destructive behaviors are real. Scholars have said that of millennials who follow the “success sequence” (finish high school, get a full-time job and have children only after marriage), 97 percent avoid poverty by their late 20s.
保守派經常強調「個人責任」的重要性，這有一定道理。自毀行為確實存在。學者們指出，在遵循「成功序列」（完成高中學業、找到全職工作、在婚後才生育）的千禧一代中，97%的人在29歲前擺脫了貧困。
Yet if we want to talk about lack of personal responsibility, we also have to have a conversation about our collective lack of responsibility, about the way we treat some kids as throwaways — or the way we’re now taking food stamps from 1.5 million children.
然而，如果要討論個人責任的缺失，我們也必須探討集體責任的缺失，探討我們如何將一些孩子視為棄兒——或者我們現在如何從150萬名兒童手中奪走食品券。
In Pine Bluff, I spoke to several elderly Black residents who said that in some ways life was actually better in the Jim Crow era. Black people can vote now, of course, and some have thrived.
在派恩布拉夫，我與幾位年長的黑人居民交談，他們表示，在某些方面，吉姆·克勞時代的生活反而更好。當然，黑人現在可以投票了，有些人也過得很好。
But these seniors lamented that the quality of life for the Black population had not progressed as they had imagined. Costs have soared, crime is a threat, obesity is common and ailments like diabetes and hypertension are widespread, taking a toll on well-being. Life expectancy in the county is shorter than in Iran or Bangladesh. Schools are still de facto segregated and lagging; the Pine Bluff school district gets an F from the state.
但這些老人感嘆，黑人群體的生活質量並沒有像他們想像的那樣得到改善。物價飛漲，犯罪肆虐，肥胖普遍，糖尿病和高血壓等疾病也十分普遍，這嚴重損害了人們的健康。該縣的人均預期壽命比伊朗和孟加拉國還短。學校實際上仍然存在種族隔離，教育水平落後；派恩布拉夫學區被州政府評為F級。
“Civil rights were probably better back then,” grumbled Barbara Ollison, 76. I don’t know that she was really arguing that there had been no progress at all, but she’s right that Black children in places like Pine Bluff still struggle to find opportunity in 2026.
「那時候的民權狀況可能反而更好，」76歲的芭芭拉·奧利森抱怨道。我不確定她是否真的認為完全沒有進步，但她說得對，在派恩布拉夫這樣的地方，黑人兒童在2026年仍然難以找到機會。
“You had enough back then to make it,” she told me. “And right now it’s terrible.”
「那時候你擁有的東西已經足夠讓你活下去了，」她對我說。「而現在糟透了。」
One of the few institutions that did not utterly fail places like Pine Bluff was the U.S. military. It offered a path upward and outward, along with skills training, discipline and respect. But some people here see Pete Hegseth, the secretary of defense, ousting Black senior officers and no longer perceive the armed forces as a path of opportunity for people of color.
少數沒有徹底令派恩布拉夫這類地方失望的機構之一是美國軍隊。它提供了向上和向外發展的途徑，同時提供技能培訓、紀律和尊重。但這裡的一些人注意到國防部長彼特·海格塞斯趕走黑人高級軍官，於是不再把軍隊視為有色人種的機會之路。
“The military is out,” said Glayton Johnson, a retired Army veteran who pointed to Hegseth’s impact on the military’s reputation. “No, we’re not going there.”
「軍隊不行了，」退役陸軍老兵格萊頓·強生說，他提到海格塞斯對軍隊聲譽的影響。「不，我們不會去那裡。」
One of the people I most wanted to see on this trip was Emanuel Laster, whom I met a decade ago in Pine Bluff when he was a sweet, smart 13-year-old boy — at risk of joining a gang. You sensed that he could go either way: Maybe he would thrive and go to college, or maybe he would succumb to the gravity of his crime-ridden neighborhood. Emanuel’s bedroom was a portrait of dysfunctional America: It had three televisions, but electricity was supposed to be cut off the day I arrived for nonpayment (his mom had stationed a pit bull to deter the man from the power company from getting near enough to accomplish this).
此行我最想見的人之一是伊曼紐爾·拉斯特。十年前我在派恩布拉夫認識他時，他是個可愛聰明的13歲男孩——正面臨加入幫派的風險。你能感覺到他可能走向兩種方向：也許他會茁壯成長，進入大學，也許他會屈服於那個犯罪猖獗社區的引力。伊曼紐爾的卧室堪稱功能失調的美國的寫照：裡面有三台電視，但我到達那天，因為欠費，電力本應被切斷（他媽媽養了一隻比特犬，阻止電力公司的人靠近完成斷電）。
What had become of Laster? It turned out that he never finished high school, had tangles with the law and is now in jail, awaiting trial this month on federal gun charges for possessing a handgun that had been converted into an automatic weapon. Visits in the jail weren’t permitted, but I spoke to him by phone. I asked him what had gone wrong, and he said, “I hung out with the wrong crowd.” His mother, when I visited her, said the same thing.
拉斯特如今怎麼樣了？原來他沒能完成高中學業，有過數次法律糾紛，現在因持有被改裝成自動武器的手槍而面臨聯邦槍枝指控，本月將接受審判。監獄不允許探視，但我通過電話與他交談。我問他哪裡出了問題，他說：「我和壞人混在一起了。」我去拜訪他母親時，她也說了同樣的話。
Laster told me that he had two boys, ages 4 and 6, with a former girlfriend, so I located them and their mother, Autumn Holman. She and her sons had been evicted and were homeless, couch surfing temporarily in a crowded apartment with a cousin and trying desperately to find a place to live.
拉斯特告訴我，他和前女友有兩個四歲和六歲的男孩。於是我找到了他們，還有他們的母親奧特姆·霍爾曼。霍爾曼和兒子們被趕出住所，無家可歸，暫時擠在表親擁擠的公寓裡借宿，正在拚命尋找住處。
Holman works in the kitchen of a nursing home, but her car isn’t working, so commuting is a daily crisis. She’s trying to get food stamps and thought they would begin in July, but they haven’t materialized.
霍爾曼在一家養老院的廚房工作，但她的車壞了，所以每天通勤都成了難題。她正在申請食品券，原以為7月就能開始領取，但一直沒有收到。
She said that she loved Laster, her high school sweetheart, but that they had quarreled because he was an irresponsible parent. “He got mad because I had to ask him for money for his kids,” she said, adding that she simply wanted him to pay for little things like the boys’ haircuts.
她說自己深愛著高中時的戀人拉斯特——但他們發生了爭執，因為他是個不負責任的父親。「他生氣是因為我不得不向他要錢養孩子，」她說，她還說，她只是想讓他支付一些小開銷，比如孩子們的理髮費。
“He knows for a fact that I’m going to be on his tail like I’m his mama,” she added. “Who else is going to do it? ‘You got kids,’ I tell him, ‘and I didn’t have them by myself.’”
「他心裡清楚，我會緊盯著他，就好像我是他媽媽，」她還說，「還有誰會去做這些事？『你有孩子，』我告訴他，『他們不是我一個人生的。』」
Laster’s sons are beautiful children full of potential, but they are now growing up without a dad, just as Laster himself did. Visiting that crowded home, I kept thinking back to Emanuel a decade ago as an equally sweet child, about the obstacle course of life that Black boys face in Pine Bluff, and I wondered if his children were on a trajectory to replicate his path.
拉斯特的兒子們都是充滿潛力的可愛孩子，但他們現在正像拉斯特當年一樣，在沒有父親陪伴的情況下長大。走訪那個擁擠的家，我不斷回想起，10年前的伊曼紐爾同樣是個可愛的孩子，想到派恩布拉夫的黑人男孩們要面對的人生障礙，我不禁會想，他的孩子們是否正在重蹈他的覆轍？
