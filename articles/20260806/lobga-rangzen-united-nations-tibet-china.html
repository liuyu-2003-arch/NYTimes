洛嘎讓贊最後的抗爭

The Tibetan Who Set Himself on Fire in Manhattan

王月眉 • 2026-08-06
It was 91 degrees in Manhattan and even hotter on the pavement, but the Tibetans gathered as planned outside the Chinese Consulate. Amid the waving flags and synchronized chants, one of the protesters, Ngawang Tashi, looked for his friend Lobga Rangzen.
曼哈頓的氣溫達到攝氏33度，人行道上更是熾熱，但藏人們仍按計劃聚集在中國領事館外。在揮舞的旗幟和整齊劃一的喊聲中，抗議者之一阿旺扎西正在尋找他的朋友洛嘎讓贊。
Lobga Rangzen was a stalwart of events like this. His friends joked that he was happier at protests than on his birthday. At protests, he felt most useful to the cause of Tibetan independence from China — a cause to which he was so devoted, he had even put it in his name: Rangzen means “independence” in Tibetan.
洛嘎讓贊是此類活動的中堅力量。朋友們開玩笑說，他在參加抗議的時候比過生日還開心。他覺得通過抗議能對西藏的獨立運動做出最大的貢獻——他對這一事業非常執著，甚至把它放進了自己的名字裡：讓贊(Rangzen)在藏語中意為「獨立」。
Ngawang Tashi scanned the crowd once, then again. It was strange that Lobga Rangzen wasn’t there.
阿旺扎西在人群中掃視了一圈，又掃視了一圈。洛嘎讓贊不在，這很奇怪。
The next evening, July 2, he was at Costco when another friend called.
第二天晚上，即7月2日，他正在Costco時另一個朋友打來電話。
“Come to the United Nations right now,” the friend said. “He is in front of there and burning.”
「快來聯合國，」朋友說。「他在大樓前，身上著火了。」
Around the world, many people had already seen what Ngawang Tashi was just learning: Lobga Rangzen, 52, had set himself on fire in front of the United Nations headquarters in Manhattan, streaming the act live on Facebook. He had planted a Tibetan flag on the pavement and taken out fliers that demanded FREE TIBET and CHINA OUT OF TIBET. Then he lit his chuba, or Tibetan robe, on fire.
在世界各地，許多人已經看到了阿旺扎西剛剛得知的事情：52歲的洛嘎讓贊在曼哈頓的聯合國總部大樓前自焚，並在Facebook上進行了直播。他在人行道上插了一面西藏旗幟，並拿出傳單，要求「自由西藏」和「中國滾出西藏」。然後，他點燃了自己的楚巴，即藏袍。
Ngawang Tashi couldn’t make sense of the news. He had just seen Lobga Rangzen a few days earlier, at their weekly Tibetan dance group. Hadn’t Lobga Rangzen just promised to make his famous Tibetan sausages for a party a few days later? Surely, he reassured himself, his friend would be fine.
這個消息讓阿旺扎西難以置信。幾天前，他剛在每週一次的藏族舞蹈組見到過洛嘎讓贊。洛嘎讓贊不是剛答應過幾天後在聚會上做他著名的藏式香腸嗎？他安慰自己，朋友一定會沒事的。

But when he arrived at the hospital where firefighters had taken Lobga Rangzen, a nurse told him that he was too late. He could go in just to pay his last respects.
但當他趕到消防員送洛嘎讓贊去的醫院時，一名護士告訴他已經太晚了。他進去只能做最後的告別。
Since then, Ngawang Tashi has been grieving. But as politicians, including Mayor Zohran Mamdani and Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, issued statements of mourning and major media outlets wrote about his friend’s death, he, and many Tibetan activists, have also struggled with a grim realization. Lobga Rangzen’s unthinkable act — the first known self-immolation by a Tibetan in the United States — may have achieved one of his greatest goals, however briefly: He made the world pay attention to Tibet.
從那時起，阿旺扎西一直沉浸在悲痛中。但隨著包括市長佐赫蘭·馬姆達尼和眾議員亞歷山德里亞·奧卡西奧-科爾特斯在內的政治人物發表哀悼聲明，以及主流媒體報導他朋友的去世，他和許多藏人活動人士也面臨著一個殘酷的現實。洛嘎讓贊令人不可思議的舉動——已知的第一起藏人在美國自焚事件——可能在短時間內實現了他最大的目標之一：讓世界關注西藏。
“The other activities we Tibetans were doing, nobody is going to care about that,” Ngawang Tashi said. “Now everybody is talking.”
「我們藏人正在做的其他活動沒有人會關心，」阿旺扎西說。「現在大家都在討論。」
A week after Lobga Rangzen’s death, Ngawang Tashi sat inside his friend’s bedroom on the third floor of a narrow house in Queens. The two had shared the place for several years. Beside Ngawang Tashi was Lobga Rangzen’s niece, Sonam Choedon, who had lived there for several months after moving to the United States from India last year.
洛嘎讓贊去世一周後，阿旺扎西坐在他朋友位於皇后區一棟狹窄房子三樓的卧室裡。兩人曾在這裡合租了幾年。阿旺扎西身旁是洛嘎讓贊的侄女索朗曲珍，她在去年從印度搬到美國後，在這裡住了幾個月。
All around them were reminders of the time they had spent with Lobga Rangzen, and how closely it had been intertwined with protest. On the dresser was a snapshot of Lobga Rangzen with friends, including Ngawang Tashi, during a 13-day walk they made from New York to Washington in 2014 for Tibetan rights. On a table, there was a framed photograph of Sonam waving a Tibetan flag at a protest her uncle had taken her to last summer.
他們周圍的一切都在提醒著他們與洛嘎讓贊共度的時光，以及這段時光與抗議活動交織得多麼緊密。梳妝台上擺著一張洛嘎讓贊與朋友們（包括阿旺扎西）的合影，那是他們在2014年為了爭取藏人權益而從紐約徒步13天前往華盛頓期間拍攝的。桌子上擺著一張裝在相框裡的照片，照片上的索朗在去年夏天叔叔帶她參加的一次抗議活動中揮舞著西藏旗幟。
A tangle of plastic badges hung from the curtain rod, showcasing volunteer stints over the years: as a security guard when the Dalai Lama, Tibet’s exiled spiritual leader, visited New York; as a videographer for an advocacy event. Awards from Tibetan activism groups covered the walls. A brightly colored tapestry of the Potala Palace, the former residence of the Dalai Lamas, was pinned over his bed. The only non-Tibetan element was a vibrating exercise machine, with a few shirts neatly draped over the arms.
窗簾桿上掛著一串塑料徽章，展示著這些年來的志願者經歷：西藏流亡精神領袖達賴喇嘛訪問紐約時擔任安保人員；在一次倡導活動中擔任攝像師。藏人活動團體的獎項貼滿了牆壁。他的床上釘著一張色彩鮮艷的掛毯，上面印著曾經的達賴喇嘛寢宮布達拉宮。唯一非西藏元素的物品是一台震動健身機，扶手上整齊地掛著幾件襯衫。
“I used to dance with him in this room,” Ngawang Tashi said, gesturing to the narrow strip of floor between the table and the bed, where they had followed along with Tibetan music videos.
「我以前常在這個房間裡和他一起跳舞，」阿旺扎西指著桌子和床之間那塊狹長的地板說，他們以前曾跟著藏語音樂影片在那裡跳舞。

Sonam smiled. Sometimes, when her uncle walked toward his friends on the street, she said, “you could see him come dancing.”
索朗笑了。她說，有時當她叔叔在街上向朋友們走去時，「你可以看到他是跳著舞走過來的。」
Lobga Rangzen’s room was a shrine to a homeland he hadn’t seen in more than 30 years. He was born in a remote part of eastern Tibet, as Lobsang Palden. (Tibetans usually do not have family names, only given names.) China had occupied Tibet since the 1950s, and he grew up hearing about how his family had resisted that occupation, Sonam said. Three of his uncles fought the Chinese Army in the 1950s, with one killed in combat and another dying in prison.
洛嘎讓贊的房間是一座供奉著他30多年未見的故鄉的神龕。他出生於西藏東部的一個偏遠地區，原名洛桑巴登。（藏人通常沒有姓氏，只有名）。索朗說，中國自20世紀50年代起佔領西藏，洛嘎讓贊是聽著家人如何抵抗的故事長大的。他的三個伯父在50年代曾與中國軍隊作戰，其中一個在戰鬥中犧牲，另一個死於獄中。
As a child, Lobga Rangzen was sent to a monastery for education. But the Chinese government allowed only one monk per family, and a relative had already taken his vows.
小時候，洛嘎讓贊被送到寺廟接受教育。但中國政府規定每個家庭只允許有一名僧人，而他的一位親戚已經受戒了。
So in 1992, when he was 18, he and an uncle fled. The journey to escape Tibet is treacherous, often involving weeks of trekking through the Himalayas at night to avoid detection by Chinese guards. Lobga Rangzen’s parents and three siblings, unable to make the journey, stayed behind. But Lobga Rangzen and his uncle made it to a refugee center in Nepal run by the Tibetan government-in-exile.
因此，在1992年他18歲時，他和一位叔叔逃跑了。逃離西藏的旅程非常危險，通常需要在夜間穿越喜馬拉雅山徒步數週，以避免被中國邊防發現。洛嘎讓贊的父母和三個兄弟姐妹無法完成這段旅程，他們留了下來。但洛嘎讓贊和他的叔叔到達了由西藏流亡政府在尼泊爾設立的難民中心。
From there, Lobga Rangzen was sent to a monastery in southern India, where he rallied other monks to attend protests against China.
而後洛嘎讓贊被送到印度南部的一座寺廟，在那裡，他號召其他僧人參加反華抗議活動。
In 2006, he arrived in the United States. Sonam wasn’t sure why he left India, but said that monasteries often sent members overseas to share their beliefs. In New York, Lobga Rangzen received asylum. He eventually left the monkhood, and threw himself into activism.
2006年，他來到美國。索朗不確定他為什麼離開印度，但表示寺廟經常派員去海外傳播他們的信仰。在紐約，洛嘎讓贊獲得了庇護。他最終還俗，投身於活動主義。
At the time, Tibet was drawing global attention. Ahead of the 2008 Olympics in Beijing, monks and nuns in Tibet led mass protests against Chinese rule. The Chinese government responded violently, with rights groups saying that possibly hundreds of people died. (The Chinese government said the death toll was 22.)
當時，西藏正在吸引全球的關注。在2008年北京奧運會之前，西藏的僧尼領導了反對中國統治的大規模抗議活動。中國政府採取了暴力回應，人權組織表示可能有數百人死亡。（中國政府稱死亡人數為22人）。
Leaders worldwide expressed outrage. President George W. Bush called for China to hold talks with the Dalai Lama.
世界各地的領導人表示憤怒。喬治·W·布希總統呼籲中國與達賴喇嘛舉行會談。
廣告
In New York, Lobga Rangzen was eager to contribute to the momentum. He built floats for regular protests outside the Chinese Consulate and staged hunger strikes near the United Nations. He shared photos of himself on social media meeting with politicians — Democrats and Republicans — at the Capitol.
在紐約，洛嘎讓贊渴望繼續推動這一勢頭。他為中國領事館外的定期抗議活動製作花車，並在聯合國附近舉行絕食抗議。他在社群媒體上分享了自己在國會山莊與兩黨政治人士會面的照片。
Initially, he had worked in construction, but his bosses wouldn’t let him take time off for protests. He turned to driving for Uber instead. He never married, which, his friends said, didn’t bother him. He could devote himself completely to the cause.
他最初在建築行業工作，但他的老闆不允許他請假去參加抗議。於是他轉而為優步開車。他從未結婚，朋友們說他對此並不介意。他可以全身心地投入到這項事業中。
But he did occasionally acknowledge the pain of one sacrifice: Ten years ago, he cut off direct contact with his family in Tibet, fearing that his work would jeopardize their safety. He communicated with them only through a cousin in the United States, Lobsang Samdup.
但他偶爾也會承認一項犧牲帶來的痛苦：十年前，由於擔心自己的工作會危及家人的安全，他切斷了與西藏家人的直接聯繫。他只通過在美國的一個堂兄弟洛桑桑珠與他們聯絡。
Samdup said he had asked Lobga Rangzen to reconsider, telling him it was OK to think about himself more. “He said, ‘That’s not even a question,’” Samdup recalled.
桑珠說，他曾勸洛嘎讓贊重新考慮，告訴他多為自己著想也沒什麼錯。「他說，『這根本無需考慮，』」桑珠回憶道。
For all his fervor, though, Lobga Rangzen was a cheerful figure. He always sat in the front row for performances by a stand-up comedian friend, though he knew he’d be picked for punchlines. He enjoyed hiking in upstate New York, singing as he trekked.
儘管行事上如此熱忱熾烈，洛嘎讓贊卻是一個歡快的人。他總是坐在前排觀看一位脫口秀演員朋友的演出，儘管他知道自己會成為笑料。他喜歡在紐約上州徒步旅行，一邊走一邊唱歌。
After late-night Uber shifts, he would meet up around 3 a.m. with other Tibetan driver friends at Indian restaurants in Midtown Manhattan, regaling them with gossip or folk tales.
在深夜開完優步後，他會在凌晨3點左右與其他的藏人司機朋友在曼哈頓中城的印度餐廳聚會，用八卦或民間故事逗他們開心。

“Sometimes I’d joke with him, ‘Hey, are those stories real?’” said one of those friends, Dechen Sangpo. “He would be like, ‘Doesn’t matter, you guys like it, right?’”
「有時我會和他開玩笑，『嘿，那些故事是真的嗎？』」其中一位朋友德慶桑波說。「他就會說，『不重要，你們喜歡就好，對吧？』」
In recent months he was often found at the High Asia Research Center, a basement library in Queens set up by a Tibetan writer, Jamyang Norbu. Lobga Rangzen had salvaged old bookshelves from the street to help fill it.
最近幾個月，人們經常在皇后區的一家地下室圖書館——高地亞洲研究中心看到他，該中心由藏人作家加央諾布建立。洛嘎讓贊從街上撿來舊書架，幫著把書架填滿。
He’d been especially enthusiastic about a pamphlet that Jamyang Norbu had written about Tibet’s history as an autonomous state. What if, Lobga Rangzen suggested, he turned the pamphlet into a slide show and drove cross-country with it playing on a screen atop his car? “He has ideas all the time,” Jamyang Norbu said. Even if some didn’t pan out, he said, “at least someone is making the effort to work things out.”
他對加央諾布寫的一本關於西藏作為自治國家歷史的小冊子特別感興趣。洛嘎讓贊建議，如果把這本小冊子做成幻燈片在汽車頂部的螢幕上播放，然後開車橫跨全國，會怎麼樣？「他總有各種點子，」加央諾布說。他說，即使有些想法沒有實現，「至少有人在努力想辦法解決問題。」
The first person to self-immolate in Tibet to protest Chinese rule was a 24-year-old monk, in 2009.That spring, when the government forbade memorials to the victims of the previous year’s protests, the monk, Tapey, set himself on fire.
2009年，一名24歲的僧人成為西藏首位為抗議中國統治而自焚的人。那年春天，政府禁止為前一年抗議活動的遇難者舉行紀念活動，這位名叫扎白的僧人點燃了自己。
Over the next decade, more than 150 other Tibetans would do the same, with 28 in November 2012 alone, according to the Tibetan exile government.
據西藏流亡政府稱，在接下來的十年裡，又有150多名藏人效仿這一行為，僅在2012年11月就有28人自焚。
Self-immolation is, for many people, perhaps the most unfathomable of political protests. When people have chosen it as their final act — whether they were Tibetans, or Vietnamese monks protesting religious suppression in the 1960s, or more recently, American activists drawing attention to climate change or the war in Gaza — the public response has been not only horror and sympathy, but also bewilderment.
對許多人來說，自焚或許是最難以理解的政治抗議形式。當人們將其作為最後的抉擇——無論是藏人，還是20世紀60年代抗議宗教鎮壓的越南僧人，抑或近期為引起人們對氣候變化或加薩戰爭關注而自焚的美國活動人士——公眾的反應不僅包含著恐懼和同情，還夾雜著困惑。
Scholars have said self-immolation can become a last resort when no other form of protest is available, at least in Tibet. (Nearly all the Tibetan self-immolations have occurred inside Tibet, with a few in India or Nepal.) “If Tibetans saw even a sliver of an opportunity to hold demonstrations, then they would not resort to self-immolation,” Tsering Woeser, a leading Tibetan poet and scholar, has written.
學者們表示，當沒有其他形式的抗議可用時，自焚可能成為最後的手段，至少在西藏是這樣。（幾乎所有藏人自焚事件都發生在西藏內部，少數發生在印度或尼泊爾）。「如果藏人看到哪怕是一絲舉行遊行示威的機會，他們就不會採取自焚手段，」西藏著名詩人兼學者唯色曾這樣寫道。
In New York, Lobga Rangzen protested all the time, to little apparent effect. As China’s economy grew, other countries became more reluctant to challenge it on human rights issues. Even some fellow Tibetans seemed to be resigned to Chinese rule.
在紐約，洛嘎讓贊一直在抗議，但似乎收效甚微。隨著中國經濟的發展，其他國家越來越不願在人權問題上挑戰中國。甚至一些藏人同胞似乎也順從於中國的統治。
The repression in Tibet, though, was unrelenting. Earlier this year, China announced that it would enact a new law to promote “ethnic unity.” The law mandates the use of Mandarin Chinese as the language of instruction in schools, and says that the Chinese government would pursue people who create “ethnic division” — the government’s label for political activism by ethnic minorities — even overseas.
然而，在西藏的鎮壓並未停止。今年早些時候，中國宣布將頒佈一項促進「民族團結」的新法律。該法律強制要求在學校使用普通話作為教學語言，並表示中國政府將追究製造「民族分裂」的人——這是政府給少數民族政治活動貼上的標籤——即使這些人身在海外。
廣告
Lobga Rangzen spent hours each week at the Tibetan library, talking to Jamyang Norbu about the need for new tactics. Jamyang Norbu sensed that the cross-country drive idea wasn’t satisfying him.
洛嘎讓贊每週花幾個小時在藏人圖書館與加央諾布討論尋找新策略的必要性。加央諾布感覺到，橫跨全國之旅的想法並不能讓洛嘎讓贊滿意。
“But you know, other than that, what can you do now? This is the problem he faced in life. We don’t have resources,” he said. “He was running out of ideas.”
「但是你知道，除此之外，現在還能做什麼？這是他一生面臨的問題。我們沒有資源，」他說。「他已經無計可施了。」
Lobga Rangzen did not raise the idea of self-immolation to his niece, cousin or any of his friends. Only Ngawang Tashi said that he once talked about it — more than 10 years ago. During the peak of the immolations in Tibet, Lobga Rangzen had asked him for advice on writing a last statement. Ngawang Tashi told him he was more useful alive. Lobga Rangzen never mentioned it again.
洛嘎讓贊沒有向他的侄女、堂兄弟或者任何朋友提出過自焚的想法。只有阿旺扎西說他曾經提起過——那是10多年前的事了。在西藏自焚最高峰的時期，洛嘎讓贊曾向他請教過如何撰寫最後的聲明。阿旺扎西告訴他，他活著更有用。洛嘎讓贊就再也沒提過這件事。
But in recent months the new law had weighed heavily on him. He called one friend to say the Chinese government was trying to cut out Tibetans’ tongues. He told another that Tibetans overseas might not be safe, either.
但最近幾個月來，這項新法律讓他感到非常沉重。他打給一個朋友說，中國政府正在試圖割掉藏人的舌頭。他告訴另一個朋友，海外的藏人可能也不安全。
On June 30, the day before the law went into effect, he called Dechen, his friend who had also driven Ubers. Dechen had recently finished law school, and Lobga Rangzen congratulated him. But, he said, Dechen should remember not to focus only on money. “I want you to contribute for Tibetan independence,” he told him.
6月30日，也就是該法律生效的前一天，他打給了也開過優步的朋友德慶。德慶最近剛讀完法學院，洛嘎讓贊向他表示祝賀。但他還說，德慶應該記住，不要只關注錢。「我希望你為西藏獨立貢獻力量，」他告訴他。
Dechen was surprised. Lobga Rangzen rarely offered him unsolicited advice. “I was like, ‘OK, why are you telling me this?’” Dechen said. “He was like, ‘It just came to mind, so I just wanted to share with you.’”
德慶感到很驚訝。洛嘎讓贊很少主動向他提供建議。「我當時想，『好吧，你為什麼跟我說這個？』」德慶說。「他說，『只是突然想到了，所以想和你分享。』」
The next day, he called again, to say hello from a hike upstate, where he’d gone with his niece and several friends. It was July 1, the day of the demonstration against the new law at the Chinese Consulate. Lobga Rangzen didn’t mention the protest.
第二天，他又打來電話，說他正和侄女及幾位朋友在紐約州北部徒步旅行，順便問候一下。那是7月1日，在中國領事館外舉行反對新法遊行示威的日子。洛嘎讓贊沒有提到抗議的事。
On July 2, he called for a third straight day. Dechen suggested grabbing lunch together, but Lobga Rangzen said it was too hot. Besides, he said, he had to take care of some things.
7月2日，他連續第三天打來電話。德慶建議一起吃午飯，但洛嘎讓贊說天氣太熱了。此外，他說他還得處理一些事情。
“Call me back when you’re done,” Dechen said. “Let’s meet.”
「處理完了給我回電話，」德慶說。「我們見個面。」
“All right,” Lobga Rangzen said.
「好的，」洛嘎讓贊說。
Four hours later, Lobga Rangzen sat down, cross-legged, under metal scaffolding across from the United Nations building. For six minutes, he spoke into his phone camera, denouncing what he called China’s efforts at “completely erasing Tibet’s existence” and asking Tibetans in exile to unite. He spoke calmly, even as sweat beaded on his forehead from the sweltering heat.
四個小時後，洛嘎讓贊在聯合國大樓對面的金屬腳手架下盤腿坐下。在六分鐘的時間裡，他對著手機鏡頭髮表講話，譴責他所稱的中國「徹底抹去西藏存在」的努力，並呼籲流亡藏人團結起來。儘管酷熱難耐，額頭上汗珠滾落，他的語氣依然沉著。
“If I do a big action today, I want you to know it is not for any personal reason. It is not because I have nothing to eat or nothing to wear, but because I am doing this for my country,” he said.
「如果我今天做出一個重大行動，我希望你們知道，這不是為了任何個人原因。不是因為我吃不飽、穿不暖，而是為了我的祖國，」他說。
“I don’t want you to mourn for me. I want you to continue the struggle for Tibetan independence.”
「我不希望你們為我哀悼。我希望你們為西藏獨立而繼續奮鬥。」
Moments later, a 911 caller reported a man on fire.
片刻之後，一位911報警者稱，有一名男子身上著火了。
In retrospect, Lobga Rangzen’s friends realized there may have been signs. He and Dechen usually spoke a few times a month, not three days in a row. At his last dance group meeting, he had given a shirt to Ngawang Tashi, insisting he take it even when Ngawang Tashi said he had plenty.
事後回想起來，洛嘎讓贊的朋友們意識到，可能曾經有過徵兆。他和德慶通常一個月只通幾次電話，而不是連續三天。在最後一次的舞蹈小組聚會上，他送了阿旺扎西一件襯衫，即使阿旺扎西說他有很多襯衫，他仍堅持要阿旺扎西收下。
No one could pinpoint when Lobga Rangzen had started planning but they were sure that his decision had not been spontaneous, but strategic. At the heart of activism, he knew, was the competition for attention. He had just been waiting for the moment when he thought he could have impact.
沒有人能確切指出洛嘎讓贊是從什麼時候開始策劃這件事的，但他們確信，他的決定不是一時衝動，而是經過深思熟慮。他知道，行動主義的核心在於爭奪關注。他一直在等待那個他認為自己能夠產生影響的時機
Some of his friends quietly acknowledge that his action might not make a difference with the U.S. government or the United Nations. Still, their responsibility is to try to make sure it does.
他的一些朋友私下承認，他的行動或許無法對美國政府或聯合國產生實質影響。儘管如此，他們的責任是盡力確保它能產生影響。
In the weeks since Lobga Rangzen’s death, hundreds of people have turned out for a stream of events in his honor. There was a 6 a.m. prayer service, a march to the Chinese Consulate and near-daily gatherings at the United Nations. Even his funeral became a protest, with attendees wearing Free Tibet baseball caps and T-shirts.
在洛嘎讓贊去世後的幾週裡，數百人參加了一系列紀念他的活動。包括早上6點的禱告儀式，前往中國領事館的遊行，還有幾乎每天在聯合國舉行的集會。甚至他的葬禮也變成了一場抗議活動，參加者穿戴著印有「自由西藏」字樣的T恤和棒球帽。
On the Wednesday after his death, about a dozen members of his dance group, including Ngawang Tashi, gathered after sunset in front of the United Nations. Metal barriers guarded the site of the immolation, where heaps of flowers and multicolored Tibetan prayer flags surrounded a photo of their friend, smiling serenely.
在他去世後的那個週三，日落之後，包括阿旺扎西在內的約十幾名舞蹈小組成員聚集在聯合國總部門前。金屬柵欄圍住了自焚現場，那裡堆滿鮮花和五彩繽紛的藏人祈禱旗，環繞著他們的朋友安詳微笑的照片。
The group sat down on the pavement in front of the makeshift altar, heads bowed, and made a promise. In his last message, Lobga Rangzen had urged Tibetans to keep dancing, to preserve their culture. But they were too sad to dance now, Ngawang Tashi said. So for 49 days — in Tibetan Buddhism, the time it takes for the soul to be reborn — they would not, though they would carry out his wishes in other ways, including protesting.
這群人在臨時祭壇前的人行道上坐下，低頭默禱，許下承諾。在洛嘎讓贊最後的留言中，他敦促藏人繼續跳舞，保護他們的文化。但阿旺扎西說，他們現在太難過了，跳不下去了。因此在49天裡——在藏傳佛教中，這是靈魂轉世所需的時間——他們不會跳舞，但會以其他方式來實現他的遺願，包括抗議活動。
Then, “after that,” Ngawang Tashi said, “we will keep on dancing, as you advised.”
然後，阿旺扎西說：「到那以後，我們會按照你的建議，繼續跳舞。」
