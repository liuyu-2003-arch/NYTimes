尼泊爾不斷融化的冰川有多危險？

A Melting Glacier in Nepal Was a Time Bomb. There Are More to Come.

JASON GULLEY • 2026-08-31
Like much of the world, I watched and rewatched the videos of the catastrophic flood that have been filtering out of Nepal since Wednesday with horror and sadness.
像世界上許多人一樣，自上週三以來，我帶著恐懼和悲傷，一遍又一遍地觀看從尼泊爾傳出的那場災難性洪水的影片。
But unlike many, I have an intimate knowledge of this part of the world, where I first traveled as an undergraduate geology student in 2005 to study how glaciers in the Khumbu region disintegrate into hazardous lakes. I’ve made six expeditions there over the last two decades.
但與許多人不同的是，我對這片地區有切身的了解。2005年，我還是一名地質學本科生時第一次來到這裡，研究昆布地區的冰川如何崩塌形成危險的湖泊。過去20年裡，我先後六次來到這裡進行考察。
Because of the remote location, scientists don’t know what precisely triggered Wednesday’s disaster — yet. What’s clear at this point is that a giant hunk of glacier peeled off the side of the mountain, tumbled down 4,000 vertical feet and slammed into a tributary of the Bhote Koshi River in China.
由於事發地點偏遠，科學家們目前尚不清楚究竟是什麼觸發了週三的災害。目前可以確定的是，一塊巨大的冰川從山體側面脫落，垂直墜落約約1200米，轟然砸入了博特科西河在中國境內的一條支流。
Originally misidentified as an earthquake, the mountain-shaking event registered as a 5.2 magnitude seismic event. Moments later, a wall of rocks and water blasted through the Gyriong Port building as the flood barreled down the valley.
這一震動山體的事件最初被誤認為是地震，記錄到的震級為5.2級。不久之後，隨著洪水沿著山谷奔涌而下，由岩石和洪水組成的巨浪沖毀了吉隆口岸的建築。

The source of the water remains a mystery.
但這些水究竟來自哪裡，仍然是個謎。
In many catastrophic floods in the Himalaya, glacier lakes are to blame. When Nepal’s glaciers were healthy, they bulldozed down mountains and pushed up piles of rocky debris hundreds of feet high to form features glaciologists call moraines.
在喜馬拉雅地區發生的許多災難性洪水中，罪魁禍首都是冰川湖。過去，尼泊爾的冰川處於健康狀態時，它們會像推土機一樣沿山體向下推進，將大量岩石碎屑堆積到數十米高，形成冰川學家所謂的冰磧壟。
As the planet has warmed and temperatures across the Himalaya have soared, glaciers have shriveled, and those moraines have trapped meltwater. That has created lakes that can grow until they replace the glacier feeding them — or until a disturbance causes them to drain catastrophically.
隨著全球氣候變暖，喜馬拉雅地區的氣溫大幅升高，冰川不斷萎縮，這些冰磧壟則把融化的冰雪水攔截，形成湖泊。這些湖泊可以不斷擴大，直到最終吞沒了補給它們的冰川；也可能因為某種擾動而突然潰決，造成災難性泄洪。
As I reported last year with the Times climate science reporter Raymond Zhong, a landslide smacked into a glacier in the Khumbu region in 2024, launching a flood wave over the moraine dam that sent 100 million gallons of water through the heart of Thame, a Sherpa village nearby. Luckily, no one died. But the flood destroyed the village’s school, medical clinic, several lodges and homes.
正如我去年與《紐約時報》的氣候科學記者鍾旻(Raymond Zhong)共同報導的，2024年昆布地區發生山體滑坡，撞擊冰川，引發的洪水衝破冰磧壩，將1億加侖的水傾瀉至附近雪巴人村莊薩梅的中心地帶。幸運的是，沒有人因此遇難，但洪水摧毀了村裡的學校、診所、幾處客棧和住宅。
Data shows that glacial failures — the scientific term for a category of events that includes ice avalanches, glacier detachments and rock-ice avalanches — have happened dozens of times since 1900 in the China-Nepal border region that was the site of this week’s devastation.
數據顯示，自1900年以來，在中尼邊境地區——也就是本週災害的發生地——已發生過數十次冰川失穩（這是一個科學術語，涵蓋冰崩、冰川滑塌和冰岩崩等事件）。
While Wednesday’s flood had a massive landslide, there didn’t appear to be any glacier lakes below it, making it a bit of an anomaly, certain to be studied intensely in the coming years. Some scientists have speculated that the impact of a piece of glacier falling 4,000 feet could have pulverized the ice into water.
儘管週三的洪災也涉及大規模山體滑坡，但其下方似乎並沒有冰川湖，這使得此次事件有些不同尋常，未來幾年勢必會成為科學家重點研究的對象。一些科學家推測，一塊冰川墜落1200米造成的衝擊力可能已將冰體直接粉碎成了水。
But we know that there will be more floods — and some may be far worse than this week’s catastrophe.
但我們知道，未來還會發生更多洪水——而其中一些可能遠比這週的災害嚴重。
Unless the carbon dioxide emissions that are heating the planet are curbed, scientists project warming across High Mountain Asia, a broad region encompassing the Himalaya, Karakoram, Pamir, Hindu Kush and Tien Shan, will erase as much as 75 percent of the remaining glaciers by 2100.
除非人類能夠遏制加熱地球的二氧化碳排放，否則科學家預計，到2100年，亞洲高山區（涵蓋喜馬拉雅山脈、喀喇崑崙山脈、帕米爾高原、興都庫什山脈和天山的廣大地區）的氣溫升高將導致多達75%的現有冰川消失。
Meltwater pooling behind moraines and in depressions exposed by the ice will transform thousands of glaciers into large lakes, each one a potential ticking time bomb for vulnerable communities downstream.
融化的冰水會在冰磧壟後方以及冰層消退後暴露出的窪地匯聚，使數千座冰川逐漸轉變成大型湖泊。每一座湖泊，都可能成為下游脆弱社區頭頂上一顆不斷滴答作響的定時炸彈。

Over the last 20 years, I’ve watched this process in action. On my earliest trips to the region, I would scamper up the Ngozumpa, Nepal’s longest glacier, to my research sites, where I would step across a long, ribbon-shaped puddle, near the Sherpa village of Gokyo.
過去20年裡，我親眼目睹了這一過程。在我最早幾次前往該地區時，我會一路輕快地爬上尼泊爾最長的冰川——恩戈尊帕冰川，前往我的研究地點，在雪巴人的村莊果覺附近，我會跨過一汪狹長、帶狀的水窪。
On return trips to Nepal over the next 20 years, I watched that puddle begin to gobble its way across the glacier. In 2025, when I returned to the region to report on glacier flooding hazards for The Times, it had become a lake, nearly 750 feet wide and 850 long.
接下來的20年裡，每次返回尼泊爾，我都看到那汪水一點點將冰川吞噬。2025年，我再次來到這裡為《紐約時報》報導冰川洪水的風險時，它已經變成了一個湖，將近230米寬、260米長。
The flood in Thame a few years ago showed that smaller lakes can be dangerous on their own, but they also play a critical role in creating larger lakes — and potentially larger flood hazards. The Ngozumpa, like many of Nepal’s glaciers, is blanketed by thick, rocky debris shed by adjacent mountains. Glaciologists once thought this debris would insulate ice from melting, but now we know that lakes can supercharge melt. Silt and mud trapped by meltwater turn lake water a dark gray color. And just as wearing a black T-shirt on a sunny day makes you warmer, that dark color heats up the water in the lakes to melt the surrounding ice.
幾年前薩梅村的洪災表明，較小的湖泊本身就具有危險性，但它們在形成更大的湖泊——進而可能造成更大的洪災——方面也起著關鍵作用。和尼泊爾許多冰川一樣，恩戈尊帕冰川被相鄰山體脫落的厚厚的岩石碎屑所覆蓋。地質學家曾認為這些碎屑會隔熱，保護冰川免於融化，但現在我們知道，湖泊反而會加劇融化。融水攜帶的泥沙和泥土使湖水變成深灰色。就像晴天穿黑T恤會讓你感覺更熱一樣，這種深色使湖水溫度升高，從而融化周圍的冰。
Warm water can create caves that turn glaciers into Swiss cheese, rotting them from the inside out. When these tunnels collapse, they create new depressions that become lakes. And so, like tumors in a cancer patient, rapid lake growth can quickly consume a glacier.
溫暖的水會在冰川內部形成洞穴，把冰川變得像瑞士奶酪一樣千瘡百孔，從內部一點點腐蝕它們。當這些地下通道坍塌後，就會形成新的窪地，而這些窪地又會變成湖泊。所以，就像癌症患者的腫瘤一樣，湖泊的快速擴張可以迅速吞噬一條冰川。
The Imja glacier, another one I’ve visited many times during my research in the Khumbu, is a dramatic example. Aerial images from the 1950s show that the lake had but a handful of small lakes on the surface. By 1978, a single lake consumed nearly the entire glacier tongue. By 2021, the lower Imja glacier had been converted into a lake that was 1.7 miles long, 0.4 miles wide and more than 400 feet deep. The only thing holding it back is an unstable dam of ice, sand and rock.
伊姆扎冰川是我在昆布研究期間多次考察的另一條冰川，它就是一個極具衝擊力的例子。20世紀50年代的航拍圖像顯示，當時冰川表面只有少數幾個小湖。到1978年，一個湖泊就幾乎吞噬了整個冰舌。到2021年，伊姆扎冰川下部已形成一個長約2.7公里、寬0.4約640米、深超過120米的湖泊。唯一阻擋它的，是一座由冰、沙和岩石構成的不穩定堤壩。
Imja’s lake is one of the largest, and once one of the most dangerous, glacier lakes in Nepal. In 2016, the Nepali Army excavated an 11-foot-deep trench to lower the level of the water — and the magnitude of the flood that would be unleashed if it were to burst. An outburst in 1985 that flooded Thame had prompted the move. Thirty eight years later, I was there to document the effects of a second burst.
伊姆扎湖是尼泊爾最大、也曾是最危險的冰川湖之一。2016年，尼泊爾軍隊挖掘了一條約3.4米深的溝渠以降低水位，從而降低一旦潰壩可能引發的洪水規模。1985年的一次潰壩洪水淹沒了薩梅村，促使了這次行動。38年後，我在那裡記錄了第二次潰壩的影響。
As a geologist focused on the science of glaciers, I don’t know what can be done to address floods like these in the future. But one thing I do know is that they are coming.
作為一名專門研究冰川科學的地質學家，我不知道未來究竟能採取什麼措施來應對這樣的洪水。但有一件事我很確定：它們還會來。
