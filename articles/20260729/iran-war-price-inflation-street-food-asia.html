街頭小吃越來越貴？伊朗戰爭改變亞洲人的餐桌

The Iran War Is Making Noodles Costlier 4,500 Miles Away

MUKTITA SUHARTONO, YAN ZHUANG, MAX KIM, SUHASINI RAJ • 2026-07-29
Samosas in India. Noodles in South Korea. Street food of all kinds in Indonesia.
印度的咖哩角。韓國的刀削麵。印度尼西亞的各種街頭小吃。
Across Asia, people are discovering that staple dishes that were once reliably cheap are no longer so affordable, as the shock waves of the Iran war spread around the world.
亞洲各地的人們發現，那些曾經價格親民的日常主食如今已不再那麼便宜。伊朗戰爭帶來的衝擊波正在全球蔓延。
Asian countries, which rely heavily on energy imports from the Middle East, have been deeply affected by the economic fallout of the conflict, which has reignited after the collapse of a preliminary U.S.-Iran cease-fire.
亞洲各國嚴重依賴來自中東的能源進口，隨著美伊初步停火協議破裂、衝突重燃，這場戰爭造成的經濟衝擊已對亞洲各國產生深遠影響。
Since the fighting began in February, tourism across Asia has plummeted, inflation has surged and prices of ingredients are soaring. Customers and food sellers alike are feeling the pinch.
自今年2月戰事爆發以來，亞洲旅遊業全面下滑，通貨膨脹飆升，食材價格飛漲。無論是消費者還是餐飲攤販，都切身感受到了經濟壓力。
廣告
“We are all in the same boat, hoping it won’t sink,” said Supatra Ruamthot, a street food seller in Bangkok.
「我們都在同一條船上，只希望它不要沉。」曼谷街頭小吃攤主素帕特拉·魯阿姆托說。
South Korea
韓國
For years, Kim Moon-jung sold bowls of kalguksu — hand-cut noodles in savory broth — out of her restaurant in southern Seoul for 9,000 won (about $6). But with the cost of ingredients steadily rising, she plans to increase the price to at least 10,000 won in the next few months.
多年來，金文貞（音）在首爾南部經營一家餐館，以9000韓元（約合6美元）一碗的價格出售刀削麵（配鮮美高湯的手切麵條）。但隨著原料成本持續上漲，她計劃在未來幾個月內將價格提高到至少1萬韓元。
A flurry of local news media reports followed the announcement in March by South Korea’s consumer agency that the average bowl of kalguksu in Seoul had exceeded that price point, an important psychological marker.
今年3月，韓國消費者機構宣布，首爾一碗刀削麵的平均售價已經突破1萬韓元。這一消息引發韓國媒體的密集報導，因為這個價格被視為一個重要的心理關口。
Residents of the capital are so eager to find affordable meals, and so sensitive to price increases, that earlier this year they created a crowdsourced map to identify restaurants selling meals for 10,000 won or less.
首爾居民迫切尋找平價餐食，且對價格上漲極其敏感，因此今年早些時候他們創建了一個網友共享地圖，專門標註餐食價格在1萬韓元或以下的餐廳。
“There’s not a single ingredient that hasn’t become more expensive,” said Ms. Kim, 59, who runs her store with her daughter. She listed materials like flour, clams and vegetables.
「每一種食材都在漲價。」59歲的金文貞說道，她和女兒一起經營這家店。她列舉了麵粉、蛤蜊和蔬菜等原料。
She said she was not worried that the price hike would deter customers. “They also see that everything’s getting more expensive,” she said. “So how can they blame us?”
她表示，並不擔心漲價會嚇跑顧客。「他們也看得到，什麼東西都在漲價。」她說，「所以他們怎麼能怪我們呢？」
India
印度
Behind the counter of Yadav Milk Dairy in Lucknow, a city about 250 miles southeast of New Delhi that is known for its street food, owner Shubham Yadav lamented that he was “barely profiting” from selling samosas, the potato-filled savory pastries that are one of India’s most famous culinary exports.
在新德里東南方向約400公里、以街頭美食聞名的勒克瑙，亞達夫牛奶雜貨店的店主舒巴姆·亞達夫站在櫃檯後感嘆，靠賣咖哩角「幾乎賺不到什麼錢了」。咖哩角是一種包著馬鈴薯餡的美味酥皮點心，也是印度最著名的美食之一。
Mr. Yadav, 29, said the price of the gas cylinders he uses had doubled to 3,600 rupees, or about $38. India imports about 60 percent of its liquefied petroleum gas, much of that via the Strait of Hormuz. The wartime disruption of the waterway has threatened the supply of the fuel that powers the country’s gas stoves.
29歲的亞達夫說，他使用的煤氣罐價格已經翻了一番，達到3600盧比（約合38美元）。印度約60%的液化石油氣依賴進口，其中大部分經由霍爾木茲海峽運輸。戰爭導致該海峽運輸受阻，也威脅到了印度燃氣灶的燃料供應。
廣告
Mr. Yadav said he could not double his prices to match the surging cost of gas, so he had increased the price of each samosa by about 1 cent. But this meant his profits were increasingly thin, he said, and he worried that he might need to raise prices further in the coming months.
亞達夫說，他無法像燃氣價格那樣把咖哩角的售價翻倍，因此每個咖哩角只漲了約1美分。但這樣一來，利潤越來越薄，他擔心未來幾個月可能需要進一步提價。
He said he hoped that his customers enjoyed his samosas enough to keep buying. “But at what price, is the question?” he asked.
他說，他希望顧客足夠喜歡他的咖哩角，願意繼續購買。「但問題在於，他們究竟能接受多高的價格？」他問道。
Thailand
泰國
Before the war began, Ms. Ruamthot, who has dished out som tum, or green papaya salad, for 20 years, would start receiving orders in person and on delivery apps before she had even finished setting up her stall in the morning. At lunchtime, she would serve a flood of customers, many of them office workers, who would wait up to 30 minutes.
魯阿姆托已經賣了20年青木瓜沙拉，戰爭爆發前，每天早上攤位還沒擺好，她就已經陸續收到現場顧客和外賣平台的訂單。午餐高峰時，總有大批顧客排隊等候，其中許多是上班族，他們最長要等30分鐘。
These days, she does the waiting.
如今，輪到她等待了。
“It’s so bad now. Business is very slow,” said Ms. Ruamthot, 62.
「現在太差了，生意非常冷清，」62歲的魯阿姆托說。
廣告
Even though her costs have risen by about 30 percent, she has not increased her prices out of fear that doing so would drive more customers away.
儘管成本上漲了約30%，但她不敢漲價，擔心這樣會失去更多顧客。
“Even with the current price, we are losing customers,” she said. “People really live frugally because everything has increased in price.”
「即使維持現在的價格，我們還是不斷流失顧客，」她說，「大家真的過得很節省，因為所有東西都漲價了。」
Her profits had fallen by more than half, to about 1,000 baht a day (around $30), leaving her unsure how long she could sustain her business. When she speaks with other food vendors, she learns that her situation isn’t unique.
她每天的利潤已經減少了一半以上，只剩約1000泰銖（約30美元），她不確定自己的生意還能撐多久。和其他餐飲攤販聊天時，她發現大家的情況都差不多。
“Everybody is experiencing the same thing,” she said.
「所有人都一樣，」她說。
Indonesia
印度尼西亞
“If I didn’t increase the price I couldn’t survive,” said Ridah, 48, who sells gado-gado, a dish of boiled vegetables dressed with peanut sauce, in Jakarta.
「如果我不漲價，就活不下去了。」48歲的麗達說。她在雅加達賣加多加多，這是一道淋著沙嗲醬的水煮蔬菜沙拉。
Standing next to her food cart, she rattled off ingredients that had increased in cost, including peanuts and plastic bags. When she goes to the market, she said, “I don’t even know how much I’ll have to pay for my shopping for the day.”
站在自己的餐車旁，她一口氣列舉了漲價的食材，包括花生和塑料袋。她說，去市場採購時，「我都不知道當天到底要花多少錢。」
廣告
Ms. Ridah — who, like many Indonesians, uses only one name — said her profits had more than halved since the war began, and business was even worse than during the coronavirus pandemic. Then, she still had customers.
麗達（和許多印尼人一樣，她沒有姓）說，自戰爭爆發以來，她的利潤減少了一半以上，生意甚至比新冠疫情期間還要糟糕。至少那時還有顧客。
“Now, no one is buying,” she said.
「她說，「現在，根本沒人買。」
While she knew her customers were also struggling, she felt that she had no choice but to increase the price of her dish by 1,000 rupiah (about five cents) to 18,000 rupiah (about $1).
雖然她知道顧客的日子也不好過，但她覺得自己別無選擇，只能把每份加多加多的售價提高1000印尼盾（約合5美分），漲到1.8萬印尼盾（約1美元）。
“This is the only thing I can do and how I earned my living,” said Ms. Ridah, who has run her food cart for nearly 30 years. “We just have to survive this.”
「這是我唯一會做的事情，也是我賴以謀生的工作。」經營這輛餐車近30年的麗達說，「我們只能設法挺過去。」
