被川普「斷油」半年，古巴為什麼還沒崩潰？

Cut Off From Fuel, Cuba Is Still Surviving

JACK NICAS, ALEX PENA • 2026-07-29
For months, President Trump has been predicting that Cuba is about to collapse.
幾個月來，川普總統一直預言古巴即將崩潰。
“It’s just going to fall,” he told reporters on Jan. 4, a day after taking control of Venezuela’s oil, which had been Cuba’s lifeline.
「它就要撐不住了，」他在1月4日告訴記者，此前一天，他剛剛控制了委內瑞拉的石油，這一直是古巴的生命線。
He then imposed an oil blockade that has pushed the island into its worst ever energy crisis, with mass blackouts now lasting nearly all day. And yet, more than six months later, the Cuban government and people are still standing.
隨後，他實施了石油封鎖，將這個島國推入了有史以來最嚴重的能源危機，如今大規模的停電幾乎持續整天。然而，六個多月過去了，古巴政府和人民依然屹立不倒。
This month, we visited the country to understand how. After a week on the ground, we found that while Cubans are clearly suffering, they are also doing what perhaps they do best: adapting.
本月，我們前往該國，以了解其中的原因。在實地考察了一周後，我們發現，儘管古巴人顯然飽受煎熬，但他們也在做著他們可能最擅長的事情：適應。
廣告
They are swapping their gas-guzzling 1950s American cars for electric motorcycles and rickshaws from China; importing hefty lithium batteries to keep televisions and fans running through blackouts; jury-rigging generators to run on more accessible natural gas; and installing solar panels on rooftops and even vehicles.
他們將耗油量大的20世紀50年代美國汽車換成來自中國的電動摩托車和三輪車；進口笨重的鋰電池，以便在停電期間維持電視和風扇的運轉；臨時改裝發電機，使其能夠使用更容易獲取的天然氣；並在屋頂甚至汽車上安裝太陽能電池板。
Together, the workarounds amount to a new Cuban energy grid — this one more in the hands of the Cuban people, propping up society with the electricity off and fuel lines dry.
這些變通辦法共同構成了一個新的古巴能源網——這個能源網更多地掌握在古巴人民自己手中，在斷電和燃料枯竭的情況下支撐著社會的運轉。
These solutions have enabled the Cuban government to ration power by turning on the lights for just a few hours a day, stretching out whatever scarce fuel it has left in an effort to withstand Washington’s intense pressure campaign.
這些解決方案使古巴政府能夠實施電力配給，每天只供電幾個小時，盡可能延長剩餘的稀缺燃料的使用時間，努力抵禦華盛頓的高壓手段。
The result is that Cuba has lasted far longer without fuel shipments than the Trump administration appears to have expected.
結果是，古巴在沒有燃料供應的情況下堅持得遠比川普政府預期的要久。
Over nearly seven decades of U.S. pressure, Cuba has defied the odds and improvised to survive. Now it seems that Mr. Trump, like many U.S. presidents before him, is also finding that toppling the government founded by Fidel Castro is not so simple.
在美國近70年的施壓下，古巴一直克服重重困難，靠著應變能力生存了下來。現在看來，川普就像之前的許多美國總統一樣，也發現要推翻菲德爾·卡斯楚建立的政府並非那麼簡單。
Secretary of State Marco Rubio told reporters last week that the Trump administration still wants to see the Cuban government fall. “We’re prepared to be very realistic about how you do that, and patient,” he said. “These are complex things that take time, and obviously we’re prepared to do what we can.”
美國國務卿魯比奧上週告訴記者，川普政府仍然希望看到古巴政府倒台。「對於如何實現這一目標，我們準備採取非常務實的態度，並保持耐心，」他說。「這些都是需要時間的複雜事情，顯然，我們已經準備好盡我們所能。」
The Cuban government is seeking a deal. “Cuba is not an enemy of the United States,” Carlos Fernández de Cossio, Cuba’s deputy foreign minister, said in an interview. “What we would like is a constructive and respectful relationship.”
古巴政府正在尋求達成一項協議。「古巴不是美國的敵人，」古巴副外長卡洛斯·費爾南德斯·德科西奧在接受採訪時說。「我們希望建立一種建設性和相互尊重的關係。」
廣告
Meantime, the Cuban people, as they have for decades, are making do. “Cubans thrive amid crisis,” said Humberto Palmero, a 55-year-old farmer, standing on the hill where he grew up along Cuba’s coastline, with a view toward Florida. His siblings had already left for Miami and his two children are fleeing to Spain, but he said he is determined to stay.
與此同時，古巴人民像幾十年來一樣，仍在設法維持生計。「古巴人在危機中茁壯成長，」55歲的農民溫貝托·帕爾梅羅說。他站在古巴海岸邊他長大的山丘上，眺望著佛羅里達的方向。他的兄弟姐妹已經去了邁阿密，他的兩個孩子也正逃往西班牙，但他表示自己決心留下來。
After hearing about the oil blockade, Mr. Palmero learned on YouTube how to distill junk plastic into homemade gasoline and diesel. He has since built a tiny, makeshift refinery in his backyard to power his motorcycle and light his stove.
得知石油封鎖的消息後，帕爾梅羅在YouTube上學習了如何將廢棄塑料提煉成自製汽油和柴油。之後，他在自家後院搭建了一個小型的簡易煉油裝置，用來驅動摩托車和給爐子點火。
“We look for solutions,” he said. “The economic restrictions are slowly killing us, but we’re pulling through with all our might, managing to get by until we see what happens next.”
「我們在尋找解決辦法，」他說。「經濟限制正在慢慢地把我們逼上絕路，但我們正在用盡全力挺過去，努力維持生計，直到看看接下來會發生什麼。」
Misery and Grit
苦難與堅韌
In Havana and beyond, the crisis’s effects were clear.
在哈瓦那及其他地區，這場危機的影響顯而易見。
We met families walking two hours for water because government pumps had run out of diesel. Women, lacking gas, cooked with firewood on the street. Powerless refrigerators became cabinets. Hitchhikers littered roadsides after the city halted public busing. And dozens of people told us they were simply exhausted after so many sleepless nights in the sweltering heat without fans or air-conditioning.
我們遇到一些家庭，因為政府水泵的柴油用盡，他們不得不步行兩小時去取水。由於缺乏燃氣，女人們在街上用柴火做飯。斷電的冰箱變成了儲物櫃。在城市停止公共交通後，路邊滿是搭便車的人。幾十個人告訴我們，在沒有風扇和空調的悶熱夜晚，他們徹夜難眠，已經筋疲力盡。
廣告
But we also saw a country that was still functioning.
但我們也看到，這個國家仍在運轉。
Vehicles were on the road — despite black-market gasoline reaching $30 a gallon — because many were electric. Restaurants were open for the few who could afford them, powered by rumbling generators out back. Hospitals, government buildings and the airport had power because they were on protected parts of the grid. Medical clinics, nursing homes and bank branches ran off solar panels donated by China.
儘管黑市汽油價格已漲至每加侖30美元，路上仍有車輛行駛——因為許多車是電動的。餐館靠著後院轟鳴的發電機供電，為少數消費得起的人開門營業。醫院、政府大樓和機場有電力供應，因為它們位於受保護的電網區域。醫療診所、療養院和銀行分行則依靠中國捐贈的太陽能電池板運轉。
Cell service was sometimes working. Televisions showing the World Cup were not hard to find. And even roadside stands were serving cold beer from refrigerators powered by the sun.
手機信號有時是暢通的。播放世界盃的電視並不難找。甚至路邊攤也供應著由太陽能冰箱冷藏的冰啤酒。
But the makeshift energy grid also exposed Cuba’s inequality.
但這個臨時拼湊的能源網也暴露了古巴社會的不平等。
The batteries, solar panels and electric vehicles overwhelmingly belong to wealthier Cubans, which usually means those with relatives abroad. Their poorer neighbors often live without power and scrape by with meager pay amid rising inflation.
電池、太陽能電池板和電動汽車絕大多數屬於較富裕的古巴人，這通常意味著他們有在國外的親屬。他們較貧窮的鄰居經常過著沒有電的生活，在不斷加劇的通貨膨脹中靠微薄的薪水勉強度日。
“The situation here is fine, because the owner can afford to buy solar panels,” said Yeny Oliva, 26, a bartender at a roadside restaurant. “But for us, at home, life is awful.” On wages of roughly $1 a day, she said, she struggles to buy milk for her 3-year-old daughter, and then their food spoils quickly without electricity.
「這裡的情況還不錯，因為老闆買得起太陽能電池板，」26歲的路邊餐館調酒師葉妮·奧利瓦說。「但對我們來說，在家裡，生活太糟糕了。」她說，她每天大約賺一美元的工資，很難為三歲的女兒買到牛奶，而且在沒有電的情況下，她們的食物很快就會變質。
Yet she also was grateful for the solar energy. Without power, the restaurant had closed. Then, the owners installed the panels and she was back at work.
然而，她也很感激太陽能。停電時，餐館曾關門歇業。後來，老闆安裝了電池板，她才恢復了工作。
Scraping the Barrel
想盡辦法
廣告
Other than a single Russian oil tanker in March, allies have not been willing to test Washington’s effective oil blockade.
除了3月的一艘俄羅斯油輪，盟國都不願去挑戰華盛頓實質性的石油封鎖。
As a result, Cuba has been powering its electric grid solely with domestic oil and gas and a growing network of solar parks sponsored by China. Together, they are enough to support roughly half of Cuban demand, according to Jorge Piñón, who studies Cuban energy at the University of Texas.
因此，古巴只能完全依靠國產油氣以及由中國資助且規模日益擴大的太陽能發電場網路供電。得克薩斯大學研究古巴能源的豪爾赫·皮農表示，這些能源假期來可以滿足古巴大約一半的需求。
To preserve that supply, the government strategically cuts power at certain times and in certain places. It also runs plants at lower capacity to keep them from failing; extracts more natural gas; and even tries to refine sludgy oil residues from the bottom of years-old tanks.
為了維持這種供應，政府會在特定時間和特定地點實施戰略性限電。政府還以較低的負荷運行發電廠以防止其發生故障；開採更多天然氣；甚至試圖提煉積存多年儲油罐底部的油泥狀殘渣。
“They are literally scraping the bottom of the barrel,” Mr. Piñón said.
「他們真的是在想盡一切辦法，」皮農說。
Mr. Piñón said he and other analysts underestimated the Cuban government’s resourcefulness when they forecast it would exhaust its fuel stocks by March. But now he is skeptical of the government’s claim in May that the island has run out of fuel. On our trip, it was clear gasoline and diesel were still circulating.
皮農說，他和其它分析師原本預測古巴政府將在3月耗盡燃料儲備，但他們低估了古巴政府的應變能力。而現在，他對政府5月聲稱該島已經耗盡燃料的說法表示懷疑。在我們的行程期間，汽油和柴油明顯仍在流通。
At least some is coming from the United States. The Trump administration has allowed limited fuel exports to private businesses in Cuba, which has relieved pressure on black-market gas prices. And last week, the U.S. government’s first batch of $100 million in aid arrived.
至少有一部分是來自美國。川普政府允許向古巴的私營企業有限度出口燃料，這緩解了黑市汽油價格的上漲壓力。而就在上週，美國政府首批1億美元的援助物資已經抵達古巴。
廣告
The moves show how the Trump administration is exerting power over Cuba, ordering other nations to halt fuel shipments but allowing its own, and then providing aid in response to the resulting crisis. A White House spokeswoman said the Trump administration viewed Cuba as a threat to national security.
這些舉措表明，川普政府如何對古巴施加影響：一方面命令其他國家停止向古巴輸送燃料，另一方面卻允許本國燃料繼續輸送，在危機爆發後提供援助。白宮一位發言人表示，川普政府將古巴視為對國家安全的威脅。
Exhausted Cubans told us they were angry at both Havana and Washington. Yet no one said they planned to take to the streets. Aside from banging pots and pans, the Cuban people have hardly staged protests.
精疲力盡的古巴人告訴我們，他們對哈瓦那和華盛頓都感到憤怒。然而，沒有人表示他們打算上街遊行。除了敲打鍋碗瓢盆外，古巴人民幾乎沒有舉行過抗議活動。
“You go out to express your opinion on the street, and they throw you in jail or fine you,” said David Hechavarría Gamboa, 24, standing alongside his wife on a darkened street. “I can’t put my family at risk.”
「如果你上街表達意見，他們就會把你關進監獄或者罰款，」24歲的戴維·埃查瓦裡亞·甘博亞和妻子站在黑暗的街邊說。「我不能讓家人冒這個險。」
Chasing Daylight
追逐日光
When U.S. forces captured Venezuela’s leader, Nicolás Maduro, in January, Nelson Vega knew Cuba had a problem. Venezuelan oil had powered Cuba for years.
今年1月，當美軍抓獲委內瑞拉領導人馬杜羅時，尼爾森·維加就知道古巴有麻煩了。多年來，委內瑞拉的石油一直是古巴的能源支柱。
A month later, Mr. Vega, a 38-year-old entrepreneur, borrowed money from relatives in Florida to buy a Chinese-made electric rickshaw for $4,850. It took 10 hours to charge, and almost immediately, the blackouts made that difficult. So he invested a further $600 to drill solar panels to its roof.
一個月後，38歲的企業家維加向佛羅里達州的親戚借錢，以4850美元的價格購買了一輛中國製造的電動三輪車。這輛車需要充電10個小時，而且幾乎就在買回來後不久，停電就讓充電變得困難起來。因此，他又投資了600美元，在車頂上安裝了太陽能電池板。
Now he can power the entire battery by parking it in the sun for a day. “To me, this is the future,” he said. “We’re going to overcome the U.S. blockade.”
現在，他只需把三輪車在陽光下停一天，就能給電池充滿電。「對我來說，這就是未來，」他說。「我們將克服美國的封鎖。」
Once we traveled outside Havana, solar panels proliferated. Cuba’s power grid has been decaying in rural areas for years, making blackouts — and solar panels — already common.
一旦離開哈瓦那，太陽能電池板便隨處可見。多年來，古巴農村地區的電網一直日益老化，這使得停電早已司空見慣，太陽能電池板也變得普遍。
We also came across a new field of panels — one of the government’s 54 solar parks, most inaugurated since 2025 and all built with China’s help.
我們還偶然發現了一片新的太陽能電池板場地——這是政府設立的54個太陽能園區之一，其中大部分是在2025年之後落成的，並且全部是在中國幫助下建成。
Few countries are adopting solar faster than Cuba. Solar energy can now meet roughly 10 percent of Cuba’s electricity needs, the government says, up from 3 percent at the start of 2025. Cuban officials aim to depend fully on renewable energy by 2050.
很少有國家比古巴更迅速地推廣太陽能。政府表示，太陽能現在可以滿足古巴約10%的電力需求，高於2025年初的3%。古巴官員的目標是到2050年完全依賴可再生能源。
Running on Fumes
極限運轉
Perhaps Cuba’s hottest product of the moment are portable lithium batteries that can be charged in a few hours and then temporarily power televisions, fans or even refrigerators.
古巴目前最熱門的產品可能是便攜式鋰電池，這種電池可以在幾個小時內充滿電，然後為電視、風扇甚至冰箱提供臨時電力。
The most popular model costs about $750 in Cuba, sold on social media by Cubans who have imported them from China. That is more than most Cubans make in a year, so most of the batteries are sponsored by relatives abroad.
最受歡迎的型號在古巴售價約為750美元，由從中國進口產品的古巴人在社群媒體上出售。這超過了大多數古巴人一年的收入，因此大部分電池都是由海外親屬資助的。
Other Cubans are electrifying their homes with the gas piped in for cooking and heating water. About 280,000 residences in Havana have such gas lines, and many are connecting them to modified generators designed to run on liquid fuel. Cuba produces the gas domestically, so it is one of the few energy sources not affected by the blockade.
還有一些古巴人利用用於烹飪和燒水管線輸送的天然氣為家裡發電。哈瓦那大約有28萬戶住宅有這種天然氣管道，許多家庭將管道連接到經過改裝、專為液態燃料設計的發電機上。古巴國內自產天然氣，因此它是少數不受封鎖影響的能源之一。
The gas is also cheap, especially for the 120,000 households that pay a flat rate regardless of use. One man said he had been running a generator off gas almost nonstop, and yet was still billed the same 50 pesos, or eight cents, for the month.
天然氣也很便宜，特別是對於12萬戶按固定費率繳費、無論用量多少都只需支付相同金額的家庭而言。一名男子說，他幾乎不停地用天然氣運行發電機，但這個月的帳單仍然是同樣的50比索（約合8美分）。
On one of our final nights in Havana, we found a bar still full of patrons past midnight, powering its string lights and Latin music with a natural gas generator. Outside, we met Kiké Rodríguez, 37, founder of a Cuban barbecue business, Manteca, that has expanded during the crisis. He now employs 18 people to cook and deliver pork and plantains to customers across Havana.
在哈瓦那的最後幾個夜晚之一，我們發現，一家酒吧過了午夜依然顧客盈門，它用天然氣發電機供電，點亮燈串，播放拉丁音樂。在外面，我們遇到了37歲的基克·羅德里格斯，他是古巴燒烤店「曼特卡」的創始人，這家店在危機期間還擴大了規模。他現在僱傭了18名員工，為哈瓦那各地的顧客烹調和運送豬肉及大蕉。
“We’re the masters of this,” he said. “We’ve been figuring things out our whole lives.”
「我們是這方面的行家，」他說。「我們一輩子都在想辦法解決各種問題。」
He pointed to the rooftop, where he said he was finishing a remodel to open his first brick-and-mortar restaurant, powered by the same generator.
他指著屋頂，說他正在那裡完成改造，準備開設他的第一家實體餐廳，由同一台發電機供電。
“How are things going to play out? Nobody knows,” he added. “But when people say the country is going to fall apart, I don’t exactly know what they mean.”
「事情會怎麼發展？沒有人知道，」他還說。「但是當人們說這個國家即將崩潰時，我並不太清楚他們到底是什麼意思。」
