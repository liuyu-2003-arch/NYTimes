伯納姆正式就任英國首相

Burnham’s Day 1 as Prime Minister: A Homeless Shelter, a Palace and Downing Street

STEPHEN CASTLE, MICHAEL D. SHEAR, MEGAN SPECIA • 2026-07-21
Andy Burnham’s day began with a visit to a homeless shelter in central London, where he chopped mushrooms and chatted with other helpers preparing breakfast.
這一天，安迪·伯納姆先是去了倫敦市中心一家無家可歸者收容所，他在那裡切蘑菇，並與其他正在製作早餐的志願者聊天。
A few hours later, by now wearing a suit and tie, he shook hands with King Charles III in the gilded splendor of Buckingham Palace. Accepting the monarch’s request to form a government, Mr. Burnham, who was named leader of the governing Labour Party on Friday, became Britain’s seventh prime minister after a decade of extraordinary turbulence.
幾個小時後，換上西裝領帶的他在金碧輝煌的白金漢宮與國王查爾斯三世握手。於週五被任命為執政黨工黨領袖的伯納姆接受了君主的組建政府請求，成為英國在這動盪不安的十年裡的第七位首相。
Mr. Burnham’s early-morning trip to the homeless charity seemed designed to send a signal to the country about his priorities, and to underscore his pledge to tackle social issues and reshape British politics.
清晨的無家可歸者慈善機構之行似乎旨在向全國發出關於其優先事項的信號，並強調他關於解決社會問題和重塑英國政治的承諾。
On a day of ritual and ceremony, Mr. Burnham pledged in a short speech outside 10 Downing Street to “put the care of people at the heart of everything I do.” He described his move into Britain’s top job as a “circuit-breaker moment” to change the direction taken by a succession of neoliberal governments over the past four decades that favored privatization and deindustrialized the country.
在這個充滿儀式和典禮的日子裡，伯納姆在唐寧街10號外發表簡短演講，承諾「將關愛民眾置於我所做一切的核心」。他將自己入主英國最高職位描述為一個「斷路器時刻」，要改變過去四十年來一系列新自由主義政府所走的方向——這些政府偏好私有化並導致國家去工業化。
“I know people at home are fed up with politics,” he told the public, speaking without notes and minus the lectern that prime ministers usually speak from outside Downing Street. “I hear you, and I want to be honest with you: We have not been good enough, and we need to be better. We will be.”
「我知道國內民眾對政治已經厭倦，」伯納姆對公眾說——他沒有講稿，也沒有使用首相通常在唐寧街外演講時會用的講台。「我聽到了你們的聲音，我想誠實地告訴你們：我們做得不夠好，我們需要做得更好。我們會的。」
The speed of Mr. Burnham’s ascent to the top job in British politics is striking. Earlier this year, his path to Downing Street seemed blocked when he was prevented from running for a parliamentary seat that he needed to challenge Prime Minister Keir Starmer. But after a disastrous set of elections in Scotland, Wales and English municipalities in May, Mr. Starmer had to relent and allow Mr. Burnham to run in a special election — which he won emphatically.
伯納姆在英國政壇升至最高位的速度令人矚目。今年早些時候，他的唐寧街之路似乎被堵死，當時他被阻止參選一個議會席位，而那是挑戰首相基爾·斯塔默所必需的。但在5月蘇格蘭、威爾斯和英格蘭地方選舉慘敗後，斯塔默不得不讓步，允許伯納姆參加補選——他以壓倒性優勢獲勝。
That put him in pole position to succeed Mr. Starmer, whose popularity had sunk to some of the lowest levels recorded for a British prime minister, just two years after winning a general election in 2024.
這讓他處於接替斯塔默的有利位置。斯塔默在2024年贏得大選僅兩年後，支持率已跌至英國首相有記錄以來的最低水平之一。
廣告
Supporters hope Mr. Burnham will revive his party’s fortunes after more than a year during which the populist right-wing party Reform U.K. has led opinion polls, albeit with about 25 percent of voter support.
支持者希望伯納姆能重振工黨的勢頭，此前一年多時間裡，民粹右翼政黨「英國改革黨」一直領先民調，儘管支持率僅約為25%。
Mr. Burnham’s rise follows a period of political turmoil that began with the Brexit vote in 2016, when voters severed ties to the European Union. In his speech Mr. Burnham, the first lifelong Roman Catholic to become Britain’s prime minister, said he would move quickly to announce measures to ease the burden on voters frustrated with stagnating living standards and impatient for change.
伯納姆的崛起之時，英國正處在以2016年英國脫歐公投為開端的一段動盪期，在那場公投中，選民決定切斷與歐盟的聯繫。作為英國首位篤信羅馬天主教的首相，伯納姆在演講中表示，他將迅速宣布措施，以減輕選民因生活水平停滯不前而產生的負擔，並滿足他們對變革的迫切期待。
Promising to “give people some breathing space now,” he said he would explain how such measures would be financed in the coming days. He also said that later this year he would propose a 10-year plan for the country.
他承諾「立即著手給人們一些喘息空間」，並表示將在未來幾天解釋這些措施的資金來源。他還說，今年晚些時候將提出一個為期10年的國家計劃。
Earlier in the day, Mr. Starmer made his final appearance as prime minister. “My work is done,” he declared in a brief farewell speech, saying that he left “with good grace and a smile.”
當天早些時候，斯塔默作為首相進行了最後一次公開露面。「我的工作完成了，」他在簡短的告別演講中宣布，並表示自己「帶著從容和微笑」離開。
By Monday evening, many of Mr. Starmer’s closest allies had been ousted from the government as Mr. Burnham began forming his cabinet. In a surprise appointment, John Healey, a former defense secretary, was given the critical job of chancellor of the Exchequer, overseeing the nation’s finances, replacing Rachel Reeves, who left the cabinet.
到週一晚上，隨著伯納姆開始組建內閣，斯塔默的許多最親密盟友已被逐出政府。一項出人意料的任命是前國防大臣約翰·希利成為財政大臣，負責國家財政，接替離開內閣的雷切爾·裡夫斯。
A political veteran, Mr. Healey is regarded as a safe pair of hands, and he was a junior minister in the Treasury in Prime Minister Tony Blair’s government. Mr. Healey resigned as defense secretary in June, arguing that a proposed increase in British military spending was insufficient, a move that destabilized Mr. Starmer’s leadership.
作為政壇老手，希利被視為可靠人選，他曾在托尼·布萊爾首相政府中擔任財政部次長。希利於6月辭去國防大臣職務，原因是認為英國增加軍費開支的提案不夠充分，這一舉動動搖了斯塔默的領導地位。
廣告
In his new job, Mr. Healey will now have to find the money needed to upgrade Britain’s armed forces. But his appointment may reassure some of those in the financial markets who were worried by speculation that the job might go to Ed Miliband, who is seen as being more to the left of the Labour Party than Ms. Reeves.
在新職位上，希利現在必須找到升級英國武裝部隊所需的資金。但他的任命可能會讓金融市場中的一些人安心，此前他們擔心該職位可能由被視為比裡夫斯更左傾的埃德·米利班德擔任。
Mr. Miliband, a former Labour leader, was named foreign secretary, while Shabana Mahmood kept her job as home secretary, a signal that Mr. Burnham is largely backing her efforts to crack down on legal and illegal migration. David Lammy, who was deputy prime minister under Mr. Starmer, announced that he would be leaving government.
前工黨領袖米利班德被任命為外交大臣，而沙巴娜·馬哈茂德繼續擔任內政大臣，這表明伯納姆大體支持她打擊合法和非法移民的努力。曾在斯塔默手下擔任副首相的戴維·拉米宣布將離開政府。
Mr. Burnham is also expected to shift some aspects of Mr. Starmer’s agenda on energy. Lucy Powell, Labour’s deputy leader and a close Burnham ally, said on Sunday that he would take “a more pragmatic approach” to North Sea gas and oil than Mr. Starmer, but added, “I don’t think it is a change of policy, more a change of emphasis.”
伯納姆還預計將調整斯塔默的能源政策的某些方面。工黨副領袖、伯納姆的親密盟友露西·鮑威爾週日表示，他對北海天然氣和石油將採取「更務實的態度」，但補充說：「我不認為這是政策改變，更像是重點的調整。」
In a post on Truth Social on Sunday, President Trump appeared to welcome — if exaggerate — the expected change in energy policy. People in the Scottish port city of Aberdeen, he claimed, were “dancing in the streets because the new Prime Minister, Andy Burnham, has stated that he will be opening up, all the way, the invaluable North Sea Oil!”
週日，川普總統在Truth Social上發帖，似乎對能源政策的預期變化表示歡迎——儘管可能有些誇張。他聲稱，蘇格蘭阿伯丁港市的人們「正在街頭起舞，因為新首相安迪·伯納姆表示，他將全面開放寶貴的北海石油！」
Mr. Burnham spoke to Mr. Trump shortly after he became prime minister, Downing Street said. According to a readout of the call, he congratulated the president on a successful run as a co-host of the World Cup. And Mr. Burnham, who was mayor of Greater Manchester for the past nine years, invited Mr. Trump to the city for a visit.
唐寧街表示，伯納姆就任首相後不久便與川普通話。根據通話摘要，他祝賀川普成功舉辦世界盃。擔任大曼徹斯特市長九年的伯納姆還邀請川普訪問該市。
In his speech, Mr. Burnham immediately announced one concrete new policy, saying he would end “rough sleeping,” a British term for homelessness. It is an ambitious — and some have argued impossible — goal, but one that is close to his heart; he donated 15 percent of his annual salary to homelessness charities during his nine years as mayor of Greater Manchester.
在演講中，伯納姆立即宣布了一項具體新政策，表示他將結束「露宿」，這是英國人對無家可歸的一種稱呼。這是一個雄心勃勃——有人認為不可能實現——的目標，但對他來說意義重大；他在擔任大曼徹斯特市長九年間，曾將年薪的15%捐贈給無家可歸者慈善機構。
廣告
Mr. Burnham pledged to end homelessness when he was mayor, too. The number of rough sleepers did drop significantly in his city by 2020, but a report earlier this year in the The Mill, a Manchester newspaper, found that it had increased steadily over the past four years.
伯納姆擔任市長時也承諾結束無家可歸現象。到2020年，該市露宿者數量確實大幅下降，但曼徹斯特報紙《The Mill》今年早些時候的報導發現，過去四年裡這一數字又穩步上升。
At the homeless shelter on Monday morning, Mr. Burnham addressed that.
週一上午在無家可歸者收容所，伯納姆談到了這一點。
“We ran a scheme every night, 600 beds, called ‘A bed every night,’” he recounted, “and we funded it through various different ways. But we were the only place that was doing it, and you won’t be able to get the numbers down if you’re the only place.”
「我們每晚都實施一項『每晚一床』的計劃，提供600張床位，」他回憶道，「我們通過各種不同方式籌集資金。但我們是唯一這麼做的地方，如果你只是孤軍奮戰，就無法把數字降下來。」
Other homeless people, he said, came to Manchester because of the support available.
他說，其他無家可歸者因為這裡提供的支持而來到曼徹斯特。
“So it taught me that the only way is to do this together,” Mr. Burnham said.
「所以這讓我明白，唯一的辦法是共同行動，」伯納姆說。
