2000萬被系統性抹去的人：阿富汗女性的孤立絕境

Female Afghans Erased From Public Life in Five Years of Taliban Rule

EPHRAT LIVNI • 2026-08-14
The Taliban regained control of Afghanistan five years ago this week, returning to power after two decades and restoring an Islamic government that had severely restricted women’s rights.
就在五年前的這一週，塔利班重新控制了阿富汗，在闊別政壇20年後重返權力中心，並恢復了曾經嚴重限制婦女權利的伊斯蘭政府。
In those few years, women and girls have been systematically erased from most public life, experts say. And, some add, the limitations imposed on half the population could hobble Afghanistan far into the future.
專家表示，在這短短几年裡，婦女和女童已被系統性地從大多數公共生活中抹去。還有一些專家指出，強加於半數人口的限制可能會在未來很長一段時間內阻礙阿富汗的發展。
The harsh restrictions on women and girls are a return to conditions that prevailed the previous time the Taliban controlled Afghanistan, in the late 1990s. More than 160 decrees have been issued targeting women and girls, barring them from secondary and higher education and restricting their employment, movement, dress, speech and decision-making.
對婦女和女童的嚴厲限制使阿富汗恢復了20世紀90年代末塔利班上一次控制該國時的狀態。目前當局頒佈了160多項針對婦女和女童的法令，禁止她們接受中等和高等教育，並限制她們的就業、出行、著裝、言論及決策權。
“No other country in modern times has dismantled the rights of half its population through law, policy and practice to the same extent,” the United Nations agency on women said on Wednesday. “At the same time, overlapping humanitarian and economic crises and deepening poverty are also having a disproportionate impact on women and girls.”
「在現代，沒有哪個國家曾通過法律、政策和實踐以如此程度剝奪半數人口的權利，」聯合國婦女署週三表示。「與此同時，人道主義危機和經濟危機相互疊加以及日益加深的貧困也對女性造成了過高的影響。」
The official restrictions have reinforced social pressures, and many women say their communities increasingly press them to conform, a new U.N. Women report found, based on recent surveys. “Half a decade of escalating restrictions has narrowed where Afghan women can go, what they can do and how they can live,” it said. “The results are alarming.”
聯合國婦女署基於近期調查發布的一份新報告發現，官方的限制加劇了社會壓力，許多女性表示，她們所在的社區越來越多地迫使她們順從。「五年來不斷升級的限制措施壓縮了阿富汗女性的出行範圍、行為空間和生活方式，」報告稱。「其結果令人震驚。」
The agency surveyed about 5,000 Afghans in rural and urban communities this past spring and last year, asking women and men about life under the Taliban. There were some similarities in the genders’ answers — but some very stark differences as well.
該機構在今年春天和去年對城鄉社區的約5000名阿富汗人進行了調查，詢問了男性和女性在塔利班統治下的生活狀況。男女雙方的回答有一些相似之處，但也有著極其顯著的差異。
Many Afghans surveyed expressed disappointment with the course of their lives in recent years. Still, women reported levels of isolation and distress that suggest a growing mental health crisis, the U.N. report said.
許多受訪的阿富汗人對近年來自己生活的發展軌跡表示失望。但聯合國報告指出，女性所報告的孤立和痛苦程度表明，心理健康危機正在加劇。
廣告
More than half of men surveyed — about 55 percent — said their lives were worse than they had expected they would be before August 2021, compared with 65 percent of women who reported a similar sentiment. But their reasoning was starkly different.
超過一半的受訪男性（約55%）表示，他們的生活比2021年8月前的預期要糟，相比之下，有65%的女性表達了類似的感受。但雙方給出這種回答的理由截然不同。
Male respondents reported frustration with a lack of economic opportunities, while many women also blamed reduced access to education, for themselves or their children, and rules restricting women’s jobs and lives.
男性受訪者對缺乏經濟機會感到沮喪，許多女性則將原因歸咎於自身或子女受教育機會的減少，以及限制女性工作和生活的各種規定。
“By closing off education and many forms of employment, these restrictions compound the economic pressures already felt by most women,” the U.N. report said, noting the “profound personal toll” the situation has had on women in Afghanistan. Many women reported rising stress, worry and pressure, “consistent with wider evidence of deteriorating mental health,” it added.
聯合國報告稱：「通過切斷教育和多種形式的就業途徑，這些限制加劇了大多數婦女原本就感受到的經濟壓力，」報告指出，這一狀況對阿富汗婦女造成了「深重的個人代價」。報告還稱，許多女性表示壓力、焦慮和重負感不斷上升，「這與心理健康惡化這一更廣泛的證據相吻合」。
The report highlights the isolation many Afghan women now experience as a result of the restrictions. A vast majority of the women surveyed — 90 percent — said they must have a male family member’s permission to leave the house. And more than half said they went out only once or twice a month. Among male survey respondents, more than 95 percent reported leaving their homes daily.
報告強調，由於這些限制措施，許多阿富汗女性目前面臨孤立處境。絕大多數受訪女性(90%)表示，她們必須獲得男性家庭成員的許可才能離家。超過一半的人表示，她們每月只出門一到兩次。而在男性受訪者中，超過95%的人表示每天都會離家外出。
Women and girls’ adherence to the new Taliban rules is increasingly reinforced by people around them, like relatives and neighbors. The U.N. report noted that nearly 40 percent of women surveyed this year said they felt more pressure than before to tailor their behavior for fear that those they know will report them for inappropriate dress, speech or actions.
親戚和鄰居等周圍的人正在不斷強化婦女和女童對塔利班新規的服從。聯合國報告指出，在今年接受調查的女性中，近40%的人表示，由於擔心認識的人會舉報她們著裝、言論或行為不當，她們在規範自身行為方面感到了比以往更大的壓力。
“The Taliban have extended state control deep into the private sphere by transforming patriarchal norms into legal obligations and conscripting families into the enforcement of their ideology,” Belquis Ahmadi, a human rights lawyer and expert on Afghanistan, formerly at the United States Institute of Peace, wrote in a recent report on Taliban rule.
人權律師、曾任職於美國和平研究所的阿富汗問題專家貝爾基絲·艾哈邁迪在近期一份關於塔利班統治的報告中寫道：「塔利班將父權制規範轉化為法律義務，並驅使家庭參與執行其意識型態，從而將國家控制深入延伸到了私人領域。」
Human rights experts say that the crisis for women in Afghanistan has reached a critical point.
人權專家表示，阿富汗女性面臨的危機已達到臨界點。
Afghanistan is the only country in the world to formally prohibit access to education for girls and women beyond the primary level. The longer women and girls are excluded from schools, institutions and public life, the more entrenched the restrictive system becomes, the further behind they fall and the fewer models there are, making their ability to recover ever less likely, jeopardizing the country’s long-term prospects.
阿富汗是世界上唯一正式禁止婦女和女童接受小學以上教育的國家。女性被排斥在學校、機構和公共生活之外的時間越長，這種限制性制度就越根深蒂固，她們落後的程度就越深，可供效仿的榜樣也就越少，這使得她們復元的可能性越來越小，並危及該國的長期前景。
廣告
“As a new generation of girls grows without an education, their future becomes increasingly dark,” UNESCO wrote in a statement this week, calling for the full restoration of the right to education for Afghan girls and women.
聯合國教科文組織在本週的一份聲明中寫道：「隨著新一代缺乏教育的女童逐漸長大，她們的未來變得越來越黯淡。」該組織呼籲全面恢復阿富汗女童和婦女的受教育權。
Ms. Ahmadi wrote in her analysis, “Behind every Taliban decree is a human story: a university student packing away her books, a teacher dismissed from the profession she loves, a judge forced into hiding, a doctor prevented from treating patients, an entrepreneur closing her business, or a young girl asking her parents why she is no longer allowed to learn while her brother walks freely to school.”
艾哈邁迪在分析中寫道：「塔利班每一項法令的背後都有一段活生生的個人故事：一名女大學生默默收起書本；一名女教師被解僱，不得不離開她熱愛的職業；一名女法官被迫東躲西藏；一名女醫生被禁止為患者治病；一名女企業家關閉了自己的生意；一個小女孩問父母，為什麼她的兄弟可以自由自在地去上學，而她卻再也不被允許學習。」
She argues that the international community must speak up for the silenced.
她認為，國際社會必須為那些被噤聲的人發聲。
“The greatest danger facing Afghanistan today is not only that 20 million women and girls are being systematically erased from public life,” Ms. Ahmadi wrote. “It is that the world is slowly learning to live with it.”
「如今阿富汗面臨的最大危險不僅在於2000萬女性正被系統性地從公共生活中抹去，」艾哈邁迪寫道。「而是世界正在慢慢學會接受這種現狀。」

Ephrat Livni是時報全球新聞記者，常駐紐約。
