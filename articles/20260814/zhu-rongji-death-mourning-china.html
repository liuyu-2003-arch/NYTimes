朱鎔基去世，中國人哀悼一個充滿希望的時代

In a Chinese Leader’s Death, Mourning for a More Hopeful Time

袁莉 • 2026-08-14
In 1998, Zhu Rongji, then China’s premier, told the staff of an investigative news program that he was a devoted viewer. He went so far as to say that the broadcast had given him ideas for reforms.
1998年，時任中國總理朱鎔基告訴一檔調查新聞節目的工作人員，自己是該節目的忠實觀眾。他甚至表示，該節目為他提供了改革的思路。
He then challenged the Communist Party’s doctrine that the media should emphasize what it called positive reporting. “Did ‘mainly positive reporting’ mean that 99 percent of reports had to be positive?” he asked rhetorically. “Wouldn’t 51 percent be enough?”
隨後，他對中共關於媒體應該強調所謂「正面報導」的宣傳原則提出了質疑。「『什麼叫以正面報導為主，是指99%都應該正面報導嗎？」他反問道。「我看51%不也行嗎？」
“Without such programs, how can the people’s voices be heard?” he asked.
「沒有這樣的節目，群眾的聲音反映不出來，」他說。
Mr. Zhu was not calling for an independent press, which the party did not permit. The investigative show, after all, was aired by the state broadcaster. But he made clear that he regarded critical reporting not as a threat to the government, but as essential to effective government.
朱鎔基並不是在呼籲建立獨立的新聞媒體——這是黨所不允許的。畢竟，那檔調查節目是由國家電視台播出的。但他明確表示，他認為批評性報導不是對政府的威脅，而是實現有效治理不可或缺的一部分。
廣告
After Mr. Zhu’s death on Wednesday at 97, Chinese people circulated that story alongside many others portraying him as a straight talker, an independent thinker and, in the words of one commentator, “the finest premier in China’s march toward modernity.”
週三，97歲的朱鎔基去世後，中國民眾紛紛轉發這個故事，以及許多其他事蹟，將他描繪成一個直言不諱、獨立思考的人，用一位評論家的話來說，他是「中國邁向現代化進程中最傑出的總理」。
Mr. Zhu, who served as premier from 1998 to 2003 under former President Jiang Zemin, was the forceful technocrat behind some of China’s most consequential economic changes. He pushed the country deeper into the market economy and the global trading system, laying the foundations for two decades of extraordinary growth. Those changes also caused mass layoffs, strengthened the central government and left enduring social and economic problems.
朱鎔基曾於1998年至2003年在前國家主席江澤民任內擔任總理。作為一名強勢的技術官僚，他是中國一些影響最為深遠的經濟變革背後的推手。他推動中國更深入地融入市場經濟和全球貿易體系，為隨後20年的非凡增長奠定了基礎。但這些改革也導致了大規模的下崗潮，加強了中央政府的權力，並留下了持久的社會和經濟問題。
The grief was about more than Mr. Zhu himself. It was also about what he had come to represent: an era when China was opening to the world, officials could publicly acknowledge the country’s problems and the future seemed more prosperous and hopeful. In remembering that era, many people were also passing judgment on present-day China, which has become more closed to the world, less tolerant of criticism and far less confident about what lies ahead.
人們的哀悼不僅僅是為了朱鎔基本人，也包含了他所代表的那個時代：那時的中國向世界敞開大門，官員們可以公開承認國家存在的問題，未來似乎更加繁榮和充滿希望。在懷念那個時代的同時，許多人也在對當今的中國做出評判——如今的中國變得對世界更加封閉，對批評的容忍度更低，人們對未來也遠沒有過去那麼有信心。
On WeChat Moments, the semiprivate equivalent of a Facebook timeline, my feed filled with tributes from former journalists, entrepreneurs and professionals.
在微信朋友圈——相當於Facebook時間線的半私密社交平台——我的信息流裡充滿了前記者、企業家和專業人士的悼念。
An investment banker posted a black-and-white photo of Mr. Zhu and remembered “an era when the country was on the rise,” a shared memory, he wrote, that seemed “extraordinarily precious today.” “We were lucky just to have lived through it,” a real estate developer replied.
一位投資銀行家發布了一張朱鎔基的黑白照片，並回憶起「那個國家正處於上升期的時代」。他寫道，這段共同的記憶似乎「在今天顯得格外珍貴」。「能親身經歷那個時代，我們已經很幸運了，」一位房地產開發商回覆道。
Others posted photos of Mr. Zhu and his boss, Mr. Jiang, leaning toward each other, whispering and laughing heartily. Some comments said that Chinese leaders in that era still showed a human touch. The images evoked a political atmosphere that appeared more relaxed, collegial and human than the rigid one under Xi Jinping, the current president.
還有人發布了朱鎔基和他的上司江澤民的照片，兩人俯身靠近，低聲交談、開懷大笑。一些評論說，那個時代的中國領導人仍然展現出人情味的一面。這些畫面令人回憶起一種政治氛圍，比起現任國家主席習近平治下僵化的氛圍顯得更加寬鬆、融洽、更有人情味。
Many of the WeChat posts came from people I know or have interviewed. I’m withholding some of their names for fear of causing them trouble. One lawyer asked me to stop contacting him on WeChat and to use encrypted messaging apps such as Signal instead.
微信上的許多帖子來自我認識或採訪過的人。為了避免給他們帶來麻煩，我隱去了其中一些人的姓名。一名律師甚至要求我不要再通過微信聯繫他，轉而使用Signal等加密通訊應用。
On China’s more public social-media platforms, searches for Mr. Zhu’s name on Weibo and Xiaohongshu, or RedNote, produced sparse results dominated by state media. On Wednesday afternoon, a search I conducted on RedNote returned only 25 results. Some ordinary users omitted his name, posting only a photograph or referring elliptically to “that era” or “him.”
在中國更公開的社群媒體平台上，通過微博和小紅書搜索朱鎔基的名字得到的結果寥寥無幾，且主要來自官方媒體。週三下午，我在小紅書上搜索，只返回了25個結果。一些普通用戶隱去了他的名字，只發布了一張照片，或者含蓄地提到「那個時代」，或者「他」。
Nearly three decades after Mr. Zhu called the media “the people’s voice” and “a mirror for the government,” the public discussion of his own death appeared to be carefully managed.
在朱鎔基將媒體稱為「群眾喉舌」和「政府鏡鑒」近30年後，關於其本人去世的公眾討論似乎受到了嚴密的管控。
廣告
The authorities have seen how mourning for former leaders could turn into criticism of the current one. After Mr. Jiang died in 2022, people remembered the relative openness and optimism of his era, drawing thinly veiled comparisons with Mr. Xi. Weibo restricted comments and deleted a popular post thanking Mr. Jiang for allowing Chinese people to watch “Titanic.”
當局深知，對前領導人的哀悼可能演變成對現任領導人的批評。2022年江澤民去世後，人們追憶起他那個時代相對的開放和樂觀，隱晦地與習近平進行比較。微博限制了評論，並刪除了一條感謝江澤民允許中國民眾觀看《鐵達尼號》的熱門帖子。
When former Premier Li Keqiang died unexpectedly the next year, videos of him promising that China would remain open to the world were deleted. Comments calling him “a good premier for the people” were censored.
次年，前總理李克強意外去世，他承諾中國將繼續向世界開放的影片遭到刪除。稱他為「人民好總理」的評論也遭到了審查。
By the time Mr. Zhu died, the censors appeared determined not to let nostalgia for another leader from the reform era become a public referendum on Mr. Xi’s China.
到了朱鎔基去世時，審查機構似乎下定決心，不能讓人們對另一位改革時代領導人的懷念演變成對習近平治下中國的公投。
Lu Yiheng, a Chinese political commentator based in Toronto, saw a bitter irony in that nostalgia. Chinese people once dwelled on the failings of the reform era and imagined that a new generation of leaders would build something better. “But who could have imagined,” he wrote on X, “that today we would be grateful simply not to be heading toward another Cultural Revolution?”
常駐多倫多的中國政治評論員盧以恆（音）認為，這種懷舊情緒中蘊含著苦澀的諷刺。中國人曾經糾結於改革時代的種種弊病，設想新一代領導人能建設一個更美好的國家。「但誰能想到，」他在X上寫道，「今天，只因為我們沒有走向另一場文化大革命，就已經讓我們感激涕零？」
That reversal in expectations animated many of the tributes. Amid pervasive job insecurity, slumping consumption and the party’s tightening grip on private enterprise, what some people seemed to miss most was not prosperity itself but the belief that hardship would eventually produce something better.
這種預期的逆轉激發了許多悼念之情。在就業普遍不穩定、消費低迷以及黨對私營企業加強管控的背景下，一些人似乎最懷念的並不是繁榮本身，而是那種相信艱苦終將帶來更好未來的信念。
In a WeChat post, an executive recalled graduating from college in 1998, when jobs were scarce and the government was shedding workers. As he looked back, he wrote, that year may have marked the bottom for China’s economy. “Hardship is not frightening,” he wrote, “as long as there is hope.”
一位高管在微信帖文中回憶起自己在1998年大學畢業時的情景，當時就業崗位稀缺，政府正在裁員。他寫道，回過頭來看，那一年可能標誌著中國經濟的谷底。「艱苦並不可怕，」他寫道，「只要還有希望。」
廣告
The nostalgia fogs over the brutality of Mr. Zhu’s reforms. Yasheng Huang, an economist at M.I.T., told me that more than 30 million state-sector workers had lost their jobs, many before China had established a meaningful social safety net. He argues that Mr. Zhu later used wrenching reforms to address problems aggravated by some of his earlier policies.
然而，這種懷舊情緒掩蓋了朱鎔基改革的殘酷性。麻省理工學院經濟學家黃亞生告訴我，當時有超過3000萬國企工人失去了工作，而其中許多人下崗時，中國尚未建立起真正意義上的社會保障網。他認為，朱鎔基後來通過痛苦的改革來解決某些被他的早期政策所加劇的問題。
“His economic legacy was very complicated,” he said. “He used his right hand to solve problems created by his left.”
「他的經濟遺產非常複雜，」他說。「他用左手創造問題，右手解決問題。」
A Shenzhen resident surnamed Jin wrote on WeChat about how family members lost their jobs or were hounded by creditors during the Zhu years.
一位姓金（音）的深圳居民在微信上寫到：在朱鎔基時代，他的家人不僅失去了工作，還被債主不斷逼債。
Yet popular culture of the period urged workers to accept their suffering as a necessary sacrifice. A hit song told them to “start over,” while a nationally televised comedy sketch declared, “If I don’t get laid off, who should?”
然而，那個時期的流行文化卻敦促工人們將他們所受的苦難視為一種必要的犧牲。一首熱門歌曲告訴他們要「從頭再來」，而一部在全國播出的喜劇小品則宣稱：「我不下崗誰下崗？」
Mr. Zhu once said he hoped to be remembered as “a clean official” who had accomplished a few practical things. Mr. Jin saw another side to such ambition. “Throughout history,” he wrote, “some of those who make ordinary people suffer most are clean officials determined to get things done.”
朱鎔基曾說，他希望人們記住他是一個做成了幾件實事的「清官」。而金先生看到了這種抱負的另一面。「縱觀歷史，」他寫道，「那些讓老百姓受苦最深的，往往是那些決心辦大事的清官。」

袁莉為《紐約時報》撰寫「新新世界」專欄，專注中國及亞洲科技、商業和政治交叉議題。
