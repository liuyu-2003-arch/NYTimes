經濟悲觀情緒蔓延，中國農民工文學引發共鳴

As Economic Pessimism Spreads, China’s Worker-Writers Strike a Chord

YAN ZHUANG • 2026-08-20
A Chinese food delivery driver rushes from gig to gig, measuring time by the number of jobs he completes instead of by the passing of the seasons. He toils in cities across China to send money back to his family, but has been away for so long that his son calls him “that man” instead of “Dad.”
一位中國外賣騎手奔波於一個又一個外賣訂單之間，衡量時間的標準是完成了多少單，而非季節的更替。他在中國各地的城市裡辛勤工作，把錢寄回家，但他離家太久，以至於兒子叫他「那個人」而不是「爸爸」。
These are scenes from the poems of Wang Jibing, a food delivery driver, one of several Chinese poets and authors who have turned their experiences of repetitive factory labor and insecure gig work into art.
這些場景出自外賣騎手王計兵的詩歌。在中國有許多這樣的詩人和作家，將他們在重複性工廠勞動和不穩定的零工經濟中的經歷轉化為藝術。
This literary genre has ballooned in popularity in recent years, as many Chinese have grown pessimistic about the economy. For a wide swath of society, wages have stagnated, youth unemployment is high and the promise of a better life in return for hard work has faded. Some authors use their writing to call attention to the harsh realities of life for migrant and gig workers, while others celebrate the humanity that flourishes in spite of those conditions. Their styles are often raw and straightforward.
近年來，隨著許多中國人對經濟前景日益悲觀，這類文學體裁急劇流行起來。對於廣大社會群體來說，工資停滯不前，青年失業率居高不下，用辛勤勞動換取更好生活的承諾已經破滅。一些作家用他們的寫作來喚起人們對農民工和零工從業者嚴酷生活現實的關注，另一些作家則讚頌在這種境況下不屈不撓的人性。他們的風格往往質樸而直率。
Writers like Hu Anyan, a courier who wrote a best-selling memoir about the jobs he has worked across the gig economy, and Fan Yusu, who left her children behind to work as a live-in nanny in Beijing, have tapped into these themes. They have published essays online that have been shared widely and led to book deals. Some best-sellers have been translated and published overseas.
許多作家都觸及了這些主題，比如寫了一本暢銷回憶錄講述自己在零工經濟中從事過的各種工作的快遞員胡安焉，以及她離開孩子到北京做住家保姆的范雨素。他們在網上發表的文章被廣泛傳播，並由此得到了圖書出版合同。一些暢銷書已被翻譯並在海外出版。
廣告
Last month, Mr. Wang’s new poetry collection, “Low Flight,” won the Lu Xun Literary Prize, in the highest institutional recognition for the genre yet.
上個月，王計兵的新詩集《低處飛行》榮獲魯迅文學獎，這是迄今為止體制給予這一文學門類的最高榮譽。
For the Chinese public, curiosity about these workers is intertwined with anxiety about their own prospects. The concern may be particularly pronounced for members of China’s middle class. They “pay more attention to these kinds of narratives because they may represent their future lives,” said Zhu Ping, a professor of Chinese studies at the University of California San Diego’s literature department.
對中國公眾而言，對這些工人的好奇心與對自身前景的焦慮交織在一起。這種擔憂在中產階級成員中可能尤為明顯。加州大學聖地牙哥分校文學系中國研究教授朱萍表示，他們「更關注這類敘事，因為它們可能代表了自己未來的生活」。
Gig and factory workers are ubiquitous, yet the hardship and indignities they endure are rarely depicted by China’s state-controlled media, which prefers a more positive spin. As the literary work’s influence has grown, Chinese officials, wary of the power of workers’ grievances to illuminate policy shortcomings or spawn unrest, have increasingly sought to shape it, experts said.
零工和工廠工人無處不在，但他們所承受的艱辛和屈辱卻很少被中國官方媒體描繪，後者更傾向於積極的報導基調。專家表示，隨著這類文學作品影響力的增長，中國官員——他們警惕工人訴求可能暴露政策缺陷或引發騷亂的力量——已越來越多地試圖對其進行塑造。
The Rise of Migrant Workers’ Literature
農民工文學的興起
The genre known as migrant workers’ literature mirrors the trajectory of a work force that now numbers more than 400 million people.
被稱為「農民工文學」的門類反映了如今已超過4億的勞動力大軍的發展軌跡。
Since the 1980s, rural workers have powered China’s factories and cities while often being denied the health insurance, unemployment and retirement benefits available to urban residents. The literary movement’s early authors wrote essays and poems about their experiences in factories, but as manufacturing jobs have begun to disappear, its new breakout stars are more often doing gig work.
自20世紀80年代以來，農民工為中國的工廠和城市提供了動力，卻常常被剝奪城鎮居民享有的醫療保險、失業和退休福利。這一文學運動的早期作者寫下了關於他們在工廠經歷的散文和詩歌，但隨著製造業崗位開始消失，新一批脫穎而出的作者更多是從事零工工作。
廣告
One such star is Mr. Hu, the courier who wrote a best-selling memoir. The book, “I Deliver Parcels in Beijing,” was published in 2023 in China, where it was deemed that year’s best book by Douban, China’s equivalent to Goodreads. It is a diaristic account of the 19 jobs he held over two decades across China, including as a sorter at a logistics company, security guard, convenience store worker and courier.
其中一位明星是寫了暢銷回憶錄的快遞員胡安焉。他的書《我在北京送快遞》於2023年在中國出版，被豆瓣評為當年最佳圖書。這是一部日記體的記述，記錄了他20年間在中國各地從事的19份工作，包括物流公司的分揀員、保安、便利店店員和快遞員。
Mr. Hu’s work first came to public attention through an essay he posted online. It detailed his year on the night shift sorting packages at a logistics firm, where the work was grueling and he struggled to get used to sleeping during the day:
胡安焉的作品最初是通過他發表在網上的一篇文章引起公眾關注。該文詳細記述了他在一家物流公司上夜班分揀包裹的一年，工作艱苦，他努力適應白天睡覺的作息：
I passed by a row of houses on my way in every day. I could smell the cooking going on inside, see the people collapsed on their couches, tired and content after a day’s work. That, to me, was what true happiness looked like. Meanwhile I was already more exhausted than those people, and I hadn’t even reached the warehouse. I cursed myself in those moments: My damned body cursed my willpower, and my damned willpower cursed it back. I swore I would go right to sleep when I clocked off in the morning. But then the morning came around, and everything went the same as the day before. I was trapped in a loop.— “I Deliver Parcels in Beijing,” translated into English by Jack Hargreaves.
在上班的路上，經過一排平房，聞到屋裡傳出的飯菜香味，看到別人已完成一天的勞動，正愜意地癱坐在沙發上，我深深感到這種休閑的時刻才是真正的幸福，而我甚至還沒有開始幹活兒就已經比他們更累了——這時候我就會惡毒地咒罵自己，我的身體咒罵我的意志，我的意志也咒罵我的身體，我發誓明早下班後要立刻睡覺。可是到了明早，情況又和前一天一樣，就這麼周而復始。——胡安焉《我在北京送快遞》
Ding Nan, a 35-year-old teacher in Wuhan, said Mr. Hu’s book was the first piece of migrant workers’ literature she had read, and she has since sought out more works in the genre.
35歲的武漢教師丁楠（音）說，胡安焉的書是她讀的第一部農民工文學作品，此後她又讀了更多這類作品。
She said she was drawn to stories of gig workers because of their constant presence in her own life. “They are the strangers we regularly interact with, but I never knew what their lives were like,” she said.
她說自己之所以被零工工人的故事吸引，是因為他們在她自己的生活中無處不在。「他們是我們經常打交道的陌生人，但我從來不知道他們的生活是什麼樣的，」她說。
Some pieces approach the experience with more hope. In his poetry collection, “Low Flight,” Mr. Wang, the delivery driver, celebrates the dignity and resilience of delivery workers:
有些作品以更富希望的態度來呈現這些經歷。在他的詩集《低處飛行》中，外賣騎手王計兵讚頌了外賣員的尊嚴和韌性：
廣告
Who prescribes that with wings spread one must fly high
誰說展翅就要高飛
Low flight is also a flight
低處的飛行也是飛行
With wind rustling like birds’ chirps
也有風聲如鳥鳴
And wheels speeding like shooting stars
有車輪如流星
The meal delivery note attached to a takeout
包裝上貼著的訂單
In clear black and white symbolizes urgency
白紙黑字，急急如律令
廣告
Like a legal document and a magic incantation— “Low Flight,” translated by Chen Du and Xisheng Chen
是公文也如神符 ——王計兵《低處飛行》
Ms. Ding said that migrant workers’ stories also changed her preconceptions about the workers. “Society has many stereotypes about them — we think their lives are wretched, that they are oppressed,” she said. “But when I read these books, I realized they are also very optimistic.”
丁楠說，農民工的故事也改變了她對這些工人的成見。「社會對他們有很多刻板印象——我們認為他們的生活很悲慘，他們受到壓迫，」她說，「但當我讀到這些書時，我意識到他們也非常樂觀」。
“Low Flight” won Mr. Wang the Lu Xun prize, which is overseen by a government body and given out once every three years. Chinese state media has celebrated Mr. Wang’s win as heralding a new, utopian literary landscape in which anyone can find success.
《低處飛行》為王計兵贏得了魯迅文學獎，這個由政府機構管理的獎項每三年頒發一次。中國官方媒體盛讚王計兵的獲獎預示著一種新的、烏托邦式的文學景觀的到來，在這種景觀中，任何人都可以取得成功。
But a tension exists between the interests of the Chinese government, which downplays stories of poverty or social inequality, and migrant workers’ literature, which lays those realities bare, said Ms. Zhu of the University of California San Diego. She saw Mr. Wang’s award as part of the government’s effort to shape the genre by elevating a more acceptable voice — one that tended toward hope and optimism, rather than criticism of the system’s inherent unfairness.
但加州大學聖地牙哥分校的朱萍表示，一邊是中國政府淡化貧困或社會不公的故事，一邊是農民工文學將這些現實暴露無遺，兩者間存在著一種張力。她認為王計兵的獲獎是政府在通過提升一種更可接受的聲音——傾向於希望和樂觀，而非批評體制固有之不公的聲音——來塑造這一文學門類。
The Chinese government has said that it wants to foster workers’ voices as part of what it describes as “new literature and arts of the masses,” stories that have taken off in the online era, unconstrained by the traditional gatekeepers of the publishing industry.
中國政府表示希望培育工人的聲音，作為其所謂的「新大眾文藝」的一部分。這些故事在網路時代興起，不受出版業傳統機制的約束。
That means more resources and opportunities for writers — but largely for the ones who tell palatable stories, said Maghiel van Crevel, a professor of Chinese literature at Leiden University in the Netherlands who studies migrant workers’ literature. Depictions of hardship are acceptable, he said, but linking that hardship to government policy “would be crossing a red line and make it impossible to publish.”
荷蘭萊頓大學研究農民工文學的中國文學教授柯雷(Maghiel van Crevel)表示，這意味著作家們獲得了更多的資源和機會，但主要是那些講述順應主流的故事的人。他說，描繪苦難是可以接受的，但將這種苦難與政府政策聯繫起來「將會跨越紅線，導致無法出版」。
廣告
But Mr. van Crevel noted that receiving institutional support did not necessarily mean that a writer had “sold out.” Mr. Wang’s reception by the Chinese establishment “doesn’t necessarily make his writing less authentic,” he said.
但柯雷也指出，獲得體制支持並不一定意味著作家已經「妥協」。他說，王計兵被中國體制所接納「並不一定使他的寫作缺乏真實性」。
From the Factory Floor to Worker Advocacy
從工廠車間到工人權益倡導
Authors like Zheng Xiaoqiong have continued to speak out about workers’ rights even after achieving mainstream success.
鄭小瓊這樣的作家在獲得主流成功後也繼續為工人權利發聲。
Ms. Zheng rose to prominence in China in the 2000s for her poems chronicling the monotony and exhaustion women endured in factories. She described exploitative bosses, workers who suffered from severed fingers or occupational diseases and young women forced into prostitution:
鄭小瓊在2000年代聲名鵲起，用詩歌記錄了女性在工廠中忍受的單調和疲憊。她描寫了剝削成性的老闆、手指被切斷或患上職業病的工人，以及被迫賣淫的年輕女性：
the flowing products and interlocking time are swallowed up quickly
流動的製品與時間交錯，吞噬，這麼快
aging ten years flowing past like water enormous weariness
老了，十年像水樣流動，巨大的厭倦
floats through the mind for many years she’s stuck by
在腦海中漂浮，多年來，她守著
the screws one screw two screws turning to the left to the right
螺絲，一顆，兩顆，轉動，向左向右
fixing her dreams and her youth to some product look
將夢想與青春固定在某個製品，看著
at her pale youth running from an inland village
蒼白的青春，一路奔跑，從內陸鄉村
to a factory by the sea all the way to a shelf in America— “Woman Worker: Youth Pinned to a Station,” translated by Eleanor Goodman.
到沿海工廠，一直到美國某個貨架——鄭小瓊《女工：被固定在卡座上的青春》，由顧愛玲(Eleanor Goodman)譯為英文。
Ms. Zheng has received literary prizes and parlayed her writing into a better life and a magazine career. She has also continued to speak out about the issues facing workers in China and around the world.
鄭小瓊獲得了文學獎項，並利用寫作改善了生活，開啟了在雜誌社的職業生涯。同時她也繼續就中國和世界各地工人面臨的問題發聲。
“I’ve always hoped that those in China who have been fortunate enough to achieve upward social mobility can look back and offer a hand to those who are still bogged down, just as they once were,” she said in an interview.
「我一直希望，那些在中國有幸實現了向上社會流動的人能夠回過頭來，向那些仍然深陷和他們當初一樣的困境中的人伸出援手，」她在一次採訪中說。
“We cannot change reality with poetry,” she said, “but we have a responsibility to speak out.”
「我們無法用詩歌改變現實，」她說，「但我們有責任發聲」。
