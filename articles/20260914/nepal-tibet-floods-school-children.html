深入尼泊爾山洪災區：毀滅、奇蹟與悲痛

Along Himalayan Floods’ Path, Wiped Out Towns and Mounting Grief

MUJIB MASHAL, BINOD GHIMIRE • 2026-09-14
Taking a break from the noisy work of leading a school of over 500 students, Madhav Lamichhane would ride his motorbike down to the riverside Betrawati Bazaar every week. The principal skipped the busy shops around the town’s clock tower, preferring a quiet moment by the Ram temple to take in the “very beautiful flow” of the Trishuli River.
瑪達夫·拉米查內校長每週都會抽出時間，從管理一所有500多名學生的學校的嘈雜工作中抽身，騎著摩托車前往河畔的貝特拉瓦蒂集市。他會避開鎮上鐘樓周圍熙熙攘攘的商店，更喜歡在羅摩神廟旁邊享受片刻的寧靜，欣賞特里蘇利河「非常美麗的流水」。
But on Aug. 26, as he was in school preparing for the first morning bell, the river below suddenly became a violent torrent that obliterated much of the town of Betrawati, erasing its square from the map. It left no trace of the temple, the market or the concrete bridges, and only fragments of the hundreds of homes that dotted either side of the river.
但在8月26日，當他在學校準備敲響第一聲晨鐘時，下方的河流突然變成了洶湧的洪流，摧毀了貝特拉瓦蒂鎮的大部分地區，將其廣場從地圖上抹去。神廟、市場和混凝土橋樑都不復存在，點綴在河流兩岸的數百棟房屋只剩下殘骸。
Mr. Lamichhane and the school, up on a hillside, escaped its wrath.
拉米查內和位於半山腰的學校躲過了這場肆虐的災禍。
But about 200 students did not. The younger children, ages 5 to 11, had been waiting in the riverside town for a yellow school bus. The oldest, backpacks strapped on, had been slowly making their way uphill to the school when the deluge swallowed them.
但大約200名學生未能倖免。五歲到11歲的年幼孩子們當時正在河邊小鎮等待一輛黃色的校車。年紀較大的學生背著書包，正緩慢地向上走向學校，就在這時，洪水吞噬了他們。
廣告
“What can I say to the river?” Mr. Lamichhane sighed. He said that fewer than a third of the town’s families had scraped through without losing anyone, with many wiped out entirely.
「我能對這條河說什麼呢？」拉米查內嘆息道。他說，鎮上只有不到三分之一的家庭沒有失去任何一名家庭成員，許多家庭則全員遇難。
“It was very beautiful previously,” he said. “It is a cultural and historical place, too. But all that became the past.”
「以前這裡非常美麗，」他說。「這裡也是一個充滿文化和歷史的地方。但一切都成為了過去。」
When part of a Himalayan glacier near Nepal’s border with Tibet collapsed late last month, the ferocious water and debris that barreled through the gorge wiped out almost everything in their path for tens of miles. Two weeks later, the death toll stands at over 1,300, with 5,000 others missing, many most likely buried beneath the mud.
上月末，尼泊爾與西藏交界處附近的一座喜馬拉雅冰川發生部分崩塌，洶湧的洪水和夾雜的碎石衝過峽谷，摧毀了沿途幾十公里內幾乎所有的東西。兩周後，死亡人數已超過1300人，另有5000人失蹤，其中許多人極有可能被埋在泥漿下。
In the days after the floods, reporters from The New York Times traveled along the path of destruction, trying to reach areas cut off in a poor country with difficult terrain. What we found was towns and villages wiped off the map. Small reminders of life before remained in the sludge: a buried roof jutting out from the mud, a student’s backpack, an empty reading glasses case.
洪水過後，《紐約時報》記者沿著被破壞的路線行進，試圖抵達這個地形崎嶇的貧困國家裡那些被切斷聯繫的地區。我們發現了那些從地圖上被抹去的城鎮和村莊。淤泥中還殘留著一些昔日生活的痕跡：泥漿中露出的被掩埋的屋頂、一個學生的背包、一個空的的老花眼鏡盒。
The dozens of bridges and the tens of miles of destroyed roads will surely be rebuilt. Technical assessments are already underway for a financing plan. The expected billions in costs will add to the debt of a small country still paying back loans it took to rebuild after a catastrophic earthquake just over a decade ago.
被毀壞的幾十座橋樑和幾十公里的道路肯定會重建。為制定融資計劃，技術評估已經展開。預計高達數十億的成本將增加這個小國的債務負擔，它目前仍在償還十多年前一場災難性地震後為重建而申請的貸款。
But helping to count the number of dead and telling the stories of wiped out communities is left to loved ones who were away at the time or who climbed to higher ground as the water closed in — now unsure whether to consider themselves lucky to have survived, or cursed to bear the crushing grief and loneliness.
但是，統計死亡人數和講述被摧毀社區故事的責任留給了當時不在現場或在洪水逼近時爬向高處的親人們，如今他們不知該慶幸自己得以生還，還是該哀嘆自己要承受巨大的悲痛和孤獨。
The only lifeline connecting the remains of those settlements is a narrow dirt track that skirts the hills. The drive between some of the towns, which would have taken 15 minutes before the floods, took us seven to eight hours — the path was jammed with traffic and frequently blocked by mudslides caused by the last of the monsoon rains.
連接這些定居點遺迹的唯一生命線是一條沿著山丘邊緣的狹窄土路。在洪水來襲前，行駛在某些城鎮之間的路程只需15分鐘，如今卻耗費了我們七八個小時——這條路上擠滿了車輛，而且經常被季風季末期降雨引起的土石流所阻斷。
The Chilime hydropower tunnel, 10 miles from the border with China, was as far as we could get before the road collapsed into a dirt track along the river that was not safe to trek. Helicopters frequently flew through the narrow gorge on search and rescue operations to several tunnels in the area, where workers are feared trapped.
距離中國邊境約16公里的奇利梅水電站隧道是我們能抵達的最遠地方，再往前，道路塌陷，成了一條沿河的土路，無法安全通行。直升機頻繁穿梭於狹窄的峽谷，前往該地區的幾條隧道進行搜救，人們擔心有工人被困在那些地方。
Officials who had flown into Syrabrubesi and Timure — the stretch beyond what was accessible by road — and survivors who were airlifted out said that hundreds of buildings had vanished, with nearly 1,000 people missing.
飛抵西亞布魯貝西和蒂穆雷（公路可達範圍之外的區域）的官員以及被空運出來的倖存者表示，那裡數百棟建築消失了，近1000人失蹤。

“The area is deserted now,” said Dolma Thangjen Sherpa, an elected member of the ward where Syabrubesi is, who was sheltering in a monastery in Kathmandu.
「那個地區現在已經沒有人煙了，」西亞布魯貝西所在選區的民選委員多爾瑪·唐詹·夏爾巴說，她當時正在加德滿都的一座寺廟裡避難。
In the urban pockets along our journey — from Kathmandu to an army base and to a city nearly 100 miles downstream from where the floods originated that saw over 350 bodies wash up on its riverbanks — people flipped through lists of the dead, which ran multiple pages, and searched for clues in the lines of mangled bodies.
我們此行沿線的城市區域——從加德滿都到一個軍事基地，再到洪水發源地下游約160公里的一座城市（那裡有超過350具遺體被衝上河岸），人們翻閱著長達數頁的死者名單，在一排排支離破碎的遺體中尋找線索。
Most left in disappointment. Kami Delma Sherpa stared at a photograph of a woman with a golden tooth posted on the wall outside a hospital in Kathmandu, thinking she had finally found her sister, who worked at the border crossing. But when she went inside and looked at the body, it was not her.
大多數人都失望地離開了。卡米·德爾瑪·夏爾巴盯著貼在加德滿都一家醫院外牆上的一張鑲有金牙的女子的照片，以為終於找到了在邊境口岸工作的姐妹。但當她走進去看到屍體時，發現那並不是她。
Miracles in the Mud
泥漿中的奇蹟
As we traveled along the floods’ path of destruction, there were still small miracles to celebrate.
我們沿著洪水的毀滅路徑行進時，仍有一些小小的奇蹟值得慶幸。
A green house in Dhunge Bazaar remained standing, saving its residents, who had watched from their balcony as a whirlpool of mud and debris threatened to close in on them. A principal evacuated all of his students to safety, before his school was washed away. Three of the hundreds of workers trapped in hydropower project tunnels were pulled out alive more than a week after the floods.
丹格市集裡的一棟綠色房子矗立不倒，拯救了裡面的居民，他們曾在陽台上看著泥漿和碎石形成的漩渦步步逼近。一位校長在學校被沖走前將所有的學生疏散到了安全地帶。洪水發生一個多星期後，被困在水電項目隧道裡的數百名工人中有三人被活著救出。
Another unlikely escape was hiding in plain sight. We watched night fall over Betrawati last Friday, staring at a three-story house buried so deep in the sludge that only its roof and a sliver of its upper windows were visible. The search and rescue teams had moved on, the disposal of a decaying cow their last act that afternoon.
另一個不可思議的生還奇蹟就隱藏在我們眼前。上週五，我們看著夜幕降臨貝特拉瓦蒂，盯著一棟掩埋在污泥中的三層樓房，它只有屋頂和樓上窗戶的一點縫隙露在外面。搜救隊伍已經離開，那天下午他們最後的行動是處理一頭腐爛的牛。
As we began our drive out of the ghost town late at night, we could hear only crickets and the roar of water. No one knew that a woman, Chandika Shrestha, was still clinging to life inside the house. Her family was among hundreds who had resigned themselves to their loss and had begun final ceremonial rites by cremating effigies of their loved ones.
深夜，當我們驅車駛出這座鬼城時，我們只能聽到蟋蟀的叫聲和水的轟鳴。沒有人知道，一位名叫錢迪卡·什雷斯塔的女子仍然在這棟房子裡頑強求生。她的家人和數百個聽天由命的家庭一樣已經接受了失去親人的事實，並開始通過火化親人的人偶來舉行最後的儀式。
The next day, locals heard a faint cry. Rescuers scrambled to dig her out.
第二天，當地人聽到了一聲微弱的哭喊。救援人員迅速行動，把她挖了出來。
But those kinds of miracles were few.
但這類奇蹟少之又少。
Haunting Phone Calls
難以釋懷的電話
The school where Mr. Lamichhane is principal had become the town’s relief hub, with trucks offloading aid material and social workers trying to console and entertain children uprooted from their homes.
拉米查內擔任校長的學校已經成為鎮上的救災中心，卡車在這裡卸下援助物資，社工們則試圖安撫、逗樂那些失去家園的孩子們。
廣告
On the morning of the floods, as Mr. Lamichhane was preparing for the day in his office, he got a call from the school bus driver saying he had heard of flooding upstream.
發生洪水的當天早上，拉米查內正在辦公室裡為新的一天做準備時，接到了校車司機的電話，說他聽說上游發洪水了。
The driver had delivered two busloads of students to the school and was on his way to the town square for his final run.
這名司機已經把兩車學生送到學校，正前往鎮廣場跑最後一趟。
“I scrolled on my mobile but did not see any news of the flood,” the principal said. He phoned a friend upriver, who told him the market had collapsed. “The flood is coming,” the friend exclaimed.
「我查看手機，但沒看到任何有關洪水的消息，」校長說。他打電話給上游的一位朋友，朋友告訴他市場已經塌了。「洪水來了，」朋友大喊道。
Mr. Lamichhane told staff to evacuate the 250 students already at the school to the forested areas above, while he and others rushed down toward the river to see what they could do.
拉米查內告訴教職員工，將已經到校的250名學生疏散到上方有樹林的區域，同時他和其他人沖向河邊，看看他們能做些什麼。
He called anyone and everyone he could reach along the river: his staff who had not arrived at school yet, the parents of students.
他給河邊所有能聯繫上的人打電話：還沒到校的教職工，還有學生的家長。
Two phone calls, in particular, haunt him.
有兩通電話讓他尤其難以放下。
廣告
The first was to his elementary schoolteacher, whose riverside house he had often visited for a cup of tea. She had told him, “We are escaping! We are escaping!”
第一通是打給他的小學老師的，他以前經常去她河邊的房子裡喝茶。她告訴他：「我們在逃！我們在逃！」
“The connection was lost,” he said. “She is among the missing.”
「電話斷了，」他說。「她在失蹤人員名單中。」
He remembers the second call, to another colleague, Ajay Kumar Yadav, with anger.
第二通電話是打給同事阿賈伊·庫馬爾·亞達夫的，現在想來他還感到生氣。
“I told him to come out. But he did not believe that the river could come up to there,” he said. “And he lost his life.”
「我叫他出來。但他不相信河水能漲到那兒，」他說。「然後他丟了命。」
As the principal spoke, he took his cap off to show his head, which he had shaved in a ritual performed to mourn dead relatives. (He lost two cousins who had been truck drivers along the border.)
校長說著，摘下帽子，露出光頭——他在悼念已故親屬的儀式上剃去了頭髮。（他失去了兩個在邊境當卡車司機的表親。）
Attendance had not yet been taken when the floods came, making identifying the missing students a challenge.
洪水來時還沒來得及點名，這使得確認失蹤學生變得困難。
廣告
When we asked if he had to rely on families reporting their children as missing, he said, “There is no family to report,” adding, “The families are gone.”
當我們問他是否必須依靠家人來報告孩子失蹤時，他說，「沒有家人可以來報告了，那些家庭都沒了。」
