台灣廟宇裡的銳舞派對

Some Temples in Taiwan Are Hosting All-Night Raves

JOHN YOON, XINYUN WU • 2026-06-09
High in the humid hills above Taipei, Taiwan, the ruins of Shun An Temple sit along a hiking trail. The air smells of damp earth. Only a fragment of the temple walls and a few old incense burners remain.
在台北市郊潮濕的山林中，順安宮的廢墟坐落於一條徒步小徑旁。空氣裡瀰漫著泥土的潮氣。斷壁殘垣與幾個舊香爐是這座廟宇僅存的印記。
Here, the quiet of the woods is broken by the thump of techno and other electronic dance music.
在這裡，林間的靜謐被電子舞曲的律動打破。
Hundreds of young people are dancing, bringing life back to a site nearly lost to the forest.
數百名年輕人在此起舞，為這處幾乎已被森林吞沒的遺址重新注入了生機。
The crowd is a mix of locals and travelers from South Korea, Denmark, Britain and France.
人群中既有台灣本地人，也有來自韓國、丹麥、英國和法國的遊客。
Temples in Taiwan, some hundreds of years old, serve as community centers where Buddhism, Taoism and local folk religions blend. Revelers said the setting gave parties a connection to traditions that the clubs in Taipei lacked.
台灣的廟宇是佛教、道教與地方民間信仰交融匯聚的社區中心，其中有些已有數百年歷史。參加派對的人說，這樣的環境賦予了派對一種台北市區的夜店所無法給予的傳統聯結。
“There is a bit of a spiritual feeling,” said Lin Hsin-hui, a university student. “There’s also a strong cultural atmosphere, a certain sense of immersion.”
「會有一點靈性的感覺，」大學生林昕慧說。「文化氣質很強，會有一種背景帶入感。」
These gatherings have become more common in Taiwan in recent years, driven by young people looking for new nightlife spots.
近年來，這類聚會在台灣越來越常見，背後的推動力量是尋找新鮮夜生活場所的年輕人。
A recent temple party drew about 300 people.
最近一場寺廟派對吸引了約300人。
“Being in a familiar religious setting, it gives me a sense of something very rooted,” said Su You-sheng, a D.J. and drummer. “It adds a layer of mystery.”
「尤其是在熟悉的宗教裡面，對我來講有土根性的感覺，」DJ兼鼓手蘇宥生說。「多了一層神秘感。」
Setting up the hillside parties is physically demanding. Andrew Dawson and Olivia Delacour, who organize them, carry speakers and other equipment up the slippery trails with the help of volunteers.
籌辦山間派對是一項體力活。在志願者的協助下，組織者安德魯·道森和奧利維婭·德拉庫爾沿著濕滑的山路將音箱和其他設備搬上山去。
Ms. Delacour said the parties aim to tap into the sense of community around Taiwan’s temples.
德拉庫爾表示，這些派對的目標之一是借勢台灣廟宇所凝聚的那種社群感。
廣告
“We also make parties more intertwined with nature — the wind, the sun, the light, everything,” said Mr. Dawson, also known by the Chinese name Chen Xuanyu.
「我們也讓派對與自然更深地交融——風、陽光、光線，一切的一切，」道森說道，他還有一個中文名字，叫陳宣宇。
“And, of course, spirits.”
「當然，還有神靈。」
The temple managers welcome the parties. They see them as a way to bring young people back to Taiwanese traditions.
廟方管理人員對這些派對表示歡迎。他們認為，這是讓年輕人重新接觸台灣傳統文化的一種方式。
“If it allows more people to engage with it, there’s nothing wrong with that,” said Chu Chih-ming, an executive secretary at a temple that recently hosted another party.
「能夠讓大家多去接觸一下，沒有什麼不好，」最近承辦了另一場派對的某廟宇總幹事初志明說。
Partygoers are well-behaved, the organizers said. They prefer not to describe the events as raves, saying illicit drug use is less prevalent in Taiwan than in rave scenes abroad.
組織者表示，參加派對的人都很守規矩。他們不太願意把這些活動稱為銳舞派對，並指出違禁藥物的使用在台灣遠比國外的同類場合少見。
People at a recent Shun An Temple party said they were happy to hike up the mountain for an escape from the city.
在最近的順安宮派對上，許多人表示，他們很樂意爬山來到這裡，暫時逃離城市。
“This place feels a bit like it’s in Taipei — but also not really in Taipei,” said Jim Wu, who works at a think tank in the city.
在台北一家智庫工作的吳旭崧說：「這個地方有一點點像在台北，但是又好像不在台北。」
The temple, he said, feels like it is “not quite in the real world.”
他說，這座寺廟感覺「很不像在現實世界」。
As 3 a.m. approached, a police officer arrived at the temple to check on the noise.
凌晨3時將近，一名警察出現在寺廟，前來了解噪音情況。
Mr. Dawson said the officer listened as the organizers promised to lower the volume. Then he told them to have fun and left.
道森說，那名警察聽完主辦方保證會把音量調低之後，告訴大家玩得開心，然後就離開了。
