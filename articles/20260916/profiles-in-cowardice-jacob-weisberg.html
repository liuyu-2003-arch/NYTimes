懦夫群像：為何這些權勢人物向川普卑躬屈膝

Why Did These Powerful People Kowtow to Donald Trump?

JOSEPH O'NEILL • 2026-09-16
PROFILES IN COWARDICE: A Study of Collaboration in the Trump Era, by Jacob Weisberg
《懦夫群像：川普時代的「共犯」研究》，雅各布·韋斯伯格 著(PROFILES IN COWARDICE: A Study of Collaboration in the Trump Era, by Jacob Weisberg)
Say what you like about President Trump, he has ushered in a golden age of cravenness. A nation long perceived, not least by its own nationals, as resilient and courageous — a people of gallant pioneers, fearless entrepreneurs, irresistible war heroes, nerveless astronauts, brave civil rights activists — is now forced to contemplate an entirely new set of data. It seems that, when placed under mild authoritarian pressure, an extraordinary number of powerful Americans, ostensibly honorable and right-thinking, quickly revealed themselves to be cowardly and unprincipled and made of the wrong stuff.
對川普總統你可以有各種評價，但你不得不承認，他開啟了一個怯懦的黃金時代。長期以來，美國一直被視為一個堅韌而勇敢的國家——至少在美國人自己的認知中。這裡的國民是英勇的拓荒者、無畏的創業者、所向披靡的戰爭英雄、沉著冷靜的宇航員和勇敢的民權活動人士，如今，這個國家卻不得不面對一組全新的事實。事實似乎表明，在些許的威權壓力下，數量驚人的美國權勢人物——表面正直、深明是非——迅速暴露出怯懦、無原則的本質，原來不過是徒有虛名之輩。
The journalist Jacob Weisberg is one of the first to attempt to chronicle this new landscape. In a dismal inversion of the John F. Kennedy classic “Profiles in Courage,” Weisberg’s “Profiles in Cowardice” offers case studies of eight representative men and women “who recognized the danger Trump posed to American values, who had the ability to say no to him and who at earlier moments showed flickers of integrity — but who, when a more serious test came, failed to act courageously, in ways that reveal both their moral weakness and the fragility of the institutions they represented.”
記者雅各布·韋斯伯格是最早嘗試記錄這一新景象的人之一。相較於約翰·甘迺迪的經典之作《勇者群像》(Profiles in Courage)，他的《懦夫群像》呈現出一幅令人黯然失望的反向圖景，書中選取了八位具有代表性的男女作為案例研究對象，他們「都清楚地意識到川普對美國價值觀構成的威脅，都有能力對他說『不』，並且在此前的某些時刻也曾閃現出正直的火花——但當真正嚴峻的考驗來臨時，他們卻未能拿出勇氣挺身而出，這種行為既暴露了他們自身道德上的軟弱，也暴露了他們所代表的制度是何等脆弱。」
His focus is 2025, the first year of the second Trump administration, when we witnessed what Weisberg terms “the Great Capitulation.” The author’s specimens of moral failure are drawn, as they should be, from the country’s supposed crème de la crème: Brad Karp, the former chair of the law firm Paul, Weiss; the private equity mogul Steve Schwarzman; the Amazon founder and Washington Post proprietor Jeff Bezos; the media personality Megyn Kelly; the former Senate majority leader Mitch McConnell; the former President George W. Bush; the Dartmouth College president Sian Beilock; and last but certainly not least, John Roberts, the chief justice of the United States.
本書聚焦於2025年，也就是川普重返白宮執政的第一年，當時我們親眼目睹了韋斯伯格所謂的「大投降」。作者選取的道德失敗樣本，理當來自這個國家所謂的精英中的精英：寶維斯律師事務所前主席布拉德·卡普，私募股權巨頭蘇世民(Stephen Schwarzman)，亞馬遜創始人、《華盛頓郵報》老闆傑夫·貝佐斯，知名媒體人梅根·凱利；前參議院多數黨領袖米奇·麥康乃爾，前總統喬治·W·布希，達特茅斯學院校長西安·貝洛克，最後當然還有美國首席大法官約翰·羅伯茨。
Weisberg’s intention isn’t simply to lambaste these individuals, even as he hopes that shaming them will serve a useful purpose. Because cowardice “wears disguises: pragmatism, realism, caution, institutional loyalty,” he investigates the specific situational and psychological forces at play in the moral downfall of each of his subjects, taking care, it must be said, to acknowledge that it isn’t straightforward to resist lawless state power.
韋斯伯格的目的並不只是痛斥這些人，儘管他希望對他們的羞辱能夠發揮一些有益的作用。因為怯懦「會披上各種偽裝：實用主義、現實主義、謹慎、對機構的忠誠」，所以他考察了每個研究對象在道德墮落過程中所面臨的具體處境和心理動因，同時必須承認的是，他也謹慎地指出，抵抗無法無天的國家權力並不容易。
Standing your ground can be frightening; it can negatively affect one’s career and net worth; it can leave one socially isolated; and, when you’re trying to discharge institutional responsibilities, it can feel ethically complicated. But in the final analysis, Weisberg suggests, the rights and wrongs were pretty clear. His subjects are voluntary members of the American elite who were at no risk of imprisonment or impoverishment.
堅守立場固然令人心生畏懼；它可能損害一個人的事業前途和身家財產；它可能讓人陷入社交孤立；而當你試圖履行一個機構賦予你的職責時，這甚至可能在道德倫理上讓人備感糾結。但韋斯伯格認為，歸根結底，是非曲直其實相當清楚。他筆下的這些人物都是自願參與其中的美國精英階層成員，根本不存在鋃鐺入獄或傾家蕩產的風險。
Nobody made Karp transform Paul, Weiss into a quisling law firm. It was not force majeure that made Beilock respond to the Republican attack on academic freedom with a uniquely deferential strategy. Bezos and Kelly always had the option of not selling their souls. Schwarzman, a billionaire many times over and a onetime Jeb Bush Republican, had no need to recast himself as a Trump loyalist. No rule of political life compelled George W. Bush to remain largely silent about his party’s assault on the American experiment. As for Mitch McConnell — well, let’s not speak ill of the undead.
沒有人強迫卡普把寶維斯變成向權力俯首稱臣的律所。面對共和黨對學術自由的攻擊時，貝洛克所採取的那種格外恭順的策略也並非出於不可抗力。貝佐斯和凱利始終可以選擇不出賣靈魂。蘇世民是身家數十億美元的億萬富翁，曾經還是傑布·布希的共和黨支持者，他根本沒有必要把自己重塑為川普的忠誠擁護者。政治生活中也沒有任何一條規則，逼著喬治·W·布希對其所在政黨對美國民主實驗的攻擊基本保持沉默。至於米奇·麥康乃爾——好吧，我們還是不要說活死人的壞話了。
And then there’s the case of John Roberts, whom Weisberg views as part doormat, part flawed Shakespearean hero: “The triumph of Roberts’s jurisprudence,” he suggests, “came at the worst possible moment. Just as a lawless president was returning to power, the immunity ruling in Trump v. the United States granted him legal cover for tyranny.” Poor Justice Roberts! There’s a divinity that shapes his ends, rough-hew them how he will.
再來看約翰·羅伯茨。韋斯伯格認為，他一半像任人踐踏的門墊，一半像有缺陷的莎士比亞式英雄人物。他寫道，「羅伯茨司法理念的勝利發生在最糟糕的時刻。就在一個無法無天的總統重新掌權之際，『川普訴美國案』中的豁免權裁決為他的暴政提供了法律掩護。」可憐的羅伯茨大法官！任他如何謀劃自己的命運，冥冥之中自有力量替他安排結局。
I assess the chief justice differently. I think that he falls into the same category as Stephen Miller and Pete Hegseth and Elon Musk. Like them, Roberts is an ideologue rather than a moral weakling: He ruled in favor of Trump not out of a misplaced juristic conscientiousness but precisely because he wanted to protect and empower a Republican president brazenly attacking the constitutional order.
我對這位首席大法官的評價則不盡相同。我認為，他其實與斯蒂芬·米勒、彼特·海格塞斯和伊隆·馬斯克屬於一類人。和他們一樣，羅伯茨是一個堅定的意識型態分子，而非什麼意志薄弱之輩：他做出有利於川普的裁決，並非出於錯位的法學良知，而恰恰是因為他想保護和賦權一位悍然攻擊憲政秩序的共和黨總統。
Political invertebrates will always be with us, of course: See the recent ignominy of Gov. Jared Polis of Colorado (freeing a would-be election tamperer at the insistence of Trump), one of many powerful Democratic officials whose fecklessness and timidity will soon receive separate examination.
當然，政治上的軟骨頭永遠都會存在：看看科羅拉多州州長賈里德·波利斯最近的不堪舉動吧——在川普堅持要求下，他釋放了一名企圖破壞選舉的人。像這樣的例子還有很多，許多手握權力的民主黨官員的懦弱和膽怯，他們很快將受到另行審視。
It must be pointed out, as Weisberg does in this valuable and dauntless book, that for every poltroon in high places, thousands of ordinary citizens have raised their heads above the parapet, none more bravely and persistently than the people of Minnesota as they protected their neighbors from rampaging federal officers. Their example upholds what’s left of this country’s reputation for courage.
必須指出，正如韋斯伯格在這本可貴、無畏之作中所述，儘管身居高位者中不乏懦夫，成千上萬的普通民眾卻挺身而出。其中最勇敢、最堅持不懈的莫過於明尼蘇達州人民，他們保護鄰居免受聯邦官員的暴行。正是他們的這種榜樣維護了這個國家殘存的勇敢聲譽。
