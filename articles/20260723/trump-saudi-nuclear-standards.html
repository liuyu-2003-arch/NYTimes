川普為沙烏地阿拉伯敞開鈾濃縮之門，引發核擴散擔憂

With Saudi Deal, Trump Loosens Nuclear Standards for One Close Ally

DAVID E. SANGER, WILLIAM J. BROAD • 2026-07-23
President Trump went to war with Iran in part to force compliance with two longstanding American demands: Tehran must stop enriching nuclear fuel for at least two decades, and it must allow international inspectors to go anywhere and see anything to verify the country is not producing a bomb.
川普總統對伊朗開戰，部分原因是為了迫使伊朗遵守美國長期提出的兩項要求：德黑蘭必須至少停止鈾濃縮活動20年，並且必須允許國際核查人員前往任何地點、檢查任何內容，以確認它沒有在製造核武器。
On Wednesday, Mr. Trump took the reverse approach to Saudi Arabia, its close ally in the region. To seal a deal for Westinghouse and other American companies to seize a big piece of the business in building two nuclear reactors on Saudi territory — and more to come — it opened the door to allowing the kingdom to begin enriching its own nuclear fuel after two years.
週三，川普對美國在該地區的親密盟友沙烏地阿拉伯採取了截然相反的做法。為了促成西屋電氣及其他美國公司拿下在沙烏地阿拉伯境內建造兩座核反應堆的重大合同——且後續還有更多項目——美國為沙烏地阿拉伯打開了大門，允許其在兩年後開始自行濃縮核燃料。
And it gave in to the kingdom’s demand that it not have to abide by an inspection agreement that applies to more than 140 other countries. Those rules, the Saudis said, are just too intrusive.
美國還同意了沙烏地阿拉伯提出的一項要求：即不必遵守一項適用於全球140多個國家的核查協議。沙烏地阿拉伯方面表示，這些規則太過侵入性。
Now Mr. Trump will have to make the case to Congress, and to allies throughout the Middle East, that in loosening the nuclear standards for one close ally, he is not ushering in a new age of proliferation, especially in a region of the world already on fire. If the daily missile exchanges with Iran finally give way to resumed negotiations, there is no question Iran will seize on the concessions made to Riyadh and insist on the same.
如今，川普將不得不向國會以及整個中東地區的盟友解釋：為一個親密盟友放寬核標準並不會開啟一個新的核擴散時代，尤其是在這個本已戰火不斷的地區。如果與伊朗的每日導彈交火最終讓位於新一輪談判，伊朗必然會援引給予利雅得的種種讓步，要求獲得同等待遇。
廣告
Already on Wednesday, administration officials were arguing that there was nothing to worry about, and that the United States will be so engaged in Saudi Arabia’s nuclear future that there is no way the kingdom could divert its nuclear fuel and know-how to a secret bomb program.
就在週三，政府官員已開始聲稱沒有什麼好擔心的，美國將深度介入沙烏地阿拉伯未來的核計劃，因此沙烏地阿拉伯根本不可能將核燃料和相關技術轉用於祕密的核武器計劃。
They may prove right. But nuclear history is littered with examples of countries that pledged to obey the rules, and surreptitiously raced for a bomb.
他們或許是對的。但縱觀核歷史，不乏信誓旦旦遵守規則、暗中加速推進制造核武器的前車之鑒。
Iran’s first research reactor was provided by the United States to the shah of Iran, complete with weapons-grade uranium — technology that fell into the hands of the mullahs who took over the country in 1979. India’s first nuclear test used plutonium from a heavy-water reactor that had been a gift from the Canadian government in the 1950s. Pakistan stole its centrifuge designs from a European firm that hired A.Q. Khan, now celebrated as the father of the Pakistani bomb. Taiwan and South Korea, stalwart American allies, had secret bomb programs of their own until American intelligence agencies discovered them and quashed the efforts.
伊朗的第一座研究性核反應堆由美國提供給伊朗國王，並附帶武器級濃縮鈾——這項技術後來落入了1979年接管伊朗的神職人員之手。印度進行第一次核試驗時使用的鈈來自加拿大政府在1950年代贈予的一座重水反應堆。巴基斯坦則是從一家歐洲公司盜取了離心機設計圖紙——阿卜杜勒·卡迪爾·汗受雇於該公司，如今他被譽為「巴基斯坦核彈之父」。台灣和韓國這兩個美國堅定盟友也曾擁有各自的祕密核武器計劃，直至被美國情報機構發現後才被迫叫停。
Mr. Trump has many reasons to solidify the American relationship with Saudi Arabia these days. He can argue that if the United States does not provide the country with its best reactors and nuclear technology, Russia or China will. At a time when tensions have flared with the Saudis — for a while Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman prohibited the United States from launching attacks on Iran from U.S. bases in Saudi Arabia — anything that draws the Saudis deeper into the American technology stack makes sense to the White House.
川普如今有許多理由鞏固與沙烏地阿拉伯的關係。他可以辯稱，如果美國不向沙烏地阿拉伯提供最先進的反應堆和核技術，俄羅斯或中國就會這麼做。在美沙關係出現裂痕之際——王儲穆罕默德·本·薩勒曼曾一度禁止美國從駐沙烏地阿拉伯基地對伊朗發動襲擊，任何能將沙烏地阿拉伯更深地拉入美國技術體系的做法對白宮來說都是有意義的。
“Rest assured, these agreements uphold the highest standards of nuclear safety and nonproliferation,” Chris Wright, the secretary of energy, said in a statement on Wednesday announcing the deal, “while relying on the world’s best nuclear technology and scientists, designed right here in the United States.”
「請放心，這些協議堅持了核安全和不擴散的最高標準，」能源部長克里斯·賴特在週三宣布該協議的聲明中表示，「同時依託於世界最優秀的核技術和科學家，而這些技術都是在美國設計的。」
Mr. Wright signed the deal with the Saudi minister of energy, Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman, but the White House did not immediately release the text — or the two secret letters accompanying it. All of those will now go to Capitol Hill for congressional review. But as a practical matter, Congress would have to assemble a veto-proof majority to override Mr. Trump’s initiative.
賴特與沙烏地阿拉伯能源大臣阿卜杜勒阿齊茲·本·薩勒曼親王共同簽署了協議，但白宮並未立即公布協議文本，也未公開隨附的兩封密函。所有這些文件都將提交國會審議。但從現實角度而言，國會必須湊夠足以推翻總統決定的票數，才能否決川普的這項計劃。
廣告
It is a process that has been used many times under the Atomic Energy Act of 1954, a key element of Dwight D. Eisenhower’s “Atoms for Peace” program. The idea was to spread nuclear energy, with safeguards to assure that fuel is not diverted to bomb projects.
這是根據1954年《原子能法》多次使用過的程序，也是德懷特·艾森豪威爾「原子用於和平」計劃的重要組成部分。其初衷是在核查機制的保障下推廣核能，以確保核燃料不會被轉作武器項目之用。
But Saudi Arabia poses a particular kind of problem, hard to imagine in Eisenhower’s time. It is a country that is racing to shed its petroleum-based past and eager to embrace nuclear energy to build data centers and power a future driven by artificial intelligence, yet fearful of what might happen if the Iranians get the bomb.
然而，沙烏地阿拉伯帶來了一種在艾森豪威爾時代難以想像的特殊難題。這個國家正全力擺脫依賴石油的發展模式，希望藉助核能建設數據中心，為一個由人工智慧驅動的未來提供能源；與此同時，它又擔心，一旦伊朗擁有了核武器，將會帶來怎樣的後果。
Saudi officials have made no secret of their determination to keep pace with Tehran’s apparent efforts to build a nuclear weapon, and to match them if they do. In 2018, Prince Mohammed pledged that his kingdom would try to develop or acquire nuclear weapons if Iran continued its work toward a bomb — despite the fact that Saudi Arabia signed on to the Nuclear Nonproliferation Treaty in 1988, after delaying for 18 years. Other Saudi officials have issued similar warnings in recent years, or talked about the need for Saudi Arabia to become a “threshold” nuclear state, a few screwdriver turns away from building its own weapon.
沙烏地阿拉伯官員毫不掩飾自己的決心：要與德黑蘭製造核武器的明顯努力保持同步，而且如果伊朗成功造出核彈，沙烏地阿拉伯也要擁有。2018年，穆罕默德王儲曾承諾，如果伊朗繼續推進核武器研發，沙烏地阿拉伯將嘗試開發或獲取核武器——儘管沙烏地阿拉伯在拖延18年後於1988年簽署了《不擴散核武器條約》。近年來，其他沙烏地阿拉伯官員也發出過類似警告，或談到沙烏地阿拉伯有必要成為核門檻國家——離製造自己的核武器只有幾步之遙的國家。
“I find this decision incredibly shortsighted,” said James M. Acton, a nuclear analyst at the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace. “It appears to provide a pathway to a country that really might want to proliferate. You cannot exclude the possibility that Saudi Arabia would nationalize this facility and use it make a bomb.”
「我認為這一決定極其短視。」卡內基國際和平基金會核問題分析師詹姆斯·阿克頓表示。「這項協議似乎為一個確實有可能尋求核擴散的國家提供了一條道路。你不能排除這樣一種可能性：沙烏地阿拉伯將這座設施收歸國有，並利用它製造核武器。」
It is hardly a new problem. President George W. Bush negotiated a similar deal with the United Arab Emirates, finalized by President Barack Obama in 2009, whose terms were so restrictive that it became known as the gold standard of nuclear agreements. In return for the technology, the U.A.E. agreed to forgo producing nuclear fuel forever, and it signed the “Additional Protocol,” as the International Atomic Energy Agency calls an agreement allowing intrusive inspections. The U.A.E. now has four civilian nuclear reactors, built largely with the help of South Korea, and there is little worry that the country is interested in anything other than electricity production.
這並不是一個新問題。小布希總統曾與阿拉伯聯合酋長國談判達成過一項類似協議，並由歐巴馬總統於2009年最終敲定。由於協議條款極其嚴格，它後來被稱為核協議的黃金標準。作為獲得核技術的交換條件，阿聯酋承諾永遠放棄自行生產核燃料，並簽署了國際原子能機構所謂的《附加議定書》，允許進行具有高度侵入性的核查。如今，阿聯酋已擁有四座民用核反應堆，大部分是在韓國的幫助下建成的，而且幾乎沒有人擔心該國對核能有發電以外的任何企圖。
When South Korea went down the same path, the United States won a 10-year moratorium on its making its own fuel. But when President Lee Jae Myung came to Washington late last year, he got an agreement from Mr. Trump to lift the restraints. (It appears that the restrictions are still in place, at least for now.)
韓國走上同一道路時，美國成功爭取到為期10年的韓國自產核燃料禁令。但當韓國總統李在明去年年底訪問華盛頓時，從川普那裡獲得了解除上述限制的承諾。（目前看來，限制似乎仍然有效，至少暫時如此。）
“It was clear Saudi Arabia was going to be different than the Emiratis,” said Robert Einhorn, a nuclear expert at the Brookings Institution who negotiated many of the toughest nuclear agreements of the past few decades as a State Department official. “They felt the inspections were just too intrusive and they weren’t going to renounce enrichment forever.”
「很明顯，沙烏地阿拉伯會與阿聯酋不同，」布魯金斯學會核問題專家羅伯特·艾因霍恩表示。他曾任國務院官員，參與談判了過去數十年間最為艱難的多項核協議。「他們覺得核查措施太過侵入性，而且也不準備永久放棄鈾濃縮能力。」
廣告
While the Saudis agreed to a two-year study of whether it was economically feasible to produce their own nuclear fuel, they seem determined to pursue an enrichment capability, even if they had to wait awhile.
雖然沙烏地阿拉伯同意用兩年時間研究自行生產核燃料是否具有經濟可行性，但他們顯然決心最終建立自己的鈾濃縮能力，哪怕需要等待一段時間。
Once the terms of the Saudi deal are made public, other nations may well insist on the same treatment. The U.A.E. has a proviso in its accord that allows it to demand “consultations” with the United States if a neighboring country gets a better deal.
一旦沙烏地阿拉伯協議條款公開，其他國家很可能會要求獲得同等待遇。阿聯酋的協議中有一項條款，允許其在鄰國獲得了更好的待遇時有權要求與美國進行「磋商」。
Heather Williams, director of the Project on Nuclear Issues at the Center for Strategic and International Studies, a Washington think tank, said the Saudi accord puts more pressure on the Trump administration to decisively eliminate Iran’s ability to build an atomic bomb.
華盛頓智庫戰略與國際研究中心的核問題項目主任希瑟·威廉斯表示，沙烏地阿拉伯協議給川普政府帶來更大壓力，要求其果斷消除伊朗製造原子彈的能力。
“Preventing a Saudi nuclear weapon now means getting a concrete and hard-hitting resolution on things like inspections, what to do with the stockpile and the status of nuclear sites, including Pickaxe Mountain,” she said, referring to a complex of deep tunnels in Iran thought to harbor atomic bomb-making gear. Failure to do so, Dr. Williams added, would raise the risk of a proliferation cascade in which many nations might race for the ability to make a bomb.
她說：「如今要阻止沙烏地阿拉伯擁有核武器就意味著，必須在核查措施、如何處理核材料庫存，以及包括鎬山在內的核設施狀態等問題上達成一個具體而強硬的解決方案。」她所說的鎬山指的是伊朗境內一處由深層隧道組成的設施群，外界認為那裡可能藏有製造核武器的設備。威廉斯博士補充說，如果無法做到這一點，將加劇出現核擴散連鎖反應的風險，即許多國家可能會競相獲得製造核武器的能力。
The Saudi push for a nuclear accord with Washington, she added, echoes “a hedging strategy we are seeing with other allies and partners. Their neighborhoods are increasingly dangerous,” in many cases because their old guarantor of security, the United States, “isn’t as reliable as it once was.” The result is that they seek alternative deals and arrangements to bolster their security.
她還表示，沙烏地阿拉伯謀求與華盛頓簽署核協議的努力折射出「我們正從其他盟友和夥伴身上看到的一種對沖策略」。它們所處的地區日益危險，在很大程度上是因為美國這個昔日的安全保障者「已不再像過去那樣可靠」。結果是，它們尋求替代協議和安排來加強自身安全。
Dr. Williams said the Saudi accord can be seen as a victory for the United States “because it maintains U.S. partnerships in the region when China is competing for them.” The problem, she added, is that its terms are weak. “We got the partnership, but not the guardrails.”
威廉斯表示，沙烏地阿拉伯協議可以被視為美國的勝利，「因為在中國也正在爭奪這些地區夥伴關係的時候，它維持了美國在該地區的合作關係。」但她補充說，問題在於協議條款太過寬鬆。「我們拿到了夥伴關係，卻沒有護欄。」
廣告
But to those who worry that the world is on the cusp of a new nuclear breakout — what John F. Kennedy warned about just before his assassination — the Saudi deal is a step toward that danger. Kennedy was wrong when he predicted there would be more than two dozen nuclear-armed nations by the 1970s. Today, there are only nine, but it may turn out he only got the timing wrong.
然而，對於那些擔心世界正處於新一輪核擴散爆發邊緣的人來說——這正是約翰·甘迺迪在遇刺前不久所警告的危險——沙烏地阿拉伯協議正是邁向這一危險的一步。甘迺迪曾預測，到20世紀70年代，擁有核武器的國家數量將超過二十多個，但他的預測並沒有實現。如今，全世界只有九個國家擁有核武器。不過，事實可能證明，他只是在時間節點上的判斷有誤。
“There’s no happy ending if Saudi Arabia gets within several weeks or months of making a bomb,” said Henry D. Sokolski, a nuclear policy official in the Defense Department from 1989 to 1993 who now heads the Nonproliferation Policy Education Center, a Washington-based nonprofit organization.
"如果沙烏地阿拉伯在距離製造核彈只有數週或數月之遙的程度上止步，不會有什麼好結果的，"亨利·索科爾斯基說，他曾於1989年至1993年在國防部擔任核政策官員，現任華盛頓非營利組織防核擴散政策教育中心主任。
“Everyone worries about a proliferation chain reaction. Granting the Saudis the right to make nuclear fuel after what Iran has done is like trying to put out a fire with kerosene. It’s a sad situation. It should never have gone this far.”
「所有人都擔心核擴散連鎖反應。在伊朗發生這些事情之後，給予沙烏地阿拉伯製造核燃料的權利就像試圖用煤油去滅火。這是一個令人悲哀的局面。事情本不該走到這一步。」他補充說。
