高市早苗曬超負荷工作日常引發爭議

Japan Is Startled by Prime Minister’s Grueling Sleep Schedule

赫海威, HISAKO UENO • 2026-07-23
Monday was Sea Day in Japan, a public holiday when many people escape the summer heat by swimming, surfing and lounging at the beach. But not Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi.
週一是日本的「海之日」，這是一個公共假日，許多人趁此機會去游泳、衝浪、在海灘消夏。但首相高市早苗並沒有休息。
Ms. Takaichi spent the holiday weekend rereading policy documents and dealing with a backlog of thousands of emails, according to a social media post on Tuesday. She wrote that she eked out a “full five hours of sleep for the first time in ages” — better than the “zero to three hours” she said she is accustomed to. And she said she spent her limited free time ironing undergarments, sewing fraying skirts and reinforcing loose jacket buttons.
她週二在社群媒體發文，稱此次三連休期間都在重讀政策文件，處理積壓的數千封電子郵件。她寫道，「久違地睡了五個小時」，比已經成為常態的「零至三小時睡眠」好多了。她還說，在難得的空閑時間裡，她熨燙了內衣、縫補了裙擺和鬆動的外套紐扣。
With her comments, Ms. Takaichi seemed eager to present herself as a down-to-earth leader who doesn’t stop working on behalf of Japan’s citizens. Instead, her post, which has been viewed more than 31 million times, has set off a national debate about work-life balance and the hazards of sleep deprivation.
高市早苗似乎想通過這些言論把自己塑造成一個腳踏實地、為日本國民不停工作的領導人。然而，她那篇瀏覽量已超過3100萬次的貼文卻在日本引發了一場關於工作與生活平衡以及睡眠不足危害的全國性討論。
Toru Kunishige, an opposition lawmaker, warned that a prolonged lack of sleep “impairs judgment — much like a state of intoxication — and could pose a risk to national crisis management.”
反對黨議員國重徹警告說，長期睡眠不足「會損害判斷力——就像醉酒一樣——可能給國家危機管理帶來風險」。
廣告
Hiroyuki Konishi, another critic of Ms. Takaichi in Parliament, called for her resignation, saying, “The prime minister’s actual job is to set policy directions and issue instructions, not to pore over every last detail of a document.”
高市早苗在國會的另一位批評者、議員小西洋之呼籲她辭職，稱「首相的真正職責是制定政策方向並下達指示，而不是逐字逐句地審閱文件的每一個細節」。
Yuichiro Tamaki, the leader of a populist conservative party, described Ms. Takaichi as hard-working but added that “resting is also a job.”
民粹保守派政黨領袖玉木雄一郎肯定了高市早苗的勤勉，但同時指出「休息也是工作」。
Ms. Takaichi’s office did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
對於置評請求，高市早苗的辦公室未立即作出回應。
Some commentators argued that Ms. Takaichi, the first woman to serve as prime minister in Japan, should be given more support in her position. While previous leaders often relied on their wives for help at home, Ms. Takaichi appeared to be tackling more domestic duties, according to Yoko Iwama, a professor at the National Graduate Institute for Policy Studies in Tokyo.
一些評論人士認為，作為日本首位女首相，高市早苗理應在工作中獲得更多支持。東京政策研究大學院大學的教授岩間陽子指出，以往的首相往往依賴妻子承擔家庭事務，而高市似乎不得不親自處理更多家務。
“Establishing a support system that allows a Japanese prime minister to focus entirely on their duties — without having to handle household chores personally — would contribute to women’s advancement in society,” Ms. Iwama wrote in the Nikkei, a prominent business newspaper.
岩間在日本知名財經報紙《日本經濟新聞》撰文稱：「建立一套支持機制，使日本首相能夠全身心投入公務而無需親自處理家務，將有助於推動女性在社會中的發展。」
Ms. Takaichi has been in overdrive recently to get her legislative priorities passed before Parliament breaks for the summer on Saturday. She is also grappling with continuing economic troubles — the yen has hit a 39-year low — and a recent dip in popularity, though her approval ratings remain relatively high.
近來，高市一直處於超負荷工作狀態，希望趕在國會於本週六進入夏季休會之前通過她優先推動的各項法案。與此同時，她還必須應對持續的經濟困境——日元匯率已跌至39年來的新低——以及近期支持率下滑的問題，儘管其整體民望仍維持在相對較高的水平。
廣告
In her post, Ms. Takaichi wrote at length about her recent workload. She said that since early July, “from early morning until late at night,” she had been holed up on weekends at her residence “poring over thick stacks of documents.”
在帖子中，高市早苗詳細描述了近來繁重的工作狀態。她說，進入7月以後的週末，「從清晨到深夜」，她都把自己關在首相官邸，「與堆積如山的文件搏鬥」。
She also shared personal details, saying she was “terrible at ironing and it takes forever.” But, she said, after her holiday ironing session, she “secured enough handkerchiefs and undergarments to last until the end of the session.”
她還分享了一些個人生活細節，稱自己「特別不擅長熨燙，花的時間總是很長」。不過，她也表示，經過假期的熨燙，「到國會會期結束前，手帕和內衣都夠用了」。
“I can dive back in and give it my all starting tomorrow!” she wrote.
「明天又能加油了！」她寫道。
Ms. Takaichi’s allies have come to her defense, praising her for offering a candid view of the pressures of balancing work with personal commitments.
高市早苗的盟友紛紛為她辯護，稱讚她坦誠地展現了在工作與私事之間尋求平衡的重重壓力。
Ayano Kunimitsu, a vice foreign minister, said that Ms. Takaichi, like other politicians, faced pressure to manage chores on her own.
外務副大臣國光文乃表示，高市早苗和其他政治人物一樣，也面臨著必須親自處理家務的壓力。
“Each person has their own circumstances and behind-the-scenes stories,” Ms. Kunimitsu wrote on X. “I would be grateful if you could understand.”
「每個人都有自己的情況和難處，」國光文乃在X平台上寫道。「如果大家能理解，我會很感激。」
廣告
Japan has a reputation for long working hours, with days at some corporations exceeding 12 hours. In business and government, workers face pressure to stay at the office until superiors have left for the day and to log excessive amounts of overtime, which is seen as a sign of loyalty and diligence.
日本一直以工作時間過長而著稱，一些企業的員工每天工作時間超過12小時。在企業和政府部門，員工普遍承受著一種壓力：必須等到上司離開後才能下班，並且經常加班很長時間，因為加班被視為忠誠和勤奮的表現。
There have been high-profile cases in Japan in recent years of karoshi, or “death from overwork.” The issue came into the spotlight in 2016, when Matsuri Takahashi, an employee of Dentsu, the advertising giant, died by suicide after clocking more than 100 hours of overtime per month. Her death prompted the government to impose a limit of 45 hours of overtime per month, which some experts say has helped ease the work culture, especially for young people.
近年來，日本發生過多起備受關注的「過勞死」案例。2016年，廣告巨頭電通的員工高橋茉莉在每月加班超過100小時後自殺身亡。她的死促使政府設定了每月45小時的加班上限。一些專家認為，這項改革有助於改善日本的職場文化，尤其是對年輕人而言。
Since entering politics in the 1990s, Ms. Takaichi, who aspires to be a transformative leader, has made her work ethic a central part of her identity. Upon taking office as prime minister last year, she promised to “work, work, work, work and work.” She has skillfully deployed social media to highlight her busy schedule, writing a lengthy post in January about dealing with geopolitical issues while moving into the prime minister’s residence during the holiday season.
自1990年代踏入政界以來，立志成為變革型領導人的高市早苗一直把勤奮工作作為自己政治形象的核心。去年出任首相時，她曾承諾要「工作、工作、工作、工作、再工作」。她也很會用社群媒體展示自己的繁忙日程——今年1月，她曾髮長文描述自己在假期期間一邊處理地緣政治問題、一邊搬進首相官邸的經歷。
This is not the first time her habits have come under scrutiny. In November, she drew criticism for summoning her aides at 3 a.m. She later explained that her fax machine at home had broken, and she needed her staff’s help getting to another office so she could prepare for a meeting.
這並非她的工作習慣第一次受到質疑。去年11月，她曾因凌晨3點召集助手而受到批評。事後她解釋稱，是因為家裡的傳真機壞了，她需要工作人員協助前往另一間辦公室，以便準備一場會議。

赫海威(Javier C. Hernández)是《紐約時報》東京分社社長，領導時報對日本及周邊地區的報導。在過去十年的大部分時間裡，他一直在亞洲進行報導，此前曾任駐華記者。

Hisako Ueno是常駐東京的記者/研究員，撰寫日本政治、商業、勞工、性別及文化相關議題。
