日本地車祭頻發傷亡事故引發安全憂慮

Two Die at Raucous Religious Festival in Japan. Will Anything Change?

HISAKO UENO, TIFFANY MAY • 2026-10-20
Every autumn, hundreds of Japanese people come together and dash down streets while pulling ornate wooden floats in the shape of shrines, a tradition known as danjiri.
每年秋天，數百名日本人聚到一起，拉著神龕形狀的華麗木製地車衝過街道，這一傳統被稱為「地車祭」。
But recent deaths and accidents are feeding anxiety about the ritual and bringing a somber note to the end of the mid-autumn festival season.
然而，近期的死亡與事故正加劇人們對這一儀式的憂慮，也給中秋祭典季的尾聲蒙上了一層陰鬱。
Osaka’s police department reported two deaths and several injuries in danjiri festivals in the cities of Izumiotsu and Izumisano on Oct. 10. In both instances, the floats, which weigh several tons, toppled over while turning corners, crushing participants who were standing on the side.
大阪警方通報，10月10日泉大津市和泉佐野市的地車祭中發生兩起死亡事件，另有多人受傷。兩起事故中，重達數噸的地車均在轉彎時傾覆，壓到路邊的參與者。
Because danjiri performances are rooted in religious tradition, a board of community leaders, rather than the police, decides the rules and safety protocols. The local police are investigating the circumstances of the deaths, but most organizers have stopped short of abolishing the sharp turns or calling for them to be taken more slowly.
地車表演根植於宗教傳統，因此規則與安全規程由社區領袖組成的委員會決定，而非警方。當地警方正在調查死亡事件的經過，但大多數組織者並未採取取消急轉彎或要求減速的措施。
廣告
“Heatstroke, falls and bone fractures are common occurrences here every year — though, ideally, no one should get hurt,” said Katsunori Fujita, 71, a chairman of the liaison council in Izumiotsu, who helps decide on the rules for the parades in his district.
「中暑、摔倒和骨折在這裡每年都很常見——儘管按理說，誰也不該受傷，」71歲的藤田克典（音）說。他是泉大津聯絡委員會主席，參與決定其所在地區遊行的規則。
Mr. Fujita witnessed the accident in Izumiotsu as he patrolled as a volunteer. He said he would continue enforcing safety precautions and instruct participants on where to escape during trial runs. But his district was unlikely to change the rules and the police were unlikely to intervene in the Shinto religious rite, he said.
藤田在作為志願者巡邏時目睹了泉大津的事故。他表示將繼續執行安全預防措施，並在試跑時指導參與者往哪裡躲避。但他也說，自己所在的地區不太可能修改規則，警方也不太可能介入這一神道教儀式。
The danjiri tradition dates back three centuries and began as a religious ritual during harvest season. Residents of Kishiwada, the southern city in Osaka prefecture where the tradition began, would carry a wooden float lit by hundreds of paper lanterns around the town before communal prayers at a Shinto shrine.
地車傳統可追溯至三百年前，最初是收穫季節的宗教儀式。在傳統發源地大阪府南部城市岸和田，居民會拉著由數百盞紙燈籠照亮的木製地車繞城巡行，隨後在神社共同祈禱。
Over time, danjiri spread to other cities across Japan. It also became more violent. Scuffles often broke out as floats jostled each other on narrow streets. The frequent tussles led to danjiri becoming known as the “fighting festival."
隨著時間推移，地車傳到了日本其他城市，也變得更加暴力。地車在狹窄街道上相互推擠時，衝突時常爆發。頻繁的扭打使地車祭得名「戰鬥祭」。
In the 1950s, community leaders in Kishiwada began to establish new rules to address the violence, including introducing a one-way system to avoid collisions between floats. It has since evolved into a parade with great displays of showmanship. Performers in traditional uniforms balance and dance on the roofs of the floats, which themselves are symbols of the vitality of each local community. Children are recruited to take part while in elementary school, practicing the sharp turns with hand-pulled wagons.
20世紀50年代，岸和田的社區領袖開始制定新規以應對暴力，包括引入單向通行制度以避免地車相撞。此後，它演變成一場極盡表演技巧的遊行。身穿傳統制式服裝的表演者在車頂保持平衡、起舞，而這些地車本身正是各地社區活力的象徵。兒童在小學時便被招募參加，用手拉車練習急轉彎。
During the parade, young and old residents run ahead of each float, pulling it with ropes. They execute sharp 90-degree turns, known as yarimawashi, at full speed, as performers riding on the float lean in one direction to prevent it from tipping over.
遊行期間，老少居民跑在每輛地車前面，用繩子牽引。他們全速完成90度急轉彎，稱為「yarimawashi」，車上的表演者則向一側傾斜，以防地車翻倒。
But safety questions have been raised year after year. The fatalities earlier this month were not the first.
然而，安全問題年復一年被提出。本月的死亡事件並非首次。
廣告
In 2022 and 2023, four people died and 32 were injured in four incidences. One danjiri participant died in Maniwa, a city in Okayama, leading to a one-year hiatus of the festival before it resumed in 2025. In 2023, police in Tondabayashi, a city in Osaka, accused three danjiri festival representatives of negligence after one death and three injuries, but prosecutors did not indict them.
2022年和2023年，四起事件造成四人死亡、32人受傷。岡山縣真庭市一名地車參與者死亡，祭典因此暫停一年，直至2025年恢復。2023年，大阪富田林市警方在一人死亡、三人受傷後，指控三名地車祭代表存在過失，但檢方未予起訴。
Other festivals involving heavy floats have suffered fatalities. On Saturday, the deputy mayor of Saijo on Shikoku Island was killed while raising on his shoulders a three-ton float called a taikodai, which serves as a moving stage for drummers.
其他涉及重型地車的祭典也出現過死亡事件。週六，四國島西條市副市長在將一輛名為「太鼓台」的三噸重地車扛上肩時身亡，該地車用作鼓手的移動舞台。
Hisao Arimoto, an associate professor who studies the danjiri festival at Konan Women’s University in Kobe, said that there had been calls for bans or changes every year, but few changes had followed. “It would be difficult to make drastic changes because it is an event that involves so many people,” he said by telephone.
神戶甲南女子大學研究地車祭的副教授有本尚央表示，每年都有人呼籲禁止或改革，但實際改變寥寥。「這是一個牽涉如此多人的活動，很難做齣劇烈改變，」他在電話中說。
Hideaki Karaki, a professor emeritus of the University of Tokyo, said that it would be impossible to eliminate risks altogether. “The very elements that create danger — its speed, its weight and the intense energy of the crowd — are also the core of what makes the festival so compelling,” he wrote in Wedge Online, a news and business magazine.
東京大學榮休教授唐木英明表示，完全消除風險是不可能的。「製造危險的那些要素——速度、重量和人群的強烈能量——恰恰也是這個祭典如此引人入勝的核心，」他在新聞商業雜誌《Wedge Online》上寫道。
But he added that the accidents would continue if organizers claim that they had taken enough precautions without conducting proper risk assessments. “To ensure that a 300-year-old tradition can endure for another 300 years, what is required is the courage to look the dangers squarely in the eye,” he wrote.
但他補充說，如果組織者聲稱已採取足夠預防措施，卻不進行恰當的風險評估，事故就會繼續發生。「要讓一個三百年的傳統再延續三百年，需要的是正視危險的勇氣，」他寫道。
