憤怒的選民開啟川普時代終結的序幕

Angry Voters Usher in Beginning of the End to the Trump Era

PETER BAKER • 2026-11-04
President Trump implored voters over the course of the midterm campaign to “pretend I’m on the ballot.” He said it not once, not twice, but 47 times this fall.
在整個期中選舉競選期間，川普總統懇求選民「就當我在選票上」。這個秋天，這樣的話他說了不是一次兩次，而是47次。
And voters did — just not the way he hoped.
選民確實照做了，只是並非以他希望的方式。
The Democratic wave in the congressional elections on Tuesday amounted to a stinging rebuke of a deeply unpopular president after more than a decade in which he has dominated the national stage. More than any of his predecessors, Mr. Trump deliberately made the midterm contest a referendum on himself, only to pay the price.
週二國會選舉中的民主黨浪潮是對一位不受歡迎的總統的尖銳否定。此前的十多年裡，他主導了國家政治舞台。川普比任何一位前任都更刻意地將期中選舉變成對他本人的公投，結果卻付出了代價。
The electoral backlash extended even into deep red states and indicated that Mr. Trump has lost his hold over the coalition that lifted him to the White House twice. Frustrated by high gas prices, an intractable war with Iran and allegations of flagrant profiteering by Mr. Trump and members of his circle, voters who previously took a chance on an outsider promising change either rejected his party or stayed home.
選舉反彈甚至延伸到了深紅州，表明川普已失去對那個兩次將他送入白宮的聯盟的掌控。高油價、與伊朗的棘手戰爭以及對川普及其圈子成員公然牟取暴利的指控令選民不滿，這些選民此前冒險支持一位承諾變革的局外人，如今要麼拒絕了他的政黨，要麼選擇不投票。
廣告
As of early Wednesday morning, the Democratic margin of victory in the overall national vote for House seats was expected to be about 13 percentage points, the largest that either party has lodged in a congressional election since at least 1976, according to estimates by The New York Times. It will dwarf even the margins that the Republicans won in their two big landslide victories of modern times when they defeated congressional Democrats when Bill Clinton held the White House in 1994 and Barack Obama was president in 2010.
據《紐約時報》估算，截至週三凌晨，民主黨在全國眾議院席位總投票中的獲勝優勢預計約為13個百分點，這是至少自1976年以來兩黨中任何一黨在國會選舉中取得的最大優勢。這甚至超過了共和黨在現代兩次壓倒性勝利中的優勢，1994年比爾·柯林頓任內和2010年巴拉克·歐巴馬任內，共和黨兩次擊敗了國會民主黨人。
Thanks to redistricting, that margin may not translate into as many seats as the winners gained in those watershed years. Gerrymandered House districts leave far fewer seats available for poaching by the other party than in the past. But the overall margin of Democratic victory indicates that while the president has two more years left in office and can still wield power to great effect, Americans are signaling the beginning of the end of the Trump era.
由於重新劃分選區，這一優勢可能不會轉化為像那些分水嶺年份獲勝者所獲得的那麼多席位。不公正劃分的眾議院選區留給另一黨爭奪的席位遠少於過去。但民主黨的勝選總體優勢表明，儘管總統任期還剩兩年、仍能運用巨大的權力，美國人正在發出川普時代開始終結的信號。
“It’s basically a repudiation of Trump and the Republicans and what they’ve done for the last two years,” said Douglas B. Sosnik, who was White House political director for Mr. Clinton and closely tracked the races this fall. “I do think starting tomorrow, we’re in this new phase and it’s a transition phase and it will last two years. And it will be much more volatile than the last two years.”
「這基本上是對川普、共和黨人以及他們過去兩年所作所為的否定，」密切關注今秋選情的前柯林頓白宮政治主任道格拉斯·B·索斯尼克說。「我確實認為，從明天開始，我們將進入一個新階段，一個過渡階段，持續兩年。而且它將比過去兩年更加動盪。」
Mr. Trump quickly tried to save face by insisting that the rout by Democrats was not really a rejection of him since he was not actually on the ballot — never mind that he told voters repeatedly to vote as if he were.
川普迅速試圖挽回面子，堅稱共和黨的潰敗並非真正對他的否定，因為他實際上不在選票上——全然不顧他曾反覆告訴選民要像他在選票上那樣投票。
“I wasn’t on the ticket, and because I wasn’t on the ticket, we couldn’t get enough people out to vote,” he told The Times in an interview Tuesday night.
「我不在選票上，因為我不在選票上，我們無法讓足夠多的人出來投票，」他在週二晚間的一次採訪中告訴《紐約時報》。
Even many Republicans would roll their eyes at that assertion, as this was a campaign that revolved so demonstrably around Mr. Trump.
甚至許多共和黨人也會對這種說法翻白眼，因為這是一場如此明顯地圍繞川普展開的競選。
廣告
It was Mr. Trump who decided to start a war with Iran that jolted energy markets and sent prices at the pump soaring. It was Mr. Trump who chose the candidates, the issues, the strategy and the message.
是川普決定發動一場攪動能源市場、推高油價的伊朗戰爭。是川普選定了候選人、議題、策略和信息。
It was Mr. Trump who demanded complete loyalty from a party whose candidates were nervous about distancing themselves from him. And it was Mr. Trump who has shrugged off questions about the ethics of his moneymaking in office, helping propel voter concerns about corruption.
是川普要求一個其候選人對於與他保持距離感到緊張的政黨給予完全忠誠。也是川普對有關他在任內牟利的道德問題不屑一顧，助推了選民對腐敗的擔憂。
The defeat of two Senate Republican candidates that he personally selected over potentially more electable contenders in deep red states — Ken Paxton in Texas and Darline Graham in South Carolina — demonstrated the consequences of his interventions. His push to throw out the normal process and redraw House districts in the middle of the decade to try to stack the deck for Republicans backfired.
在深紅州，兩名由他親自挑選的共和黨參議員候選人——得克薩斯州的肯·帕克斯頓與南卡羅來納州的達琳·格雷厄姆——雙雙落敗，說明了他干預的後果，這兩個州原本有勝算更高的競爭者。他推動拋棄正常程序、在十年週期的中途重新劃分眾議院選區以試圖為共和黨人製造不公平優勢，結果適得其反。
Many voters told pollsters and interviewers that they felt hoodwinked by Mr. Trump, believing that he was going to bring down prices and not start new wars. They expected him to close the border, but bristled at what they saw as heavy-handed arrests of people living in the country for years. They rebelled against the tariffs that increased costs for consumers.
許多選民告訴民意調查員和採訪者，他們感到被川普愚弄了：他們本以為他會降低物價，而不是發動新戰爭。他們期望他關閉邊境，卻對他所看到的、對居住在該國多年的人實施粗暴逮捕感到憤怒。他們反對那些增加了消費者成本的關稅。
The way Mr. Trump spent his last day before the election proved to be a metaphor for how out of sync his political instincts were with the season. Instead of emphasizing plans to address the cost of living worries weighing on many voters, he delivered his final pre-election address at the Reflecting Pool, talking about algae and pipes. It was a vivid reminder of his obsession with pet construction projects in Washington, despite the warning signs of how it rankled voters — as if Richard M. Nixon had made his final pre-election speech in front of the Watergate complex.
川普在選舉前最後一天的日程顯示了他的政治本能與當前情勢何其脫節。他沒有強調解決許多選民所感受到的生活成本擔憂的計劃，而是在倒影池發表選前最後的講話，談論藻類和管道。這生動地提醒人們，儘管有警告信號表明這如何激怒選民，他卻痴迷於自己心愛的華盛頓建設項目——就像理查德·M·尼克森在水門綜合大樓前發表了他最後的選前演講一樣。
Even if Trumpism may be receding, Mr. Trump has the power of the presidency for two more years and will do everything he can to remain a dominant voice for as long as he can. His allies warned against assuming that he is a spent force.
即使川普主義可能正在消退，川普仍掌握兩年的總統權力，並將盡其所能，盡可能長久地保持主導性聲音。他的盟友警告說，不要以為他已經是過眼雲煙。
廣告
“Trump’s critics have written his political obituary before and been wrong every time,” said David McIntosh, president of the Club for Growth, a group that promotes conservative economic policies.
「川普的批評者以前寫過他的政治訃告，但每次都錯了，」增長俱樂部(Club for Growth)主席戴維·麥金托什說。該組織倡導保守經濟政策。
“The fix is Republicans getting back to our principles,” he added. “That’s how we win in 2028.”
「解決辦法是共和黨人回歸我們的原則，」他補充說。「這就是我們在2028年的取勝之法。」
There is, of course, a natural tendency to draw great lessons from a big midterm wave, to assume that it represents a fundamental shift in the American electorate. It certainly may. But history has seen plenty of examples when it did not last long. And it remains to be seen whether Tuesday’s results signal a broader ideological shift among voters.
當然，人們自然而然地有種傾向，想從一場期中選舉大浪潮中汲取重大教訓，假定它代表了美國選民的根本性轉變。這當然有可能。但歷史上也有很多例子表明這種轉變並未持續多久。週二的結果是否標誌著選民中更廣泛的意識型態轉變仍有待觀察。
The post-Watergate wave of 1974 was thought to presage a long winter in the political wilderness for Republicans, but Ronald Reagan brought the party back to the White House just six years later. The 1994 and 2010 Republican sweeps were expected to presage the end of the Clinton and Obama presidencies, but both came back to win re-election handily two years later.
1974年水門事件後的浪潮被認為預示著共和黨在政治荒野中的漫長寒冬，但羅納德·雷根僅六年後就帶領該黨重返白宮。1994年和2010年的共和黨橫掃被認為預示著柯林頓和歐巴馬總統任期的終結，但兩人在兩年後都輕鬆贏得連任。
Lynn Vavrek, a professor of American politics and public policy at the University of California, Los Angeles, said the most important factor in the election was sky-high gas prices so it may not follow that the country’s political character has profoundly changed.
加州大學洛杉磯分校美國政治與公共政策教授林恩·瓦夫雷克說，選舉中最重要的因素是極高的汽油價格，因此可能並不意味著美國政治特性已經發生深刻變化。
“It doesn’t mean the MAGA coalition is crumbling,” she said. “Does it mean the shine has come off Donald Trump a little bit? Yes. Are people less enamored of him? I think the answer to that is yes.” But she said it does not necessarily mean that Republicans are going to abandon the “identity-inflected policies” on issues like immigration and diversity that have animated the party under Mr. Trump.
「這並不意味著MAGA聯盟正在瓦解，」她說。「唐納德·川普的光環是否有所褪去？是的。人們是否不那麼迷戀他了？我認為答案是肯定的。」但她表示，這並不一定意味著共和黨將放棄在移民和多元化等問題上「帶有身份色彩的政策」，這些政策在川普任內一直在調動共和黨人的情緒。
This is, after all, a fundamentally altered party, one that Mr. Trump forged in his own image, empowering those who defer to him and purging those who exhibit independence. “We don’t know how the president’s going to react, but thus far we’ve only seen members of his party acquiesce to him,” said Christina M. Greer, a political science professor at Fordham University.
畢竟，這是一個從根本上改變了的政黨，一個川普按照自己的形象鍛造的政黨：賦予那些順從他的人權力，清除那些表現出獨立性的人。「我們不知道總統會如何反應，但到目前為止，我們只看到他黨內的成員默許他，」福特漢姆大學政治學教授克里斯蒂娜·M·格里爾說。
Joe Grogan, who served as Mr. Trump’s domestic policy adviser in his first term, said that regardless of the election results, the president has changed the political landscape by advancing issues that were not adequately addressed enough before, like pushing for better trade deals and securing greater military spending by NATO allies. The party, he said, is not moving back to what it once was.
喬·格羅根曾在川普第一任期擔任其國內政策顧問，他說無論選舉結果如何，總統都通過推進此前未得到充分解決的問題改變了政治格局，比如推動更好的貿易協議和確保北約盟國增加軍費開支。他說，共和黨不會回到過去的樣子。
“We’re still in Trump’s party, regardless of what happens tonight,” Mr. Grogan, now at Covington & Burling, said before the polls closed. “People who expect Trumpism to get repudiated I think are mistaken. There will be some debates that I think will be healthy for the party. But I just don’t see there being a huge wellspring for the party to go back to the one that George W. Bush led for two successful elections.”
「無論今晚發生什麼，我們仍然在川普的政黨裡，」現就職於科文頓·柏靈律師事務所的格羅根在投票結束前說。「那些期望川普主義被否定的人，我認為是錯的。將會有一些辯論，我認為這對黨是健康的。但我始終沒看到有巨大的動力在讓黨回到過去，就是在喬治·W·布希領導下兩次成功勝選的那個共和黨。」
廣告
At the same time, Democrats will debate how to interpret their victory. Their main identity this year was simply being against Mr. Trump, which was more than enough. But progressives will say that it was energy on the left that powered the success, while moderates will say it was the appeal to disaffected Republicans in states like Texas and Kansas that was crucial.
與此同時，民主黨人將就如何解讀他們的勝利展開辯論。他們今年的主要身份僅僅是反對川普，而這已經足夠了。但進步派會說，是左翼的能量推動了成功；溫和派則會說，對得克薩斯州和堪薩斯州等州心懷不滿的共和黨人的吸引力才是關鍵。
“The biggest story is non-MAGA Republicans have broken with the Republican Party,” said Rahm Emanuel, a former Democratic Chicago mayor and ambassador exploring a run for president. “The question is do we want to make this election transactional or transformational? What we do in 2027 will determine that.”
「最重要的是非MAGA共和黨人已經與共和黨決裂，」拉姆·伊曼紐爾說。這位前民主黨芝加哥市長和大使正在考慮競選總統。「問題是，我們想讓這次選舉是利益交換性的還是變革性的？我們在2027年的所作所為將決定這一點。」
James Blanchard, who was first elected to Congress in the post-Watergate Democratic sweep of 1974 and later served as governor of Michigan, said this year’s election felt similar to that historic year. But he said that does not mean that American politics will return to the way they were before Mr. Trump. “We can never go back, unfortunately,” he said. “We’re going to have to go forward.”
詹姆斯·布蘭乍得在1974年水門事件後民主黨浪潮中首次當選國會議員，後來擔任密西根州州長。他說今年的選舉感覺與那個歷史性年份相似。但他表示，這並不意味著美國政治將回到川普之前的狀態。「不幸的是，我們永遠無法回去，」他說。「我們將不得不向前走。」
“It’s the end of Trump,” he added, “even though he’s still going to be there."
「這是川普的終結，」他補充說，「儘管他仍將在那裡。」

Peter Baker是《紐約時報》首席白宮記者。川普是他報導的第六任美國總統，有時他也撰寫將總統和美國政府置於更宏觀的背景和歷史框架下的分析性文章。
