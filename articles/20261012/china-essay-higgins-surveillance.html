國安機器「草木皆兵」

Where Every Tree Hides ‘an Enemy’: How I Watched China’s Paranoia Grow

ANDREW HIGGINS • 2026-10-12
It was an enjoyable day of temple and factory visits organized by China Daily, the Communist Party’s English-language mouthpiece, but it ended on an uncomfortable note.
那是中共官方英文媒體《中國日報》組織的一次令人愉快的寺廟與工廠探訪之旅，結尾卻令人頗感不適。
The group I was traveling with in the northern province of Shanxi — mostly foreign influencers hungry for followers and Chinese freebies — was told to line up behind a long banner and be filmed chanting: “Amazing China, Amazing Shanxi.”
與我一同前往北方省份山西的這群人主要是渴望粉絲和免費福利的外國網紅，他們被要求在一條長橫幅後排開，並對著鏡頭合唱：「Amazing China, Amazing Shanxi」（大美中國，大美山西）。
I declined to join in, scowling in silence. But, back in my hotel room in the provincial capital of Taiyuan, I felt I had perhaps been too churlish: Getting a bunch of foreigners to chant on cue for the camera about China’s wonders was a transparent but harmless propaganda stunt.
我拒絕配合，面帶不悅地冷眼旁觀。不過，回到省會太原的酒店房間後，我又覺得自己的態度或許過於生硬了：讓一群外國人對著鏡頭按指令合唱對中國奇蹟的讚歌，不過是個顯而易見卻也無害的宣傳把戲而已。
And, to be honest, the 11th century temples we visited — and also the new highway that took us to them — were pretty amazing.
況且說實話，我們參觀的11世紀古剎——以及把我們送到那裡的新建高速公路——確實相當驚人。

Then I received a message on WeChat, a Chinese messaging app, from an old friend in Beijing, a lawyer whose earnest decency and movie star looks always reminded me of the “good party cadre” characters in propaganda films. I had not seen him for more than 30 years and was planning to visit the following day.
隨後，我的微信收到了一條來自北京一位老朋友的信息。他是一名律師，為人真誠得體，長相如明星般英俊，總讓我想起宣傳電影裡那些「優秀黨員幹部」形象。我已有30多年沒見過他，本計劃第二天去拜訪他。
Sadly, his message said, he would not be able to keep our appointment because state security officers, somehow apprised of our plans, had turned up at his door and ordered him to cancel.
遺憾的是，他在信息中說自己無法赴約了，因為國家安全官員不知通過什麼管道獲悉了我們的行程，直接找上了他的家門，命令他取消見面的計劃。
China suddenly appeared not so much amazing as alarming.
一瞬間，中國帶給我的不再是驚艷，而是驚恐。
After decades away from the country where I studied classical Chinese in 1980-81 and then worked as a journalist from 1987 until 1991, I returned in September last year for a six-month assignment. I marveled at how much had changed, sharing the amazement of first-time visitors awe-struck by China’s high-speed trains, dazzling modern cities and gung-ho embrace of the future.
我曾於1980至1981年間在中國學習古漢語，後於1987年至1991年擔任駐華記者。在離開這個國家數十年後，我於去年9月返回中國執行為期六個月的報導任務。我驚嘆於這裡的巨變，和那些初次踏足這片土地的遊客一樣，我被中國的高鐵網路、耀眼的現代都市以及對未來的熱切擁抱所震撼。
My old friend’s message broke the reverie:
然而，我這位老友發來的消息打碎了這份美好：
“Every bush and tree seems like an enemy soldier,” he warned, using a Chinese idiom to describe the paranoia of a security apparatus that has also changed dramatically. It has grown bigger, more intrusive and, thanks to high-tech marvels like WeChat and omnipresent surveillance cameras, much more capable of monitoring just about everyone.
「草木皆兵，」他用這句中國成語提醒我，道出了如今安全機器的極度疑神疑鬼——這套機構自身同樣經歷了劇烈變化。它現在規模更大、侵入性更強；藉助微信及無處不在的監控攝像頭等高科技手段，它具備了對幾乎所有人實施全天候監控的能力。
China’s economic transformation over the last four decades has been extraordinary, and it infuriates Chinese officials that Western media outlets display what they see as “anti-China bias.” That is shorthand for reporting that dwells on anxiety about the economy, still growing but at a slower pace, and anything else that deviates from China’s undeniable achievements and charms.
過去40年來，中國的經濟轉型無疑是卓越的，而西方媒體展現出的所謂「反華偏見「令中國官員極為惱火。所謂「反華偏見「，指的是那些著重渲染中國經濟焦慮的報導—​​—儘管中國經濟仍在增長，但增速放緩——以及任何與中國無可否認的成就和魅力相悖的內容。
廣告
Throughout my stay, I struggled to reconcile the reality of a country that has never been richer and seemingly full of promise with a parallel reality of brittle insecurity, in which every bush and tree hides a potential enemy.
在華停留期間，我一直在試圖調和這種撕裂感：一方面，這個國家從未如此富裕，似乎充滿希望；另一方面，它又存在著一種脆弱的安全感缺失，彷彿每一株草木背後都潛伏著未知的敵人。
Based in Shanghai, I often went on weekends with my wife to a park in the city’s northern Hongkou district, where groups of older Chinese gather to sing songs from their youth. Their favorite numbers are patriotic anthems but the singers, who often included a Uyghur man dressed and made up to look like Charlie Chaplin, were there of their own accord.
在上海期間，我和妻子經常在週末前往該市北部的虹口公園。那裡聚集著許多中老年人，他們自發湊在一起演唱年輕時代的歌曲。他們最愛唱的是愛國歌曲——經常能看到其中有一位裝扮成查理·卓別林的維吾爾族男子——但完全是出於自願參與其中。
Their boisterous camaraderie and national pride seemed entirely genuine.
他們歡聚的熱烈氛圍與國家自豪感看起來完全出自真心。
An official sign nearby, however, clouded the carefree mood: “Opposing Traitors and Guarding Against Spies Starts With Us,” it warned, with a phone number to “report espionage activity.”
然而，附近掛著的一塊官方告示牌卻為這種無憂無慮的氛圍蒙上了陰影，牌子上警告道：「反間防諜，從我做起，」並附上了「舉報間諜行為」的電話號碼。
The hunt for spies has escalated sharply since Xi Jinping took power in 2012 and began throttling the flow of information in and out of China, building up the Great Firewall to prevent Chinese from viewing many foreign websites.
自習近平於2012年執政並開始收緊跨境信息流動、修築防火長城以阻斷國內民眾訪問眾多外國網站以來，針對間諜行為的打擊急劇升級。
There are seemingly no limits on what the state can see and hear, and ordinary Chinese have adapted accordingly — most noticeably with regard to the leader himself.
國家的監控範圍似乎遼闊無邊，普通民眾也相應地作出了調適——最明顯的莫過於對待最高領導人本人的態度。
Mr. Xi is everywhere, on television, on billboards and on the front pages of newspapers and websites. But, throughout my stay in China, not one of the hundreds of Chinese I spoke to, mostly in private, ever uttered his name out loud. Even foreigners often avoided it, pointing at the ceiling when they wanted to refer to China’s president.
習近平的身影無處不在，電視上、廣告牌上、報紙和網站的頭版上都有他。然而，在我停留中國期間私下接觸的數百名中國人中，沒有一個人敢大聲念出他的名字。甚至連外國人也往往避開提及，當想指代中國國家主席時，他們只是用手指指天花板。
The Chinese people whom I met on my travels invariably skirted politics, except when I encountered a zealous patriot during a visit to Dazhai, a former Maoist model commune in a remote mountain area of Shanxi. “Which is better, China or America? Tell the truth!” he shouted, in between praise of China’s leadership and ridicule of the United States and Europe.
我在行程中接觸到的中國人無一例外地避談政治，只有一次例外：在訪問山西偏遠山區一處毛澤東時代示範公社大寨時，我遇到了一位狂熱的愛國者。「到底是中國好還是美國好？說實話！」他在讚美中國領導層、嘲弄美歐之餘，對我大聲嚷嚷道。
Nobody voiced open political dissent but many grumbled about the economy, particularly in places like Hainan Island where a runaway property boom has ground to a halt. A retiree from Shandong who had spent all his savings and borrowed money to buy a Hainan apartment that later lost more than a third of its value told me his much younger wife, his second, was threatening to leave him because he had fallen deeply into debt.
沒有人公開發表政治異見，但對經濟的不滿卻比比皆是，尤其是在海南島這樣狂熱的房地產熱潮猝然停滯的地方。一位來自山東的退休老人傾盡畢生積蓄並舉債在海南買了一套房子，隨後該房產貶值了三分之一以上。他告訴我，他那年輕得多的第二任妻子正以他背負重債為由威脅要與他離婚。
About the closest anyone came to openly challenging the authorities was in Shanghai when I met an out-of-town party member and his daughter outside a museum celebrating early revolutionaries.
在上海一座紀念早期革命者的博物館門外，我遇到了一位來自外地的黨員和他的女兒。當時發生的大概是我遇到的最接近公開挑戰當局的情況了。
Pushed and insulted by police officers when he started to video the entrance, which they said was forbidden, he frothed with anger, cursing them as “hooligans in uniform.” When I asked him what was wrong, he replied, using a Mao-era mantra, that the party and its servants “should serve the people but often forget that.”
當這位黨員試圖拍攝博物館入口時，警察稱該行為是被禁止的，並對他進行了推搡和斥責。他氣得口吐飛沫，咒罵警察是「穿制服的流氓」。當我問他發生了什麼時，他借用毛澤東時代的口號回答道，黨和官員「本該為人民服務，但他們往往忘了這點」。

His daughter, aware of the surveillance cameras planted on the narrow street, told him to shut up.
他的女兒注意到了狹窄街道上密布的監控攝像頭，急忙喝止他不要再說了。
On the 150-yard walk from my Shanghai apartment in a gleaming new tower overlooking Suzhou Creek to the nearest subway, I counted more than 30 cameras trained on pedestrians. There is also a security check at the station, where guards, unfailingly polite, put all passengers’ bags through a scanner. Behind them was a cabinet stuffed with black batons, helmets and other riot control gear. Next to an ad for high-end dog food, a propaganda poster demanded vigilance against unspecified threats.
從我在蘇州河畔閃亮的新建高樓裡的公寓步行到最近的地鐵站，短短一百多米的距離內，我數出了30多個正對著行人的攝像頭。地鐵站內還設有安檢，態度極其禮貌的安檢員要求所有乘客將包袋放進掃描儀。在他們身後，擺放著一個塞滿黑色警棍、頭盔和其他防暴裝備的器材櫃。一則高端狗糧廣告的旁邊貼著一張宣傳海報，要求警惕未指明的安全威脅。
Vigilance against supposed spies in China has cursed foreign journalists looking for information, no matter how anodyne, for many decades.
對所謂間諜的高度警惕幾十年來一直像咒語一樣困擾著試圖獲取信息的外國記者，哪怕這些信息極其尋常。
When I worked there as a journalist in the 1980s, communism was crumbling next door in the Soviet Union and restlessness over corruption and one-party rule led to huge protests in Tiananmen Square. The party’s account of events after a bloody crackdown routinely accused foreign journalists of spreading “bourgeois liberalism” and stoking “counterrevolutionary turmoil.” Two American reporters were expelled for “obtaining state secrets through illegal means.”
1980年代我擔任駐華記者時，鄰國蘇聯的共產主義體制正走向崩潰，對腐敗和一黨統治的不滿引發了天安門廣場的大規模抗議。在流血鎮壓發生後，官方對事件的報導例行公事地指責外國記者傳播「資產階級自由化」並煽動「反革命暴亂」。兩名美國記者因「通過非法手段獲取國家祕密」而被驅逐出境。
John Burns, the veteran New York Times foreign correspondent, who died in March, was arrested in China in 1986 and charged with espionage after embarking on long motorcycle trip through the hinterland. The charges were later dropped and he was deported.
今年3月去世的《紐約時報》資深戰地記者約翰·伯恩斯曾於1986年在騎行穿越中國腹地時被捕，並被指控犯有間諜罪。這些指控後來被撤銷，他隨後被驅逐出境。
I was expelled from China in 1991 after obtaining Communist Party internal documents marked top secret and detailing a crackdown on groups accused of fomenting “splittist” sentiment — party code for cultural self-awareness — among ethnic Mongolians in the Chinese region of Inner Mongolia.
我本人則因獲取了標記為絕密的中共內部文件於1991年被驅逐出中國。這份文件詳細記錄了針對被控在內蒙古自治區蒙古族人中煽動「分裂主義」情緒——官方對文化自我意識的隱語——的團體進行的鎮壓。
Back then, reporters were followed by furtive men who carried satchels fitted with cameras. The watchers are still there, now equipped with cellphone cameras and smart glasses.
在那個時代，跟蹤記者的是一些神秘人員，手上拿著內藏相機的挎包。如今，監視者依然存在，只是換上了智慧型手機攝像頭和智能眼鏡。
In Dazhai, the former Maoist commune, a photographer and I were followed throughout on foot and by cars crammed with blank-faced men. In Hainan, a man followed us from the street to a sunny beach where we had decided to take a break. Pretending to talk into his cellphone, he kept its camera turned in our direction as we moved around. But the bulk of the surveillance these days is done by A.I.-powered systems that alert security agencies to communication involving people considered potential troublemakers — like foreign journalists.
在那座毛澤東時代的公社大寨，我和一名攝影師全程被步行和乘車的一些面無表情的男子跟蹤。在海南，一名男子從街頭一直跟蹤我們到陽光明媚的沙灘。在我們活動時，他假裝在打電話，卻始終將攝像頭對準我們的方向。不過如今，絕大部分監控工作已交由人工智慧驅動的系統完成，該系統會自動對涉及外國記者等潛在「麻煩製造者」的通訊發出預警。
廣告
WeChat and other apps available in China promise privacy, and most Chinese value the convenience they offer. But the apps are required by cybersecurity and other laws to share user data with the Chinese authorities. This facilitates snooping that, at least to many foreign journalists, smacks of digital dystopia.
微信及在中國使用的其他應用軟體承諾保護隱私，大多數中國人也十分青睞其帶來的便利。然而，網路安全法等法律規定，這些應用必須與中國當局共享用戶數據。這使得針對個人的監控變得極為便利，在許多外國記者眼中，這種體驗充滿了數字反烏托邦色彩。
Quickly realizing that any communication over WeChat risked putting old friends in the cross hairs of the security apparatus, I avoided contacting them. But one friend — the daughter of a former senior official, who was on a business trip back to China after years abroad — got in touch.
在迅速意識到通過微信進行的任何聯絡都可能將老朋友置於安全機構的監視之下後，我選擇避免與他們聯繫。但有一位朋友主動與我取得了聯繫，她是一名前高級官員的女兒，在海外生活多年後因公回國。
When we met for lunch in Shanghai, we reminisced about the 1980s and friends we had in common, nearly all of whom now live outside China. She raved about “Blossoms Shanghai,” a lush, 30-episode television series suffused with nostalgia for China’s roaring ’90s, a period of explosive economic growth and relative openness as the Communist Party relaxed the suffocating grip it imposed after the 1989 Tiananmen massacre.
當我們在上海共進午餐時，我們敘舊回憶起了1980年代和共同的朋友，他們中幾乎所有人如今都生活在海外。她對電視劇《繁花》讚不絕口。這部30集的精緻劇集充滿了對中國蓬勃發展的90年代的懷舊情緒，那是一個經濟飛速增長、社會相對開放的時期，當時中共放鬆了在1989年天安門事件後實施的嚴酷管制。
We entirely avoided the political gossip that used to figure in our conversations. The food was excellent.
我們完全避開了過去談話中常出現的政治小道消息。午餐的菜肴非常美味。
When I first went to China as a student, I lived in Jinan, an eastern center for state-owned heavy industry that reeked of coal. The city was still recovering from Mao’s Cultural Revolution, a decade of political turmoil that ended in 1976.
當我以學生身份第一次來到中國時，住在東部重工業中心濟南，那是一座空氣中瀰漫著煤煙味的城市。當時，這座城市仍處於從1976年結束的十年政治動盪——毛澤東發起的「文化大革命」中復甦的階段。
The economy was a wreck. Rice, cloth and even matches were in such short supply they could be bought only with ration coupons. The canteen at Shandong University, where I studied, served only greasy gruel with bits of mystery meat and steamed buns.
當時的經濟一片凋敝。大米、布匹甚至火柴都極度短缺，只有憑票才能購買。我就讀的山東大學食堂只供應油乎乎的粥和饅頭，稀飯裡漂著幾塊不知是什麼的肉。
Time magazine had just named Deng Xiaoping, the supreme leader at the time, its “man of the year,” hailing his “visions of a new China.”
《時代》週刊剛剛將當時的最高領導人鄧小平評選為「年度風雲人物」，盛讚他對「一個新中國的構想」。
Deng was clear that the party would remain in control, but his path for China’s future was deliberately tentative. It was called “crossing the river by feeling the stones,” an admission of uncertainty that filled my university with excited debate about how to reach the ultimate goal — a China “rich and strong.”
鄧小平明確表示黨將牢牢掌握控制權，但他為中國未來設計的道路刻意保持著試探性。這被稱為「摸著石頭過河」，這種對不確定性的坦承，讓我的大學校園裡充滿了關於如何實現「富強」中國這一終極目標的熱烈討論。

Mr. Xi has embraced the same goal but has replaced Deng’s invitation to “feel for stones” with dogmatic certainty. The country is exhorted to show “unswerving loyalty” to Xi Jinping Thought, his blueprint for a “new era” with no room for debate.
習近平繼承了這一終極目標，卻用教條式的確定性取代了鄧小平「摸著石頭過河」的倡議。全國上下被要求對習近平思想——他為「新時代」制定的藍圖——表現出「堅定不移的忠誠「，不容置疑。
The campus of Shandong University, open in my student days to the public, is now, like other college grounds in China, sealed off by security guards and studded with surveillance cameras.
在我求學時代，山東大學校園是向社會開放的，如今這裡跟中國的其他高校一樣，校園被保安嚴密把守，四處都是監控攝像頭。
One of the few buildings near the campus still standing from when I was a student is a huge, German-built Roman Catholic church, which had been converted into a storeroom and workshop. Lavishly renovated since, the church is now again used for religious services, though these are restricted to Christians willing to register and show their passports or ID cards.
校園附近有幾座從我學生時代保留至今的建築，其中包括一座由德國人建造的宏偉天主教堂，當年曾被改作倉庫和廠房。後來，這座教堂經過大規模翻修，如今重新用於宗教活動，但只有願意登記並出示護照或身份證的基督徒才能參加。
When I first traveled by train to Jinan from Beijing, the journey took all night. It now takes under two hours. Jinan’s main railway station, a grand Art Nouveau building in the center of town built by Germans at the start of the 20th century, has been torn down, replaced by a hyper-modern structure on the outskirts.
我第一次從北京坐火車去濟南時，花了整整一夜。現在只需要不到兩小時。濟南曾經的主要火車站是20世紀初德國人在市中心建造的一座宏偉的新藝術風格建築，現已被拆除，取而代之的是位於郊區的一座超現代建築。
I ran into a former classmate whom I remembered as having been something of a maverick. Now a businessman in Shanghai selling private planes, he reminisced about a time when everything was still open to discussion, except the authority of the Communist Party.
我偶遇了一位老同學。記憶中，他曾是個頗為特立獨行的人。如今，他在上海經商，銷售私人飛機。他懷念那個除了共產黨的權威之外一切都還可以公開討論的年代。
Having to constantly bite one’s tongue, he said, was a modest, if regrettable, price to pay for China’s many other changes for the better.
他說，不得不時時管住自己的嘴，雖然令人遺憾，但與中國在其他許多方面發生的積極變化相比，代價不算大。
In Jinan, I visited the grave of the man I had often sought out as a student for his intimate knowledge of China’s now muffled past. I knew him as “Lao Wen” but his real name was James Veneris, a former American soldier who, after being taken prisoner by Chinese troops during the 1950-53 Korean War, had opted to stay with his captors and been given a job in Jinan.
在濟南，我去拜謁了一個熟人的墓。學生時代，我經常去找他，因為他對中國那段如今已被壓抑的歷史有著切身的了解。我稱他為「老溫」，但他的真名是詹姆斯·溫納瑞斯。他曾是一名美國士兵，在1950年至1953年的朝鮮戰爭期間被中國軍隊俘虜後，選擇留在俘虜他的人身邊，後來被安排在濟南工作。
Branded an American spy during the Cultural Revolution, he lost his factory job and had been assigned to haul a street cart. His Chinese wife at the time — the second of three — divorced him under duress.
文化大革命期間，他被打成美國間諜，失去了工廠的工作，被派去拉板車。他當時的中國妻子——他三任妻子中的第二任——迫於壓力與他離了婚。
When I got to know him in 1980, he had just been rehabilitated. He had a new wife and a new job teaching English at the university. Eager to avoid getting in trouble again, he plastered his campus apartment with portraits of Mao and Hua Guofeng, the new Communist Party chairman after Mao’s death.
當我在1980年認識他時，他剛剛獲得平反。他有了新妻子，並在大學裡找到了一份教英語的新工作。為了避免再捲入麻煩，他在校園的公寓裡貼滿了毛澤東和華國鋒的畫像。華國鋒是毛澤東去世後接任的中共中央主席。
He was alarmed when I told him that Hua’s portraits had suddenly disappeared from my lecture hall, a sure sign that he, too, had been purged.
當我告訴他，華國鋒像突然從我的教室裡消失時，他大為驚恐——這是華國鋒也已被清洗的明確信號。
“It’s all so damn confusing,” he groaned. Lao Wen died in Jinan in 2001.
「這一切真他媽的讓人摸不著頭腦，」他嘆道。老溫於2001年在濟南去世。
His marble tombstone, paid for by his son, who moved to America, is in a serene new cemetery on the outskirts of Jinan. A gold inscription in English says he had not died, “just disappeared.” A Chinese inscription on the reverse side remembers him as an “American who loved peace, loved life and loved the people of China.”
他的兒子移居美國後，出資為他修建了一塊大理石墓碑，墓地位於濟南郊外一處寧靜的新公墓。墓碑上的金色英文銘文寫道，他並未離去，「只是消失了」。背面的中文銘文則稱他是一位「熱愛和平、熱愛生活、熱愛中國人民的美國人」。
His many travails are passed over in silence.
至於他生前遭遇的種種磨難，隻字未提。
The silence that engulfs so much of China’s past reaches a sinister crescendo in Tiananmen Square, which I had last visited on the night of June 3-4, 1989, when soldiers seized it back from protesting students amid a hail of gunfire.
籠罩著中國諸多過去的沉默在天安門廣場達到了令人膽寒的高潮。我上一次來到這裡，是在1989年6月3日深夜至4日清晨，當時軍隊大肆開槍，從抗議學生手中強行奪回了廣場。
By the time I left Beijing in 1991, roads around Tiananmen had all been repaved to remove traces of tank tracks. The square had again become a public space filled with people flying kites and playing with their children.
到1991年我離開北京時，天安門周圍的道路都已重新鋪設，抹去了坦克履帶留下的痕跡。廣場重新變回了公共空間，滿是放風箏和帶著孩子玩耍的民眾。
Today, the Chinese capital’s main public square is no longer a place to relax like Red Square in Moscow or Trafalgar Square in London, but a by-appointment-only dead zone. Kites are banned without a permit. All visitors, mostly Chinese from the provinces, are carefully screened.
如今，這座中國首都的核心公共廣場不再是像莫斯科紅場或倫敦特拉法加廣場那樣令人放鬆的去處，而變成了一個僅限預約的死寂之地。未經許可嚴禁放風箏。所有訪客——主要是來自外省的國內遊客——都必須接受嚴格審查。
Before my wife and I could visit in January, we had to apply for permission via WeChat. Once approved, we waited in line for two hours, showing our passports and computer-generated permission notification at three separate checkpoints with airport-style security.
在今年1月前往遊覽之前，我和妻子必須提前通過微信提交申請。獲得批准後，我們在三個配備了機場級安檢設施的檢查站排隊等待了兩個小時，出示護照和電子預約通知。
At the final check, a guard pounced on a pen carried by my wife, insisting it contained red ink, which she said was prohibited. She did not say why, but during the 1989 student protests several people hurled red paint at a famous portrait of Mao that faces the square. A security supervisor finally let us through after writing with the pen on a piece of paper and seeing that it was brown, not red.
在最後一道檢查關口，一名安檢員盯上了我妻子攜帶的一支筆，堅稱筆芯裝有紅墨水，而紅墨水是被禁止帶入的。她沒有解釋原因，但在1989年學生抗議期間，曾有人向面向廣場的那幅著名的毛澤東畫像潑灑紅色油漆。安檢主管最終拿這支筆在紙上試寫，確認墨水是褐色而非紅色後，才放我們通行。
Now out of China, I often wonder: How can it be that a proud, nuclear-armed world power is scared of red ink?
如今離開中國後，我常常感到困惑：一個自豪且擁有核武器的世界大國，究竟為何會害怕一支裝有紅墨水的筆？
