黑暗歷史的迴響：韓國民主紀念館背後的創傷與反思

Confronting South Korea’s Violent Past and Its Echoes Today

JIN YU YOUNG • 2026-08-27
The matte black brick building has stood in the heart of Seoul for 50 years. It is also unusual for the slit-like windows that run across its fifth floor. Each looked out of small cells, some of which were painted blood red to evoke fear.
這座啞光黑色磚砌建築在首爾市中心已矗立了50年。它還有一個與眾不同之處：五樓橫貫整層的狹縫狀窗戶。每扇窗戶後面都是一個狹小的牢房，其中一些牢房被漆成血紅色，以營造令人恐懼的氛圍。
There, in 1981, South Korea’s military dictatorship tortured Yoo Dong-woo, a former factory worker, for organizing labor protests. His ribs were broken, some of his teeth were knocked out, and blood constantly pooled in his mouth.
1981年，韓國軍人獨裁政權在這裡對柳東宇（音）施以酷刑。他曾是一名工廠工人，因組織勞工抗議活動而遭到迫害。他的肋骨被打斷，牙齒也被打掉了幾顆，嘴裡一直血流不止。
“So, this is how a person dies,” he recalled thinking at the time.
「原來，人就是這樣死的，」他回憶當時自己這樣想道。
Mr. Yoo and his cause survived. South Korea is now a raucous democracy. And, as of last summer, the site where he was beaten mercilessly, as were hundreds of others, is part of the National Museum of Korean Democracy. The exhibits — one describing the torture meted out to detainees is so gruesome that it is off-limits to young children — remind visitors of a past that must not be repeated.
柳東宇活了下來，他的事業也延續了下去。如今，韓國已經成為一個充滿活力的民主國家。去年夏天，他和另外數百人曾經遭受殘酷毆打的地方成為了韓國國家民主紀念館的一部分。館內的展覽提醒著參觀者那段絕不能重演的歷史——其中一處介紹對拘押者施加酷刑的展覽由於過於慘烈而不允許幼童參觀。

But six months before the museum opened, South Koreans got a shocking echo of that period: President Yoon Suk Yeol declared martial law. The crisis was quickly averted, and Mr. Yoon is serving a life sentence in prison for insurrection. The museum is still deciding on how to acknowledge that episode, but in recent months South Koreans have gotten alarming reminders of the country’s dark history.
然而，就在該紀念館開館前六個月，韓國人又突然聽到了那段歷史的驚人迴響：總統尹錫悅宣布戒嚴。危機很快得到化解，而尹錫悅則因發動內亂罪被判處無期徒刑。紀念館目前仍在考慮如何記錄和呈現那一事件，但近幾個月來，韓國人已經一次又一次受到這個國家黑暗歷史的令人不安的提醒。
In May, a Starbucks promotion appeared to mock a massacre in the city of Gwangju in 1980, when the dictator Chun Doo-hwan deployed tanks and paratroopers to crush pro-democracy protesters. Shortly after, a high school baseball team from Seoul made headlines for taunting its opponents from Gwangju by chanting “Let’s go to Starbucks!” and “Tank Day!” Both episodes struck a nerve.
今年5月，星巴克的一項促銷活動似乎在拿1980年光州發生的大屠殺開玩笑。那一年，獨裁者全斗煥出動坦克和傘兵鎮壓爭取民主的抗議者。沒過多久，首爾一支高中棒球隊也登上新聞頭條，他們對來自光州的對手高喊「去星巴克吧！」和「坦克日！」來挑釁對方。這兩起事件都在韓國社會引發了強烈反響。
“I can’t help but feel that our past is still unfolding,” said Na Ji-hyun, who was visiting the museum last month. She had been detained at the building, known as the Namyeong-dong Anti-Communist Interrogation Office (dong means neighborhood in Korean) for more than a week in 1983.
「我不禁覺得我們的過去仍在延續，」上個月參觀該紀念館的羅智賢（音）說。1983年，她曾在這裡被關了一週多。這棟建築當時的名字叫「南營洞對共分室」（洞在韓語裡為街區的意思）。
Then 22, she got caught in the dictatorship’s dragnet because she was part of a group that gave night classes on literacy, math and social sciences to factory workers and housekeepers, and was seen by officials as a possible breeding ground of dissent. Interrogators coerced her cooperation by pointing to officers who were notorious for torture and recounting stories of past detainees who had died in their hands. She heard the screams of other prisoners who were being tortured down the hall.
當時她22歲，之所以落入獨裁政權的羅網，是因為她加入了一個為工廠工人和家政人員開設夜校的團體，教人識字、學習數學和社會科學。在當局看來，該團體可能成為滋生異議思想的溫床。審訊人員指著那些以酷刑聞名的軍官，並講述過去拘押者死在他們手中的故事，以此逼迫她配合。她還聽見走廊另一頭其他囚犯遭受酷刑時發出的慘叫。
“I didn’t know what was happening, and I couldn’t dare to ask,” Ms. Na said. While she was not beaten, she was questioned for hours on end and forced to write statements about her political ideology, education and social circle.
「我不知道發生了什麼，也根本不敢問，」羅智賢說。雖然她沒有遭到毆打，但被連續審訊數小時，並被迫就自己的政治思想、受教育經歷以及社會交往圈子寫材料。
Even today on the fifth floor, sunlight seeps in thin slivers through windows that are barely a foot wide. They were designed to prevent inmates from jumping to their deaths or escaping. A circular staircase winds up, built in a way to disorient detainees as they were blindfolded and marched up the stairs to interrogation rooms.
即便在今天，陽光也只能透過五樓那些僅不到30厘米寬的窗戶滲進室內。這些窗戶當初就是為了防止犯人跳樓自殺或逃跑而設計的。一座環形樓梯盤旋向上，這種設計是為了讓被蒙住雙眼的拘押者沿著樓梯上到審訊室時失去方向感。
Some of those cells have been recreated, and part of the grounds have been revamped; a tennis court where guards would enjoy their leisure time is now a courtyard.
其中一些牢房已經按照當年的樣子復原，部分場地也經過改造；過去警衛們休閑娛樂時使用的網球場如今已經變成了一座庭院。
But one cell has been preserved as a tribute to one of its former occupants, Park Jong-cheol, a student activist who died after being waterboarded at the building in 1987. His killing, which the authorities tried to cover up, helped catalyze mass protests that forced the government to agree to a free and direct presidential election.
不過，其中一間牢房被完整保留下來，以紀念曾經關押在這裡的朴鍾哲。他是一名學生運動人士，1987年在這棟建築裡遭受水刑後死亡。當局一度試圖掩蓋真相，他的死後來成為了引發大規模抗議的導火線之一，最終迫使政府同意舉行自由、直接的總統選舉。

The Starbucks promotion, for a tumbler, included a phrase — “Thump it on the desk” — that some critics said evoked Mr. Park’s death. The authorities had initially claimed that Mr. Park gasped and died after an interrogator slammed a desk in his cell. The company apologized and fired its chief executive in South Korea. In the other episode, the high school apologized and its baseball team was barred from nationwide competition for a month. The players went to the Gwangju school to apologize and 12 of them were punished by the school, though it did not disclose how.
星巴克那次保溫杯促銷活動中有一句宣傳語——「在桌上啪的一聲」——在一些批評者看來，它讓人聯想到朴鍾哲的死亡。當局最初聲稱，在審訊人員猛拍他牢房裡的桌子後，朴鍾哲因喘不過氣而死亡。星巴克隨後道歉，並解僱了公司在韓國的首席執行官。至於另一事件，那所高中也公開道歉，其棒球隊被禁止參加全國性比賽一個月。球員們還前往光州那所學校當面道歉，其中12人受到校方處分，但校方沒有公布具體處分。
“They are not matters that can simply be apologized away,” said Kim Chanho, a manager at the Korea Democracy Foundation, which operates the museum.
「這些事情不是簡單道個歉就能過去的，」運營該紀念館的韓國民主基金會經理金燦浩（音）說。
Many in South Korea have blamed the episodes on a lack of understanding of the country’s history. The museum can help fill this void, Mr. Kim said, but added that he believed society as a whole — civic groups, private companies and schools — had not fulfilled its collective duty in making sure that the country’s history is not forgotten.
許多韓國人將這兩起事件歸咎於對本國歷史缺乏了解。金燦浩說，這座紀念館可以幫助填補這一空白，但他還表示，他認為整個社會——公民團體、私營企業和學校——都沒有盡到共同的責任，確保這個國家的歷史不被遺忘。
“It’s great that we have a space like this to educate people and raise awareness,” Ms. Na said. “But doesn’t it feel like only people who already care or are experts come here? Shouldn’t people who don’t care also come?”
「有這樣一個地方來教育人們、提高大家的意識當然很好。」羅智賢說。「但你不覺得好像只有那些本來就關心這段歷史的人或者專家才會來這裡嗎？那些對此漠不關心的人難道不也應該來嗎？」
When Mr. Yoo was hauled in, guards demanded to know whether he was a communist or a socialist. He said he was neither, drawing their wrath. “They punched my face from both sides and kicked me so hard in the ribs I collapsed,” he said. “They hit me without mercy.” Accused of being a communist and anti-state agitator, he faced a trial for organizing an association of democratic workers and was released on probation after more than a month in detention at Namyeong and several more in another prison.
柳東宇當年被押進來時，警衛逼問他究竟是共產主義者還是社會主義者。他說自己兩者都不是，這激怒了他們。「他們從兩邊同時朝我的臉上打，還狠狠踢我的肋骨，把我踢倒在地，」他說。「他們打起人來毫不手軟。」他被指控為共產主義者和反國家煽動者，因組織民主工人協會而受到審判。在南營洞被關押一個多月、隨後又在另一所監獄關押了幾個月後，他獲緩刑釋放。
The trauma haunted him for years. He said that he suffered hallucinations and nightmares of being arrested again. Being at home induced panic, and he often wandered the streets and slept in public spaces for long stretches of time.
這段創傷折磨了他很多年。他說，自己會產生幻覺，還會做噩夢，夢見自己再次被捕。待在家裡會讓他陷入恐慌，因此他常常在街頭遊盪，一連很長時間都睡在公共場所。

After military rule ended in 1987, the building was still used in secret by the police for interrogations targeting labor and left-leaning organizations, according to the museum. It was officially shut down in 2004 and renamed the National Police Agency Human Rights Center in 2005. But the continued police ownership prompted backlash from civic groups and victims, who argued that the agency that had carried out state torture should not have control over its memory. In 2018, President Moon Jae-in approved its conversion into a museum and the grounds were handed over to the Democracy Foundation.
據紀念館介紹，1987年軍人統治結束後，這棟建築仍被警方祕密用於審訊勞工組織和左翼組織的成員。它於2004年正式關閉，2005年更名為警察廳南營洞人權中心。但警方繼續擁有該建築，這引發了公民團體和受害者的強烈反對，他們認為實施國家酷刑的機構不應掌控對其歷史的敘述。2018年，總統文在寅批准將這裡改建為紀念館，場地移交給了民主基金會。
Still, many South Koreans across the political spectrum hold conflicting views on their country’s turbulent modern history, with some far-right conservatives baselessly claiming that the Gwangju uprising was instigated by North Korean Communist agents.
然而，即使在今天，韓國社會各個政治陣營的人對這個國家動盪的現代史仍然存在相互衝突的看法。一些極右翼保守派甚至毫無根據地聲稱，光州起義是由朝鮮共產主義特工煽動的。
“In such a polarized society like Korea how do you build an edifice that’s supposed to memorialize democracy when, in fact, a lot of the stuff hasn’t even been resolved by historians?” said Theodore Jun Yoo, a professor of cultural studies and history at Yonsei University in Seoul. “The public hasn’t agreed on all these issues,” he said.
「在像韓國這樣兩極分化的社會，當歷史學家實際上連許多基本事實都尚未釐清時，你該如何建造一座本應用來紀念民主的建築？」首爾延世大學文化研究與歷史教授柳俊（音）說。「公眾在這些問題上尚未達成共識，」他表示。
Mr. Yoo, who is now in his late 70s, has worked as a docent at the museum, showing visitors the very places he was tortured.
如今已年近八旬的柳東宇在這座紀念館擔任講解員，帶遊客參觀當年他遭受酷刑的地方。
“At first, even catching a glimpse of this building would make me shiver with dread,” he said. “But one day a thought struck me: Why should I be the one to be scared? It’s the perpetrators who should be scared.”
「起初，哪怕只是瞥見這棟建築，我都會嚇得渾身發抖，」他說。「但有一天我忽然想到：為什麼該害怕的人是我？真正應該害怕的是那些施暴者。」
