當法國人將憤怒砸向奢侈品櫥窗

In France, Raging Against the Luxury Machine

VANESSA FRIEDMAN • 2026-10-22
Amid all of the images and videos that emerged of the riots that took place in France over the weekend, the most shocking are those of the clock tower collapsing in Limoges after its 19th-century town hall was set alight.
在週末法國騷亂的眾多圖片和影片中，最令人震驚的莫過於利摩日那座19世紀市政廳被縱火後鐘樓倒塌的畫面。
Close behind, however, are the shots of looters storming the gates of luxury brands like Celine, Hermès, Louis Vuitton and Saint Laurent, breaking through the plate-glass windows that act as a scrim between the outside world and the hushed interiors that cater to the ultrarich, smashing their sparkling vitrines and looting their goods.
緊隨其後的，是搶劫者衝進思琳、愛馬仕、路易威登和聖羅蘭等奢侈品牌門店的畫面：他們衝破將外界與店內超級富豪專屬靜謐空間隔開的落地玻璃窗，砸碎閃閃發光的陳列櫃，搶走商品。
Of the estimated more than 3,000 shops that have been affected by the spiraling protests, including grocery stores, pharmacies and family-run operations, it is the luxury destruction that has caught much of the attention, and the imagination, of the watching world.
受不斷升級的抗議活動影響的店鋪估計超過3000家，包括雜貨店、藥店和家族經營店鋪，真正吸引外界大量關注、令人浮想聯翩的，是奢侈品遭到的破壞。
Whenever demonstrations are coupled with looting, they set off a conversation about wider, often inchoate, expressions of anger over inequality, not just in France but across the world. And though it would be easy to see this as yet another blip in a long history of using luxury products as a stand-in for social and economic injustice (one that stretches back to the French Revolution), it is also a response to a peculiarly contemporary reality the luxury industry itself has created.
每當示威活動伴隨搶劫發生，就會引發討論，關乎對不平等問題的更廣泛、往往並不明確的憤怒表達，這樣的討論不僅存在於法國，也遍布全世界。長期以來，奢侈品被當作社會和經濟不公的替身，這樣的歷史可追溯到法國大革命，人們很容易將此次的現象當做這個漫長歷史中的又一次小插曲，但它也是對奢侈品行業自身製造出的一種特殊當代現實的回應。
The current situation is, after all, the third instance of physical attacks against luxury brands in the last eight years in the city that has styled itself the capital of luxury. In 2023, during the movement against government pension reform, protesters stormed the doors of the LVMH headquarters on Avenue Montaigne in Paris, waving sparklers and ultimately burning an effigy of Bernard Arnault, the billionaire owner of the group (and that year briefly the richest man in the world).
畢竟，在這座自詡奢侈品之都的城市，這已是八年來第三次發生針對奢侈品牌的實體攻擊。2023年，在反對政府養老金改革的運動中，抗議者衝上巴黎蒙田大道的路威酩軒集團總部大門，揮舞煙花棒，最終焚燒了該集團億萬富翁老闆貝爾納·阿爾諾的人像（當年他曾短暫成為世界首富）。
In 2019, Dior and Chanel shops on the Champs-Élysées in Paris, among other luxury emporiums, were looted during demonstrations by the Gilets Jaunes (Yellow Vests), and boarded-up storefronts became a familiar sight.
2019年，巴黎香榭麗舍大街上的迪奧和香奈兒等奢侈品商店在「黃背心」示威運動期間遭到搶劫，被木板封住的店面成為街頭常見的景象。
According to Serge Carreira, the director of the emerging brands initiative at the Fédération de la Haute Couture and an affiliated professor at the School of Management and Impact at Sciences Po, “France has always cultivated a love-hate relationship with luxury — proud of its global influence, suspicious of its morality and blaming its unequal essence.”
高級定製時裝聯合會新興品牌項目主任、巴黎政治學院管理與影響力學院兼職教授塞爾日·卡雷拉說：「法國對奢侈品一直保持著一種愛恨交織的感情——既為其全球影響力感到驕傲，又對其道德性心存疑慮，並指責其不平等的本質。」

In some ways, it is exactly that influence, so assiduously cultivated by the brands themselves, that is now backfiring.
在某些方面，正是品牌自身苦心經營的影響力，如今正在反噬它們。
Luxury has used social media and its own cultural cachet to make itself ubiquitous in the lives of even those who can’t afford its products. Thanks to celebrity ambassadors, the transformation of fashion shows into entertainment and the red carpet into a marketing opportunity, everybody knows Dior, even if they will never buy a Dior. Ditto Louis Vuitton, Chanel, Burberry and more.
奢侈品利用社群媒體和自身文化聲望，甚至讓那些買不起其產品的人也無時無刻不感受到它的存在。得益於明星代言、時裝秀向娛樂的轉變，以及紅毯秀成為營銷契機，人人都知道迪奧，即使他們永遠不會買迪奧。路易威登、香奈兒、博柏利等也是如此。
(Ironically, the weekend protests coincided with LVMH’s Journées Particulières, or Open Days, an initiative in which many of the group’s brands welcome the public into their ateliers and workshops, to share how the products are made and invite consumers into their otherwise closed world.)
（具有諷刺意味的是，週末的抗議恰逢路威酩軒集團的「特別開放日」活動。這是一項由該集團旗下眾多品牌共同發起的活動，向公眾敞開工坊和工作室的大門，展示產品製作過程，並邀請消費者進入這個平時不對外開放的世界。）
But paradoxically, as these brands have held themselves up as the epitome of aspiration and taste, they have also raised their prices to such levels that they are out of reach for almost everyone. They are emotionally and psychologically seductive but practically unattainable. As a result, “most of the younger generation feels excluded from this world,” Carreira said. “There is no more dream, just envy and rage.”
但矛盾的是，當這些品牌把自己樹立為追求和品味的典範時，它們也把價格提高到幾乎所有人都無法企及的水平。它們在情感和心理上具有誘惑力，但在現實中遙不可及。結果，「大多數年輕一代覺得自己被排除在這個世界之外，」卡雷拉說。「不再有夢想，只有嫉妒和憤怒。」
Those feelings found their expression in physical annihilation. By destroying the display windows of the opulent stores, Carreira said, the protesters are destroying a system that dangles only the promise of inclusion. “They escape the norm and reach a form of materialistic fulfillment,” he said.
這些情緒以物理破壞的方式得到表達。卡雷拉說，通過破壞奢華商店的櫥窗，抗議者摧毀的是一個僅以包容為誘餌的體系。「他們逃離規範，獲得了一種物質層面的滿足。」
By appropriating the products themselves, many of which have ended up on the luxury resale and black markets, the looters seized some of what had been otherwise denied them and literally brought it out to the street.
通過佔有這些產品本身——其中許多最終流入奢侈品轉售和黑市——搶劫者奪走了一些原本被拒絕給予他們的東西，並且實實在在地把它們帶到了街頭。
In this context, rather than being symbols of artisanship and patrimony, as luxury brands tend to describe themselves and their offerings, their goods become emblems of excess and capitalist rot.
在這種背景之下，奢侈品商品不再是品牌通常自我描述的手工藝和遺產象徵，而成為過度消費和資本主義腐朽的標誌。

As far as what will happen next, Dior, Celine and Hermès either did not respond to requests for comment, declined to comment on what happened with their stores or did not say what measures they had taken to protect them. Ralph Lauren did not respond to emails.
至於接下來會發生什麼，迪奧、思琳和愛馬仕要麼沒有回應置評請求，要麼拒絕就門店遭遇置評，要麼沒有說明採取了何種保護措施。拉夫·勞倫沒有回覆電子郵件。
An internal note sent to LVMH employees on Oct. 19 by Antoine Arnault, the group head of communications and sustainability, and seen by The New York Times, noted that the group had “taken the appropriate measures to ensure everyone’s safety and the continuity of our operations.” (Reportedly, private security has been hired.) He also thanked the “artisans, virtuosos and all members of our teams,” calling them “our finest ambassadors.”
《紐約時報》看到的一封路威酩軒集團傳播與可持續發展主管安托萬·阿爾諾10月19日發給員工的內部通知指出，集團已「採取適當措施，確保所有人安全以及我們運營的連續性」。（據報導，該集團已聘請私人安保。）他還感謝了「工匠、大師和我們團隊的所有成員」，稱他們為「我們最優秀的大使」。
As curfews are imposed in France and cities gear up for another round of protests, the government is promising increased security. “I believe Avenue Montaigne and Rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré will be Fort Knox next weekend and any other day of protest in the next weeks,” Carreira said.
隨著法國實施宵禁、各城市準備迎接新一輪抗議，政府承諾加強安保。卡雷拉說：「我相信，蒙田大道和聖奧諾雷郊區街下週末以及未來幾週任何抗議日都會變成諾克斯堡。」
Billionaires famously love a bunker, but do the brands that serve them really want to be stuck inside?
眾所周知，億萬富翁們都喜歡地堡，但服務於他們的品牌真的願意被困在裡面嗎？

Vanessa Friedman自2014年起一直任《紐約時報》時尚總監、首席時尚評論人。
