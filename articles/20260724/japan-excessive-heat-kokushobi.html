多地40度高溫，日本創造新詞「酷暑日」

It’s So Hot That Japan Needs a New Word for It

KIUKO NOTOYA, FRANCESCA REGALADO • 2026-07-24
Health and weather authorities in Japan were at a loss. A record number of people suffered from heat stroke last year as more cities experienced their highest-ever temperatures, despite official warnings about the dangers of excessive heat.
日本的衛生和氣象當局一度束手無策。儘管官方針對過高溫危險發出警告，但隨著越來越多的城市出現有史以來的最高氣溫，去年中暑人數達到了創紀錄的水平。
They decided to tackle the problem by giving it a name. Earlier this year, the Japan Meteorological Agency held a public poll to select a name for a day of temperatures of 40 degrees Celsius (104 degrees Fahrenheit) or higher, to signal that precautions were needed. The winner, with more than 40 percent of the vote, was kokushobi, meaning “cruel heat day.”
他們決定通過給這種現象起個名字來應對這個問題。今年早些時候，日本氣象廳舉行了一次公開投票，為氣溫達到或超過40攝氏度的日子徵集名稱，以提醒人們需要採取預防措施。最終勝出的是獲得超過40%選票的「kokushobi」，意為「酷暑日」。
The term got its first use on Tuesday, when three cities in central Japan recorded 40-degree temperatures, a first for the country this year. Officials urged people to stay indoors, use air-conditioning and drink water.
該詞於週二首次被使用，當時日本中部的三個城市錄得了40度的高溫，這是該國今年首次出現這種情況。官員們敦促民眾留在室內、使用空調並多喝水。
There were signs that many people had heeded the warnings, as popular sites such as Osaka Castle and Kyoto’s Kiyomizu Temple saw smaller crowds than usual on Tuesday.
有跡象表明，許多人聽從了警告，因為大阪城和京都清水寺等熱門景點週二的遊客比往常要少。
廣告
“Numbers are difficult to understand because people perceive them differently. By giving it the name ‘cruel heat day,’ people will understand it better just by hearing it, and they’ll feel that today is dangerous,” said Taro Kawasato, the assistant director of the Japan Meteorological Agency’s climate prediction division.
「數字令人很難理解，因為人們對它們的感知各不相同。通過命名『酷暑日』，人們一聽就能更好地理解，並且會感覺到今天是危險的一天，」日本氣象廳氣候預測部門助理主任河裡太郎（音）說。
The agency’s public poll in February and March garnered more than 400,000 votes, according to a statement. Kokushobi bested a dozen other terms that translate to “dangerously hot day,” “sweaty day” and “sauna day.” Mr. Kawasato said that meteorologists consulted with media and linguistics experts to form the list of names.
據聲明，該機構在2月和3月進行的公開投票獲得了超過40萬張選票。「酷暑日」 從十幾個其他候選詞中脫穎而出，這些詞翻譯過來包括「危險高溫日」、「汗流浹背日」和「桑拿日」。 河裡太郎表示，氣象學家在制定這份名稱清單時，徵詢了媒體和語言學專家的意見。
And the dangers persist, even with slightly cooler weather on the horizon. Last week, even before the first kokushobi, 14 people died from heat-related illnesses, Japan’s Fire and Disaster Management Agency said.
儘管天氣預計將略微轉涼，這種危險依然存在。日本消防廳表示，上週，甚至在第一個「酷暑日」到來之前，已有14人死於與高溫相關的疾病。
Japan is the latest country where officials have sought new terms to communicate the risks of rising temperatures.
多個國家使用新術語來傳達氣溫上升的風險，日本是最新的一個。
In Britain, the Met Office began issuing extreme heat warnings in 2021 to highlight the impact of high temperatures on health, infrastructure and public services. (A red warning, the highest level, was issued when a heat wave baked European countries last month.) The United Kingdom Health Security Agency also issues “heat health alerts” for England to inform those in the health sector about the potential effects of high temperatures, especially for older people and others who are more vulnerable.
在英國，英國氣象局於2021年開始發布極端高溫警告，以強調高溫對健康、基礎設施和公共服務的影響。（上個月熱浪炙烤歐洲國家時，該機構發布了最高級別的紅色預警。）英國衛生安全局還針對英格蘭發布「高溫健康警報」， 向醫療衛生界人員通報高溫的潛在影響，特別是對老年人和其他脆弱群體的影響。
In recent years, Chinese-language media have also searched for phrases to describe the summer heat. News headlines in Taiwan have described the weather as being like a sauna, with people “roasting like a sweet potato.”
近年來，中文媒體也在尋找詞句來描述夏季的炎熱。台灣的新聞頭條將這種天氣比作桑拿，人們「熱成烤番薯」。
廣告
Health authorities in Shenzhen, a city on China’s southern coast, said in a social media post that temperatures in early July had risen to “barbecue mode.” During that period, 62 people called for ambulances because of heat stroke, two of whom died, the authorities said.
中國南部沿海城市深圳的衛生部門在社群媒體上發文稱，7月上旬的氣溫已升至「燒烤模式」。該部門表示，在此期間，有62人因中暑呼叫救護車，其中兩人死亡。
Mr. Kawasato said that the Japanese government started issuing heat stroke alerts in 2021, as sweltering days grew more frequent across the country. Last year, 30 locations recorded temperatures of at least 40 degrees Celsius, the highest of which was 41.8 degrees — or 107 degrees Fahrenheit — in Isesaki, a city north of Tokyo, according to the Japan Meteorological Agency.
河裡表示，隨著全國各地酷熱天氣日益頻繁，日本政府於2021年開始發布中暑預警。根據日本氣象廳的數據，去年共有30個地點的氣溫達到至少40攝氏度，其中最高氣溫出現在東京北部的伊勢崎市，達到41.8度。
Heat stroke is among the heat-related illnesses that occur when the body loses the ability to cool itself, putting pressure on the heart. Symptoms can range from confusion, dizziness and nausea to a rapid heartbeat and shallow breathing.
中暑是與高溫相關的疾病之一，當人體失去自我降溫能力時就會發生，會給心臟帶來負擔。其癥狀可能包括意識模糊、頭暈和噁心，甚至心跳加速和呼吸急促。
Last summer in Japan, emergency workers responded to more than 100,000 cases of heat stroke from May to September, the most since the Fire and Disaster Management Agency began collecting data in 2008.
去年夏天，在日本，從5月到9月，急救人員處理了超過10萬起中暑病例，這是自日本消防廳2008年開始收集數據以來的最高紀錄。

Kiuko Notoya是一名常駐東京的記者/研究員，報導日本新聞。
