歐洲的夏天為何變得如此「要命」？

The Summer That Broke Europe

ALICIA P.Q. WITTMEYER • 2026-07-30
I’m writing this on another sweltering 33-degree day in London; at this point, they’ve almost started to feel routine. We’re watching wildfires consume hundreds of thousands of hectares across France and Spain. This year’s fire season is already breaking records, and it’s not over yet.
我在倫敦又一個 33 度的悶熱天氣裡寫下這些文字。如今這種天氣幾乎已經成了家常便飯。我們眼看著野火在法國和西班牙肆虐，吞噬數十萬公頃的土地。今年的野火季已經打破了多項紀錄，而且一切還未結束。
Europe has had a rough summer. A season that not so long ago was associated with holidays and relaxation doesn’t feel quite the same when thousands of excess deaths are being attributed to heat. Today, my colleagues Mark Landler, our Paris bureau chief, who’s been covering the fires in France, and Chico Harlan, our global climate correspondent, write about whether we’re witnessing a moment of reckoning for the continent and climate change.
歐洲度過了一個艱難的夏天。不久前，這個季節還與假期和休閑聯繫在一起，但當數以千計的超額死亡被歸咎於高溫時，這種感覺已大不相同。今天，我的同事——一直報導法國野火的巴黎分社社長馬克·蘭德勒、全球氣候通訊記者奇科·哈蘭——將撰文探討我們是否正在見證歐洲大陸和氣候變化的一個清算時刻。
A very bad summer
一個非常糟糕的夏天
By Mark Landler and Chico Harlan
作者：馬克·蘭德勒、奇科·哈蘭
廣告
Ravenous fires. Searing heat waves. Freakish storms. The summer of 2026 has already shattered meteorological records in several European countries. On a continent that has weathered centuries of upheaval — ruinous wars, deadly pandemics — it’s the weather that now has Europe feeling close to a breaking point.
肆虐的野火。灼人的熱浪。反常的暴風雨。2026年的夏天已經打破了歐洲多個國家的氣象紀錄。歐洲大陸經歷了幾個世紀的動盪——毀滅性戰爭、致命大流行病——如今卻是天氣讓它感覺快要到達崩潰邊緣。
It has displaced more than 300,000 people in France and Spain, where the largest fires in those countries’ modern histories have led to their largest peacetime evacuations. It has left millions desperate for relief from temperatures that routinely soar above 40 degrees Celsius.
天氣異常導致法國和西班牙超過30萬人流離失所，兩國現代史上最大規模的野火引發了和平時期最大規模的人員撤離。氣溫通常飆升至40攝氏度以上，數以百萬計的民眾迫切期盼著酷暑能夠有所緩解。
It has forced the cancellation of concerts and festivals, curtailed the final stage of the Tour de France, and closed the Louvre Museum and the Eiffel Tower early. It has brought wildfires to the Scottish Highlands. And it has caused at least 10,000 heat-related deaths across Europe in June alone — a number that may turn out to be an undercount.
高溫迫使音樂會和節日慶典取消、縮短了環法單車賽的最後賽段、導致羅浮宮和艾菲爾鐵塔提前關閉。它還引發了蘇格蘭高地的山火。僅在6月，歐洲就記錄了至少1萬例與高溫相關的死亡，而這個數字可能還被低估了。
The most frightening aspect of Europe’s terrible summer? Scientists say it’s just the beginning.
歐洲這個可怕夏天最令人恐懼的地方是什麼？科學家說，這僅僅是個開始。
“It will inevitably get worse,” said Johan Rockström, a Swedish scientist who is a director of the Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research in Germany. “It will get worse and worse and worse, to the point where, I’m afraid, we’ll be passing the limits of adaptation.”
「情況不可避免地會變得更糟，」德國波茨坦氣候影響研究所所長、瑞典科學家約翰·羅克斯特倫說。「情況會越來越糟，越來越糟，恐怕會達到我們突破人類適應極限的地步。」
The fastest-warming continent
變暖最快的大陸
Europe is not alone, of course. Heat waves gripped Australia and Japan this year. Floods devastated Kenya and Angola. A snowstorm paralyzed the Eastern United States. Developing countries in the Southern Hemisphere are still more vulnerable than wealthy European ones to the effects of a changing climate.
當然，並非只有歐洲如此。今年，熱浪也席捲了澳洲和日本。洪水摧毀了肯亞和安哥拉。一場暴風雪讓美國東部陷入癱瘓。面對不斷變化的氣候所帶來的影響，南半球的發展中國家仍然比富裕的歐洲國家更加脆弱。
廣告
Yet no other continent has warmed as rapidly as Europe, which has been heating up at nearly twice the global average since the mid-1990s.
然而，沒有哪個大陸變暖的速度能與歐洲相提並論，自20世紀90年代中期以來，歐洲的變暖速度幾乎是全球平均水平的兩倍。
Europe’s status as the fastest-warming continent is a consequence of both changing weather patterns and its proximity to the Arctic, where reduced snow and ice lead to more dark surfaces that absorb heat.
歐洲成為變暖速度最快的大陸既源於天氣模式的變化，也與其靠近北極的地理位置有關。北極地區冰雪減少，導致更多吸收熱量的深色地表暴露出來。
The continent is more than 1.6 degrees Celsius warmer than it was three decades ago, and the rise in temperature is swift enough to be noticeable over a single generation. By the start of this week, France had recorded 53 heat waves since 1947; 28 of them occurred in the last 15 years.
歐洲大陸的氣溫比30年前升高了1.6攝氏度以上，而且氣溫上升的速度之快，足以在一代人的時間裡被明顯察覺。截至本週初，法國自1947年以來錄得53次熱浪，其中28次發生在過去15年間。
Bordeaux, which is currently being threatened by wildfire, never experienced a day with temperatures at or above 39 degrees Celsius between 1921 and 2000, according to Météo-France, the national climate and weather service. This summer, though, it has happened eight times, and the season is not even half over.
據法國國家氣候和氣象局的數據，1921年至2000年間，目前正受到野火威脅的波爾多從未經歷過氣溫達到或超過39攝氏度的日子。但在今年夏天，這種情況已經發生了八次，而且夏天還沒過完一半。
While it is still cooler than other parts of the world, Europe is less able to cope with heat. Its buildings often lack air-conditioning and were built to retain heat. And the homogeneity of the trees on its hillsides and coastal plains makes them dangerously flammable.
儘管歐洲仍比世界上其他一些地區涼爽，但其應對高溫的能力卻較弱。這裡的建築往往缺乏空調，且在建造時就設計為保溫結構。此外，山坡和沿海平原上樹木種類的單一性使其極易引發火災。
All this has forced Europeans to confront, more starkly than others, the harsh realities of a changed climate. But whether this leads to drastic new measures — either to mitigate the effects of a warmer climate or to adapt to them — is another question.
所有這一切迫使歐洲人比其他地區的人們更需要直面氣候變化的嚴峻現實。但是，這是否會導致採取激進的新措施——無論是為了減輕氣候變暖的影響還是為了適應這一變化——則是另一個問題。
Great urgency — and yet
情況極其緊迫，然而——
In Spain, Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez declared a “climate emergency,” which he said would necessitate a state pact to “attack the root of the problem in a structural way.” In France, President Emmanuel Macron vowed to replant the vast pine forests that were burned in the Gironde region over the last week with different species of trees that are better suited to a dry, hot climate.
在西班牙，首相佩德羅·桑切斯宣布進入「氣候緊急狀態」，他表示這將需要達成一項國家協議，以便「從結構上根治這個問題」。在法國，總統馬克宏承諾，將在吉倫特地區重新種植更適應乾熱氣候的樹種，這裡廣闊的松樹林在過去一週被大火焚毀。
廣告
“The situation we’re in today is the most difficult we’ve ever recorded, the most difficult since World War II,” Macron said.
「我們今天所處的局勢是有紀錄以來最艱難的，是自二戰以來最嚴峻的，」馬克宏說。
But for all the urgency, Europe’s commitment to reducing carbon dioxide emissions is in danger of being watered down. This month, the European Union proposed to adjust rules so that some industries could pollute at slightly higher levels, further into the future.
然而，儘管形勢緊迫，歐洲減少二氧化碳排放的承諾卻面臨被削弱的危險。本月，歐盟提議調整規則，允許某些行業在未來更長的時間內將污染排放水平略微提高。
“Net zero” policies, which aim to balance the amount of greenhouse gases released into the atmosphere with that removed, have become targets for critics, particularly those on the right, who condemn them as a luxury that Europe can’t afford during a period of weak economic growth and energy insecurity.
旨在平衡大氣中溫室氣體排放量與清除量的「凈零」政策已經成為批評者的靶子，尤其來自右翼人士。他們譴責這些政策是歐洲在經濟增長疲軟、能源安全面臨挑戰的時期無法承擔的奢侈之舉。
And no matter what Europe does, the global trajectory of climate change depends on China and the U.S. — whose president has called climate change a “con job” — as well as on energy-hungry developing countries.
而且，無論歐洲採取什麼行動，氣候變化的全球軌跡都取決於中國和美國——美國總統曾稱氣候變化是「騙局」——以及能源需求量巨大的發展中國家。
The planet is already on course to miss the central target of the Paris Agreement of 2015: to hold global warming well below two degrees Celsius compared with preindustrial levels. France’s weather and climate service has said that, by 2050, heat waves could be five times more frequent.
地球已經註定無法實現 2015年《巴黎協定》的核心目標：將全球平均氣溫較工業化前水平的升幅控制在2攝氏度以內。法國的氣象和氣候服務部門表示，到2050年，熱浪的發生頻率可能會是現在的五倍。
The past few months have been the summer that broke Europe. But they could, in retrospect, come to seem tame.
過去的幾個月是令歐洲不堪重負的夏天。但如果在未來回想起來，它們或許還算溫和。

最新消息：波爾多和馬德里附近的野火勢頭有所減弱，但目前風力依然很強勁，且氣溫正在上升。西班牙首相稱接下來的12小時具有「決定性」意義。
