中國應屆生就業前景暗淡

China’s New Graduates, Facing a Dire Job Market, Must Also Contend With A.I.

YAN ZHUANG • 2026-09-07
A few months out from graduating with a master’s degree in urban planning, Jin Shangyu is watching artificial intelligence upend the industry she is trying to enter.
距離碩士畢業還有幾個月，學習城市規劃專業的金尚宇（音）正眼睜睜地看著人工智慧顛覆她試圖進入的行業。
Job boards are filled with roles that require A.I. expertise, while listings for traditional roles seem to be dwindling. Much of the work involved in the early stage of a design project, which used to involve several people, can now be executed with a few clicks of a mouse.
招聘網站上充斥著需要人工智慧專長的職位，而傳統崗位的招聘信息似乎正在減少。設計項目前期階段的大量工作過去需要好幾個人完成，現在點幾下鼠標就能搞定。
Since getting her degree from the University of Hong Kong, Ms. Jin, 26, who is from northern China, has applied for about 150 internships and entry-level jobs in Hong Kong and mainland China, but has been met with silence. She took a $400 professional A.I. course in hopes of standing out in the marketplace. It does not seem to have helped. No wonder, she thought: If even seasoned workers are being undercut by A.I., what chance was there for those seeking to enter the work force?
26歲的金女士來自中國北方，自香港大學畢業後，已在香港和內地申請了約150個實習和初級職位，但均石沉大海。她花400美元上了一門人工智慧專業課程，希望能在求職市場中脫穎而出，但這似乎沒什麼用。她心想，這也不奇怪：如果連經驗豐富的從業者都被人工智慧搶了飯碗，那剛想入行的新人還有什麼機會？
“My feeling is that we, new graduates, are exactly the kind of people that A.I. will replace,” she said. When she thinks about planning for her future, she said, “I really don’t know how to even begin because I don’t see hope.”
「我的感覺是，我們這些應屆畢業生正是人工智慧要取代的那類人，」她說。談到規劃未來時，她說：「我真的不知道該如何開始，因為我看不到希望。」
廣告
An estimated 12.7 million new graduates are joining China’s work force this year, the largest yet of a cohort that has been steadily growing over the years. They are facing two intractable and connected challenges. Like previous classes, they are entering an oversaturated job market and competing for a shrinking pool of entry-level positions in a sluggish economy. But they also face a new obstacle in the added uncertainty of A.I., which is beginning to restructure industries and change recruiters’ priorities.
據估計，今年將有1270萬應屆畢業生加入中國的勞動力大軍，這是多年來持續增長的畢業生群體中人數最多的一次。他們正面臨兩個相互關聯且難以解決的挑戰。和往屆畢業生一樣，他們進入的是一個飽和的就業市場，在經濟疲軟的環境下爭奪日益減少的初級職位。但他們還面臨一個新障礙：人工智慧帶來的額外不確定性。人工智慧正開始重構行業格局，改變招聘方的優先考量。
It is a global problem. College graduates around the world are grappling with the prospect that entry-level jobs could simply disappear. But in China, the concern is acute. The country has adopted A.I. at an almost unmatched pace, spurred by government support and broad public enthusiasm, while youth unemployment remains stubbornly high.
這是一個全球性問題。世界各地的大學畢業生都在應對初級職位可能直接消失的前景。但在中國，這種擔憂尤為緊迫。在政府支持和廣泛的公眾熱情推動下，中國以前所未有的速度引入人工智慧，而與此同時青年失業率始終居高不下。
This July, the rate for urban residents aged 16 to 24 was 17.9 percent. The supply of desirable, well-paying graduate jobs has not kept pace with the ballooning number of young people who entered college.
今年7月，16至24歲城鎮居民的失業率為17.9%。優質的高薪畢業生崗位供給未能跟上激增的大學入學人數。
“A.I. has arrived at a particularly difficult moment, though it did not create the underlying problem,” said Christian Yao, a senior lecturer at Victoria University of Wellington in New Zealand who has studied how young Chinese people are navigating the job market.
「人工智慧在一個特別困難的時刻到來，儘管它並非造成根本問題的根源，」紐西蘭威靈頓維多利亞大學高級講師克里斯蒂安·姚說道，他一直在研究中國年輕人如何在就業市場中尋找出路。
Unemployed and Unhappy
失業與不滿
For China’s leaders, who have long derived much of their political authority from the promise of prosperity, there is a risk that high youth unemployment could be a source of political unrest.
對中國領導人而言，其政治權威的很大一部分長期來源於對繁榮的承諾，青年失業率高企可能成為政治動盪的源頭。
廣告
Any negative effect that A.I. may have on youth unemployment, however minor, is acutely sensitive for the party, said Scott Singer, a fellow and co-director of the China AI Initiative at the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace. More broadly, he said, “China is extremely concerned about the labor market impacts from A.I. It’s perhaps their greatest concern right now” in relation to A.I.
卡內基國際和平基金會中國人工智慧項目研究員兼聯席主任史考特·辛格表示，人工智慧可能對青年失業率產生的任何負面影響，無論多麼微小，都對執政黨極為敏感。更廣泛地說，「中國極其關注人工智慧對勞動力市場的影響。這可能是他們目前在人工智慧方面最關心的問題。」
Wary of the potential human and political fallout from the A.I. revolution, Beijing is leaning on companies to avoid layoffs. It has signaled through several court rulings that it is unwilling to accept A.I. cost savings as a justification for job losses.
出於對人工智慧革命可能帶來的人員和政治後果的警惕，北京正在向企業施壓以避免裁員。通過幾項法院裁決，政府已表明不願接受以人工智慧節省成本作為裁員的理由。
In January, the government announced “targeted employment support for key industries,” and some party officials have proposed more sweeping interventions, such as encouraging employers to offer vocational training to help workers adapt to an A.I.-centric job market.
今年1月，政府宣布了「重點行業針對性就業支持」，一些黨內官員則提出了更全面的干預措施，如鼓勵僱主提供職業培訓，幫助工人適應以人工智慧為核心的就業市場。
So far, the impact of A.I. and automation has been felt most strongly in manufacturing and food delivery, two of China’s largest employment categories. The effect on white-collar jobs is only starting to be felt, experts said.
專家表示，迄今為止，人工智慧和自動化的影響在製造業和外賣配送這兩個中國最大的就業類別中感受最為強烈。對白領崗位的影響才剛剛開始顯現。
In interviews, young job seekers described trying to find their footing in a landscape that was changing rapidly because of A.I. They spoke of anxiety, uncertainty and, in some cases, despair as their job searches dragged on. Their only consolation: Everyone seemed to be struggling together.
在採訪中，年輕求職者描述了在因人工智慧而快速變化的就業環境中努力尋找立足之地的經歷。他們談到了焦慮、不確定，以及在求職拖延日久時產生的絕望。他們唯一的安慰是：似乎所有人都在一起掙扎。
“My spirit has been ground down into nothingness,” said Zinnia Zhou, who graduated this year with a master’s degree in television broadcasting.
「我的精神已經被消磨殆盡，」廣播電視專業碩士畢業生周津妮亞（音）說。
廣告
Ms. Zhou has submitted more than 2,800 job applications over the past year. That has led to fewer than 20 interviews and zero job offers so far, she said. She used to carefully comb through job listings and apply only for positions that fitted her skill set — now she applies for anything. Even landing an interview no longer gives her hope.
周女士在過去一年中提交了超過2800份求職申請。她說，這僅帶來了不到20次面試機會，至今零錄取。她過去會仔細篩選招聘信息，只申請符合自己技能的崗位——現在她什麼都投。現在就算拿到面試，她也不再抱什麼希望了。
Ms. Zhou said she had noticed an increasing number of job openings for roles around producing A.I.-generated dramas and shows. But her university degree had done little to prepare her for the way the labor market was changing because of A.I., she said. And employers, at least in Hangzhou where she was looking, seemed to be interested only in candidates who had significant experience, she said.
周女士說，她注意到越來越多關於製作人工智慧生成劇集和節目的職位空缺。她說，大學裡學的知識對她適應人工智慧帶來的就業市場變化幾乎沒什麼幫助。而且，至少在她求職的杭州，僱主似乎只對具有豐富經驗的候選人感興趣。
“There’s too many applicants, and they want someone who can get started right away,” she said. “They’re not interested in training you.”
「申請者太多了，他們想要的是能馬上上手的人，」她說。「他們沒興趣培訓你。」
A Qualifications Arms Race
學歷內卷
Though A.I. is creating some job opportunities, they are largely open to a small pool of applicants who are already skilled in the technology, said Mr. Yao, the Victoria University lecturer. At the same time, he said, junior white-collar work, such as administrative tasks, research, basic analysis and coding, are more susceptible to automation.
維多利亞大學的姚老師表示，儘管人工智慧確實創造了一些就業機會，但主要集中在少數已掌握該技術的求職者群體中。同時，行政、研究、基礎分析和編程等初級白領崗位更容易被自動化取代。
“China is especially exposed because its entry-level professional market was already highly competitive,” he added. A qualifications arms race is emerging, he said, adding that “we now see master’s degrees, and sometimes doctorates, requested for relatively junior roles.”
「中國尤其容易受到衝擊，因為其初級專業人才市場本就競爭激烈，」他補充道。一場資質軍備競賽正在出現，他說，「我們現在看到相對初級的職位也要求碩士甚至博士學位。」
For some new graduates, even a high-level degree can no longer feel enough. Luna Wang, who graduated this year with a master’s degree in management, has applied for nearly 200 jobs in Hong Kong and mainland China in fields like project management, product management and procurement planning, with little success.
對一些應屆畢業生來說，即使是高學歷也不再夠用。管理學碩士畢業生王露娜（音）在香港和中國內地申請了近200個項目管理、產品管理和採購規劃等領域的職位，但收效甚微。
Still, Ms. Wang, 25, felt relatively sanguine about the impacts of A.I. on her career prospects. She said she did not think that the technology would significantly affect her because the jobs she is applying for cannot easily be automated, as they require negotiations and face-to-face interactions.
不過，25歲的王女士對人工智慧影響其職業前景的態度相對樂觀。她說，她不認為這項技術會對她產生重大影響，因為她申請的崗位不容易被自動化，因為它們需要談判和面對面交流。
廣告
“You need to have skills that robots and A.I. can’t do to survive,” she said.
「你需要擁有機器人和人工智慧做不到的技能才能生存，」她說。
For Ms. Jin, the urban planning student, the opportunities that were available to her predecessors appeared to be vanishing. In the faltering economy, there were fewer projects for designers to work on and a lack of openings at design institutes, where many new graduates go after university, she said. And companies seemed to be automating the tasks traditionally done by interns that would help them gain skills and experience.
對城市規劃專業的金女士來說，前輩們曾擁有的機會似乎正在消失。她說，在經濟低迷的情況下，設計師可參與的項目越來越少，設計院——許多畢業生的去向——也缺乏空缺職位。而且，企業似乎正在將傳統上由實習生完成、幫助他們積累技能和經驗的工作自動化。
She saw new roles in areas like A.I. operations opening up, but found it difficult to gauge whether these would lead to a long-term career or were just the product of temporary hype. Some companies seemed to be rushing to adopt A.I. without having a clear idea of what they wanted to achieve with it, she said, describing the landscape as “very chaotic.”
她看到人工智慧運營等領域的新崗位正在出現，但很難判斷這些崗位是能帶來長期職業發展，還是僅僅是短暫炒作的結果。她說，一些公司似乎在匆忙引入人工智慧，卻沒有明確目標，形容現狀「非常混亂」。
While she keeps applying for jobs, she is trying to make use of A.I. to help her create a portfolio that she hopes will help her stand out. She hopes that the labor market’s A.I. convulsions will settle down in the next few years.
在繼續投簡歷的同時，她正嘗試利用人工智慧來幫助自己製作作品集，希望這能讓她脫穎而出。她希望就業市場的人工智慧動盪能在未來幾年平息下來。
“I think we’re going through a period of upheaval right now,” Ms. Jin said. “I just happen to have the bad luck of living through such a period.”
「我認為我們正在經歷一段劇變期，」金女士說。「我只是運氣不好，偏偏生活在這樣一個時期。」
