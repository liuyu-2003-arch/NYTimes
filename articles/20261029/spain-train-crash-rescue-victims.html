西班牙兩列火車相撞，至少68人死亡

‘A Twisted Tangle of Metal and Wreckage’: Spain Train Crash Kills at Least 68

JASON HOROWITZ, JOSÉ BAUTISTA, CARLOS BARRAGAN • 2026-10-29
Traveling home on a high-speed train from Madrid to Seville in Spain, Fernando Seco was annoyed when congestion caused the train to stop about a half-hour after its 8 a.m. departure Wednesday. Not long after, it restarted, picked up speed and slammed into another train stopped on the tracks.
週三，費爾南多·塞科乘坐從馬德里開往塞維利亞的高鐵回家。列車早上8點出發，大約半小時後因壅堵停車，這讓他很惱火。不久後，列車重新啟動，加速行駛，然後撞上了停在軌道上的另一列火車。
Mr. Seco, 62, and other panicked passengers climbed out of their carriage at the back of the train and into a field in central Spain. They saw the derailed carriages of the train they had barreled into in an embankment farther ahead. A rail beam, he said, had “pierced right through a carriage from bottom to top, like carving meat.”
62歲的塞科和其他驚慌的乘客從列車後部車廂爬出來，進入西班牙中部的一片田野。他們看到，他們撞上的那列火車脫軌的車廂躺在更前方的路堤上。他說，一根鐵軌「從下到上刺穿了一節車廂，像切肉一樣。」
He and others rushed to those carriages and found the dead and injured, those “trapped in a twisted tangle of metal and wreckage.”
他和其他人沖向那些車廂，發現死者和傷者，「被困在扭曲的金屬和殘骸中」。
The scene was eerily quiet because people were either dead or so hurt they couldn’t speak. He said survivors used parts of the destroyed train as makeshift stretchers to extract the injured from the wreckage. That’s when the screaming started. Doctors among the passengers treated people in what became a field hospital among almond groves.
現場異常安靜，因為人們要麼已經死亡，要麼傷重無法說話。他說，倖存者用損毀列車的部件作為臨時擔架，將傷者從殘骸中抬出。這時，叫喊聲開始了。乘客中的醫生在扁桃樹林間臨時搭建的野戰醫院裡救治傷員。
廣告
At least 68 people died in the crash. More than 50 others were injured, some critically.
至少68人在事故中死亡。另有50多人受傷，其中一些人傷勢危重。
Spain is living through a recurring nightmare. For the second time in less than a year, a high-speed crash between the same two train operators on the same busy line from Madrid to Seville threw the country into official days of mourning, shook its confidence in the security of it once-vaunted rail system and undercut its international image as a modernizing tourist mecca helping to thrust Europe forward.
西班牙正經歷一場反覆出現的噩夢。不到一年內，同一條從馬德里開往塞維利亞的繁忙線路上，同兩家鐵路運營商運營的高速列車第二次發生相撞事故。此次事故使該國進入為期數天的官方哀悼期，動搖了對人們曾經引以為傲的鐵路系統安全性的信心，損害了西班牙作為推動歐洲前進的現代化旅遊勝地的國際形象。
In January, a collision between two high-speed trains near Adamuz, farther south, left 46 dead in what had been the country’s worst rail disaster since 2013. The crash on Wednesday surpassed that toll and became one of the deadliest train crashes in Spanish history. And coming a month before national elections in an already bitter campaign, it heaped more pressure on a left-wing government that often seems in a state of perpetual crisis.
今年1月，兩列高鐵在更南部的阿達穆斯附近相撞，造成46人死亡，這是該國自2013年以來最嚴重的鐵路災難。週三的撞車事故死亡人數超過了那一次，成為西班牙歷史上最致命的列車事故之一。而此時距離全國選舉僅一個月，競選活動本已激烈，這給這個看似始終處於危機狀態的左翼政府帶來了更大的壓力。
“It’s not possible for a country like Spain to suffer two high-speed train accidents in one year,” Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez told reporters at the scene of the crash. Even though the two episodes seemed unrelated, they showed that Spanish rail’s security systems needed to be reinforced, he said.
「像西班牙這樣的國家，一年內發生兩起高速列車事故是不可接受的，」首相佩德羅·桑切斯在事故現場告訴記者。他說，儘管這兩起事件看似無關，但它們表明西班牙鐵路的安全系統需要加強。
As he joined Spain’s king, international leaders, his election opponent and local officials in expressing condolences to the families of the victims, he said the country would turn its focus to why the crash had occurred.
他與西班牙國王、各國領導人、他的競選對手和當地官員一起向遇難者家屬表示哀悼，同時表示，國家將把重點轉向調查事故原因。
His government had already given an explanation. Óscar Puente, Spain’s transportation minister, told reporters that the horrific crash appeared to have resulted from an “inexplicable” decision by train traffic control operators in Madrid.
他的政府已經給出解釋。西班牙交通部長奧斯卡·普恩特告訴記者，這起可怕事故似乎源於馬德里列車調度員一個「無法解釋」的決定。
廣告
After watching a reconstruction of the crash and listening to relevant audio recordings of communications between the control center and the trains involved, Mr. Puente recounted the unfolding of a national disaster.
在觀看事故重建並聽取控制中心與涉事列車之間相關通信錄音後，普恩特講述了這場國家災難的經過。
About 8:30 a.m., half an hour after the latter train — run by the national operator, Renfe — departed, the aging safety system along that section of the railway went down. Its failure forced the six trains traveling in that area to stop.
上午8點30分左右，即由國家運營商Renfe運營的後一列火車發車半小時後，該段鐵路沿線的老舊安全系統發生故障，迫使該地區行駛的六列火車停車。
Following protocol, Mr. Puente said, a train traffic control center in Madrid took over. It allowed the Renfe train to resume its journey. “Was that the correct decision?” Mr. Puente asked. He clearly did not think it was.
普恩特說，按照規程，馬德里一個列車交通控制中心接管了指揮。它允許Renfe的列車繼續行駛。「那是正確的決定嗎？」普恩特問道。他顯然認為不是。
He said one conductor of a train not involved in the crash had even corrected the operator at the control center, saying that the trains were not supposed to resume normal operation and had to proceed at low speeds of about 40 kilometers an hour (about 25 miles an hour) that would allow their drivers to stop within the visible distance ahead. Mr. Puente recounted that the Madrid operator, who was not identified, replied, “Oh, yes.”
他說，另一列未涉事列車的司機甚至糾正了控制中心的操作員，稱列車不應恢復正常運行，必須以約40公里/小時的低速行駛，以便司機能在前方可見距離內停車。普恩特敘述說，那位身份未公開的馬德里控制中心工作人員回答道：「哦，是的。」
According to Mr. Puente, the railway control operator told the Renfe train conductor to resume regular travel “despite there being another train ahead of it.” The minister called the instruction “incorrect.” The Renfe train traveled 15 miles, or about 25 kilometers, before rear-ending the other train, which had remained stopped on the tracks.
據普恩特說，鐵路控制操作員告訴Renfe列車司機恢復常規行駛，「儘管前方還有另一列火車」。部長稱這一指令「不正確」。Renfe列車行駛了25公里後追尾了另一列仍停在軌道上的火車。
Mr. Puente said the stopped train, which was run by the private company Iryo, had apparently received instructions to move on, as well. According to the Spanish newspaper El País, which on Wednesday evening obtained audio of the exchanges between the conductors and the operators in Madrid, the operator authorized the driver of the Iryo train to resume “normal speed.” But seven minutes later the Iryo driver called to report that she could not restart the train. The Madrid operators failed to tell the driver of the Renfe train, which was closing in, that it needed to brake.
普恩特說，那列停著的火車由私營公司Iryo運營，顯然也收到了繼續行駛的指令。據西班牙《國家報》報導，該報週三晚間獲得了火車司機與馬德里調度員之間的通話錄音，操作員授權Iryo列車司機恢復「正常速度」。但七分鐘後，Iryo司機打電話報告稱無法重新啟動列車。馬德里操作員未能告知正在逼近的Renfe列車司機需要剎車。
At the moment of impact, Antonio de la Corte, 77, was napping in the third carriage in the back of the Renfe train. “Bam!” he said, recalling that he was immediately awakened, lurched forward and banged his knees while his brother, with whom he was traveling, hit his head. A rail shot through the inside of his train car like a skewer, killing people seated to his right, he said.
撞擊發生時，77歲的安東尼奧·拽哥爾特正在Renfe列車後部第三節車廂裡打盹。「砰！」他回憶說，他立刻被驚醒，身體前傾，撞到了膝蓋，與他同行的兄弟撞到了頭。他說，一根鐵軌像串肉簽一樣刺穿了他所在的車廂，奪走了他右側座位上乘客的生命。
As he and his brother climbed down to the field, farmhands cut the fences around the embankment so passengers could take shelter in the fields of alfalfa and almond trees. Word of the disaster quickly spread, and Yahadi Mohamed Maulud, 41, a resident in the nearby town of Yébenes, loaded his car with water bottles and rushed to help.
當他和兄弟爬下路堤進入田野時，農場工人剪開了路堤周圍的圍欄，讓乘客可以在苜蓿田和扁桃樹林中避難。災難的消息迅速傳開，附近耶韋內斯鎮41歲的居民亞哈迪·穆罕默德·毛盧德把水瓶裝上車，趕去幫忙。
“We found a massacre,” he said, describing torn limbs and one woman, drenched in blood, searching for her brother. He said he was one of the first people on the scene and helped move the injured passengers on makeshift stretchers.
「我們看到了一場大災難，」他描述，現場有斷肢，一名渾身是血的女人正在尋找她的兄弟。他說自己是最早到達現場的人之一，協助將受傷的乘客用臨時擔架抬走。
廣告
Soon, a long procession of ambulances, police cars and fire trucks arrived under the whir of rescue helicopters flying low overhead. Dozens of Civil Guard officers, many wearing the white protective suits and masks used by forensic investigators, inspected the scene and gathered evidence.
很快，一長串救護車、警車和消防車在低空飛行的救援直升機轟鳴聲中抵達。數十名國民警衛隊官員——許多人穿著法醫調查人員使用的白色防護服，戴著口罩——檢查現場並收集證據。
Broken cables and fallen utility poles were scattered around. The derailed carriages looked pulverized, a heap of charred wheels, smoldering metal and crushed luggage. Some workers hovered over still bodies covered in Mylar blankets as firefighters used cranes to lift the wreckage in search of survivors and more dead.
斷裂的電纜和倒下的電線杆散落四周。脫軌的車廂看起來支離破碎，只剩下成堆燒焦的車輪、冒著煙的金屬殘骸和被壓扁的行李。一些工作人員俯身在蓋著聚酯薄膜應急毯的屍體上，消防員用起重機吊起殘骸，尋找倖存者和更多遇難者。
As family members and friends rushed to sports pavilions to which survivors, including Mr. Seco, had been evacuated in Urda, a group of Roman Catholic priests approached the crash site.
親友們趕往烏爾達的體育場館——包括塞科在內的倖存者已被疏散到那裡——一群羅馬天主教神父來到事故現場。
“We came to pray for the dead,” said the Rev. Luis Lucendo, 63, a parish priest in the nearby town of Villacañas. “One man told us that his wife had died, and I was able to make the sign of the cross over her,” he said.
「我們來為死者祈禱，」附近比利亞卡尼亞斯鎮的63歲教區神父路易斯·盧森多說。「一名男子告訴我們他的妻子去世了，我得以在她身上畫十字，」他說。
Mr. Maulud, who had brought water and other supplies, was also in Urda, watching the scenes of both thankful reconciliation and grief behind his cigarette smoke. He frequently took the train himself for business in Madrid, he noted.
毛盧德帶來了水和其他物資，他也在烏爾達透過香煙煙霧目睹了現場既充滿感恩與和解，又瀰漫著悲痛的景象。他說，他自己也經常坐火車去馬德里出差。
Then he added, “I’m never getting on that train again.”
然後他說：「我再也不會坐那趟火車了。」

Jason Horowitz是《紐約時報》馬德里分社社長，報導西班牙、葡萄牙以及歐洲各地人們的生活方式。
