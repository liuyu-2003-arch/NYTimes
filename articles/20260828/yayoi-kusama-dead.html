「波點女王」草間彌生逝世：拓展藝術疆界的波普先鋒

Yayoi Kusama, Pop Artist Who Expanded and Endured, Dies at 97

HOLLAND COTTER • 2026-08-28
Yayoi Kusama, a Japanese artist whose polka-dot-covered paintings, phallic sculptures and public performances caused a stir in New York City and Europe in the 1960s, and whose mirrored “Infinity Room” installations became selfie sensations and blockbuster museum and gallery attractions in recent decades, died on Aug. 14 in Tokyo. She was 97.
上世紀60年代，日本藝術家草間彌生以布滿波點圖案的繪畫、陽具主題的雕塑和公共行為藝術在紐約和歐洲引發轟動；近幾十年來，她的鏡面裝置「無限鏡屋」成為自拍的熱門背景以及博物館和畫廊的重磅吸睛展品。她於8月14日在東京去世，享年97歲。
The Yayoi Kusama Museum and the Yayoi Kusama Foundation announced her death, at a hospital, on Thursday.
草間彌生美術館和草間彌生基金會週四宣布了她在醫院去世的消息。
Ms. Kusama first gained attention in the late 1950s and early ’60s with immense abstract paintings that she called “Infinity Nets.” They were composed of countless small, curving strokes of paint on all-white or all-black surfaces, suggesting a giant, swirling mesh pierced with pinprick holes.
在上世紀50年代末至60年代初，草間彌生憑藉一系列巨幅抽象繪畫初獲矚目。她將這些作品稱為「無限網」。畫面以無數細小的彎曲筆觸組成，鋪滿全白或全黑的表面，猶如一張巨大的漩渦狀網格，布滿針尖般的小孔。
Far larger in scale than the Abstract Expressionist works that were then dominating New York galleries, her pieces quickly attracted the notice of young artists who were trying to take American art in new directions. One was Donald Judd, who was working at the time as an art critic and formulating what would become Minimalism. He bought a painting, helped out in her studio and began championing her work.
她作品的尺幅遠超當時主導紐約畫廊的抽象表現主義作品，迅速吸引了那些試圖將美國藝術帶至新方向的年輕藝術家的注意。其中包括當時擔任藝術評論家、正醞釀後來成為極簡主義藝術理念的唐納德·賈德。他購買了她的一幅畫，還在她的工作室幫過忙，並開始積極為她的作品奔走。
Creating art incrementally from repeated components became Ms. Kusama’s mode of working, and she applied it to sculpture as well. In the early 1960s, she began sewing thousands of stuffed fabric phalluses, painting them white or gold and gluing them onto the surfaces of everyday objects — tables, armchairs, coats, shoes, a baby carriage, a rowboat — to create bristling, room-filling installations.
用重複元素累積成作品成為草間彌生的創作方式，她也將此應用於雕塑。20世紀60年代初，她開始縫製數以千計的布制陽具填充物，將其塗成白色或金色，粘貼在桌子、扶手椅、外套、鞋子、嬰兒車、划艇等日常物品表面，由此製作出密密麻麻、布滿整個房間的裝置作品。
These “Accumulation Sculptures” made their debut, along with work by Claes Oldenburg, James Rosenquist and Andy Warhol, at a group show at the Green Gallery in New York in 1962. It was among the first exhibitions of Pop Art, and with it, Ms. Kusama was recognized as a pioneer in the two major avant-garde styles of the day, Pop and Minimalism.
1962年，這些「聚積雕塑」與克拉斯·奧登伯格、詹姆斯·羅森奎斯特和安迪·沃荷的作品一起在紐約格林畫廊的群展中首次亮相。這是波普藝術的早期展覽之一，草間彌生也因此被公認為當時兩大前衛藝術風格——波普藝術和極簡主義——的先驅。
Yayoi Kusama was born on March 22, 1929, in the city of Matsumoto, northwest of Tokyo. She was the youngest of four children in a prosperous middle-class family. Her father, Kamon Kusama, and mother, Shigeru Kusama, helped oversee the family’s wholesale seed business.
草間彌生1929年3月22日出生在位於東京西北方向的松本市。她出身於一個富裕中產家庭，是四個孩子中最小的一個。父親草間嘉門和母親草間茂一起打理家族的種子批發生意。
廣告
As a teenager, Yayoi aspired to become a professional artist, an unusual ambition for a woman in conservative postwar Japan. In 1948, over the objections of her parents, she enrolled in art school in Kyoto and studied Nihonga painting, a style that merged Japanese and Western traditions.
少女時代，草間彌生便立志成為一名職業藝術家。在戰後保守的日本，這對一個女性而言是頗為不尋常的志向。1948年，不顧父母反對，她進入京都的一所藝術學校學習日本畫，這種繪畫融合了日本與西方的傳統。
But chafing at academic discipline and the style’s association with wartime nationalism, she soon went her own way. Immersing herself in reports of Surrealist-inspired art in Paris and New York, and keeping a close eye on avant-garde developments in Japan, Ms. Kusama began producing hundreds of semiabstract paintings that set floral, astronomical and sexual forms against fields of dots and spiky lines.
但她很快厭倦了學院派規訓，以及這種畫風與戰時民族主義之間的聯繫，於是走上了自己的道路。她沉浸於巴黎和紐約超現實主義藝術思潮的報導中，同時密切關注日本國內的先鋒藝術發展，開始創作數百幅半抽象繪畫：將花卉、天文和性愛形態置於圓點和尖刺狀線條的背景之上。
She began exhibiting in her teens, and gaining attention. In 1955, three of her paintings were sent to New York to be included in an international watercolor exhibition at the Brooklyn Museum, and she began to make plans to move to the United States.
她十幾歲時就開始參展，並逐漸受到關注。1955年，她有三幅畫被送往紐約，參加布魯克林博物館舉辦的國際水彩畫展；與此同時，她開始籌劃移居美國。
In need of advice on how to establish a career there, she wrote to Georgia O’Keeffe — the only female American artist she knew of — after tracking down her address in New Mexico through the United States Embassy.
為了尋求在美國建立藝術生涯的建議，她寫信給喬治亞·歐姬芙——她所知道的唯一一位美國女藝術家。她通過美國大使館找到了歐姬芙在新墨西哥州的地址。
Ms. O’Keeffe replied with encouragement, and in 1957 Ms. Kusama flew to Seattle, where she spent about six months before moving to New York City.
歐姬芙回信鼓勵她。1957年，草間彌生飛往西雅圖，在那裡待了大約半年後，搬到了紐約。
The allure of her personal presence increasingly became a component of her art. She was aware that as an Asian woman in New York she was seen as an exotic outsider in the city’s art world, an identity that had useful marketing potential. Calibrated orientalism became the substance of some of the public performance work she developed in the mid- to late 1960s, when she aligned herself with the flourishing hippie culture.
她的個人魅力日漸成為其藝術的一部分。她意識到，作為紐約的亞洲女性，她在當地的藝術圈被視為一個帶有異國情調的局外人，這種身份具有營銷潛力。在20世紀60年代中後期，她將自己融入當時蓬勃發展的嬉皮士文化，精心設計的東方主義也成為其部分公共行為藝術創作的核心。
In a series of anti-authoritarian and specifically anti-Vietnam War happenings starting in 1966, Ms. Kusama made guerrilla appearances in strategic locations in New York City — on Wall Street, at the Statue of Liberty and on the Brooklyn Bridge — leading a group of volunteers. On her cue, they would strip down and pose as she painted their naked bodies with polka dots, to which she sometimes attributed magical properties.
從1966年開始，草間彌生創作了一系列反權威、尤其是反越戰的行為藝術，她帶領一群志願者，在紐約一些具有象徵意義的地點——華爾街、自由女神像和布魯克林大橋——突然現身。在她的示意下，他們會脫光衣服擺出姿勢，她則在他們赤裸的身體上畫滿波點；她有時會聲稱這些圓點具有某種魔力。
“With those polka dots,” she said, “the people will self-obliterate and return to the nature of the universe.”
她說：「有了這些波點，人們就會自我消失，回歸宇宙的自然狀態。」
She herself remained fully clothed, although she often wore a dot-covered costume.
她自己始終穿著整齊，不過經常身穿布滿波點的服裝。
廣告
These events, which she called Body Festivals and Anatomic Explosions, had pragmatic dimensions. The display of bare flesh was calculated to grab public attention; if the police showed up, all the better. Controversy was valuable for promoting Ms. Kusama’s extra-aesthetic activities, which included a pop-up boutique, Kusama’s Korner, at Bloomingdale’s and a gay bar called Kusama’s Omophile Klub.
這些被她稱為「身體節慶」和「解剖爆炸」的活動也有非常現實的考量。展示裸露的身體是為了吸引公眾注意；如果警察出現，那效果就更好了。爭議對於宣傳草間彌生那些超越純藝術範疇的活動很有價值，其中包括在布魯明戴爾百貨開設快閃精品店「草間屋」，以及一家名為「草間彌生的同性相吸俱樂部」的同性戀酒吧。
All of this represented success, for a while. But trends were fleeting in that chaotic time, and as the flower-child 1960s gave way to the grimmer 1970s, Ms. Kusama’s art spectacles began to look old. Sensing that Americans had lost interest in her, she returned to Japan in 1973.
這一切都帶來了成功，但只是暫時的。在那個混亂的年代，潮流轉瞬即逝；當那個嬉皮士文化盛行的60年代漸漸退去，取而代之的是更為陰鬱的70年代，草間彌生的藝術奇觀開始顯得過時。察覺到美國人對她失去了興趣後，她於1973年返回日本。
In Tokyo, to support herself, she sold modern and contemporary Western art until the mid-1970s oil crisis ended the Japanese economic boom and forced her to find other sources of income. She began making collages inspired by the American artist Joseph Cornell, with whom she had earlier formed an intense but platonic relationship.
回到東京後，為了維持生計，她一直從事西方現代及當代藝術品的買賣，直到70年代中期的石油危機終結日本的經濟繁榮，迫使她尋找其他收入來源。她開始創作受美國藝術家約瑟夫·康乃爾啟發的拼貼畫。此前，她與康乃爾曾有過一段強烈卻柏拉圖式的感情關係。
She also began writing fiction, publishing more than a dozen novels — some of them, like “Manhattan Suicide Addict,” autobiographical — and wrote several volumes of poetry. But all of that activity was not enough to stave off her panic attacks and hallucinations, which became acute. In 1977, she admitted herself to the Seiwa psychiatric hospital in Tokyo. She would eventually make it her permanent home, building a studio nearby.
她還開始寫小說，出版了十多部作品，其中一些如《曼哈頓自殺癮君子》帶有自傳性質；她還出版了數卷詩集。然而，所有這些活動都不足以遏制她日益嚴重的驚恐發作和幻覺。1977年，她主動入住東京的晴和精神病院。最終，她把那裡變成了自己的永久住所，並在附近建起了工作室。
With the emotional security that the hospital environment afforded, she continued to work at high volume, producing brightly colored paintings, some with patterns suggesting capillary webs or swarms of spermatozoa, others incorporating figures and flowers and recalling her Surrealist-tinged work of the 1950s.
醫院環境給她帶來了情感上的安全感，她得以繼續高強度創作，繪製了許多色彩鮮艷的繪畫。有些作品中的圖案像毛細血管交織成的網路，或成群的精子；另一些則融入人物和花朵，讓人聯想到她在50年代帶有超現實主義色彩的作品。
In the late 1990s and early 2000s, she returned to creating encompassing installations for the first time in three decades: rooms covered entirely in polka dots and mirrored “Infinity Rooms.”
20世紀90年代末至21世紀初，她重新開始創作大型沉浸式裝置，這是她時隔30年再次涉足這一領域：整個房間被波點覆蓋，或布滿鏡面的「無限鏡屋」。
Her persistent efforts to establish herself and her art as an international brand paid off. In recent years, as Japanese contemporary art and popular culture have gained global attention, interest in her work has soared, along with its prices — a phenomenon she called “Kusamania.”
她堅持不懈地將自己和自己的藝術打造為國際品牌的努力，最終得到了回報。近年來，隨著日本當代藝術和流行文化在全球受到越來越多的關注，人們對她作品的興趣也急劇上升，其價格隨之飆漲——她將這種現象稱為「草間狂熱」。
廣告
When one of her 1960 “Infinity Net” paintings sold for $7.1 million at Christie’s in 2014, it was the highest-priced work by a living female artist ever bought at auction.
2014年，她1960年創作的一幅「無限網」畫作在佳士得以710萬美元成交，創下當時在世女性藝術家作品的最高拍賣紀錄。
Ms. Kusama, who was represented by David Zwirner Gallery in New York, also made lucrative agreements to endorse commercial products, including cosmetics and cars. With her dot-patterned wardrobe and fluorescent pageboy wigs, she was an instantly recognizable pop star, invited to contribute to international art shows and given shows of her own, often of new work produced with a platoon of assistants.
由紐約的卓納畫廊代理的草間彌生還簽下利潤豐厚的商業代言協議，產品包括化妝品和汽車。憑藉波點圖案的衣服和熒光色的齊耳短假髮，她是一個極具辨認度的流行明星，受邀參與國際藝術展並舉辦個展，而展出的新作往往是在一大批助手的協助下完成的。
One museum show, “Love Forever: Yayoi Kusama 1958-1968,” organized by the Los Angeles County Museum of Art and the Japan Foundation in 1998, which traveled to the Museum of Modern Art, celebrated her early years. In 2011-12, a larger retrospective was held at the Tate Modern in Britain, the Whitney Museum of American Art in New York, the Centre Pompidou in Paris and the Reina Sofía in Madrid. In 2017, a major survey of her work was organized by the Hirshhorn Museum in Washington. The same year, a Yayoi Kusama Museum opened in Tokyo.
1998年，由洛杉磯郡立藝術博物館和日本基金會聯合策劃的展覽《愛永恆：草間彌生1958—1968》巡迴至紐約現代藝術博物館，重點回顧了她早期的創作生涯。2011年至2012年，一場規模更大的回顧展先後在英國泰特現代美術館、紐約惠特尼美國藝術博物館、巴黎蓬皮杜中心和馬德里的索菲婭王后國家藝術中心博物館舉行。2017年，華盛頓赫希洪博物館策划了一場大型草間彌生作品回顧展。同年，東京的草間彌生美術館開館。
Also in 2017, an article in Vice magazine drew attention to repeated racist comments — specifically, racist descriptions of Black people — that Ms. Kusama had made in her autobiography. In 2023, on the occasion of a solo exhibition at the San Francisco Museum of Modern Art, the accusations resurfaced and Ms. Kusama publicly apologized.
同樣在2017年，《Vice》雜誌的一篇文章引發關注，文章指出草間彌生在其自傳中多次發表種族歧視言論，特別是對黑人的種族主義描述。2023年，她在舊金山現代藝術博物館舉辦個展之際，這些指控再次浮出水面，草間彌生隨後公開道歉。
Iterations of Ms. Kusama’s poetic, endlessly reflective “Infinity Room” — a mirror-lined chamber housing an intricately twinkling LED display — have been box-office gold for museums and galleries, as part of larger exhibitions and on their own. They have drawn enormous crowds, with visitors lining up for hours and entering one at a time for 45 seconds each, just long enough to take a selfie, an exercise in aestheticized narcissism that the artist promoted.
草間彌生那充滿詩意、無窮反射的「無限鏡屋」——這種房間四壁鋪滿鏡子，內部裝有精心布置、閃爍不停的LED燈光裝置——不斷以各種形式出現，已經成為博物館和畫廊的「票房金礦」，它們既可以作為大型展覽的一部分，也可以單獨展出。「無限鏡屋」吸引了龐大的人流，參觀者往往排隊數小時，每次只有一個人進入，停留45秒——時間剛好夠拍一張自拍。這是一種由藝術家本人所推動的將自戀審美化的行為。
Japan, though slow in coming to appreciate Ms. Kusama, has also honored her with exhibitions. She represented her home country at the 1993 Venice Biennale. (She also took part in the 1966 Biennale, representing herself.) In 2006, she was awarded the Praemium Imperiale for painting, an international prize sponsored by Japan’s imperial family on behalf of the Japan Art Association.
日本雖然較晚才開始欣賞草間彌生，但最終也通過各種展覽向她致敬。1993年，她代表日本參加威尼斯雙年展。（1966年她也參加過該雙年展，不過當時代表的是她自己。）2006年，她獲得繪畫領域的高松宮殿下紀念世界文化獎，這是由日本藝術協會主辦、日本皇室贊助的國際獎項。
Information on her survivors was not immediately available.
目前尚無其在世親屬的信息。
