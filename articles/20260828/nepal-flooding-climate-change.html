氣候變化加劇喜馬拉雅緻命災害風險

Climate Change Raises Risk of Disasters Like Nepal Floods

MAXINE JOSELOW, LEO SANDS • 2026-08-28
Climate change is heating up the Himalayas and supercharging the risk of disasters like the deadly flash flooding in Nepal and Tibet, though scientists are still determining the exact cause of this week’s catastrophe.
氣候變化正使喜馬拉雅地區升溫，大幅增加類似尼泊爾和中國西藏地區此次致命山洪的災害風險，不過科學家們仍在確定本週這場災難的確切原因。
A group of glaciologists and geologists said on Thursday that the flooding was probably unleashed after an enormous chunk of bedrock collapsed underneath a glacier, triggering a landslide high in the mountains. At the same time, a chunk of ice snapped off the glacier itself and cascaded thousands of feet to the valley below, the scientists said.
一組冰川學家和地質學家週四表示，此次洪水很可能是由冰川下一大塊基岩坍塌，在高山上引發滑坡所致。科學家還表示，與此同時，一塊冰體從冰川脫落，沿著山坡傾瀉數千英尺墜入下方山谷。
As humans warm the planet by burning fossil fuels, the Himalayas are losing their permafrost, a mixture of soil, rocks and organic matter that has remained frozen for thousands of years. The thawing permafrost is destabilizing the foundation underneath glaciers, raising the risks of precisely these kinds of landslides and glacial collapses.
隨著人類燃燒化石燃料導致全球暖化，喜馬拉雅地區正在失去永久凍土——一種由土壤、岩石和有機物混合而成、已凍結數千年的物質。永久凍土融化正使冰川底部的基礎變得不穩定，從而增加了此類滑坡和冰川崩塌的風險。
Data shows that glacial failures — the scientific term for a category of events that includes ice avalanches, glacier detachments and rock-ice avalanches — have happened dozens of times since 1900 in the China-Nepal border region that was the site of this week’s devastation.
數據顯示，自1900年以來，在本週受災的中尼邊境地區已發生數十次冰川潰決（一個涵蓋冰崩、冰川脫落和岩冰崩的科學術語）。
廣告
“Permafrost has been the cryospheric glue that’s held the rock and the ice and the glaciers together for millennia, and now we’re getting increasing evidence that it’s being weakened by warming trends,” said Alton C. Byers, a senior research associate at the Institute of Arctic and Alpine Research at the University of Colorado at Boulder.
「永久凍土幾千年來一直是把岩石、冰和冰川粘合在一起的冰凍膠水，而現在越來越多的證據表明，它正因變暖趨勢遭到削弱，」科羅拉多大學博爾德分校北極與高山研究所高級研究員奧爾頓·C·拜爾斯說。
Dr. Byers, who plans to travel to Nepal next month to meet with communities confronting flood risks, suggested a new name for the phenomenon: “not-so-permanent permafrost.”
拜爾斯計劃下個月前往尼泊爾，與面臨洪水風險的社區會面，他為這一現象提出了一個新名稱：「並不永久的永久凍土」。
The landslide and glacial collapse took place on the north face of Langtang Lirung, a mountain in northern Nepal near the border with Tibet, at an elevation of around 17,000 feet. Millions of metric tons of rock and ice fell, pulverizing into mud and water as the torrent surged down the mountainside.
山體滑坡和冰川崩塌發生在朗唐裡隆峰北麓海拔約5200米的地方，這座山峰位於尼泊爾北部靠近西藏的邊境。數百萬噸岩石和冰塊墜落，在洪流衝下山坡的過程中粉碎成泥漿和水。
Ashim Sattar, an assistant professor at the Indian Institute of Technology Bhubaneswar who studies Himalayan glaciers, said that while it was too early to rule out other factors, warming temperatures caused by climate change may have destabilized the mountain slope.
布巴內什瓦爾印度理工學院助理教授、研究喜馬拉雅冰川的阿希姆·薩塔爾表示，雖然現在排除其他因素為時過早，但氣候變化導致的升溫可能已經使山坡變得不穩定。
“As the glaciers retreat and the permafrost melts, we see that these slopes become more susceptible to failure,” Dr. Sattar said, adding, “There’s a very straight link between climate change and these types of disasters.”
「隨著冰川退化和永久凍土融化，我們看到這些山坡變得更容易失穩，」薩塔爾說，並且「氣候變化與這類災害之間存在非常直接的聯繫」。
Still, scientists cautioned that climate change was not the sole, immediate factor behind the flooding, which has killed hundreds of people and left more than 1,400 missing. They noted that teams of researchers were still studying a wide set of data from satellite imagery and other sources, which could take months.
儘管如此，科學家們提醒說，氣候變化並非此次造成數百人死亡、1400多人失蹤的洪水的唯一直接因素。他們指出，研究團隊仍在研究來自衛星圖像和其他來源的大量數據，這可能需要數月時間。
廣告
“I don’t think we can simply say this is as a result of climate change,” said Stuart Dunning, a professor of applied geomorphology at Newcastle University in England. “It’s likely a whole variety of factors that are long-term, along with possible short-term things like a strong snow melt component in the last few days.”
「我認為我們不能簡單地說這是氣候變化的結果，」英國紐卡斯爾大學應用地貌學教授斯圖爾特·鄧寧說。「這很可能是多種長期因素造成的，同時還可能有短期因素，比如過去幾天的強融雪成分。」
Dr. Byers said that thawing permafrost was a relatively new concept for some residents of flood-prone communities in northern Nepal. He said residents and researchers alike have tended to focus on a related risk: Melting glaciers are creating unstable lakes all across the Himalayas.
拜爾斯說，對於尼泊爾北部易發洪水社區的居民來說，永久凍土融化是一個相對較新的概念。他說，居民和研究人員都傾向於關注一個相關的風險：冰川融化正在整個喜馬拉雅地區形成不穩定的湖泊。
When an avalanche or earthquake causes falling rocks or snow to land on these lakes, it can set off a flood that engulfs everything in its path, including entire villages, tourist lodges and hydropower plants. The phenomenon is known as a glacial lake outburst flood, or GLOF, and the result can be similar to this week’s tragedy.
當雪崩或地震導致岩石或積雪落入這些湖泊時，就可能引發洪水，吞噬沿途的一切，包括整個村莊、遊客客棧和水電站。這種現象被稱為冰湖潰決洪水，其結果可能與本週的悲劇類似。
At the outset of the flooding on Wednesday, some scientists speculated that such an outburst had caused the destruction. But initial satellite data appears to show that an overflowing glacial lake was not involved, said Umesh K. Haritashya, the director of the sustainability program at the University of Dayton.
在週三洪水發生之初，一些科學家推測可能是這種潰決導致了破壞。但初步衛星數據似乎顯示，此次事件不涉及冰湖泛濫，代頓大學可持續發展項目主任烏梅什·K·哈利塔什亞說。
“There is common confusion about glacial lakes whenever something like this happens in this area,” Dr. Haritashya said. “But there are other hazards in these mountains that, going forward, scientists and policymakers need to be looking at carefully after these recent events.”
「每當該地區發生此類事件時，人們通常會對冰湖產生誤解，」哈利塔什亞說。「但這些山區還存在其他危險，在近期這些事件之後，科學家和政策制定者需要仔細研究。」
Nepal’s Department of Hydrology and Meteorology operates a network of sensors and gauges that monitor the water levels of some glacial lakes and connected rivers. When the network detects a potential outburst, officials alert downstream communities.
尼泊爾水文氣象局運營著一個傳感和測量儀器網路，用於監測部分冰湖及其相連河流的水位。當該網路檢測到潛在的潰決時，官員們會向下游社區發出警報。
廣告
But Austin Lord, a senior fellow in the Energy, Water and Sustainability program at the Stimson Center, a nonprofit research organization, said that this early warning system was not designed to detect this week’s flash flooding.
但非營利研究機構斯廷森中心能源、水資源與可持續發展項目高級研究員奧斯汀·洛德表示，這個預警系統並非為檢測本週這種山洪而設計。
“This type of flash flooding, because of the speed at which it moves, means that kind of conventional system is ill-equipped,” he said.
「這類山洪由於移動速度極快，意味著傳統系統根本無法應對，」他說。
