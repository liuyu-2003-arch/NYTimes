時報創刊175年：1500萬篇報導講述的美國故事

A.G. Sulzberger: The Story of America, Told in 15 Million First Drafts of History

A.G. SULZBERGER • 2026-09-21
Above my desk hangs an engraving of an island. It depicts a still-young city in a still-young nation. Its cobblestone streets are lined with squat buildings, none taller than a church spire. No bridges or tunnels in sight, schooners and paddle-steam ferries crowd a shoreline bustling with working piers. This is New York City in 1851.
我的辦公桌上方掛著一幅版畫，描繪的是一座島嶼。畫中呈現的是一個仍然年輕的國家裡一座仍然年輕的城市。鵝卵石街道兩旁是一座座低矮的建築，沒有一座高過教堂的尖頂。放眼望去，既無橋樑也無隧道，縱帆船與明輪蒸汽渡輪擠滿岸邊，碼頭上一片繁忙。這是1851年的紐約。
On Sept. 18 of that year, at the south end of the island, on Nassau Street, the newspaper we know today as The New York Times published its first issue. Put together in unfinished lofts by candlelight, it consisted of four dense, colorless pages of text. It sold for a penny.
那年9月18日，在該島最南端的拿騷街上，一份日後我們稱之為《紐約時報》的報紙發行了它的創刊號。它是在尚未完工的閣樓裡藉著燭光排印而成，總共四個版，密密麻麻、灰撲撲的全是字。售價一便士。
On Friday, The New York Times turns 175. Over the past several months, I’ve found myself drawn to that engraving on my office wall. How unlikely it is that a newspaper born before indoor electricity — let alone cars, radio, telephones, planes and computers — is still here. And how fortunate The Times is to have witnessed and chronicled and interrogated so much history in all those years.
週五，《紐約時報》迎來175歲生日。過去幾個月，我發現自己總被辦公室牆上那幅版畫吸引。這份報紙誕生於室內尚未通電的時代——更遑論汽車、廣播、電話、飛機和電腦——竟能延續至今，這是多麼難得的事。而《紐約時報》能在這漫長歲月中見證、記錄並探究如此多的歷史，又是多麼幸運。
At The Times, we believe the job of a newspaper is to seek the truth, to help people understand the world and to hold the powerful to account. This means equipping people with the information they need to make decisions. It means bonding our diverse communities with a fuller understanding of the lives, hopes and concerns of our neighbors. It means asking tough questions of government and business leaders on the public’s behalf. It means doing all this with an ironclad commitment to fairness, accuracy and independence — delivering the news, as our longstanding credo puts it, “impartially, without fear or favor.”
在《紐約時報》，我們相信報紙的使命在於追尋真相，幫助人們理解這個世界，並讓掌權者承擔責任。這意味著為人們提供做出決定所需的信息；意味著讓多元社群更充分地理解鄰裡的生活、期望與關切，從而連接彼此；意味著代表公眾向政府和商界領袖提出尖銳問題；意味著以對公平、準確和獨立的堅定承諾來做這一切——正如我們那句由來已久的信條所言，報導新聞要「不偏不倚、無所畏懼、不徇私情」。
廣告
It’s fitting therefore that the 175th anniversary of The Times arrives as we celebrate a bigger milestone: America’s 250th. Truth, understanding and accountability are essential to a strong nation and a healthy democracy.
因此，當我們迎來一個更為重大的里程碑——美國建國250週年之際，《紐約時報》也迎來創刊175週年，這再合適不過了。真相、理解與問責，正是一個強大國家和一個健康民主體制不可或缺的基石。
Through 61,011 editions and some 15 million articles, the pages of The Times tell the story of America’s remarkable growth from an upstart nation of 24 million people to a global superpower of nearly 350 million. The pages announce the arrival of new technologies and track the upheavals that followed. They chronicle battles for equal rights and the expansion of who is recognized as an American. They reveal the damage inflicted by wars, economic busts and moments of tragedy — and document the triumphs, economic booms and can-do resilience of a country that usually emerges stronger.
透過61011期報紙和約1500萬篇報導，《紐約時報》的版面講述了美國從一個人口2400萬的新興國家成長為擁有近3.5億人口的全球超級大國的非凡歷程。這些版面宣告新技術的到來，並追蹤隨之而來的劇變。它們記錄了爭取平等權利的鬥爭，以及「誰被視為美國人」這一範圍的不斷擴大。它們揭示了戰爭、經濟蕭條和悲劇時刻造成的破壞，也記錄這個國家所迎來的勝利、經濟繁榮與百折不撓的進取精神——而這個國家往往也會因此變得更加強大。
Taken together, they’re the first draft of American history. A new volume is added daily, a report so robust and wide-ranging that, on average, it could fill a 1,000-page book. The primary authors of this work are the reporters we’ve sent out to witness the world and to share what they have seen — not just establishing the facts but also wrapping them in context and explaining why they matter.
綜合起來看，這些文字就是美國歷史的初稿。每天都會新增一卷，內容如此豐富詳實、包羅萬象，平均而言足以填滿一本1000頁的書。這部作品的主要執筆者正是我們派往世界各地、見證世界並分享所見所聞的記者——他們不僅僅確認事實本身，更是將事實置於背景中，並解釋這些事實為何重要。
We were there when the first shots of the Civil War rang out at Fort Sumter — our correspondent was promptly arrested as a Union spy — and then through the years of battles that followed, some of which President Abraham Lincoln’s war secretary read about in our pages before the generals could send word of them.
南北戰爭的第一槍在薩姆特堡打響時，我們就在現場——我們的通訊員隨即被當作聯邦間諜逮捕。此後數年，戰事連綿，而我們始終在場。有些時候，相比比將軍們送來的戰情通報，林肯總統的戰爭部長甚至是先讀到了我們的報導。
In the Roaring Twenties, our Wall Street reporting warned of recklessness in the markets, upsetting powerful financiers. After the country toppled into the Great Depression, a leading banker declared, “Our American community would have been better off today if it could have heeded that voice.”
在「咆哮的二十年代」，《紐約時報》對華爾街的報導曾對金融市場上的冒險行為發出警告，惹惱了有權有勢的金融家。美國陷入大蕭條後，一位銀行業巨頭感嘆道：「如果當初美國社會能夠聽進去那個聲音，我們今天的處境會好得多。」
Our correspondents braved the battlefields of World War II; some lost their lives and others were taken prisoner in Germany, Italy and Japan. The Times, in turn, used its scarce government-rationed paper supplies to ensure all these reports reached the American public, turning away advertisements in the process.
第二次世界大戰期間，我們的記者們勇赴戰場；一些人為此獻出了生命，還有一些人在德國、義大利和日本淪為戰俘。與此同時，《紐約時報》用政府配給的緊缺紙張確保這些戰地報導能夠傳遞給美國公眾，為此甚至不惜放棄廣告。
廣告
In one of the darkest moments of the civil rights movement, one of our reporters was in Memphis at the Lorraine Motel when an assassin shot Martin Luther King Jr. “Write. Write, write down everything you see,” he told himself as he took in the scene. “Get it all down.”
在民權運動最黑暗的時刻之一，我們的一位記者當時正在孟菲斯的洛林汽車旅館，親眼目睹刺客槍殺了馬丁·路德·金。「寫下來。寫，把所有你看到的都寫下來，」他一邊觀察現場，一邊對自己說。「全都記下來。」
And 25 years ago this month, when others fled the collapse of the twin towers, our reporters charged toward the burning rubble. Over the next two decades, our journalists lived in Afghanistan and Iraq full time to tell the stories of the wars that followed. We continue to report from both countries today. If we don’t document the lessons and consequences of America’s longest wars, how will the country learn from them?
25年前的這個月，當雙子塔轟然倒塌、旁人紛紛逃離之際，我們的記者卻徑直衝向那片燃燒的廢墟。此後20年間，我們的記者長期駐紮在阿富汗與伊拉克，報導隨後發生的戰爭。時至今日，我們仍在這兩個國家堅持報導。倘若我們不去記錄美國歷史上這些最漫長的戰爭所留下的教訓與後果，這個國家又該如何從中汲取教訓？
Although our commitment to the highest journalistic standards is unsurpassed, we’ve also made mistakes, both big and small, along the way. When we err, we strive to be transparent with our audience about what went wrong and push ourselves to do better.
儘管我們對最高新聞標準的堅守無人能及，但一路走來，我們也犯過或大或小的錯誤。每當出錯，我們都致力於坦誠面對讀者，說明問題出在哪裡，並鞭策自己做得更好。
This is what it means to be the country’s paper of record: a great and imperfect newspaper steadfastly chronicling a great and imperfect nation.
這就是作為這個國家歷史記錄者的意義所在：一份偉大卻並不完美的報紙，始終如一地記錄著一個偉大卻並不完美的國家。
The Times, and the model of independent reporting we have championed, would not have endured were it not for the abiding commitment of the United States to free inquiry and free expression. America’s bold experiment with democracy, likewise, would have stumbled long ago were it not for news organizations exercising their rights under the First Amendment. Self-governance requires citizens to have access to reliable sources of information to make informed choices and know when their leaders aren’t acting in their best interests. This is why the press is the only profession explicitly protected in the Constitution. It is, as the founders believed, “one of the great bulwarks of liberty.”
如果沒有美國對自由探究和自由表達的堅定支持，《紐約時報》以及我們所倡導的獨立報導模式不可能延續至今。而如果沒有新聞機構行使第一修正案賦予的權利，美國民主的大膽實驗也早已步履蹣跚。自治要求公民能夠獲得可靠的信息來源，從而做出知情選擇，並知道他們的領導人何時沒有以他們的最佳利益行事。這就是為什麼新聞業是憲法中唯一被明確保護的職業。正如開國元勛們所相信的那樣，它是「自由的偉大堡壘之一」。
Today, the future of America’s free press is uncertain. As the advertising-based business model that long supported original reporting has collapsed, the country has lost nearly 3,500 newspapers and a vast majority of local journalism jobs in just two decades. This erosion appears poised to continue as the journalism produced at great cost by news organizations is stolen and repurposed at unprecedented scale by the tech giants behind artificial intelligence. And in this financially weakened state, the press is simultaneously confronting, and sometimes buckling in the face of, the most direct political attacks on its rights and legitimacy in more than a century. All this has left fewer reporters backed by the institutional resources and institutional courage needed to do this difficult work — to witness events that might otherwise go unseen, to uncover abuses of power that have never been easier to obscure.
今天，美國自由新聞的未來充滿不確定性。隨著長期支撐原創報導的廣告商業模式崩塌，美國在短短二十年內失去了近3500家報紙和絕大多數地方新聞工作崗位。這種侵蝕看來還將繼續，因為新聞機構以巨大成本生產的新聞正被人工智慧背後的科技巨頭以前所未有的規模竊取和重新利用。在財務狀況惡化同時，新聞界又要面臨幾百年來對其權利和合法性最直接的政治攻擊，時常在壓力下屈膝。這一切意味著，能在機構資源和勇氣的支持下從事這項艱難工作的記者將越來越少，這項工作意味著去見證那些原本可能被忽視的事件，去揭露那些從未如此容易被掩蓋的濫用權力行為。
廣告
Over 175 years, The Times has become well practiced in working in the face of intense pressure. My colleagues have been kidnapped, arrested and shot at in the course of their work. One survived a land mine detonating underfoot, and another survived a helicopter crash. Some have been surveilled and expelled by foreign nations. Others have been protested, investigated and dragged into court.
經過175年的磨練，《紐約時報》已經非常擅長在巨大壓力下工作。我的同事們在工作中曾被綁架、逮捕和槍擊。有人踩中地雷後活了下來，還有人在直升機墜毀事故中倖存。一些人曾被外國監視和驅逐。還有人面臨抗議、調查並被拖上法庭。
In the 1870s, New York’s William “Boss” Tweed, whose political machine controlled just about every corner of city and state government, worked to block a Times exposé that would trigger his downfall. His associates started with threats and eventually offered a bribe worth well over $100 million today, which The Times’s publisher quickly rejected. Almost a century later, when fists, billy clubs and bullets didn’t discourage our reporters from covering the civil rights movement, segregationists weaponized the courts by filing a wave of lawsuits intended to bankrupt The Times.
19世紀70年代，紐約的「老闆」威廉·特威德的政治機器幾乎控制著市和州政府的每一個角落，他曾試圖阻止《紐約時報》一篇將引發他倒台的揭露報導。他的同夥先是威脅，最終提出一筆按今天價值遠超1億美元的賄賂，《紐約時報》的出版人迅速拒絕。近一個世紀後，當拳頭、警棍和子彈未能阻止我們的記者報導民權運動時，種族隔離主義者將法院武器化，提起一波旨在讓《紐約時報》破產的訴訟。
Presidents of both parties have done their best to pressure the press and discourage reporting they haven’t liked. John F. Kennedy spied on one of our reporters. Richard Nixon tried to block the publication of the Pentagon Papers. George W. Bush and Barack Obama both used the courts to try to force Times reporters to reveal confidential sources. No president in modern history has been more hostile than Donald Trump to a press he’s dubbed “the enemy of the people.” He accuses reporters of treason, a crime punishable by death. He personally sues news organizations when their coverage displeases him. He directs his administration to use the levers of government power to investigate, subpoena and prosecute journalists for doing their jobs. But in a reminder of the press’s lasting importance, Mr. Trump, like his predecessors, still reads The Times. He still relies on us, still takes questions from us and still quotes us when convenient. Indeed, during a visit to The Times, he told my colleagues and me that he thinks of us as “a great, great American jewel, a world jewel.”
兩黨總統都竭力施壓新聞界，打擊他們不喜歡的報導。約翰·F·甘迺迪監視過我們的一名記者。理查德·尼克森試圖阻止五角大廈文件的發表。喬治·W·布希和貝拉克·歐巴馬都曾利用法院試圖迫使《紐約時報》記者透露機密消息來源。在現代歷史上，沒有哪位總統比唐納德·川普更敵視新聞界，他稱新聞界為「人民的敵人」。他指控記者叛國，而這是可判處死刑的罪行。當新聞報導令他不悅時，他親自起訴新聞機構。他指示其政府利用權力槓桿調查、傳喚和起訴履行職責的記者。但提醒人們新聞界持久重要性的是，川普和他的前任一樣，仍然在讀《紐約時報》。他仍然依賴我們，仍然接受我們的提問，仍然在有利於他的時候引用我們。事實上，在到訪《紐約時報》時，他告訴我和我的同事們，他認為我們是「一顆非常、非常偉大的美利堅寶石，一顆世界寶石」。
The mounting pressure on the free press makes this an especially opportune time to remind ourselves that our country depends on trustworthy sources of information, context and accountability as much as it ever has. Indeed perhaps more so, as the remaining news outlets committed to original, independent reporting in the public interest are overtaken by the surge of social media influencers, partisan propagandists and A.I. bots peddling fear, outrage and misinformation.
自由新聞面臨的壓力日益增大，這使得我們更應提醒自己：我們的國家一如既往地依賴可信的信息來源、背景和問責。事實上，也許如今更加如此，因為那些仍致力於為公共利益進行原創、獨立報導的新聞機構正被社群媒體網紅、黨派宣傳者和AI機器人推銷恐懼、憤怒和虛假信息的浪潮所淹沒。
Reporters are very often how we know what we know. They have not just chronicled our country’s first 250 years; they’ve also empowered the public and kept its officials honest. The current period of democratic erosion around the world offers a clear warning that when a free press is undermined, other freedoms will soon fall as well. If this sounds overstated, I challenge anyone to find a country where people are free but the press isn’t.
我們之所以知道某些事，往往正是受惠於記者。他們不僅記錄了我國建國以來的250年；他們還賦權公眾，並讓官員保持誠實。當前全球民主受侵蝕的時期明確警告我們：當自由新聞被削弱時，其他自由很快也會倒下。如果這聽起來言過其實，請找出一個人民自由但新聞不自由的國家。
The confluence of America’s and The Times’s anniversaries is a welcome reminder of this enduring connection. It contains an implicit promise of continuity: Our nation has been through a lot and will find a way through whatever comes next. But these anniversaries also remind us that our history must be recorded to be learned from. Is our moment really unprecedented? How did similar events unfold the last time? What worked? What didn’t? What comes next? As always, the lessons of our shared past and hopes for our shared future can be found in the pages of The New York Times.
美國和《紐約時報》的週年紀念在此時交匯是對這一持久聯繫的令人欣慰的提醒。它包含一種延續性的隱含承諾：我們的國家經歷了很多，也會找到辦法度過接下來的一切。但這些週年紀念也提醒我們，我們的歷史必須被記錄下來，才能從中學習。我們真的是在經歷前所未有的時刻嗎？類似事件上一次是如何展開的？哪些起了好的效果？哪些不好？接下來會發生什麼？一如既往，我們共同的教訓和對未來的希望都能在《紐約時報》的版面上找到。

A.G. Sulzberger是《紐約時報》出版人兼《紐約時報》公司董事長。他是奧克斯-蘇茲伯格家族自收購時報以來第六位領導這份報紙的家族成員。
