在中國，「女凝」正在推動一場新的娛樂消費熱潮

In China, the ‘Female Gaze’ Is Driving a New Entertainment Boom

MURPHY ZHAO, 艾莎 • 2026-11-27
Toned, topless male dancers tear off their robes before sold-out theaters. Costumed actors at theme parks are pinched, pulled and photographed by hundreds of women. Strapping young men race up mountains carrying female hikers in their arms.
身材健美、赤裸上身的男舞者在座無虛席的劇場裡一把扯下長袍。主題樂園裡，身著戲服的男演員被數百名女性捏來捏去、拉扯著拍照。身強力壯的年輕男子抱著女性徒步者奔上山。
Across China, businesses are cashing in on a lucrative new market: women’s enjoyment.
在中國各地，商家正在一個利潤豐厚的新興市場掘金：女性的享樂。
Even as China’s economic growth slows and consumers spend less and save more, women with rising incomes are fueling the entertainment segment of the $1.5 trillion “sheconomy,” a phrase used by the Chinese government to describe women’s spending power.
儘管中國經濟增長放緩、消費者減少開支並增加儲蓄，但收入不斷上升的女性正在推動規模達10萬億人民幣的「她經濟」中的娛樂消費增長。中國政府用「她經濟」一詞來描述女性的消費能力。
Women flush with disposable income are pursuing virtual romances with A.I. companions, playing video games that appeal to female romantic fantasies and devouring television dramas and novels featuring women as business tycoons, military commanders and political leaders — characters not traditionally portrayed by women.
擁有充裕可支配收入的女性正在與人工智慧伴侶展開虛擬戀愛、玩迎合女性浪漫幻想的電玩遊戲，津津有味地觀看大女主電視劇和小說——她們是商業大亨、軍事統帥和政治領袖，而傳統上這些角色並不由女性來呈現。
廣告
Among the fastest-growing offerings are sometimes risqué entertainment services that put men on display. The trend reverses the “male gaze,” in which women are objects of male desire, said Minglu Chen, an associate professor at the University of Sydney who studies gender and politics in China. Now women are paying to admire men.
增長最快的娛樂項目中，有一些以展示男性魅力為賣點，有時甚至帶有幾分情色意味。雪梨大學研究中國性別與政治問題的副教授陳明璐說，這一趨勢顛覆了傳統的「男性凝視」，在這種凝視下，女性是男性欲望的對象。如今，女性正在花錢欣賞男性。
The spending spree poses a dilemma for Beijing. For years, the government has tried to spur consumer spending to offset a prolonged property crisis. Yet its censors go into overdrive whenever feminism, gender and power become topics of national conversation. The state-owned People’s Daily recently warned businesses against “suggestive flirtation and indecent physical contact” and called for “strict regulation” after a video showing a male actor feeding a lollipop to a woman at a theme park went viral online.
這種消費熱潮讓北京陷入兩難。多年來，政府一直試圖刺激消費支出，以抵消曠日持久的房地產危機帶來的影響。然而，每當女權主義、性別和權力成為全國性話題時，審查機構就會進入高度戒備狀態。最近，一段主題樂園裡男演員給一名女遊客喂棒棒糖的影片在網上瘋傳，官媒《人民日報》隨後警告商家不得進行「曖昧挑逗和不雅的肢體接觸」，並呼籲「嚴格監管」。
For many women, the attention is long overdue.
對許多女性來說，這種對她們需求的關注早就該有了。
“It’s finally our turn,” said Linda Xiong, 32, an office worker who paid $90 to see a performance by the Gonggou Theater, a troupe of toned men in their 20s billed as China’s answer to the Chippendales.
32歲的辦公室職員琳達·熊（音）花600塊觀看了公狗劇場的演出。這個劇團由20多歲、身材健碩的男性組成，被譽為美國猛男舞團奇彭代爾斯(Chippendales)的中國版。
Onstage, bare-chested men in traditional Chinese embroidered skirts writhe to pounding drums as women scream from the audience.
舞台上，赤裸上身的男子穿著中國傳統刺繡裙裝，隨著震耳的鼓點扭動身體，台下的女觀眾發出陣陣尖叫。
“I found it incredibly joyful,” said Ms. Xiong, who is single. She noted that the show’s tickets had sold out in seven seconds. (She counted.)
「我覺得特別快樂，」單身的熊女士說。她指出，演出票七秒就售罄了。（她特意數過。）
廣告
“Whether in the past or modern times in China, wasn’t it always a group of beautiful women dancing for men?”
「中國無論是古代還是現代，不一直都是一群美女跳舞給男人看嗎？」
The show is so popular that, according to its founder Junyi Ge, most of its performances in the past 15 months have sold out. A discussion forum about the show on Douyin, TikTok’s Chinese sister app, has racked up more than 2.1 billion views.
劇團創始人葛俊逸說，演出太受歡迎了，過去15個月裡大多數場次的門票都是銷售一空。抖音上關於該演出的話題累計瀏覽量已超過21億次。
“It has to be sexy, powerful, dripping with testosterone,” Mr. Ge said in a video interview with an online influencer. “That is our secret weapon.”
「完全不迴避，」葛俊逸在接受一名網路部落客的影片採訪時說。「就是要性感，就是要很有力量，感覺是荷爾蒙的。」
Theme parks are also realizing the power of the female gaze. Online job advertisements seek “sunny, handsome and outgoing” young men to pose for photographs and play interactive games with visitors. Some positions pay as much as $4,500 a month, more than six times the average monthly income of the country’s urban residents, with no degree required.
主題公園也意識到了「女凝」的力量。網上有招聘廣告尋找「陽光、帥氣、開朗」的年輕男子，與遊客合影並開展互動遊戲。一些崗位月薪高達3萬人民幣，是中國城鎮居民月平均收入的六倍多，而且不要求學歷。
Tour operators at some of China’s most famous mountains are hiring tall, muscular young men to wear matching uniforms, promoting them as “security guard boy groups” to draw tourists. Other young men work as freelance hiking companions, providing fresh fruit, cheering women along and sometimes carrying exhausted clients uphill in their arms.
在中國一些名山景區，旅遊經營者僱用身材高大健壯的年輕男子，讓他們身穿統一制服，以「保安男團」為賣點吸引遊客。還有一些年輕男子以自由職業者的身份提供陪爬服務，為女性遊客提供新鮮水果，一路加油打氣，有時甚至把精疲力竭的客戶抱上山。
Driving the boom is a generation of increasingly affluent, educated urban women shaped by three decades of China’s one-child policy.
推動這一熱潮的是一代日益富裕、受過良好教育的城市女性，中國長達30年的獨生子女政策塑造了這一群體。
廣告
Official statistics show that the proportion of women among first-year undergraduates has risen about 20 percentage points over the past two decades. The gender wage gap has also narrowed to 13 percent from 23 percent over the past seven years, according to reports by Zhaopin, China’s biggest job recruitment platform.
官方統計數據顯示，過去20年間，本科新生中女生的佔比提高了約20個百分點。中國最大的招聘平台智聯招聘發布的報告還顯示，過去七年間，男女薪酬差距已從23%縮小至13%。
Businesses are selling what is called “emotional value” services — or feel-good experiences — to women who are more financially independent than their mothers and grandmothers.
商家正在向經濟上比母輩和祖母輩更獨立的女性銷售所謂的「情緒價值」服務，也就是讓人獲得愉悅感的體驗。
The success of these services “reflects dissatisfaction and also maybe disenchantment with romantic relationships in real life,” said Kailing Xie, an associate professor at the University of Birmingham who studies gender and society in China.
伯明翰大學研究中國性別與社會問題的副教授謝凱玲說，這些服務的成功「反映了對現實戀愛關係的不滿，或許還有幻滅」。
It exposes a mismatch, she said, between women’s growing economic power and men’s slower acceptance of female expectations for intimacy in relationships.
她說，這暴露出女性的經濟實力不斷增強與男性未能以同樣速度接受女性對親密關係期待之間的錯配。
Chenglin Liu, 21, discovered an opportunity while struggling to make ends meet as a waiter. He started charging female hikers $100 to $150 to accompany them on six-hour treks up Mount Tai in eastern China. His social media account was widely followed after he and three other guides carried a woman who had lost a leg to bone cancer all the way to the summit.
21歲的劉成林以前做服務生，收入入不敷出時發現了一個機會。他開始向女性爬山者收取600到1000元的費用，陪她們花六小時攀登中國東部的泰山。在他和另外三名導遊把一名因骨癌失去一條腿的女子一路抬到山頂後，他的社群媒體帳號獲得了廣泛關注。
But hiking up and down the mountain as often as three times a day battered Mr. Liu’s knees. He traded the trails for a job as an actor at a theme park in Kaifeng, a central Chinese city.
但每天最多三次上下山的工作給劉成林的膝蓋造成了嚴重損傷。他告別了山間步道，來到中國中部城市開封的一座主題樂園當演員。
Now, he spends 90 minutes applying makeup to transform himself into an ancient scholar or warrior. He poses for photographs with, on some days, 200 visitors, most of them women.
現在，他用90分鐘的時間化妝，把自己變成古代書生或武士。他有時一天要和200名遊客合影，其中大多數是女性。
For the men, the work can be rewarding, too.
對這些男性來說，這份工作也能帶來滿足感。
“People come here to have fun — I naturally want to provide them with the emotional value they’re looking for,” said Ren Jisen, 23, an actor working at another theme park in Kaifeng.
「人們來這裡是為了開心，我自然想提供他們尋找的情緒價值，」23歲的任繼森（音）說。他在開封另一家主題公園工作。
廣告
Mr. Ren said he enjoyed the praise and compliments from visitors — attention he rarely received in previous jobs.
任繼森表示，他很享受遊客的讚美和誇獎——在以往的工作中，他很少受到這樣的關注。
But the interactions can be “a bit awkward and uncomfortable,” he said, especially when women pinch him or ask him to rest his chin in their palms for photographs.
但他說，互動可能「有點尷尬和不舒服」，尤其是當女性捏他或要求他把下巴放在她們手掌上拍照時。
“We are just strangers,” he said.
「我們只是陌生人，」他說。
Such encounters have elicited scrutiny. The People’s Daily called them vulgar and “borderline clickbait traps” in condemning the video that showed the actor in Shangrao, in south-central China, passing lollipops from his mouth to visitors’ mouths.
這類互動已經引起了有關方面的關注。《人民日報》在譴責中國中南部城市上饒一名男演員用嘴給遊客送棒棒糖的影片時，稱這類行為低俗，是「『擦邊式』流量陷阱」。
Theme parks across China responded by banning physical contact between actors and visitors, according to Xueying Wang, marketing manager at Wansui Mountain Martial Arts City where Mr. Ren works.
任繼森所在的萬歲山武俠城營銷中心品宣部經理王雪瑩表示，中國各地的主題樂園隨後紛紛禁止演員與遊客發生肢體接觸。
“Part of it is just the state constantly policing the boundaries of what the entertainment industry can do,” said Hanzhang Liu, an assistant professor of politics at Florida State University. She compared the crackdown to earlier restrictions on suggestive clothing worn by female workers.
佛羅里達州立大學政治學助理教授劉含章說：「部分原因在於，國家一直在對娛樂產業可以做什麼進行管控，不斷劃定其行為邊界。」她將此次整治與此前對女性從業者穿著具有性暗示意味的服裝所作的限制相提並論。
What’s more, birthrates in China are plunging and the government has responded in part by promoting traditional roles for women in the family.
此外，中國的出生率正在急劇下降，政府採取的應對措施之一是倡導女性在家庭中承擔傳統角色。
廣告
The government, Ms. Liu added, “may be particularly allergic to these kinds of consumption patterns.”
劉含章補充道，政府「可能尤其反感這類消費模式」。
Still, demand is not slowing.
然而，市場需求並沒有放緩。
The Wansui Mountain theme park employs more than 1,500 actors and actresses and attracts more than two million visitors a month.
萬歲山武俠城雇有1500多名男女演員，每月吸引超過200萬遊客。
“Many visitors come specifically to interact with the actors,” said Ms. Wang, the park’s marketing manager. “People nowadays want to pay for emotional experiences.”
「很多遊客就是專門來跟演員互動的，」這裡的品宣部經理王雪瑩說。「現在人們願意為情緒體驗付費。」
This shift goes beyond live entertainment. In popular television dramas with female leads, women run businesses, command armies and wield political power.
這種轉變並不局限於現場娛樂活動。在廣受歡迎的大女主電視劇中，女性經營企業、統帥軍隊、掌握政治權力。
But consuming fantasies of female power is not the same as possessing it, said Ms. Chen, the University of Sydney professor. The changing gaze “does not alter women’s position in family, workplaces and the political sphere.”
但雪梨大學的陳明璐說，消費有關女性掌權的幻想並不等於真正擁有權力。凝視對象的轉變「並不會改變女性在家庭、職場和政治領域的地位」。
Melissa Yuan, 32, sees that distinction clearly.
32歲的梅麗莎·袁（音）清楚地看到了這種區別。
On a recent visit to Mount Tianzhu, near her home city of Wuhan, she reached the summit and found a line of handsome young men in sunglasses and blue-and-black uniforms. They had been hired to pose for photos with tourists.
最近，她前往距離家鄉武漢不遠的天柱山遊玩。登頂後，她發現一排戴著墨鏡、身穿藍黑色制服的帥氣年輕男子。他們受雇與遊客拍照合影。
The sight made her smile. Ms. Yuan has paid good-looking men to accompany her while shopping or eating, but she does not think such transactions change the balance of power between men and women.
眼前的這一幕讓她露出笑容。她曾花錢請帥哥陪自己逛街或吃飯，不過，她並不認為這類交易能夠改變男女之間的權力關係。
Even when she is paying men to entertain her, she still “habitually and subconsciously” worries about their feelings.
即使是她花錢讓男性取悅自己，她仍然會「習慣性地、下意識地」顧及他們的感受。
“In my experience, consumption doesn’t directly translate into power,” Ms. Yuan said.
「以我的經驗，消費並不直接轉化為權力，」她說。
