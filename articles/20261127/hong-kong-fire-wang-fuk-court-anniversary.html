香港民眾紀念宏福苑大火週年，北京警告「以災亂港」

Hong Kong Grieves Fire Victims Under Watchful Eye of Authorities

ANJALI TSUI • 2026-11-27
A year ago Thursday, a blaze tore through a sprawling high-rise apartment complex in Hong Kong, spreading from one tower to the next, killing 168 people. For the families left behind, how to mourn the dead has become a politically fraught question.
一年前的週四，一場大火席捲香港一座佔地廣闊的高層公寓樓群，火勢從一棟樓蔓延到另一棟樓，造成168人遇難。對於留下的家屬來說，如何哀悼逝者已成為一個政治敏感問題。
In the days leading up to the anniversary, Beijing warned against efforts to “exploit the disaster to destabilize Hong Kong,” reflecting concerns that commemorations could fuel anti-government sentiment. The city government said that it would not hold memorial events.
在週年紀念日前幾天，北京警告稱，要警惕「以災亂港」的企圖，這反映出當局擔心紀念活動可能會助長反政府情緒。香港政府表示不會舉行紀念活動。
Still, people found their own ways to remember what became the deadliest fire in Hong Kong in nearly 80 years. They laid flowers on a park bench near the charred towers of the complex, Wang Fuk Court. They gathered in churches to pray. And at an exhibition in a basement art space, they shared stories and photographs of those they had lost.
儘管如此，人們還是找到了自己的方式來悼念這場近80年來香港最致命的火災。他們在宏福苑燒焦樓宇附近的公園長椅上獻花。他們聚集在教堂祈禱。在一個地下藝術空間的展覽上，他們分享逝去親人的故事和照片。
Among those who visited the exhibition at the Fringe Club was Phyllis Lo, a resident of Wang Fuk Court, whose 74-year-old mother died in the fire.
參觀藝穗會展覽的人中，包括宏福苑居民菲利斯·羅（音），她74歲的母親在火災中喪生。
On Wednesday, Ms. Lo walked under a canopy of paper flowers and paused at a display to flip through the pages of a zine that the curators had made using her photos. Her eyes welled up as she gazed at a photograph of her as a child next to her mother, Ying Lo. She lingered on a page of photographs taken of her mother’s charred apartment, showing an iPad eerily frozen at 3:05 p.m., shortly after the fire began.
週三，菲利斯·羅穿過一片紙花構成的拱門，在展台前駐足，翻閱著策展人用她的照片製作的雜誌。她凝視著一張自己小時候和母親羅英（音）在一起的照片，眼眶濕潤。她久久駐足在一頁展示母親被燒焦的公寓的照片前，照片中一台iPad的螢幕詭異地定格在下午3點05分——就在火災發生後不久。
The exhibit, which will be on display for two weeks, is organized by Forget Thee Not, a group that offered free funeral and burial services in the aftermath of the fire.
這個展覽將展出兩週，由「毋忘愛」組織，該組織在火災後曾提供免費殯葬服務。
The organizers have framed the exhibition as an exploration of grief rather than a commemoration of the victims of the fire, even though most of the 18 stories on display were shared by people who lost their loved ones in the deadly blaze.
儘管展出的18個故事大多來自在這場致命大火中失去至親的人，但組織者將此次展覽定位為對悲傷的探索，而非對火災遇難者的紀念。
廣告
“This exhibit is not about remembering Wang Fuk Court,” said Dr. Fan Ning, the founder and chairman of Forget Thee Not. “This is about how to help people cope with sudden death and loss.”
「這個展覽不是關於紀念宏福苑，」毋忘愛創始人兼主席范寧說。「而是關於如何幫助人們應對突然死亡和失去。」
The fire burned for more than 40 hours with such ferocity that some bodies were reduced to ashes. It fueled widespread anger and grief across Hong Kong when signs emerged that negligence and hazardous materials had most likely turned homes into firetraps, and that residents’ warnings had been ignored.
大火燃燒了40多個小時，火勢猛烈，一些遺體化為灰燼。當有跡象表明，疏忽和危險材料極有可能將住宅變成火的陷阱，且居民的警告被忽視時，這場火災在香港引發了廣泛的憤怒和悲痛。
Major government-mandated renovations had been underway at the residential towers when the fire broke out, sparked by a cigarette butt. Residents said that for months they had filed complaints with the authorities that workers were smoking on site and that they had concerns the building materials used were substandard.
火災發生時，這些住宅樓正在進行政府強制的大規模翻修，起火原因是一支煙頭。居民說，幾個月來他們一直向當局投訴工人在現場吸煙，並擔心使用的建築材料不合格。
Over the past year, the Hong Kong government has cracked down on criticism, reporting and independent organizing related to the fire, according to a report from Human Rights Watch released on Wednesday. The group cited the arrests of at least two people who demanded more government accountability, on national security offenses. A Wang Fuk Court resident, Jason Kong, was arrested on charges unrelated to the fire not long after delivering a petition from homeowners requesting a meeting to discuss resettlement plans.
根據人權觀察週三發布的報告，過去一年，香港政府打壓與火災相關的批評、報導和獨立組織。該組織指出，至少兩人因要求政府加強問責，以國家安全罪名被逮捕。宏福苑居民傑森·江（音）在遞交一份業主請願書，要求開會討論安置計劃後不久，因與火災無關的指控被捕。
An independent government inquiry said the fire had been fueled by flammable foam boards and safety nets installed by contractors who were trying to cut costs. It identified gaps in regulation and named four government agencies, including the fire and building departments, for failing to oversee the renovations.
一項獨立政府調查稱，承包商為降低成本而安裝的易燃泡沫板和安全網加劇了火災。調查指出了監管漏洞，並點名包括消防和建築部門在內的四個政府部門未能有效監督翻修工程。
As part of a criminal probe, seven people and two companies involved in the renovations have been charged with crimes including manslaughter, conspiracy to defraud and money laundering.
作為刑事調查的一部分，參與翻修的七人和兩家公司被控過失殺人、共謀詐騙和洗錢等罪名。
廣告
In a rarely seen gesture, Hong Kong’s leader, John Lee, bowed in apology last month and announced the resignation of the housing secretary. Mr. Lee has promised policy changes to improve regulation of the construction sector and to investigate dozens of civil servants.
香港領導人李家超上月罕見地鞠躬道歉，並宣布房屋局局長辭職。李家超承諾改變政策，加強建築行業監管，並調查數十名公務員。
Yet many residents and victims’ relatives feel that true accountability has not been delivered.
然而，許多居民和遇難者家屬認為，真正的問責尚未落實。
“Why has only one politician resigned? It’s unacceptable,” said Ms. Lo, who attended dozens of public hearings that were held as part of the inquiry in the past year. “We blamed ourselves for my mother’s death. Why can’t they take responsibility?”
「為什麼只有一名政客辭職？這不可接受，」菲利斯·羅說。她過去一年裡，她參加了作為調查的一部分舉行的數十場公開聽證會。「我們一直為母親的死而自責。他們為什麼不能承擔責任？」
The fire has also displaced thousands of residents in one of the most expensive places in the world to live. Another resident, Clara Cheung, who lost her mother, is living in cramped temporary housing provided by the government, a 130-square-foot studio apartment where she shares a bunk bed with her widowed father. They used to live in a two-bedroom apartment.
這場火災還使數千居民流離失所，而香港是全球生活成本最高的地區之一。另一位失去母親的居民克拉拉·張（音）住在政府提供的狹窄臨時住房裡，這是一套約12平米的單間公寓，她與喪偶的父親共用一張雙層床。他們以前住的是兩居室公寓。
“I’m still waiting for more people to take responsibility,” Ms. Cheung said. “I hope they will.”
「我還在等待更多人承擔責任，」克拉拉·張說。「我希望他們會。」
Although Ms. Cheung and her father live in close quarters, she said they rarely talk about their feelings. “I don’t want him to worry,” she said. “I also don’t know if he’s OK.”
儘管克拉拉·張和父親共同生活在狹小的空間裡，但她說，他們之間很少談論彼此的感受。「我不想讓他擔心，」她說。「我也不知道他好不好。」
廣告
Other former residents of the complex in the northern district of Tai Po are also struggling with the emotional aftermath of the fire. Mind HK, a mental health service provider, has been offering hundreds of displaced residents free therapy and subsidies for psychiatric care over the past year.
位於大埔區北部的該住宅區其他前住戶也正飽受火災帶來的心理創傷困擾。心理健康服務機構香港心聆過去一年一直在為數百名流離失所居民提供免費治療和精神科護理補貼。
Many of the survivors are older adults who are reluctant to seek help or talk about their struggles, said Gen Chan, a senior project manager of the organization’s Tai Po support group.
該組織大埔支持小組高級項目經理詹·陳（音）說，許多倖存者是老年人，不願尋求幫助或談論自己的困境。
“Mental health is not the first priority for Hong Kong people,” Mr. Chan said. His organization is working to reduce the stigma around seeing a therapist by holding community events and exercise classes.
「心理健康不是香港人的首要關注，」詹·陳說。他的組織正在通過舉辦社區活動和鍛煉課程，努力消除人們對尋求心理治療的偏見。
He said that in the weeks leading up to the anniversary, his group had received more calls from Wang Fuk Court residents, some of whom the organization identified as showing signs of post-traumatic stress disorder. Over 90 percent of people the group has worked with reported having trouble sleeping, Mr. Chan said.
他說，在週年紀念日前幾週，他的小組接到更多宏福苑居民的來電，其中一些人被該組織認定有創傷後應激障礙跡象。詹·陳說說，該組織接觸過的人中，超過90%報告有睡眠問題。
The anniversary has also been shadowed by political sensitivities in Hong Kong, where the authorities are wary of a repeat of the antigovernment protests of 2019. On Tuesday, China’s Hong Kong and Macau Affairs Office said that any attempt to “provoke social division and confrontation” or “undermine Hong Kong’s stability and unity” would not succeed.
週年悼念活動也籠罩在香港的政治敏感性陰影下，當局擔心2019年反政府抗議重演。週二，中國國務院港澳事務辦公室表示，任何試圖「挑動社會分化對立」或「破壞香港安定團結」的企圖都不會得逞。
In the absence of an official memorial service, a steady stream of people traveled to Tai Po on Thursday and laid bouquets of white flowers with handwritten messages on a bench. Some of them bowed.
在沒有官方紀念活動的情況下，週四不斷有人前往大埔，在長椅上放下白花，附上手寫留言。一些人鞠躬致哀。
On Wednesday night, dozens of victims’ relatives filled the pews at a Catholic mass held at the Church of the Immaculate Conception, the city’s largest cathedral, to honor the dead. They were joined by hundreds of other people offering prayers for the deceased. Nearly every seat in the neo-Gothic cathedral, which accommodates more than 1,000 people, was taken and some people stood in the aisles, kneeling on the checkered marble floor during parts of the service.
週三晚上，數十名遇難者家屬擠滿聖母無原罪主教座堂的座位，參加天主教彌撒紀念逝者。還有數百人一同為逝者祈禱。這座可容納1000多人的新哥特式教堂是香港最大的主教座堂，幾乎座無虛席，一些人站在過道裡，在彌撒進行期間跪在棋盤格狀的大理石地板上。
“I feel very moved,” said Agnes Ng, who traveled from Canada to attend the service. Her mother, 87-year-old Mary Ng, died in the fire alongside her caregiver, Novita, a domestic worker from Indonesia in her early 30s.
「我非常感動，」從加拿大趕來參加儀式的艾格尼絲·吳（音）說。她的母親、87歲的瑪麗·吳與她的看護、30歲出頭的印尼家政工人諾維塔一起在火災中喪生。
廣告
During the mass, Ms. Ng and a few other volunteers read aloud the 168 names of people who died in the fire. Then, she joined around 50 other families of the deceased to receive a blessing from the Rev. Dominic Chan.
彌撒期間，艾格尼絲·吳和其他幾名志願者大聲念出火災中遇難的168人姓名。然後，她與其他約50名遇難者家屬一起接受陳志明神父的祝福。
“I could have attended a mass in Canada or anywhere in the world, but participating in this in Hong Kong was especially meaningful for me,” Ms. Ng said.
「我本可以在加拿大或世界任何地方參加彌撒，但在香港參加這個彌撒對我來說特別有意義，」艾格尼絲·吳說。
“I don’t think it needs to be so sensitive,” she added. “Tonight was very peaceful.”
「我覺得沒必要這麼敏感，」她還說。「今晚非常平和。」
