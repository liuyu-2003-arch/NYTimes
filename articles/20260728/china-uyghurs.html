參與鎮壓維吾爾人的前新疆警察：我也是受害者

He Helped China Repress Uyghurs. He Says He’s a Victim, Too.

袁莉 • 2026-07-28
Halfway through a short speech he was giving at the German Bundestag, Zhang Yabo began to choke back tears.
在德國聯邦議院發表的簡短演講進行到一半時，張亞博開始強忍淚水。
Mr. Zhang had served for nine years as a police officer in Xinjiang, an enforcer in China’s campaign to surveil, detain and indoctrinate that region’s Uyghur population. At the campaign’s height, an estimated one million Uyghurs and other Muslim minorities were held in internment camps.
張亞博曾在新疆當了九年警察，是中國對該地區維吾爾人口實施監視、拘禁和思想灌輸運動的執行者。在這場運動的最高潮，據估計有100萬維吾爾及其他穆斯林少數民族被關押在拘禁營中。
Tens of thousands of police officers carried out China’s state oppression in Xinjiang, but Mr. Zhang is a rare voice speaking out about it. Standing before German lawmakers and human rights activists in June, he described Xinjiang as a giant prison enclosed by an “ironclad wall.” “That wall imprisoned not only countless innocent Uyghurs,” he said, “but also the souls of those ordered to enforce it.”
數以萬計的警察在新疆執行中國的國家壓迫政策，但張亞博是極少數站出來發聲的人之一。今年6月，站在德國議員和人權活動人士面前，他將新疆描述為一座被「鐵壁」包圍的巨大監獄。「那堵牆不僅囚禁了無數無辜的維吾爾人，」他說，「也囚禁了那些奉命執行者們的靈魂。」
Mr. Zhang related the pressure of participating in China’s security apparatus: endless shifts, sleeping in the office and constant fear of punishment. After he requested asylum in Germany last summer, Chinese authorities froze his bank accounts, he said, branded him a traitor and harassed his family.
張亞博講述了在中國安全機構工作面臨的壓力：無休止的倒班、睡在辦公室，以及對受罰的持續恐懼。他說，自去年夏天他在德國申請庇護後，中國當局凍結了他的銀行帳戶，給他貼上了叛徒的標籤，並騷擾他的家人。
廣告
By the end of the speech, he was sobbing “like a child,” he later told me. He hurried from the room and did not return.
他後來告訴我，在演講結束時，他哭得「像個孩子」。他匆忙離開了房間，沒有再回來。
“All the pressure, all the fear, all the emotions that he had carried with him” seemed to break loose, said Adrian Zenz, a Xinjiang expert who attended Mr. Zhang’s speech, “like a huge dam holding back a huge amount of water.”
「他身上背負的所有壓力、所有恐懼、所有情緒」似乎都在那一刻爆發了，出席了張亞博演講的新疆問題專家鄭國恩(Adrian Zenz)說。「就像一座擋住巨量洪水的大壩決堤了一樣。」
Mr. Zhang’s account of his time in Xinjiang corroborates what has long been documented about the Chinese Communist Party’s brutal repression of the Uyghurs while adding detail and an inside perspective to that record. It also reveals what an ordinary man pursuing upward mobility could gain, and lose, by helping to carry out those repressive policies.
張亞博對他在新疆經歷的描述印證了長期以來有關中國共產黨殘酷鎮壓維吾爾人的記錄，也為這一記錄增添了細節和內部視角。這也揭示出，在協助執行這些鎮壓政策的過程中，一個追求社會地位提升的普通人會得到什麼，又會失去什麼。
Mr. Zhang provided extensive documentation of his service history, including copies of his police identification and pay and social security records, as well as photos of him at interrogations and political propaganda sessions. But I could not corroborate exactly what he did or did not do in Xinjiang; I relied on his telling of the story, and there may have been things he did not share.
張亞博提供了關於其服役歷史的大量文件證明，包括他的警察身份證明、工資和社會保障記錄的複印件，以及他在審訊和政治宣傳會議上的照片。但我無法確切證實他在新疆到底做了什麼或沒做什麼；我只能依賴於他的敘述，而且他可能還有所隱瞞。
The Chinese government denies committing human rights abuses in Xinjiang, describing the camps as vocational training centers created to combat terrorism and religious extremism. It says the centers were closed in 2019.
中國政府否認在新疆侵犯人權，稱這些營地是為打擊恐怖主義和宗教極端主義而設立的職業培訓中心。中國政府表示這些中心已於2019年關閉。
With China’s record of retaliating against citizens in exile, Mr. Zhang has shown considerable courage in coming forward as a witness to injustice. But he is asking for something more than praise; he wants to be recognized as a victim himself — a victim of the apparatus he participated in for nearly a decade.
鑒於中國有報複流亡公民的記錄，張亞博站出來指證不公展現出了極大的勇氣。但他所尋求的不僅僅是讚譽，他希望自己也能被承認為受害者——一個他參與了近十年的國家機器的受害者。
A Grim Path Out of Poverty
擺脫貧困的艱難之路
廣告
For Mr. Zhang, moving to Xinjiang, and later joining its police force, offered a way to move up in the world. Born in 1986 into a poor rural Han Chinese family, Mr. Zhang lived in a village in central Henan Province that did not have electricity until he was 8. He moved to Xinjiang for college in 2006 and stayed to teach mathematics at a rural primary school. He married a woman from his home province, and they had a son in 2011.
對張亞博來說，搬到新疆、隨後加入當地警察隊伍為他提供了一條往上爬的出路。他1986年出生於一個貧困的漢族農村家庭，生活在河南省中部的一個村莊裡，那裡直到他八歲時才通上電。他於2006年前往新疆讀大學，畢業後留在一所鄉村小學教數學。他與一位來自老家省份的女子結婚，兩人於2011年生下一個兒子。
In late 2014, he left teaching for better pay as a guard at a detention center in the Hotan region, where about 90 percent of the population was Uyghur. His monthly wages rose to about $650, from $400 as a teacher.
2014年底，為了獲得更高的薪酬，他辭去教職，到和田地區的一家拘禁中心擔任看守，和田地區約90%的人口是維吾爾族。他的月薪從當老師時的約400美元漲到了約650美元。
For nearly two years, he said, his duties included monitoring surveillance footage, distributing food and medicine and gathering incriminating information from detainees, most of them Uyghurs. He heard screams from the interrogation rooms and saw detainees beaten up and deprived of sleep. He sometimes found broken batons when picking up the detainees. At the time, he said, he accepted the government’s claims that the men being interrogated were terrorists and separatists.
他說，近兩年裡，他的職責包括監控錄像、分發食物和藥品，以及收集被拘禁者（其中大多數是維吾爾人）的罪證。他聽到過審訊室裡傳出的尖叫聲，看到過被拘禁者遭到毆打和被剝奪睡眠。去提審被拘禁者時，他有時會發現打斷的警棍。他說，在當時，他接受了政府的說法，認為那些被審訊的人都是恐怖分子和分裂分子。
Seeking even higher pay and greater social status, Mr. Zhang took the civil service exam and formally joined the police force in late 2016 just as Beijing was about to intensify its mass detention campaign in Xinjiang. He was proud of his uniform, he said, and his monthly pay rose to about $900.
為了尋求更高的薪水和更高的社會地位，張亞博參加了公務員考試，在2016年底正式加入警察隊伍，當時北京正準備加強在新疆的大規模拘禁運動。他說，他為自己的制服感到自豪，並且他的月薪增加到了約900美元。
In early 2017, Chen Quanguo, the new Communist Party secretary of Xinjiang, called for a “smashing, obliterating offensive,” including, according to leaked government documents, the detention of anyone who displayed “symptoms” of religious radicalism or antigovernment views. Those symptoms could be the wearing of long beards, studying Arabic and praying.
2017年初，新疆維吾爾自治區新任黨委書記陳全國呼籲發起一場「粉碎性打擊、毀滅性打擊」。根據洩漏的政府文件，這包括拘留任何表現出宗教激進主義或反政府觀點「癥狀」的人。這些癥狀可能包括留長鬍須、學習阿拉伯語和祈禱。
Mr. Zhang took part in nighttime raids on Uyghur homes to confiscate Qurans and other religious items. “I was usually the first to charge in,” he recalled. “I believed they were bad people. That was what had been drilled into us at the time, that they were all bad people.”
張亞博參與了夜間突襲維吾爾人家庭沒收《古蘭經》和其他宗教物品的行動。「我通常是第一個衝進去的，」他回憶道。「我相信他們是壞人。那是當時灌輸給我們的思想，認為他們都是壞人。」
廣告
For much of 2017, Mr. Zhang traveled in bus convoys transporting hundreds, and sometimes thousands, of Uyghur detainees to prisons across Xinjiang, which is nearly four times the size of California. The detainees were handcuffed, hooded and ordered to use plastic buckets as toilets during the journey. Mr. Zhang distributed bread and water.
在2017年的大部分時間裡，張亞博跟隨大巴車隊，將數百名、有時甚至數千名維吾爾人運送到新疆各地的監獄（新疆的面積幾乎是加利福尼亞州的四倍）。在旅途中，被拘禁者被戴上手銬、套上頭罩，並被命令使用塑料桶作為廁所。張亞博負責向他們分發麵包和水。
“My job was serving the passengers on the bus,” he said.
「我的工作就是為車上的人服務，」他說。
In late 2018, when he was assigned to a police station, he began to grow despondent. His colleagues brought in Uyghur prisoners at all hours, he said, and he was responsible for searching them and helping determine where they would be sent. His sleep was constantly interrupted, and his superiors were short-tempered.
2018年底，他被分配到一個派出所後逐漸開始感到沮喪。他說，他的同事隨時都會帶入維吾爾囚犯，而他負責對囚犯進行搜身，並協助決定將他們送往哪裡。他的睡眠經常被打斷，上司的脾氣也很暴躁。
He was pleased to be briefly reassigned to supervise about 3,000 Uyghur laborers who had been sent to pick cotton for Han Chinese farmers. They rose at 5 a.m., traveled for hours to the cotton fields and were not returned to their dormitories until late at night.
他很高興能被短暫調走，去監督大約3000名被派去為漢族農民摘棉花的維吾爾勞工。他們清晨5點起床，乘車數小時前往棉花田，直到深夜才被送回宿舍。
For Mr. Zhang, it was a nice break. His main task was to make sure no one ran away or prayed.
對張亞博來說，這是一次很好的喘息機會。他的主要任務是確保沒有人逃跑或祈禱。
He learned of the international criticism of forced labor in Xinjiang through statements by China’s foreign ministry and accepted the government’s claim that the accusations were false and intended to vilify China. “I didn’t think much about it,” he said. “I never paused to consider whether it was true or not.”
他通過中國外交部的聲明了解到了國際社會對新疆強迫勞動的批評，並接受了政府的說法，即這些指控是虛假的，意在抹黑中國。「我沒有想太多，」他說。「我從未停下來思考這是不是真的。」
Privilege and Punishment
特權與懲罰
For years, Mr. Zhang largely accepted the orders he was given. But during the pandemic, governmental coercion began to feel personal. He said he had once been confined for about 10 days inside a village police post and, after a work trip, quarantined for nearly a month in an abandoned police station. “The pandemic was more painful because it involved me personally,” he said.
多年來，張亞博在很大程度上接受了上級下達給他的命令。但在疫情期間，政府的強制措施開始讓他有了切膚之痛。他說，他曾在一個村莊的警務室裡被限制了大約10天，一次出差後，又在一個廢棄的派出所裡被隔離了近一個月。「疫情令人更加痛苦，因為它直接關係到我個人，」他說。
廣告
In August 2021, he was assigned to Yurushikai, a village of about 1,700 Uyghurs. About 40 percent of the adults in the village had spent time in internment camps, and some 100 were still serving time in jail, which meant that some households consisted of only children and grandparents. It was Mr. Zhang’s job, with the help of two Uyghur assistants, to investigate reports of people praying at home or missing the weekly national flag-raising ceremony or even jogging or exercising.
2021年8月，他被派往約有1700名維吾爾人的玉如什開村。村裡約40%的成年人曾在拘禁營裡待過，約100人仍在服刑，這意味著有些家庭裡只剩下孩子和祖父母。在兩名維吾爾助手的幫助下，張亞博的工作是調查在家中祈禱、缺席每週升國旗儀式、未能參加強制性中文課程，甚至未參加慢跑或鍛煉的舉報。
The government’s surveillance system generated automatic alerts for “abnormal” activity. If a household’s electricity use rose, he said, the officers would visit and ask what had kept the family awake.
政府的監控系統會對「異常」活動自動生成警報。他說，如果一戶人家的用電量增加，警察就會上門詢問這家人為什麼熬夜。
Police work brought Mr. Zhang money, status and privileges: expedited treatment at hospitals, free parking. By 2023, his monthly take-home pay had risen to about $1,250.
警察工作給張亞博帶來了金錢、地位和特權：在醫院享受優先治療、免費停車。到2023年，他每月的稅後收入已升至約1250美元。
But within the police hierarchy, he said, he was disposable. Superiors routinely insulted officers, calling them donkeys that needed to be whipped to work, Mr. Zhang said. After he missed a walkie-talkie call in December of his first year in the village, a superior punished him with 72 hours of continuous duty at a road checkpoint in freezing conditions without adequate clothing.
但他說，在警察等級制度中，他是一個可有可無的消耗品。張亞博說，上級經常侮辱警員，稱他們為需要被鞭打才會幹活的驢。在他到村裡工作第一年的12月，因為漏接了一個對講機呼叫，一位上司懲罰他在寒冷的天氣中在一個道路檢查站連續值班72小時，而且沒有足夠禦寒衣物。
The overtime was relentless, and he was separated from his family for long periods. He had not celebrated Lunar New Year with his parents since 2017 and was denied leave for his sister’s wedding. On average, he could visit his wife and his son once a month.
加班任務接踵而至，他長期與家人分離。自2017年以來，他就沒有和父母一起過過春節，請假參加姐妹婚禮的申請也被拒絕。平均下來，他每個月只能見妻兒一次。
There were also quotas to meet. Starting in 2023, he was required to produce one lead a week that could be used to justify a detention. Mr. Zhang said he had frequently failed to hit the quota and had to submit self-criticism reports and work 24-hour shifts as punishment. Working closely with the villagers began to challenge the stereotypes he had absorbed about Uyghurs. He became increasingly reluctant to enter their homes or search their private communications, even as he continued to carry out the surveillance his job required.
此外，還有必須要完成的指標。從2023年開始，他被要求每週提供一條可以作為拘禁依據的線索。張亞博說，他經常不能完成指標，不得不提交檢討書，懲罰是24小時連軸工作。與村民的密切接觸開始挑戰他此前吸收的關於維吾爾人的刻板印象。他變得越來越不願意進入村民的家中或搜查他們的私人通信，儘管他仍在繼續執行其工作所要求的監控。
Self Re-education
自我再教育
By September 2023, Mr. Zhang left his post.
到2023年9月，張亞博再也無法忍受了，於是辭職。
廣告
He moved to Guangzhou in southern China to study English, hoping eventually to study abroad. He began using a VPN to get around China’s internet firewall. The first subjects he searched were “Xinjiang” and “Chen Quanguo,” the name of the region’s hard-line former party secretary. He could not believe, he said, that the facilities where he had worked, known to him as “re-education centers” and “vocational training centers,” were described online as concentration camps. Until then, he had encountered the term only in movies about World War II.
他搬到中國南方城市廣州，在那裡學英語，希望最終能出國留學。他開始使用VPN繞過中國的互聯網防火牆。他最先搜索的兩個詞是「新疆」和「陳全國」——後者是該地區前強硬派黨委書記的名字。他說，他無法相信，自己曾工作過的那些被他稱為「再教育中心」和「職業培訓中心」的設施在網上竟被描述為集中營。在那之前，他只在有關二戰的電影中見過這個詞。
“What I found gave me terrible headaches: One side of my head said one thing, and the other said something completely different,” he said. “The conflicting accounts battled in my mind, and I couldn’t sleep at night.”
「我查到的信息讓我頭疼欲裂：我腦子裡的一邊說著一件事，另一邊卻說著完全相反的東西，」他說。「兩種截然不同的說法在我腦海裡交戰，我夜不能寐。」
After officially resigning in early 2024, feeling wounded and in need of solace and strength, he turned to faith. He had found peace in a book about Bible stories that he had read to his son at bedtime, and in April 2025, Mr. Zhang was baptized as a Catholic in Guangzhou. Four months later, he joined a European tour with his 14-year-old son. He had already decided that if he could get away from the group, he would seek asylum.
在2024年初正式辭職後，他內心受創，急需慰藉與力量，於是轉向了宗教信仰。他在一本睡前給兒子讀的聖經故事書中找到了平靜。2025年4月，張先生在廣州受洗成為天主教徒。四個月後，他帶著14歲的兒子參加了一次歐洲旅行。他早已決定，如果能脫團，就去尋求庇護。
As their tour group entered Neuschwanstein Castle, the Bavarian palace that inspired Disney’s Sleeping Beauty Castle, Mr. Zhang and his son sneaked away. They traveled to Munich and went to the headquarters of the World Uyghur Congress, an advocacy group whose name Mr. Zhang had heard many times in Xinjiang, told it was a separatist terrorist organization.
當他們的旅行團進入新天鵝堡——這座巴伐利亞宮殿是迪士尼睡美人城堡的藍本——張亞博和兒子悄悄溜走了。他們前往慕尼黑，去了世界維吾爾代表大會。張亞博在新疆曾多次聽說過這個倡導組織的名字，據稱那是一個分裂主義恐怖組織。
He has applied for asylum and lives in a shelter in southern Germany, where his son is staying at a foster facility not far away. Over a dinner of pasta, the 15-year-old told me that he missed Xinjiang’s spicy stir-fried rice noodles. Mr. Zhang’s wife remains in Xinjiang, and the couple have filed for divorce.
他已經申請了庇護，現住在德國南部的一處收容所裡，他的兒子則住在附近的寄養機構，就讀於一所德國學校。一次晚餐吃意大利麵時，這個15歲的男孩告訴我，他很想念新疆的爆辣炒米粉。張亞博的妻子仍在新疆，兩人已提出離婚。
Mr. Zhang now spends his time speaking with academics, journalists and human rights groups to document how the persecution of Uyghurs was carried out.
張亞博現在把時間都花在與學者、記者和人權組織交流上，記錄對維吾爾人的迫害是如何實施的。
“Of the millions of Chinese police officers, he is the only one who has come forward to speak the truth,” said Haiyuer Kuerban, an official at the World Uyghur Congress who has been helping Mr. Zhang settle in Germany. He said his organization had initially viewed Mr. Zhang with deep skepticism, but that its doubts had begun to ease after reviewing his documents.
「在中國數百萬警察中，他是唯一一個站出來說出真相的人，」幫助張亞博在德國安頓下來的世界維吾爾代表負責人之一海玉爾·庫爾班表示。他說，他的組織最初對張亞博深表懷疑，但在審閱了他的文件後，疑慮開始逐漸消散。
Mr. Zenz, the Xinjiang expert who is director of China studies at the Victims of Communism Memorial Foundation, a nonprofit organization in Washington, said Mr. Zhang supplied “the ground-level reality of what we had mostly inferred” from government documents, state media reports and survivor testimony.
華盛頓非營利組織「共產主義受難者紀念基金會」中國研究主任、新疆問題專家鄭國恩表示，張亞博提供了「我們此前主要」從政府文件、官方媒體報導和倖存者證詞中「推斷出的基層現實情況。
Bjorn Alpermann, a China scholar at the University of Würzburg who has interviewed Mr. Zhang extensively, said his account offered unusually granular evidence of how repression continued after the height of the mass-internment campaign and became “routinized” in village life.
維爾茨堡大學研究中國問題的學者安曉波(Björn Alpermann)曾對張亞博進行過深入的採訪，他說，對方的陳述提供了非同尋常的具體細節，說明在大規模拘禁運動的高潮過後，鎮壓是如何繼續存在並在鄉村生活中變得「常規化」。
But people who have worked with Mr. Zhang in Germany told me that we knew only what he wanted us to know and that he probably knew more than he shared.
然而，曾在德國與張亞博共事過的人告訴我，我們只了解他願意讓我們知道的部分，而他掌握的情況很可能遠多於他所透露的。
He remembers the forms he filled out, the orders he followed and the humiliations he endured in vivid detail, but is fuzzier on the worst abuses. He described hearing screams and seeing detainees after interrogations but offered fewer details about death, sexual violence and torture in detention facilities. He insisted he had been honest and forthcoming.
他清晰生動地記得他填寫過的表格、執行過的命令以及他所承受過的屈辱，但對於最嚴重的虐待問題卻記憶模糊。他描述過聽到慘叫聲，也談到審訊後看到被拘押者的情形，但對於拘留設施內的死亡、性暴力和酷刑等細節則提供得較少。他堅稱自己一直是誠實和坦率的。
He also wants the world to recognize what he endured.
他也希望世界承認他作為一名基層警員所遭受的苦難。
“My suffering was equal to, or in some respects greater than, that of the Uyghurs,” he told me repeatedly. “Why don’t you journalists write about me? Why do you only focus on the Uyghurs?”
「我的苦難與維吾爾人相當，甚至在某些方面更為深重，」他反覆對我說，「你們記者為什麼不寫寫我？為什麼只關注維吾爾人？」
Han Chinese officers, of course, were not forced to abandon their language or culture and, however difficult it might be, they could leave the police or Xinjiang. They were not subject to arbitrary detention, forced labor or torture. They would not be considered potential terrorists for playing basketball or doing push-ups.
當然，漢族警察並沒有被迫放棄自己的語言或文化，而且無論多麼困難，他們都可以離開警隊或離開新疆。他們不會遭受任意拘押、強迫勞動或酷刑。他們也不會因為打籃球或做俯卧撐就被視為潛在的恐怖嫌疑人。
“No matter how much you suffer at work, you can still go home, eat a good meal, and imagine a future,” said Mr. Kuerban, the Uyghur activist. “You do not live with the constant, paralyzing fear that something you said 10 years ago will make you disappear tomorrow.”
「無論你在工作中受了多少苦，你終究可以回家，好好吃頓飯，想像一個未來，」維吾爾活動人士庫爾班說。「你不會生活在持續的、令人窒息的恐懼中——擔心你10年前說過的話會讓你明天就消失。」
Still, Mr. Zhang’s experience showed how inhumanely the system treated its own operatives, Mr. Alpermann said. And for Mr. Zhang, leaving China and speaking publicly was itself a way of confronting the system.
安曉波說，儘管如此，張亞博覺得受到虐待的經歷表明，該系統對待內部的工作人員是多麼的不人道。而對於像張亞博這樣低級別的警員來說，離開中國並公開發聲本身就是對抗該系統的一種方式。
Mr. Zhang insisted that he had never personally tortured or mistreated any Uyghurs. He described himself as a small cog in the state machinery who filled out forms, guarded facilities and handled logistics. Responsibility lay with senior officials and with the Uyghur police assistants who, he said, carried out many of the most direct and abusive tasks. Low-level Han Chinese police officers like him, he insisted, were innocent.
張亞博堅稱，他本人從未對任何維吾爾人實施過酷刑或虐待。他形容自己只是國家機器中的一個小齒輪，負責填寫表格、看守設施和處理後勤事務。他表示，責任在於上級和維吾爾族輔警，是後者執行了許多最直接、最粗暴的任務。他堅稱，像他這樣的底層漢族警察是無辜的。
It is unclear whether Mr. Zhang’s self-exoneration is a form of self-protection, an inability to bear what a fuller recognition would mean or simply a habit of mind formed by years inside the system. Mr. Kuerban said he did not believe the blindness was deliberate. After repeatedly pressing Mr. Zhang, I had come to a similar conclusion: He seemed genuinely unable to see his role in the wider context of the immense injustice inflicted on the Uyghurs.
目前尚不清楚張亞博的自辯清白到底是一種自我保護，還是因為無法承受更充分地承認事實所意味的後果，抑或僅僅是多年在體制內形成的一種思維習慣。庫爾班表示，他不相信這種無視是故意的。在反覆追問張亞博之後，我也得出了類似的結論：在維吾爾人遭受巨大不公這一更廣闊背景下，他似乎真的無法看清自己所扮演的角色。
His struggle also reflected the country he came from, where the state has suppressed public memory of the Great Famine, the Cultural Revolution and the 1989 Tiananmen crackdown. Unlike Germany, where there has been decades of reckoning with Nazi complicity, China provides little space for sustained debate about complicity, guilt and responsibility.
他的掙扎也折射出他背後那個國家的真實狀況，在那裡，官方長期壓制公眾對大饑荒、文化大革命和1989年天安門鎮壓事件的記憶。與德國數十年來一直在清算納粹幫凶不同，中國幾乎沒有為圍繞幫凶、罪行和責任進行持續性的公共討論留下空間。
Working inside an abusive system did not automatically make someone a monster, Mr. Kuerban said. But neither did performing a minor function, or not personally carrying out the worst abuses, eliminate responsibility.
庫爾班說，在一個暴虐的系統內工作並不會自動地把人變成怪物。但只承擔次要職責、沒有親身實施最嚴重的虐待行為亦不能免責。
Mr. Zhang said his faith had taught him that his responsibility now was to speak out.
張亞博表示，他的信仰告訴他，自己現在的責任就是公開發聲。
“If you stay silent,” he said at the end of his Bundestag speech, “you’re part of the problem.”
他在德國聯邦議院演講的結尾說道：「如果保持沉默，你就是問題的一部分。」
