日本百歲老人數量突破10萬，88%為女性

Japan Reaches a Unique Milestone, With More Than 100,000 People Aged 100 or Over

HISAKO UENO, TIFFANY MAY • 2026-09-17
There are now more than 100,000 centenarians in Japan, a record for a country famous for its longevity.
如今，日本的百歲老人數量已超過10萬，在這個以長壽著稱的國家，這一數字創下了新紀錄。
Japan’s health ministry released data on Tuesday showing that there are now 107,677 people who are at least 100 years old, an increase of nearly 8,000 since last year. The oldest woman, Fuyo Kishimoto from Kyoto, is 114 and the oldest man, Hikaru Kato from Kumamoto, is 112. Women make up the vast majority of the group, at 88 percent.
日本厚生勞動省週二發布的數據顯示，目前日本100歲及以上人口達107677人，比去年增加了近8000人。最長壽的女性是來自京都的岸本芙世（音），現年114歲；最長壽的男性是來自熊本的加藤光，現年112歲。女性占該群體的絕大多數，比例高達88%。
Japan’s health minister, Kenichiro Ueno, welcomed the news on Tuesday, and pledged to build a strong social security system that supports the elderly in living long and healthy lives. “I am very happy that so many people have been able to stay active for so long,” he said at a news conference in Tokyo.
日本厚生勞動大臣上野賢一郎週二對這一消息表示歡迎，並承諾建立一個強大的社會保障體系，以支持老年人過上健康長壽的生活。「看到這麼多人能夠長期保持活躍，我感到非常高興，」他在東京的新聞發布會上說。
Stuart Gietel-Basten, a professor of social science at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, said the long lives of many Japanese could be attributed to healthy diet, active daily routines, and an affordable health system with regular check ups.
香港科技大學社會科學教授貝樂天(Stuart Gietel-Basten)表示，許多日本人的長壽可歸因於健康的飲食、積極的日常生活，以及提供定期體檢且費用可負擔的醫療體系。
Women are overrepresented as centenarians because many men of that generation died in World War II, and those who survived smoked and drank more than women, he said. Men are also more likely to be involved in industrial work, which could have long-term health impacts. Many men became isolated after retirement or the loss of a spouse, he added, and women tended to be more engaged in their communities, which improved their mental health.
他說，女性在百歲老人中比例較高，是因為那一代的許多男性在第二次世界大戰中喪生，而且那些倖存下來的男性吸煙和飲酒的習慣比女性更嚴重。男性也更有可能從事工業勞動，這可能會對健康產生長期影響。他還說，許多男性在退休或失去配偶後變得孤立，而女性往往更多地參與社區活動，這改善了她們的心理健康。
Tetsuo Sato, 106, is one of the 100,000, living in the rice-growing village of Osonogo in the mountainous Niigata Prefecture with his son, daughter-in-law, dog and cat.
106歲的佐藤哲雄是這10萬名百歲老人中的一員。他與兒子、兒媳以及貓狗一起，生活在新潟縣山區種植水稻的遲鄉。
Hard of hearing and visually impaired, Mr. Sato fills his days by making notebooks from old calendars and wrapping paper. He also tells stories about his experiences fighting in the Battle of Imphal during World War II.
儘管聽力不佳且視力受損，佐藤哲雄每天都會用舊日曆和包裝紙製作筆記本。他還經常講述自己二戰期間在因帕爾戰役中打仗的經歷。

His daughter-in-law, Kuniko Sato, 68, said he was determined to be self-sufficient and continued to bathe himself without help.
他的兒媳、68歲的佐藤久仁子（音）說，他決心自己照顧自己，至今仍堅持不靠他人幫助，自己洗澡。
“When I injured my leg last year, Grandpa started washing his own rice bowls,” she said. “We ended up caring for each other in our old age.”
「去年我的腿受傷時，爺爺開始自己洗飯碗，」她說。「到了晚年，我們開始互相照顧。」
As well as a rapidly aging population, Japan is grappling with one of the lowest birthrates in the world, a predicament that Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi has called a “quiet emergency.”
除了人口迅速老齡化之外，日本還在努力應對世界上最低的出生率之一，首相高市早苗將這種困境稱為「無聲的緊急狀態」。
Japan’s population peaked at 128 million in 2008, and is projected to drop to 87 million by 2070, according to government projections. The country now has roughly the same number of people as it did in 1989. For every new birth in the country, there are two deaths.
日本人口在2008年達到1.28億的峰值，根據政府預測，到2070年將降至8700萬。目前日本的人口數量大致與1989年持平。在這個國家，每出生一個新生兒，就有兩人死亡。
The demographic crisis poses challenges for the government, as it drives up the tax burden on a shrinking work force. For decades, the Japanese authorities have tried to rebalance the rapidly aging population by encouraging couples to have more children, to little avail.
人口危機給政府帶來了挑戰，因為它加重了不斷萎縮的勞動力的稅收負擔。幾十年來，日本當局一直試圖通過鼓勵夫婦多生孩子來重新平衡迅速老齡化的人口結構，但收效甚微。
Some experts argue that Japan needs to allow more immigrants to offset the population decline. But a wave of nationalist sentiment has recently swept the country, with some activists calling for even stricter controls as part of a “Japan First” movement.
一些專家認為，日本需要允許更多移民來抵消人口下降的影響。但最近一股民族主義情緒席捲日本，一些活動人士呼籲實施更嚴格的控制措施，作為「日本優先」運動的一部分。

At the same time, the country’s centenarians are determined to remain active. Some are even still working.
與此同時，日本的百歲老人們決心保持活躍。有些人甚至還在工作。
In two days, Mr. Sato will turn 107. More than a dozen members of his family plan to celebrate at a traditional hot spring hotel, where they hope to photograph the four generations gathered together.
再過兩天，佐藤哲雄將迎來107歲生日。他的十幾位家人計劃在一家傳統的溫泉酒店慶祝，他們希望在那裡拍下四世同堂的合影。
Ms. Sato said her father-in-law “seems to feel a sense of responsibility, believing that a parent must always look out for their children, he likely feels he needs to stay strong and capable.”
佐藤久仁子說，她的公公「似乎有一種責任感，認為做父母的必須始終照看好自己的孩子，他可能覺得自己需要保持強健和能力。」
“His original goal was to live to 100, but now he aims to reach 110,” she added.
「他原本的目標是活到100歲，但現在他的目標是活到110歲，」她還說。
