11歲逃美，44歲被遣返：一個越南難民的半生

Fled to America at 11. Stole at 18. Deported to Vietnam at 44.

AMY QIN, ALBERT SUN • 2026-08-07
Thousands of Southeast Asians, many of them long spared from deportation since the end of the Vietnam War, are being sent back as part of a Trump administration push.
隨著川普政府推行大規模驅逐行動，數以千計自越戰結束以來長期免於遣返的東南亞移民正被遣送回原籍國。
As of early March, Immigration and Customs Enforcement had deported more than 1,200 people to Vietnam and 400 to Laos, far more than in the past three presidential terms combined, according to a New York Times analysis of official records released by the Deportation Data Project.
根據《紐約時報》對「驅逐數據項目」公布的官方記錄的分析，截至3月初，美國移民與海關執法局已向越南遣返了逾1200人，向寮國遣返了逾400人，遠遠超過此前三屆總統任期內遣返人數的總和。
Among them is Mong Tran.
其中就包括陳夢（音）。
Born in Vietnam, she arrived in the United States in 1993 as an 11-year-old after living in a refugee camp in the Philippines. Her family settled in Fairfax, Va.
她出生於越南，1993年，11歲時的她從菲律賓的難民營隨家人來到美國，在維吉尼亞州費爾法克斯安家。
廣告
But at 18, she upended her life, stealing thousands of dollars from her workplace, a doctor’s office. She ended up serving four months in jail and losing her permanent residency status.
但18歲那年，她的人生走了樣——她從工作的診所偷了數千美元。最終，她被判入獄四個月，並因此失去了永久居留身份。
Normally, she would have been quickly sent back to her home country. But Vietnam and Laos had long refused to take refugees back. And the United States did not aggressively press the issue, even for those who had committed serious felonies — partly in recognition of America’s involvement in the Vietnam War and other regional conflicts that had displaced these refugees in the first place.
通常情況下，她本應很快被遣返原籍。但越南和寮國長期以來拒絕接收遣返難民，美國也沒有積極向其施壓——即便面對那些犯下嚴重重罪的人也如此。這在一定程度上是因為，美國自知對越戰等區域衝突負有責任，而正是這些戰爭令這些難民流離失所。
But the Trump administration, in both its first and second terms, has pressured Vietnam, Laos and other countries into accepting more deportees.
但兩屆川普政府都向越南、寮國等國施壓，要求它們接收更多被遣返人員。
Ms. Tran, 44, was sent back last November. She left behind her husband, four children and a nail salon business. Her record was otherwise clean, said her lawyer, Vicky Dobrin.
44歲的陳夢於去年11月遭到遣返，被迫拋下了丈夫、四個孩子和一家美甲店。她的律師維基·多布林表示，陳夢此前的記錄並無其他不端之處。
Now in a country she barely knows, Ms. Tran, who also goes by the name Melissa, is living with distant relatives outside Ho Chi Minh City.
如今，身處一個自己幾乎不了解的國家，陳夢（英文名梅麗莎）借住在胡志明市郊區的遠親家中。
Her husband and children remain in Hagerstown, Md.
丈夫和孩子們仍留在馬里蘭州的黑格斯敦。
“Although this is the country where I was born,” she said in an interview, “I don’t feel like it’s home.”
「儘管這是我出生的國家，」她在採訪中說，「但我並不覺得這裡是家。」
The Trump administration’s aggressive deportation push has revived a painful question about America’s historical obligation to these Southeast Asian refugees. Should those who fled here decades ago as children and committed crimes — including nonviolent offenses — be subject to permanent exile?
川普政府強硬推動遣返的做法再次提出了一個關於美國對東南亞難民歷史責任的痛苦問題。那些幾十年前還是孩子時逃到美國、後來犯過罪——包括非暴力犯罪——的人，是否應該被永久放逐？
There are an estimated 15,000 Southeast Asian refugees in the United States with final removal orders.
據估計，目前美國約有1.5萬名東南亞難民收到最終驅逐令。
廣告
Like Ms. Tran, many of these deportees fought or had parents who fought alongside U.S. troops, endured years in Communist re-education camps or fled the chaotic aftermath of the fall of Saigon. They came legally as refugees when they were children and have called America home for decades.
和陳夢一樣，許多被驅逐者本人或者他們的父母曾與美軍並肩作戰；有些人曾在共產黨設立的再教育營中度過多年，或在西貢陷落後的混亂中逃離。他們年幼時以合法難民身份來到美國，並在這裡生活了幾十年，早已將此地視為故土家園。
But compared to the broader deportee population, these Southeast Asian deportees were much more likely to have a past criminal conviction, the data shows. And the 400 or so Vietnamese deportees who entered before 1995 all had past criminal convictions. Their crimes range in severity, from check fraud to homicide, which are grounds for revoking green cards.
然而，數據顯示，與更廣泛的被驅逐群體相比，這些東南亞裔被驅逐者有犯罪前科的比例要高得多。在1995年前入境的約400名越南被遣返者無一例外都有犯罪記錄，罪名輕則支票欺詐，重至殺人罪，而這些都是吊銷綠卡的法定依據。
Some lawmakers and immigrant groups point out that most served their sentences and were then left in limbo for years, even decades, because of diplomatic reasons beyond their control. And, they note, some people facing imminent deportation were born in refugee camps elsewhere and have never been to their home countries. Others have faint memories, and English is the only language they speak fluently.
一些議員和移民權益團體指出，大多數人已經服完刑期，卻因為他們無法控制的外交原因，被置於懸而未決的尷尬境地長達數年甚至數十年。他們指出，一些面臨驅逐的人出生在其他地方的難民營，從未去過自己的祖國。還有一些人對故國僅剩下模糊記憶，而且英語是他們唯一能夠流利使用的語言。
To deport them decades later, advocates argue, is effectively a second punishment.
權益倡導者認為，數十年後再將他們驅逐實際上是一種二次懲罰。
“We have laws against double jeopardy,” said ThaoMee Xiong, executive director for the Coalition of Asian American Leaders, a nonprofit based in Minnesota.
「我們有禁止一罪二罰的法律，」明尼蘇達州非營利組織亞裔領袖聯盟的執行主任熊韜美說。
The Trump administration has argued that expelling criminals is necessary to maintain the rule of law and protect the safety of Americans.
川普政府則認為，驅逐有犯罪記錄的人是維護法治、保障美國公民安全的必要舉措。
廣告
“Every single illegal alien removed on these flights had full due process,” said the Department of Homeland Security, which oversees immigration enforcement, in a statement. The department credited President Trump’s “strong leadership” for convincing the Southeast Asian countries to take back the deportees.
負責移民執法的國土安全部在一份聲明中說：「這些航班上被遣返的每一名非法外國人都經歷了完整的正當程序。」該部門還將說服東南亞國家接收被遣返者歸功於川普總統的「強有力領導」。
Some supporters of immigration enforcement also say that the United States has already repaid these refugees by resettling them and offering pathways to citizenship.
一些支持加強移民執法的人士也表示，美國已通過安置這些難民、為其提供入籍途徑對他們給予了充分的回饋。
“These individuals have already been accorded significant grace by the United States,” said Matthew O’Brien, deputy executive director at the Federation for American Immigration Reform, which lobbies for curbing immigration. “Does that mean that the United States is also obligated to turn a blind eye to criminal behavior?”
「美國已經對這些人給予了相當大的恩惠，」主張收緊移民政策的遊說組織美國移民改革聯合會副執行主任馬修·奧布萊恩說，「這是否意味著美國還有義務對他們的違法行為睜一隻眼閉一隻眼？」
Between 1975 and the late 1990s, more than 1.3 million refugees from Vietnam, Laos and Cambodia were resettled across the United States, in what was the largest humanitarian resettlement initiative in modern U.S. history.
1975年至20世紀90年代末，全美各地安置了130多萬名來自越南、寮國和柬埔寨的難民。這是美國現代史上最大規模的人道主義安置行動。
Once in the United States, adults, with little schooling or savings, often scraped out a living in low-paying jobs. Many resettled with their children in high-crime neighborhoods.
抵達美國後，那些幾乎沒受過多少教育、手頭積蓄寥寥無幾的成年人只能靠低薪工作勉強維持生計。許多人帶著子女落腳在治安不佳的社區。
Mark Phommachanh was 5 when he and his mother landed in the United States from Laos around 1980. They eventually settled in Fresno, Calif., which later experienced high crime rates related to crack cocaine.
大約在1980年前後，年僅五歲的馬克·蓬馬占與母親一起從寮國來到美國。他們最終定居在加利福尼亞州弗雷斯諾。這裡後來因快克古柯鹼泛濫犯罪率居高不下。
廣告
“I became part of a gang, to protect myself and to kind of find my own family,” he said.
「為了保護自己，也為了尋找歸屬感，我加入了幫派，」他說。
Mr. Phommachanh said that like many refugees at the time, his mother, who worked full time at a doughnut shop, did not realize the advantages of acquiring citizenship. Most thought that permanent residency was all they needed.
蓬馬占說，和當時許多難民一樣，他在甜甜圈店全職工作的母親並沒有意識到入籍的重要性。大多數人都認為，擁有永久居民身份就已經足夠。
In 2003, Mr. Phommachanh was in the process of applying for citizenship when he was arrested on charges of trafficking marijuana. In 2006, after serving more than three years in prison, he was ordered deported. Laos would not take him, and he was allowed to remain on supervised release.
2003年，蓬馬佔在申請入籍期間因涉嫌販賣大麻而被捕。2006年，他服刑三年多後被下令驅逐出境。寮國拒絕接收，他獲准以監督釋放身份留在美國。
The number of deportations of Southeast Asian refugees remained low over the years, until Mr. Trump’s first term, when deportations increased significantly.
多年來，對東南亞難民的驅逐人數始終維持在較低水平，直至川普第一任期，驅逐人數才大幅增加。
But the Trump administration still faced obstacles.
但川普政府依然面臨重重阻礙。
The Laotian and Vietnamese governments continued to resist pressure to take back large numbers of people with old removal orders like Mr. Phommachanh.
寮國和越南政府繼續抵制美國壓力，不願接收大量像蓬馬占這樣持有多年前的驅逐令者。
廣告
Vietnam in particular had long refused to accept people who first entered the United States before 1995, seeing them as more likely to be loyal to the former South Vietnamese government. On the U.S. side, there was also widespread criticism, including from the U.S. ambassador to Vietnam, who resigned from the State Department in protest.
越南尤其長期拒絕接收1995年前入境美國的人員，認為這一群體更可能效忠於前南越政府。在美國內部，此舉也遭到廣泛批評，甚至引發美國駐越南大使辭職以示抗議。
But in 2020, at the end of the Trump administration, U.S. officials quietly reached a new agreement with Vietnam to allow the deportation of people who first entered the United States before 1995. The agreement laid the groundwork for Mr. Trump’s second-term deportation campaign.
然而，2020年川普第一任期結束之際，美國官員悄然與越南達成新協議，允許驅逐1995年前入境的人員。這一協議為川普第二任期的驅逐行動奠定了基礎。
Since last year, the administration has deported more than 400 people in this pre-1995 group to Vietnam. Under the first Trump administration, only 26 people in this group were sent back over four years.
自去年以來，政府已將400餘名屬於「1995年前」群體的人員遣返越南。而在川普第一任期的四年裡，這一數字僅為26人。
Laos, too, has been accepting deportees in much greater numbers during the second Trump administration.
在川普第二任期內，寮國接收的被驅逐者人數同樣大幅增加。
Mr. Phommachanh was working as a manager at an R.V. company in Virginia last fall when he learned that immigration authorities wanted to see him, even though he had just had his annual check-in.
去年秋天，在維吉尼亞州一家房車公司擔任經理的蓬馬佔得知移民部門想見他，儘管他剛剛完成了例行的年度報到。
“I was out shopping for steaks to grill for me and my wife, and I just dropped everything,” Mr. Phommachanh, 51, recalled in an interview.
「我當時正在買牛排，準備回家和妻子吃烤肉，結果就這樣扔下一切，」51歲的蓬馬佔在採訪中回憶道。
In November, he was deported to Laos. He considers himself one of the lucky ones. So far, he has been able to live off savings. And his wife, who had never traveled abroad until she recently came to visit, plans to join him. He documents his new life in Vientiane, the capital city, on YouTube.
去年11月，他被驅逐到寮國。他自認為已是幸運的一個：目前尚能靠積蓄維持生計。他的妻子此前從未出過國，最近已經來寮國探望他，並計劃在這裡定居。蓬馬占還通過YouTube記錄自己在寮國首都萬象的新生活。
Other deportees have had a much harder time, said Chanida Phaengdara Potter, director of ROOTS Laos, which helps people resettle in Laos. Separated from their families, some have struggled to find jobs or medical care. Depression is common.
其他被遣返者的處境要艱難得多，幫助人們重新在寮國安頓下來的「寮國之根」負責人查尼達·潘達拉·波特說道。他們與家人天各一方，許多人在求職和就醫方面面臨很大困難，憂鬱症狀十分普遍。
“There’s a lot of uncertainty and isolation,” Ms. Potter said. “America was supposed to be their safe haven.”
「存在很多不確定性和孤立感，」波特說，「美國本來應該是他們的避風港。」
Some refugees facing imminent deportation have found ways to stay by applying for state-level pardons or reopening their old criminal cases. But for most, the options are limited.
部分面臨驅逐命運的難民通過申請州級赦免或重新審理舊案找到了留下的辦法。但對大多數人而言，選擇空間十分有限。
Ms. Tran is trying to adjust to her new life in Vietnam.
陳夢正努力適應在越南的新生活。
She is studying Vietnamese while working toward her English teaching certification. She plans to learn how to ride a motorbike for local transportation. She talks to her family every day and helps her younger children, ages 11 and 8, with their homework via FaceTime.
她一邊學習越南語，一邊備考英語教學資格證書，並計劃學習騎摩托車以便出行。她每天與家人通話，還通過FaceTime輔導兩個年幼的孩子（一個11歲，一個八歲）做作業。
But she is up at night. She worries about her husband, Danny, who now has to run the nail salon on his own while solo parenting. She worries about her daughter Rachel, who recently paused her undergraduate studies because of stress from the deportation. And there is Jacob, her son with autism.
但她常常夜不能寐。她擔心丈夫丹尼，現在他不得不獨自經營美甲店，同時還要獨自照顧孩子。她也擔心女兒瑞秋，瑞秋因為遣返帶來的壓力暫時中斷了本科學業。還有她的兒子雅各布，他患有自閉症。
“For the last 20 years, I tried to be a better person and teach my children that there are always consequences,” Ms. Tran said. “But being apart from them now, that’s the hardest part for me to accept.”
「過去20年裡，我一直努力成為一個更好的人，也一直教育孩子們，告訴他們做任何事情都要承擔後果，」陳夢說道。「但現在要和他們分開，這是我最難接受的事情。」
