可能拯救美國製造業的台積電工廠

The Factory in Phoenix That Could Save American Manufacturing

DAN WANG • 2026-10-27
On July 5 the Northwest Chinese Baptist Church in Phoenix opened its weekly Mandarin service with a verse from the Old Testament. A pianist, a guitarist and a drummer prepared to lead the 100 congregants in song. The deacon read out the preamble to the Declaration of Independence — “We hold these truths to be self-evident” — and then invited the congregation to rise and sing “The Battle Hymn of the Republic.” It was a good moment to exalt American ideals. The prior night, fireworks marked America’s 250th anniversary of independence.
7月5日，位於菲尼克斯的西北華人浸信會以一段《舊約》經文開啟了每週的華語主日儀式。鋼琴、吉他與鼓手準備帶領百餘名會眾齊唱讚美詩。執事宣讀了《獨立宣言》序言——「我們認為這些真理是不言而喻的」，隨後邀全體會眾起立合唱《共和國戰歌》。此時正是頌揚美國理想的絕佳時刻。前一天晚上，絢爛的煙花剛剛慶祝了美國獨立250週年。
The next speaker was a guest, Timothy Cheng, the chief executive of a Christian media company. Flicking through a slide presentation, he spoke about the significance of semiconductors, noting that chips made in Taiwan are the lifeblood of the American economy. He clicked to the next slide. The faces of Steve Jobs, Bill Gates, Elon Musk and Jensen Huang appeared on the screen.
緊接著登台的是特邀嘉賓、一家基督教媒體公司的首席執行官蒂莫西·鄭（音）。他翻著幻燈片，談起半導體的重要性，並指出台灣製造的晶片是美國經濟的命脈。下一頁幻燈片出現了史提夫·賈伯斯、比爾·蓋茲、伊隆·馬斯克與黃仁勛的面孔。
“In earlier years, Western missionaries brought knowledge, education and English to the East,” Mr. Cheng said. “They brought the Gospels and Western medicine. Now that Taiwanese have become fortunate and strong, why should they not transfer knowledge to the United States to make it powerful?”
鄭先生在發言中表示：「早期，西方傳教士將知識、教育與英語傳入東方，帶來了福音和西醫。如今台灣人已變得富足而強大，為什麼不把知識轉移給美國，讓它強大呢？」
This sermon would be less surprising if you knew that one of the church’s pastors was a former Intel engineer. And he was preaching in a church 20 minutes south of one of the most advanced semiconductor factories in the world, a facility known as TSMC Arizona.
如果事先知道教會有一位牧師曾是英特爾公司的工程師，就不會對這番佈道感到意外。不僅如此，他所在的教會距離全球最頂尖的半導體製造基地之一——台積電亞利桑那工廠——僅有20分鐘車程。
TSMC, the Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company, is the world’s most advanced chip maker. It is the company that Apple and Nvidia turn to for the most complex components of their most intricate technologies. That skill has propelled TSMC’s market capitalization to $2 trillion. It might be worth even more if TSMC didn’t manufacture nearly all of its chips in Taiwan, which China’s Communist Party has long coveted. When typically bullish investment banks imagine a war over the island, they reach for words like “global economic depression,” “trillions in losses” and, sometimes, “World War III.”
台灣積體電路製造公司（TSMC，簡稱台積電）是全球最先進的晶片製造商。無論是蘋果還是英偉達，在其最複雜技術的最關鍵組件上均會求助於台積電。這項製造實力推動其市值跨越2萬億美元大關。若非台積電幾乎將全部晶片產能集中在中共覬覦已久的台灣，其估值本可更高。向來持樂觀態度的投資銀行在設想圍繞該島爆發戰爭時，會用上「全球經濟蕭條」、「數萬億美元損失」乃至「第三次世界大戰」等等措辭。
In 2020, TSMC announced it would build a semiconductor production facility in Arizona. It was lured by proximity to its American customers, the carrot of federal subsidies and unsubtle political pressure from the Trump administration, which has, correctly, praised the effort as the largest foreign direct investment in the United States. In July, TSMC announced its intention to invest a total of more than a quarter of a trillion dollars for 10 chip production plants, two packaging facilities and a research and development site, all in Arizona. TSMC now employs over 3,500 people in the state, including skilled Taiwanese engineers on temporary visas. This month, TSMC’s chief executive said that the company was exploring an investment in Texas.
2020年，台積電宣布將在亞利桑那州建立半導體生產基地。此舉受到與美國客戶毗鄰的地利以及聯邦補貼的吸引，還有來自川普政府毫不掩飾的政治壓力。政府曾明確盛讚該項目為美國最大的外國直接投資。今年7月，台積電宣布計劃累計投資超過2500億美元，在亞利桑那州建設10座晶片生產廠、兩座封裝設施及一個研發基地。目前，台積電在該州聘用的員工已超3500人，其中包括持臨時簽證的高技術台灣工程師。本月，台積電首席執行官表示，公司正探討在得克薩斯州進行投資。
廣告
The United States invented the semiconductor, the tiny computer processor that has powered the digital age, and its biggest companies dominated the industry for decades. Then chipmakers like Intel and Texas Instruments fell behind. The hardware powering the A.I. boom is now made mostly in East Asia.
美國發明了推動數位時代發展的微處理器半導體，其本土巨頭更曾主導該行業數十年之久。然而，隨著英特爾、德州儀器等晶片製造商逐步落後，如今支撐人工智慧繁榮的核心硬件已大部分轉由東亞製造。
TSMC Arizona is rebuilding America’s manufacturing backbone by teaching Americans the skills that they have lost, just as the speaker at the Northwest Chinese Baptist Church predicted. For the most important asset TSMC offers is not money, equipment or local employment but what’s known as process knowledge. The United States doesn’t lack capital. American companies are flush with cash and can conjure marvelous funds to chase new technologies. It doesn’t lack the ability to buy advanced tooling, either. What America really lacks is skill: the practice of building semiconductors, automobiles, electronics and many other industries in which U.S. companies used to lead the world.
正如西北華人浸信會的那位講者所預言的那樣，台積電亞利桑那項目正在通過向美國人傳授他們已經失去的技能，重建美國的製造業支柱。因為台積電提供的最重要資產不是金錢、設備或當地就業，而是所謂的工藝知識。美國不缺少資本，現金充裕的美國企業隨時能調集驚人資金追逐新技術；美國同樣不缺乏購買先進工具設備的能力。美國真正缺乏的是技能：製造半導體、汽車、電子產品以及許多美國公司曾經引領世界的行業的實踐經驗。
Thousands of teachers of these skills, in the form of Taiwanese engineers, have moved to North Phoenix. Many bring their families. Earlier generations of immigrants departed from their homelands on their individual initiative, as my parents did from China and Canada, to make a new life in America. New immigrants from Taiwan more closely resemble corporate shock troops on a mission to build chips fast.
數以千計傳授這些技能的台灣工程師如今已搬到北菲尼克斯，許多人帶來了家人。早期的幾代移民往往出於個人意願離開故土，正如我的父母當年離開中國和加拿大，去美國開創新生活那樣。這些來自台灣的新移民則更像是一支背負著快速製造晶片使命的企業突擊隊。
Over two trips to Phoenix this year, I saw a winning formula for American reindustrialization. For six years, I lived in China and observed how Chinese companies became global leaders, in part because American companies brought them better technology and management. In Arizona, I saw how Taiwanese engineers are training American workers to build some of the most complex technologies in the world. TSMC is doing for America what Apple did for China.
今年兩次前往菲尼克斯，我看到了美國再工業化的致勝秘訣。我曾在中國生活了六年，觀察中國企業如何成為全球領軍者，這在一定程度上得益於美國公司為他們帶來了更好的技術和管理。在亞利桑那州，我看到了台灣工程師如何培訓美國工人製造世界上一些最複雜的技術產品。台積電對美國所做的正是蘋果公司曾經對中國所做的事。
And guess what? TSMC engineers and their families are having a great time. In interviews, they marveled at their new lives in the Sun Belt — the large homes, the friendly community, the opportunities for their kids.
讓人意想不到的是，台積電工程師及其家屬正過得很愉快。在接受採訪時，他們對自己身處陽光地帶的新生活讚嘆不已：寬敞的居所、友善的社區，以及他們的子女得到的機會。
The question is how long this can last. Arizona has a mixed record when it comes to welcoming immigrants. And one year ago, on the other side of the country, officers from Immigration and Customs Enforcement detained hundreds of South Korean citizens at a battery plant that was being built in Georgia, which had also been hailed as a crucial investment for America’s manufacturing future. At any moment, the jerkiness of President Trump’s immigration policy could also upend the lives of Taiwanese workers and, by extension, this vital experiment in reindustrialization.
問題在於這種情況能持續多久。亞利桑那州在接納移民方面的記錄好壞參半。一年前，在美國另一端的喬治亞州，移民與海關執法局突擊並拘留了數百名韓國公民，事發地點正是一座同樣被譽為關乎美國製造業未來的在建電池廠。川普總統移民政策反覆無常，隨時可能顛覆台灣工人的生活，並進而顛覆這一至關重要的再工業化實驗。
Geopolitics brought TSMC to Arizona; domestic American politics might send the Taiwanese back home. So far, businesses, political leaders and churches in Arizona have rallied around to help, paying attention to whether the transplants can find doctors or ingredients for oyster omelets. The United States can address both its manufacturing needs and its national security — but only if the administration fully embraces the newcomers who wish to build their lives here. When a Taiwanese company brings a work force to the United States, it is imparting the world’s most precious thing: knowledge about advanced technological production that can help America regain its edge.
地緣政治將台積電帶到了亞利桑那，美國國內政治卻可能將台灣人送回老家。到目前為止，亞利桑那州的企業、政界領導人和教會紛紛伸出援手，密切關注這些移居者能否找到醫生或買到做蚵仔煎的食材。美國完全可以兼顧製造業需求與國家安全，但前提是政府必須充分接納希望在這裡立足的新移民。當一家台灣公司將技術隊伍帶到美國時，它傳授的是世界上最寶貴的東西：能夠幫助美國重獲競爭優勢的尖端技術生產知識。
America created the chip industry. It was also an early mover in airplanes, cars, rare earths, telecommunications equipment and more. The United States remains the world’s second-largest manufacturer. But it’s hard to remain proud of its most storied companies. Detroit automakers have been in genteel decline for decades. The poor health of apex manufacturers such as Boeing and Intel has grown palpable more recently. Despite spending billions of dollars to accelerate production, the U.S. military is running low on critical munitions.
美國開創了晶片產業。它也是飛機、汽車、稀土、電信設備等領域的先行者。美國至今仍是世界第二大製造業國家，但很難再為那些曾經輝煌的企業感到自豪。底特律的汽車製造商幾十年來一直在悄然衰退，波音和英特爾這樣頂級製造商的頹勢最近變得更加明顯。儘管斥資數十億美元以加速生產，美國軍方的關鍵彈藥仍面臨短缺。
Instead, it is America’s chief geopolitical competitor that leads in so many of these advanced technologies. Today, China is responsible for around one-third of the world’s manufacturing output — producing twice as much, by value, as the United States does. It has wrested leadership from the United States in rare earths and provides a vast majority of the world’s supply, as well as cars, especially the electric kind. Chinese A.I. companies are the only ones in the world that can look on their Silicon Valley counterparts as peers.
而在如此多的先進技術領域處於領先地位的卻是美國的主要地緣政治競爭對手。今天，中國貢獻了全球約三分之一的製造業產值，按價值計算是美國的兩倍。中國已從美國手中奪走了稀土領域的領導地位，供應了全球絕大部分稀土；在汽車尤其是電動汽車領域同樣如此。中國的人工智慧公司是世界上唯一能夠和矽谷等量齊觀的企業。
廣告
Tales of factory closings in the United States have often been mirrored by headlines of American corporations opening factories in China. It’s not an accident that American companies lost their leadership in various manufacturing sectors as they started shifting production overseas. They offshored labor-intensive production to Asia, betting they could hold on to the more valuable work of research and development.
美國本土工廠相繼關閉的故事往往與美國企業在華建廠的新聞相互印證。美國公司在開始將生產轉移到海外的過程中失去了在各個製造業領域的領導地位，這絕非偶然。他們將勞動密集型生產環節離岸外包給亞洲，押注自己能夠保留更具價值的研發工作。
I’ve been writing for much of the decade about the idea that design and manufacturing are one and the same thing. I watched how Chinese companies — including Huawei, BYD and Xiaomi — used their mastery of manufacturing to make ever more sophisticated products and take on more complex R&D. Over the past two decades, American manufacturers lost their edge when they lost their manufacturing. Today the center of hardware innovation is not Silicon Valley but Shenzhen, which leveraged its position manufacturing iPhones to build the future of electronics.
過去十年的大部分時間裡，我一直在撰寫文章闡明一個觀點：設計與製造本是同一回事。我觀察了華為、比亞迪和小米等中國企業如何利用對製造業的精通生產出越來越精密的產品，並承擔起更複雜的研發工作。在過去的二十年裡，美國製造商在失去製造業的同時也失去了競爭優勢。今天硬件創新的中心不是矽谷，而是深圳。深圳利用其製造iPhone的地位打造了電子產業的未來。
The most important ingredient American companies lost was process knowledge. That is the part of production that cannot be written down, because it lives in the hands and heads of workers. It’s the years of industrial expertise about chip production, which is extraordinarily sensitive to minute changes in the thousands of steps involved. When American factories close, they take this accumulated knowledge with them.
美國公司失去的第一要素是工藝知識。那是生產過程中無法被文字記錄下來的部分，因為它存在於工人們的雙手與大腦之中。那是關於晶片生產多年的工業專門經驗，而晶片生產對成千上萬道工序中的微小變動都極度敏感。當美國工廠關閉時，它們帶走了這些積累的知識。
TSMC did not locate its factories where the bulk of the Asian population in Arizona already lives. Instead, it chose a spot next to a desert preserve, where giant cactuses and dry brush grow along rocky hiking trails, a region of North Phoenix with the odd but not unappealing name of Biscuit Flats. Longtime residents told me its most notable landmark used to be a gun range. Taiwanese brands have followed the influx of Taiwanese immigrants. Phoenix and its surrounding areas have gained 85C Bakery, several new bubble tea shops and two branches of Din Tai Fung, whose soup dumplings are perhaps the best-known Taiwanese products after semiconductors. (The entire East Coast has but a lone outpost, in New York City.)
台積電沒有將工廠設在亞利桑那州大多數亞裔人口的既有聚居區，而是選擇了沙漠保護區旁的一處地點，那裡巨大仙人掌與乾枯灌木沿著多石的步道生長，是北菲尼克斯一個擁有奇特卻不乏吸引力名字的區域——餅乾平地(Biscuit Flats)。老居民告訴我，這裡曾經最著名的地標是一處射擊場。台灣品牌隨著台灣移民的湧入紛至沓來。菲尼克斯及其周邊地區迎來了85度C麵包房、幾家新的珍珠奶茶店，以及兩家鼎泰豐分店——鼎泰豐的小籠包或許是僅次於半導體的最知名台灣產品。（在整個美國東海岸，鼎泰豐也僅在紐約市設有一家分店。）
Pastor Michael Lin, who led the Phoenix church service I attended, said he has felt a new sense of purpose since so many TSMC families arrived from Taiwan. He lived in Taiwan before moving to Arizona for schooling; after his master’s, he went to work for Intel nearby. Later in life, he became a full-time pastor. When TSMC announced it would establish its plants so close to the Northwest Chinese Baptist Church, he took it to be an act of providence. “I feel overwhelmed with gratitude,” he told me as we had lunch.
主持了我參加的那場菲尼克斯教會禮拜的麥可·林（音）牧師表示，自從這麼多台積電家庭從台灣來到這裡，他感受到了一種新的使命感。他曾在台灣生活，後前往亞利桑那求學。在獲得碩士學位後，他在附近為英特爾工作。後來，他成為了一名全職牧師。當台積電宣布將在離西北華人浸信會如此之近的地方建廠時，他認為這是上帝的旨意。「我感到無比感恩，」我們在共進午餐時他告訴我。
The pastor and the church have set up English classes for new arrivals. Volunteers engage groups of people for language practice, sometimes at their homes, focused on conversation for everyday tasks. They help sign children up for school; if a child gets into a fight, they might assist with the mediation.
牧師與教會為新遷入的人員開設了英語班。志願者們分批組織大家練習語言，有時會去到他們家中，重點圍繞日常事務展開情景對話。他們還協助家長為孩子辦理入學手續；一旦孩子捲入衝突，同工也會出面協助調解。
廣告
Kimberly Chiu moved with her husband, an engineer at TSMC, and their two teenage daughters to Phoenix at the end of 2022. Asian stores weren’t around then. Ms. Chiu had to visit multiple supermarkets to complete what was for her a basic grocery run. She kept finding herself on a 12-lane highway for simple errands. “We’d consider it a short drive if it took only 20 minutes to get somewhere,” she said.
金伯利·邱（音）與她的丈夫——台積電的一名工程師——以及兩個十幾歲的女兒於2022年底搬到了菲尼克斯。當時周邊尚無亞洲超市，為了完成基礎的日雜採買，邱女士不得不奔波於多家超市之間。她經常為了辦些小事而不得不開車上12車道的高速公路。「只要去某個地方單程在20分鐘以內，我們現在都覺得算近的了，」她說道。
TSMC helped the family find housing. They enjoyed getting to live in a home that’s much more spacious than their apartment in Taiwan. They found it novel to have a yard. But it was hard to get used to the heat. “I’m scared that the air-conditioning might fail,” said Ms. Chiu. It did twice, each time after a sandstorm tore through the city.
台積電協助這家人落實了住所。住進一棟比台灣公寓寬敞得多的房子讓他們感到十分愜意，擁有獨立的院落更是一樁新鮮事。然而，當地酷熱的天氣卻讓人難以適應。「我最擔心的就是空調出故障，」邱女士表示。而在沙塵暴席捲該市之後，家中的空調確實壞過兩次。
The temperature rarely dipped below 95 degrees at night when I visited Phoenix in July. It peaked at 113 degrees during the day. Phoenix might be how non-Americans imagine Los Angeles: long drives through sprawl and sandy beauty. It bears little resemblance to the dense and charming streets of Taipei.
7月探訪菲尼克斯時，夜間氣溫基本很少降至攝氏35度以下，白天最高氣溫更飆升至45度。菲尼克斯或許就像外國人想像中的洛杉磯：無邊無際的城市蔓延、沙色荒漠伴隨著漫長的驅車路途。這與台北擁擠而富有市井魅力的街道毫無相似之處。
Ms. Chiu’s older daughter is attending Arizona State University. Her younger daughter, Olivia, is a senior at a high school about 10 miles south of TSMC. The number of Asian students enrolled in this mostly white high school has grown by around 90 since 2021, pushing their share to 8.5 percent of the student body. Olivia, who is outgoing and smiles easily, told me she has embraced her new life. When she had a chance to perform at a charity concert, she sang the role of Anna from Disney’s “Frozen” with gusto.
邱女士的大女兒正在亞利桑那州立大學就讀。她的小女兒奧利維亞是台積電以南約10英里處一所高中的高三學生。自2021年以來，這所以白人為主的高中新招收了約90名亞裔學生，將亞裔比例推高至8.5%。性格外向且愛笑的奧利維亞告訴我，她已經適應了新生活。在一次慈善音樂會上獲得表演機會時，她全情投入地演唱了迪士尼動畫《冰雪奇緣》中安娜一角的曲目。
Sean Chang, a TSMC engineer, invited me to a potluck one night at his home. Mr. Chang and his wife, Shelly, invited a few other families with teenagers to join. I asked a half-dozen of the kids about their experiences in high school. They were relieved that American classes are so much easier than those in Taiwan. The language gap can be a challenge, but math and science classes are a breeze.
一天晚上，台積電工程師肖恩·張（音）邀請我到他家參加自帶菜聚會。張先生和他的妻子雪莉還邀請了其他幾個有青少年的家庭加入。在向席間六位孩子詢問他們的高中生活時，他們如釋重負地表示，美國的學業壓力遠比台灣輕鬆得多。語言隔閡或許是一項挑戰，但數學和科學課程簡直易如反掌。
Was anyone bullied? They shook their heads: Arizona kids are nice. Then several students brought up food fights. Amanda Chen is a senior who has already been accepted to a university in Texas to play varsity tennis. “Kids start food fights so often,” she said. “They’ve thrown carrot sticks, tomato slices and a cheeseburger.”
當被問及是否遭遇過霸凌時，孩子們紛紛搖頭：亞利桑那的學生很友善。不過，隨後幾名學生提到了學校裡的食物大戰。高三學生阿曼達·陳（音）已獲得得克薩斯州一所大學的錄取，即將代表校隊出戰網球比賽。她回憶道：「學生們經常挑起食物大戰，他們扔過胡蘿蔔條、番茄切片和一個芝士漢堡。」
The parents are more concerned. Not about food fights — though the idea perplexes them — but about the laxity of education. Arizona’s public schools rank among the lowest in the country for graduation rates and the third-lowest in spending per pupil.
家長們則要更擔憂一些。令他們困惑的並非食物大戰，而是教育環境的寬鬆。統計顯示，亞利桑那州公立學校的高中畢業率在全美各大州中位列末尾，生均教育支出亦居全美倒數第三。
廣告
Taiwanese families were enticed to the United States in substantial part by the promise of sending their children to American colleges. Now parents fret that the Arizona educational system won’t help their kids get there. They’re nonplussed that teachers sometimes wave off assigning homework to high school seniors; some schools allow seniors to take Friday afternoons off. The idea astonishes parents used to spending big sums on extracurricular prep.
許多台灣家庭當初決定赴美很大程度上正是受到將子女送入美國頂尖高校這一許諾的吸引。如今家長們開始擔憂，亞利桑那州的教育體系恐怕無法幫助孩子實現升學目標。令他們大惑不解的是，高中任課教師有時竟然不給畢業班學生布置作業，部分學校甚至允許高三學生週五下午放假。對於習慣在課外輔導上投入重金的家長而言，這種做法令他們深感震驚。
The engineers at TSMC are strikingly similar. They are mostly men and universally skittish to chat about work.
台積電的工程師們呈現出高度的一致性：他們絕大多數為男性，且在面對工作話題時普遍顯得謹慎而戒備。
Their routines are pretty similar, too. They are out the door around 7:30 a.m. and return home in the evening. Sometimes they may have to hop on a videoconferencing call at night with colleagues in Taiwan. TSMC job postings make clear that engineers should expect night shifts and weekend work.
他們的日常作息同樣十分相似。早晨7點30分左右離家出門，直至傍晚返家。入夜後，他們有時還必須參加與台灣同事的影片會議。台積電對外公布的招聘啟事中亦明確寫明，工程師應當對夜班與週末加班做好準備。
Some engineers were not eager to leave Taiwan. At home, there is no more prestigious employer than TSMC. After work, they could go to the many local barbecue or karaoke bars. Now they spend more time with their families. Some have bonded by hiking the desert trails. A few, including Ms. Chiu, have visited the gun range.
一些工程師此前並不渴望離開台灣。在台灣本土，沒有任何一家僱主擁有高過台積電的聲望；下班之後，他們隨時可以去眾多的當地燒烤店或卡拉OK聚會。如今遠赴異鄉，他們把更多的時間留給了家庭。部分人通過結伴徒步荒漠步道增進感情，包括邱女士在內的少數人還去了射擊場。
Ms. Chiu and others like her are among the more successful of the transplants. They left professional lives behind but are glowing about their personal growth after moving to Arizona. Many of the wives have learned to cook, a previously unneeded skill, relying as one could on parents or Taiwan’s splendid array of food options. Although they cannot get fresh oysters for omelets, they have instead the novelty of Trader Joe’s and the chance to shop at an American Costco.
邱女士和像她這樣的人屬於移居群體中適應相對成功的一批人。儘管放棄了原先的職業生涯，但她們對遷居亞利桑那後的個人成長感到非常滿意。許多妻子學會了下廚做飯，這在以前是一項完全不需要的技能，因為過去大可仰賴長輩幫襯或台灣極為豐富多樣的餐飲選擇。雖然在當地採買不到做蚵仔煎的新鮮生蠔，但逛一逛充滿新奇感的Trader Joe\'s或前往美國好市多大賣場購物成了生活中的替代體驗。
Others have not adjusted as well. One woman spoke to me of her regret that her children went from reading as a family to preferring television and video games. She feels that she understands them less than before.
有些人的適應情況則沒有那麼理想。一位母親向我訴說了她的遺憾，她提到自己的孩子赴美後從全家共同閱讀變成了偏愛看電視和玩電玩遊戲，她感到自己比以前更難理解孩子了。
廣告
Taiwanese folks are especially anxious about navigating the American medical system. In Taiwan, medical clinics are plentiful, and the state foots much of the bill. In America, the only thing more baffling than the process for receiving medical service is figuring out insurance.
台灣人對周旋於複雜的美國醫療體系感到尤為焦慮。在台灣，基層診所星羅棋布，且由公立醫保兜底大部分費用；而在美國，唯一比就醫流程更令人費解的是錯綜複雜的醫療保險條款。
Large American homes can be comfortable; they are also air-conditioned boxes. It’s challenging to be a woman raising a family and barely supported by a husband who is away for most of the day, plus certain nights and weekends. An overseas posting can pile immense stress on a marriage. One person told me of couples who divorced when they returned to Taiwan.
寬敞的美國住宅居住舒適，但它們也是裝了空調的盒子。身為女性，要獨自操持家庭是一項艱巨的挑戰，尤其是丈夫大部分時間不在家，某些夜晚和週末還要加班，幾乎得不到他的支持。海外派駐會給婚姻帶來巨大壓力，有人告訴我，有幾對夫妻在返回台灣後離了婚。
As I chatted with the Taiwanese families, I found myself recalling my own childhood. I am twice an immigrant: the first time when my parents and I moved from southwestern China to eastern Canada when I was 7 years old, the second when we moved to the suburbs of Philadelphia when I was 16.
在與這些台灣家庭交流時，我發現自己回想起了童年。我是經歷過兩次遷徙的移民：第一次是在我七歲時，我和父母從中國西南部搬到加拿大東部；第二次是在我16歲時，我們搬到了費城郊區。
In all ways, the Taiwanese immigrants to Arizona have an easier time than my parents did. These new immigrants possess skills the American government has declared critical. They arrive with a job and can plug into a community of expats. They often have the resources to buy a home. The local government has put in enormous effort to make them feel welcome.
無論從哪個方面來看，前往亞利桑那州的台灣移民都比我的父母當年更為輕鬆。這些新移民擁有被美國政府認定為關鍵戰略儲備的專業技能；他們帶著現成的工作抵美，能夠迅速融入當地的外派社群；他們往往有足夠的財力購置房產；當地政府也付出了巨大努力，讓他們感受到由衷的歡迎。
My parents had none of these things. My dad was a software developer who spent years jobless; we moved to Canada right after the dot-com crash. My mom was a radio and TV news anchor in China who supported us in Canada by picking up odd jobs as a janitor or in the garment industry. We struggled economically until my dad found employment in Pennsylvania. We had to figure everything out ourselves.
我的父母當年完全不具備這些條件。我的父親是一名軟體開發者，我們在互聯網泡沫破裂後立刻搬到了加拿大，隨之而來的是他長達數年的失業窘境；我的母親在中國曾是一名廣播和電視新聞主播，到了加拿大後，她通過打零工做保潔或在服裝廠做工來維持全家生計。我們在經濟拮据中苦苦支撐，直到父親在賓夕法尼亞州重新找到工作。當時我們只能靠自己摸索解決一切。
In other ways, my immigration experience is not so different from that of the Taiwanese families. Is it difficult to be outside in the bludgeoning heat of Arizona for months of the year? Well, so, too, is it a bitter prospect to be outside in the lashing cold of Ottawa.
但在其他方面，我的移民經歷與這些台灣家庭並無太大不同。一年中有幾個月要經歷亞利桑那州戶外難耐的酷熱確實非常不易，但渥太華寒風刺骨的嚴寒冬日同樣令人望而生畏。
Church groups are early to reach out to newcomers, as they did with my family. We accepted their overtures, gingerly. My parents and I could not see ourselves as churchgoers. But I liked the group of friendly people my age, and my parents were happy to see me receive language instruction, even if it came with an unbidden helping of Scripture on the side.
各類教會團體總是最早向新移民伸出援手的力量，正如他們當年對待我的家庭一樣。我們當時小心翼翼地接受了他們的善意。我的父母和我並未將自己視為常去教堂做禮拜的信徒；但我喜歡那群友善的同齡人，父母也樂見我能接受語言輔導，即便這附帶著一份不請自來的經文分享。
In January, the vice president of Taiwan asked the mayor of Phoenix, Kate Gallego, the secret for encouraging Taiwanese families to have kids. More than 300 Taiwanese children have been born to TSMC families in Arizona. “I said that with the sunny weather, everyone’s in a good mood,” she said, recalling the conversation in an interview.
今年1月，台灣副總統詢問菲尼克斯市長凱特·加萊戈，鼓勵台灣家庭生孩子的秘訣是什麼。亞利桑那州的台積電家庭已經誕生了300多名台灣新生兒。「我回答說，因為這裡陽光充足，每個人的心情都很舒暢，」她在一次採訪中回憶那次談話時說。
The mayor is one of several Phoenix power players who worked relentlessly to bring TSMC to Arizona. I was taken aback by the level of enthusiasm that the local government and business leaders have brought to greet the company. There are now nearly daily flights between Phoenix and Taipei. Ms. Gallego told me that even hospitals have stepped up, and an expectant mother can now get a labor and delivery tour in Mandarin. Arizona State University now offers a program to teach students to operate semiconductor machinery.
加萊戈是不懈努力將台積電引入亞利桑那州的數位菲尼克斯政要之一。當地政府和商界領袖為迎接這家企業所展現出的熱情程度令我感到吃驚。菲尼克斯與台北之間如今開通了幾乎每日一班的直航航班。加萊戈告訴我，甚至當地醫院也跟進改善服務，准媽媽現在可以獲得華語的分娩和產房導覽；亞利桑那州立大學也開設了一個新項目，專門傳授學生操作半導體機械設備的技能。
Still, no immigrants really know what they signed up for — not them, not my family nearly three decades ago. The new life is never exactly as advertised. Next year I’ll be the age that my dad was when we moved to Canada. I am certain I do not have the courage that my parents brought to establish a new life in a country they hadn’t previously visited, bringing few assets, no community and a 7-year-old child. These Taiwanese families, too, must display courage of their own.
儘管如此，沒有任何移民在啟程時能真正料到自己將面對什麼，無論是眼下這批台灣家庭，還是近三十年前我的家人。異國的新生活從來不會完全如宣傳描繪的那樣美好。明年我就到了我父親當年帶我們移居加拿大時的年紀。我確信自己沒有父母當年的非凡勇氣：帶著微薄的家當，毫無熟人社群依靠，還要拉扯一個七歲的孩子，在一個此前從未到過的陌生國度開啟全新的生活。而眼前這些台灣家庭同樣必須展現出屬於他們自己的勇氣。
Arizona boosters like to say the state is naturally friendly because a majority of residents are not born there — about 60 percent of the population, according to U.S. census estimates. Business and government officials say the Taiwanese are the latest beneficiaries.
亞利桑那州的支持者喜歡強調，該州天生就具有包容友善的氛圍，因為絕大多數常住居民並非土生土長——根據美國人口普查局的估算，約佔總人口的60%。當地商界和政府官員表示，這批台灣人正是這種環境的最新受益者。
Still, immigration politics were often on my mind. Arizona is, at best, selectively friendly to new arrivals. In recent years it has been strict about enforcing cultural standards, mostly against its Latino population. Joe Arpaio styled himself “America’s toughest sheriff” and rounded up immigrants in Maricopa County, which encompasses Phoenix. In 2010, Arizona enacted S.B. 1070, which expressly called for “attrition through enforcement” to reduce illegal immigration. The Supreme Court later struck down several of its provisions. Moreover, Mr. Trump came to power for a second time vowing to end illegal immigration and restrict the legal kind, too.
然而，移民政治的陰影卻始終盤桓在我的心頭。往好裡說，亞利桑那州對外來者的友好向來是有選擇性的。近年來，該州一直在嚴格推行文化和執法標準，主要針對當地的拉美裔人口。曾自封為「全美最嚴厲治安官」的喬·阿爾帕約在涵蓋菲尼克斯的馬利科帕縣大規模搜捕移民。2010年，亞利桑那州頒佈了第1070號參議院法案(S.B. 1070)，明文主張通過「高壓執法消耗」來減少非法移民。最高法院後來依法推翻了其中的多項條款。此外，川普第二度入主白宮，誓言徹底終結非法移民，並且同樣主張限制合法移民。
As I interviewed people in Arizona, I also thought about a large Asian investment on the other side of the country.
當我在亞利桑那州走訪受訪者時，思緒也聯想到了美國另一端的一筆大規模亞洲投資。
The South Korean giants Hyundai and LG invested billions of dollars to build a battery-cell factory in Georgia. Immigration officials raided the site in September 2025. Officers arrested nearly 500 people, putting them in handcuffs and leg shackles. They spent about a week in an Immigration and Customs Enforcement detention center as Kristi Noem, the secretary of homeland security at the time, vowed to deport them. A South Korean news agency published the account of one detainee, who talked about sleeping on moldy mattresses and drinking foul-smelling water. Over 300 flew home on the basis of a negotiated “voluntary departure.”
韓國工業巨頭現代與LG此前曾投資數十億美元，在喬治亞州建造動力電池工廠。聯邦移民官員於2025年9月突擊搜查了該工地，執法人員當場拘留了近500人，並給他們戴上了手銬和腳鐐。他們在移民與海關執法局的拘留中心被關押了大約一週，時任國土安全部部長克里斯蒂·諾姆誓言要將他們全數驅逐出境。一家韓國通訊社隨後報導了一名被拘留者的親歷自述，提及自己不得不睡在發霉的床墊上並飲用發臭的水。最終，300多人在達成協議辦理「自願離境」後乘機返回韓國。
ICE officials never laid out clear evidence that all of these South Korean workers had violated the terms of their stay. Reports emerged that at least some had valid visas. The State Department reportedly restored the visas of many detainees after a few weeks. Mr. Trump hosted the chairman of Hyundai in March 2025, when the company announced it would invest $21 billion in the United States. He met the president of South Korea less than two weeks before the raid.
移民與海關執法局的官員從未拿出確鑿證據，證明所有這些韓國工人均違反了其居留條款。有報導披露，至少其中一部分人持有有效簽證。據報導，美國國務院在數週後恢復了多名被扣人員的簽證。川普曾於2025年3月接見現代汽車董事長，當時該公司宣布將在美國投資210億美元；而在搜查發生前不到兩週，他還會見了韓國總統。
Can Taiwanese immigrants be confident that federal officials will not visit this humiliation upon them? I posed that question to every Arizona government and business official I interviewed.
台灣移民能否確信聯邦官員不會將這種屈辱施加於他們頭上？我向採訪過的每一位亞利桑那州政府和商界人士提出了這個問題。
“Immigration is top of mind for us,” said Christine Mackay. She was one of the economic development officials who helped to secure TSMC’s investment in Arizona. Now she is the chief executive of the Greater Phoenix Economic Council, a public-private partnership to boost economic development.
「移民問題是我們首先考慮的事項，」克里斯汀·麥凱表示。她曾是幫助確保台積電落戶亞利桑那州的經濟發展官員之一，如今擔任大鳳凰城經濟發展促進會(Greater Phoenix Economic Council)的首席執行官，這是一個旨在促進經濟發展的公私合作機構。
Local and state government officials try to educate the White House on the national security importance of these companies in Arizona. Nearby Tucson is a major production hub for Tomahawk cruise missiles. And officials extensively promote TSMC.
地方和州政府官員試圖就這些公司對國家安全的重要性遊說白宮。鄰近的圖森市是戰斧巡航導彈的主要生產樞紐，官員們也藉此廣泛推介台積電。
Ms. Mackay has written letters for corporate leaders she tried to woo to Arizona, which they could show when they entered the United States. Though the documents carried no legal force, they could smooth entry if Customs and Border Protection officers had questions. She wrote her personal number on these letters and sometimes fielded calls from border guards.
麥凱曾為她試圖招攬至亞利桑那州的企業管理層寫信，供其入境美國時出示。儘管這些文件不具法律效力，但若海關和邊境保護局官員提出疑問，信件有助於順利通關。她在信上留下了私人電話，有時還會接聽邊境警衛打來的核實電話。
For now, the workers and the families I spoke with were not much concerned about Mr. Trump’s whims. They assume he knows that TSMC is important. Workers and local officials assured me that TSMC is rigorous about following all the rules, including on immigration.
目前而言，與我交談過的工人及其家屬並不太擔心川普的心血來潮。他們認為川普深知台積電的重要性。工人和當地官員也向我保證，台積電在遵守各項規定方面極其嚴格，包括在移民法規方面。
In some ways, the president gets it. After the ICE raid in Georgia, he expressed a degree of regret, telling The Times, “You have to allow them to bring some of their experts with them, or they’re never going to be able to open their plant or factory.”
從某些層面來看，這位總統是明白這一點的。在喬治亞州針對韓國工人的突擊搜查發生後，他流露出一定程度的遺憾，並在接受《紐約時報》採訪時表示：「你必須允許他們帶上一些專家，否則他們將永遠無法讓工廠運轉起來。」
Yet his administration still detained the South Korean workers. At the start of his second term, the president denounced the subsidies that the U.S. government has offered semiconductor makers, calling the CHIPS Act a “horrible, horrible thing.”
然而，他的行政當局依然扣留了那些韓國工人。在第二任期伊始，川普便譴責美國政府向半導體製造商提供的補貼，直指《晶片法案》(CHIPS Act)是一個「非常、非常可怕的東西」。
I’m thinking not only of ICE raids. His administration has twice attempted to charge a $100,000 fee for H-1B work visas. Technology workers I know in Silicon Valley feel deeply uncertain about their futures in the United States. A.I. researchers from China and software developers from India are rethinking whether they ought to build their lives here.
我想到的不僅是移民局的突擊搜查。他的政府曾兩度企圖對H-1B工作簽證徵收10萬美元的費用。我在矽谷認識的科技從業者對自己在美國的未來深感迷茫；來自中國的人工智慧研究人員和來自印度的軟體開發人員正在重新權衡是否應該在這裡定居。
TSMC Arizona’s first plant has been producing chips for two years, and its rate of output in Phoenix is comparable to that in Taiwan. Despite some turbulence with permitting and union relations, Ms. Mackay told me, the second and third factories are being built ahead of schedule. TSMC’s announcement in July that it would raise its total investment to $265 billion is an obvious sign of confidence in Arizona’s operations.
台積電亞利桑那第一工廠已開始晶片生產兩年之久，其在菲尼克斯的產出率已與台灣本部相當。麥凱告訴我，儘管在行政審批和工會關係方面經歷了一些波折，但第二和第三座工廠的建設進度依然超前。台積電在7月宣布將總投資額提高至2650億美元，這顯然是對亞利桑那運營充滿信心的體現。
Morris Chang, the founder of TSMC, has sounded positively bitter that the company has invested so much in the United States. He has called America’s effort to reshore semiconductors “a very expensive exercise in futility.” He grumbles that costs in the United States are much higher than in Taiwan. He retired in 2018 but has barely restrained himself from weighing in on Arizona. No matter how successful TSMC Arizona becomes, it is a small part of TSMC’s total chip production.
台積電創始人張忠謀對公司在美國投入如此巨資顯得十分不滿。他曾直言美國推動半導體迴流的努力是「一個極其昂貴的徒勞之舉」。他抱怨稱，美國的製造成本遠高於台灣。張忠謀雖已於2018年退休，但在亞利桑那項目上幾乎從未停止公開發聲。然而無論台積電亞利桑那未來取得多大成功，其產量終究只佔台積電晶片總產量的一小部分。
I feel one more worry when I think about Mr. Trump’s volatility: It is a twinge of fear that Mr. Chang is wrong and that this facility will be a success and that its success will destabilize the Taiwan Strait.
當我思考川普的多變特質時，心頭湧起又一層擔憂：那是一種隱隱的恐懼——張忠謀的判斷是錯的，這座工廠不僅會取得成功，而且它的成功反而會削弱台灣海峽的穩定性。
It is possible that TSMC Arizona endears more Americans to the fate of the island. It’s also possible Mr. Trump will decline to send American troops to defend Taiwan if he believes that its core technologies are already on American soil. Beijing has promised “reunification” with the self-governing island, though it does not appear committed to any immediate invasion. Mr. Trump displays, at best, a lukewarm commitment to defending liberal democracies. Either way, Arizona is now complicating the already uncertain geopolitics of the Pacific.
台積電亞利桑那項目有可能讓更多美國人對台灣產生好感，關切這座島嶼的命運。但同樣可能的是，一旦川普認定核心晶片技術已轉移至美國本土，他可能會拒絕派遣美軍協防台灣。北京重申要讓這個自治的島嶼實現「統一」，儘管目前看來並未打算立即發動入侵。而川普對捍衛自由民主制度的態度充其量也只是不溫不火。無論走向何方，亞利桑那的投資項目正在讓太平洋地區本已不確定的地緣政治博弈變得更加錯綜複雜。
TSMC sends engineers to Arizona on contracts of up to three years, which means that families in the first wave to move to the United States are returning home. Some of them told me they want to build lives in the United States in large part to give their kids the chance to integrate — and there are legal pathways for doing so. Several others want to as well, for their kids' education or because they prefer the American lifestyle. Others return to their old lives in Taiwan with relief.
台積電派遣工程師赴亞利桑那的合同期最長為三年，這意味著第一批移居美國的家庭正在陸續回國。他們中的一些人告訴我，他們之所以想在美國安家，很大程度上是為了給孩子提供融入當地社會的機會，而且他們有合法的途徑可以做到這一點。還有幾位也抱有同樣的想法，或是為了孩子的教育，或是更青睞美式生活方式。也有人如釋重負地返回台灣，重拾原有的生活。
TSMC Arizona ought to be embraced as a beacon of America’s revitalized industrialization. A few decades ago, Japanese automakers introduced management techniques and engineering processes to the United States that gave a boost to the competitiveness of American automakers. Beijing understands this principle. Chinese policymakers know that American companies like Apple and Tesla, in addition to training the local work force to the world’s highest standards, lit a fire under Chinese companies to improve their own products.
台積電亞利桑那項目理應被視作美國重振工業化的燈塔而受到歡迎。幾十年前，日本汽車製造商向美國引進了管理技術和工程流程，有力推動了美國汽車製造商競爭力的提升。北京深諳這一原則：中國政策制定者深知，像蘋果和特斯拉這樣的美國企業，除了將當地勞動力培訓至世界最高標準之外，還激勵了中國本土企業提升自身產品水平。
You don’t hear that from American policymakers. It’s more common to find short-term complaints that a worker coming from Taiwan is displacing an American who could get that job.
然而，在美國政策制定者那裡，你聽不到這樣的論調。更常見的反而是短視的抱怨，聲稱來自台灣的工人正在擠占原本屬於美國人的就業崗位。
One could see the community efforts to support the Taiwanese families as an example of spontaneous American welcome. One could also see them as improvised and contingent. If there hadn’t been a friendly Taiwanese pastor next to the factory, fewer families would have received language and education support.
人們可以將社區支持台灣家庭的努力視為美國民間自發歡迎新移民的範例，但也可以將其視作一種臨時拼湊的偶發現象。如果工廠旁邊沒有一位友善的台灣牧師，能夠獲得語言和教育支持的家庭勢必會少得多。
Though Phoenix has surpassed itself in helpfulness, it was not that long ago that the State of Arizona passed a law attempting to achieve “attrition through enforcement.” The White House appears to pay scant attention to the Taiwanese newcomers. If Mr. Trump’s attention drifts, immigration enforcement can wreck this whole project over the course of a morning, which is about how long it took to arrest a few hundred South Koreans.
儘管菲尼克斯在幫扶方面傾盡所能，但不久之前，亞利桑那州還曾通過一項試圖「通過執法造成消耗」來減少非法移民的法律。目前白宮似乎很少關注這些台灣新來者。一旦川普的注意力發生轉移，移民執法部門完全可以在一個上午的時間內毀掉這整個項目——這差不多就是當初抓捕數百名韓國人所花費的時間。
I consider TSMC Arizona to be an example of American success in the technological competition with China. Could we imagine Germany or France getting a quarter of a trillion dollars of TSMC investment and integrating many happy Asian families? I doubt it. Though the United States has fallen behind on manufacturing, it still enjoys geopolitical heft, as well as an advantage few other industrialized countries have: Ambitious people want to live in America. They are attracted to economic opportunity, the chance to build their families and, to varying degrees, American values — “We hold these truths to be self-evident.”
我認為台積電亞利桑那項目是美國在對中國技術競爭中取得成功的典範。我們能想像德國或法國獲得台積電逾2500億美元的投資，並接納許多幸福的亞洲家庭嗎？我對此深表懷疑。儘管美國在製造業領域已經落後，但它仍然享有地緣政治影響力，以及極少數其他工業化國家所具備的獨特優勢：有雄心抱負的人渴望生活在美國。他們被這裡的經濟機遇、建立家庭的前景所吸引，在不同程度上，也受到美國價值觀的吸引——「我們認為這些真理是不言而喻的」。
People on the coasts rarely talk about the American dream, an idea that still means something to these Taiwanese arrivals. TSMC is grafting a Taiwanese engineering community onto the American economy. The United States has the opportunity to prove once again it can renew itself through the ambitions of newcomers — and transform into something greater in turn.
東西海岸的人們如今很少談論美國夢，而這個概念對這些剛抵達的台灣人來說依然意義非凡。台積電正在將一個台灣工程師社群嫁接到美國經濟之中。美國完全有機會再次證明，它能夠藉助新移民的抱負實現自我更新，進而蛻變為更為強大的國家。
