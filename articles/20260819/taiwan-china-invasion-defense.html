如果北京武力攻台，普通人能「保衛台灣」嗎？

The Defenders

BROOK LARMER • 2026-08-19
Albee Lee leads a double life. During the week, the 29-year-old with a tattoo of a skateboarding duck cleans houses in the city of Hsinchu, the hub of Taiwan’s semiconductor industry. On weekends, she prepares for a Chinese invasion.
阿爾比·李（音）過著雙重生活。這位29歲、身上文著滑板鴨圖案的女子平日在台灣半導體產業中心新竹市做家政打掃；到了週末，她則在為應對中國可能的武力攻台做準備。
China’s intensifying military maneuvers around Taiwan — at a time when the United States is distracted and depleted by the war in Iran — seem designed to intimidate the self-governed island’s 23 million inhabitants. For Lee, however, China’s scare tactics have only sharpened her desire to defend Taiwan, an outpost of democracy that produces more than 90 percent of the world’s most advanced computer chips. Along with thousands of other civilians — artists and academics, firefighters and engineers — Lee has joined a growing civil-defense movement that often engages in live-simulation disaster training.
美國因伊朗戰爭精力分散、資源消耗巨大之際，中國在台灣周邊的軍事演習日益頻繁，其意圖似乎是為了震懾這個自治島嶼上的2300萬居民。但對李而言，中國的恐嚇戰術反而更加堅定了她保衛台灣的決心。台灣是一個民主前哨，生產全球超過90%的最先進計算機晶片。與包括藝術家、學者、消防員和工程師的數千名普通民眾一起，她加入了一個不斷壯大的民防運動，該運動常組織針對災難的實景模擬訓練。
Now, along with her cleaning supplies, Lee carries a meticulously assembled emergency first-aid kit. “I don’t want to live under Chinese Communist Party rule,” she says. “If China were to invade, I refuse to hand my life over.”
如今，除了清潔用品，李還隨身攜帶一個精心配置的急救包。「我不想生活在中共的統治下，」她說。「如果中國入侵，我絕不甘心把命運拱手讓人。」
Ever since 1949, when the Communists defeated the Nationalists in China’s civil war, the Chinese government has insisted that Taiwan — the island to which many of the defeated Nationalist forces retreated — must be unified with the mainland. This “unwavering historic mission,” as President Xi Jinping of China calls it, may be closer to becoming a reality. According to U.S. intelligence, Xi has ordered the People’s Liberation Army, the world’s largest standing military, to be capable of annexing the island by 2027, the year of the P.L.A.’s 100th anniversary.
自1949年中共在內戰中擊敗國民黨以來，中國政府一直堅持認為，戰敗的國民黨軍隊退居的台灣島必須與大陸統一。中國國家主席習近平所說的這一「矢志不渝的歷史使命」似乎正逐漸接近實現。據美國情報部門稱，習近平已下令，要求中國人民解放軍——全球規模最大的常備軍隊——必須在2027年（解放軍建軍100週年之際）具備吞併該島的能力。
No imminent plan to invade is evident, but China is parading its superiority over Taiwan’s armed forces. In the past year, the P.L.A. has ramped up “gray zone” tactics that stop short of war. There has been a live-fire simulation of a naval blockade and near-daily incursions across the median line in the Taiwan Strait, the narrow gulf between China and Taiwan’s main island. This summer, China’s ships even patrolled the waters east of Taiwan, through which its primary backer, the United States, would most likely send any military support.
雖然目前沒有跡象表明存在迫在眉睫的進攻計劃，但中國正不斷展現其對台灣武裝力量的優勢。過去一年裡，中國人民解放軍加大了對「灰色地帶」戰術的運用，也就是保持在戰爭門檻以下的行動。其中包括海上封鎖進行實彈模擬演練，以及幾乎每天穿過台灣海峽（即中國大陸與台灣本島之間的狹窄海峽）的中線進行越界巡航。今年夏天，中國的艦艇甚至在台灣以東海域巡邏，這裡正是台灣的主要支持者美國最可能運送軍事支援的通道。
For nearly 50 years, the tenuous status quo in Taiwan has been held together by a slippery U.S. policy known as “strategic ambiguity.” The United States, which backed the Nationalists in the civil war and has tacitly supported Taiwan’s right to exist ever since, recognizes Beijing as the sole legal government of China. But it also sends billions of dollars in weapons to Taiwan every year without stating if it would defend the island in case of attack.
近50年來，台灣脆弱的現狀一直靠美國一項被稱為「戰略模糊「的模稜兩可的政策維繫。美國在內戰中曾支持國民黨，此後也一直默許台灣的存在權利，但同時承認北京是中國的唯一合法政府。但與此同時，美國每年還向台灣出售價值數十億美元的武器，卻從未明確表示在台灣遭受攻擊時是否會出兵協防。
That ambiguous stance is more difficult to sustain today. China is not only stepping up its cognitive warfare — flexing its military muscle, flooding Taiwan with propaganda — but the United States is proving to be a more fickle, transactional ally. President Trump, at times a harsh critic of China, has also expressed admiration for Xi, the Chinese strongman. After a visit to China in May, he even dangled a $14 billion U.S. weapons package to Taiwan — approved by Congress but not yet signed by him — as “a very good negotiating chip” in discussions with Beijing. The episode alarmed many Taiwanese, who fear that Trump, at his next meeting with Xi in September, might leave them to face China weakened and alone.
如今，這種模糊的立場正變得越來越難以維持。不僅中國在加大認知戰力度——炫耀軍事實力、向台灣灌輸大量宣傳，而且事實證明，美國是一個更加反覆無常、講求利益交易的盟友。川普總統雖然有時對中國持嚴厲批評態度，但也曾對強人領袖習近平表示欽佩。在今年5月訪問中國後，他甚至把國會已批准、但他尚未簽署的一項140億美元對台軍售案當作與北京「非常好的談判籌碼」拋了出來。這令許多台灣人感到不安，他們擔心，在9月與中國領導人的下一次會面中，川普可能會讓台灣在實力被削弱、孤立無援的情況下面對中國。
Taiwan is considered one of the world’s most dangerous geopolitical flash points. A military conflict there, involving two superpowers, could have a devastating effect on a global economy dependent on the island’s computer chips. This perilous situation has spurred the growth of Taiwan’s civil-defense movement. Over the past several years, more than 30 volunteer groups have popped up across the island — some motivated by Beijing’s saber-rattling in the Taiwan Strait, some inspired by Ukraine’s resistance to Russia’s invasion, some exasperated by the deepening political divide in Taiwan itself.
台灣被認為是世界上最危險的地緣政治熱點之一。一旦那裡爆發涉及兩個超級大國的軍事衝突，可能會對依賴台灣晶片的全球經濟造成毀滅性打擊。這種危險的局勢刺激了台灣民防運動的發展。過去幾年裡，全島湧現出了30多個志願者團體，有些是受北京在台灣海峽武力威懾的刺激，有些是受烏克蘭抵抗俄羅斯入侵的啟發，還有一些則是對台灣內部日益加劇的政治分歧感到不滿。
These groups received a boost in 2024 when the newly elected President Lai Ching-te promoted a policy of “whole of society” defense. This month, as part of that approach, Taiwan conducted civil-defense exercises alongside the military’s annual drills to test how ordinary people could respond to a Chinese invasion that knocks out all communication, including internet service. As Lai said last year when he unveiled strategies for building societal resilience: “Every citizen stands on the front line of the defense of democracy and freedom.”
2024年，新當選的賴清德總統提出「全社會」防衛政策，為這些團體注入了動力。本月，作為該計劃的一部分，台灣在進行軍方年度演習的同時舉行了民防演練，以測試普通民眾如何應對中國入侵導致所有通訊（包括互聯網服務）中斷的情況。正如賴清德去年提出構建社會韌性戰略時所言：「每一位國民，都是守護民主自由的第一線。」

Though their numbers are still relatively small — a few thousand core participants — the civil-defense groups reflect an evolution in Taiwanese identity, especially among young people. In 1992, 18 percent of the population identified as primarily Taiwanese rather than displaced Chinese; today, according to a recent poll, more than two-thirds do. Only 3 percent identify as solely Chinese — a share equal to the size of the island’s Indigenous population.
儘管這些民防團體的規模仍然相對較小——核心參與者只有數千人，但它們反映了台灣人身份認同的演變，尤其是在年輕人只中。1992年，只有18%的人口認為自己主要是台灣人而非外省人；而最近一項民調顯示，如今超過三分之二的人持此看法。僅3%的人認為自己完全是中國人——這一比例與台灣原住民人口佔比相當。
That shift in identity shapes the vision for Taiwan’s future. Over the past 30 years, the portion of those who favor becoming part of China has declined to less than 10 percent, from what was once a fifth of the population. Most people in Taiwan now say they support a status quo that navigates between independence and unification. They want their island to continue as a prosperous, democratic society. And if a Chinese invasion threatens that status quo, according to a January poll by Academia Sinica, Taiwan’s premier research institution, 60 percent of the population would be willing to fight.
這種身份認同的轉變塑造了對台灣未來的願景。過去30年裡，支持成為中國一部分的人口比例從曾經的五分之一下降到不足10%。如今，大多數台灣人表示，他們支持一種介於獨立與統一之間的現狀。他們希望台灣繼續成為一個繁榮、民主的社會。而根據台灣最高研究機構中央研究院今年1月的一項民調，如果中國入侵威脅到這一現狀，60%的民眾表示願意為此而戰。
A decade ago, there was no civil-defense movement in Taiwan, just a handful of military-simulation enthusiasts like Anfernee Hwang. An industrial designer now in his early 40s, Hwang first gravitated to survivalist games that were “a bit dangerous and exciting,” he says. (His given name comes from the former N.B.A. star Anfernee Hardaway.) In 2016, Hwang and his friends started undertaking intense technical and physical training — he still runs marathons — and styled themselves as an elite special-forces unit, face paint and all. Five years later, one of Hwang’s friends started a Facebook group, Doomsday Preppers Association, Taiwan, that has attracted nearly 20,000 members.
十年前，台灣還沒有民防運動，只有少數像安芬尼·黃（音）這樣的戰術模擬玩家者。他是一名工業設計師，剛過而立之年。他說，他最初被生存類遊戲吸引，因為它們玩起來「有點危險又刺激」。（他的英文名源自原NBA球星安芬尼·哈達威。）2016年，他和朋友們開始進行高強度的技術和體能訓練（他至今仍在堅持跑馬拉松），並將自己打造成一支精英特種部隊的風格，臉上塗著迷彩油彩。五年後，他的一位朋友在Facebook上創建了「台灣末日生存社」，這個社群目前已吸引近2萬名成員。
It was easy to dismiss these early preppers as a sort of fringe militia. But Hwang and a small group of them formed a community emergency response team to prepare civilians for disaster — and a possible Chinese invasion. As tensions between China and Taiwan escalate, these preppers are drawing on their experience to help train people new to civil defense. “China’s infiltration and military exercises,” Hwang says, “made me realize the need for civilians to take initiative and prepare.”
人們很容易把這些早期的備災者誤以為某種外圍民兵組織。但安芬尼·黃與其中一小部分人組建了一支社區應急響應小組，以幫助普通民眾應對災難以及可能發生的中國入侵。隨著兩岸緊張局勢升級，這些備災者正利用自身經驗幫助培訓新加入民防的人。安芬尼·黃說：「中國的滲透和軍事演習讓我意識到，普通民眾需要主動採取行動並做好準備。」
The most influential civil-defense group in Taiwan is Kuma Academy, a four-year-old nonprofit that says it has trained more than 100,000 Taiwanese in everything from survival skills to “pre-bunking” Beijing’s barrage of propaganda. With millions of dollars in funding from a semiconductor tycoon, Kuma also organizes a couple of large-scale live-action drills every year, such as the one Albee Lee joined. The academy, whose name is derived from the Japanese word for bear, also sells “black-bear warrior” dolls and charms on its website.)
台灣最具影響力的民防組織是黑熊學院，這是一個成立四年的非營利組織，據稱已培訓了10多萬名台灣人，內容涵蓋從生存技能到「提前識破」北京密集宣傳攻勢的各種能力。在一位半導體大亨數百萬美元資金的支持下，黑熊學院每年還會組織幾場大規模的實景演練。比如阿爾比·李參加的那次。該學院名稱源自日文「熊」一詞，其網站上還出售「黑熊勇士」玩偶和掛飾。
A vast majority of participants in Kuma’s training sessions are 25 to 45 years old. And while older preppers tend to be men, more than 70 percent of the civil-defense activists are women. “Women are more willing to save their country,” says Chu Fu-ming, Kuma Academy’s male chief executive. “Many women are highly motivated to understand what they can actually do in a crisis,” he says, “to protect their families and the people around them.”
黑熊學院培訓課程絕大多數參與者的年齡在25歲到45歲之間。雖然較年長的備災者多為男性，但民防活動人士中超過七成為女性。黑熊學院執行長朱福銘表示：「女性更願意保家衛國，」他說，「許多女性有強烈的積極性，想了解在危機中自己究竟能做些什麼，以此來保護家人和身邊的人。」
The standoff between China and Taiwan is a study in asymmetry. In size, population and military prowess, the nation of 1.4 billion people overwhelms the island of 23 million. The Chinese military has two million soldiers compared with 230,000 in Taiwan, and China’s naval vessels outnumber Taiwan’s by a similar margin. Even in warplanes, a centerpiece of Taiwan’s defense, China has a nearly five-to-one advantage.
兩岸的對峙是一場典型的非對稱較量。在國土面積、人口數量和軍事實力方面，有14億人口的中國對人口2300萬的台灣形成了壓倒性優勢。中國軍隊有200萬兵力，而台灣僅有23萬；中國海軍艦艇數量也以類似比例領先台灣。甚至在作為台灣防務核心的戰鬥機數量上，中國也擁有近五比一的優勢。
In such an unbalanced contest, Taiwan would have to rely on a so-called porcupine defense, using an array of smaller tactical measures to make an invasion too costly and painful to carry out. Taiwan’s newly developed fleet of drones, for example, could help disrupt an amphibious landing. Another quill of the porcupine would be civilian groups. Refusing to surrender in advance, they would work to maintain lines of communication, conduct search and rescue, evacuate refugees and offer emergency medical care.
面對如此懸殊的對決，台灣將不得不依賴所謂的豪豬防禦策略，即採取一系列較小規模的戰術手段，使對方發起入侵的代價過於昂貴、難以承受。例如，台灣新研發的無人機編隊可以幫助破壞兩棲登陸行動。民防團體則是豪豬身上的另一根尖刺——他們拒絕提前投降，並將致力於維持通信線路、開展搜救、疏散難民以及提供緊急醫療救護服務。
H.E. Huang, a 20-year-old studying animal science, thinks civil defense can make a crucial difference. Like all his peers, Huang grew up in prosperity and freedom — the fruits of the island’s turn to democracy nearly 40 years ago — alongside growing threats from China. As a teenager, he says, “I kept wondering what regular people could actually do to prepare if a war broke out.”
現年20歲、正在攻讀動物科學專業的黃先生(H.E. Huang)認為，民防能夠發揮至關重要的作用。與同齡人一樣，黃先生在繁榮與自由——該島近40年前轉向民主制度的果實——中長大，但同時也目睹來自中國日益增長的威脅。他說，在十幾歲時，「我一直在想，如果爆發戰爭，普通人究竟能做些什麼來應對。」
Huang became certified as an emergency medical technician and then, after entering university, joined a civil-defense group. “History has shown many times that even overwhelming military power doesn’t guarantee victory,” he says, pointing to Ukraine’s spirited resistance to the Russian invasion. “The morale, determination and organization of the local population can play a huge role in the outcome of a conflict.”
黃先生考取了急救員資格證，進入大學後便加入了一個民防團體。「歷史多次表明，即便擁有壓倒性的軍事力量也並不能保證勝利，」他指出烏克蘭對俄羅斯入侵的頑強抵抗，「當地民眾的士氣、決心和組織能力能夠對衝突的結果發揮舉足輕重的作用。」
Building that kind of solidarity is not easy in a society so deeply divided. As civil-defense groups accelerate their training, many Taiwanese remain complacent about the threat or, more pointedly, criticize civil-defense proponents for sowing panic and promoting war. Attitudes toward civil defense cleave to the central divide in Taiwanese politics between the president’s Democratic Progressive Party (D.P.P.) and the Nationalists, also known as the Kuomintang (KMT), which controls the legislature through a coalition. Virtually all participants in civil-defense exercises back the D.P.P., according to one informal survey. KMT loyalists accuse them of provoking a conflict with China for political gain, while D.P.P. supporters have called the KMT Beijing’s proxy for parroting Chinese propaganda.
在一個如此分裂的社會裡，建立這種團結並非易事。隨著民防團體加速訓練，許多台灣人依然對威脅不甚關心，或者更不客氣地批評民防倡導者是在製造恐慌、鼓吹戰爭。對民防的態度與台灣政治的核心分歧如出一轍，也就是總統所屬的民進黨與通過一個政黨聯盟控制立法院的國民黨之間的對立。根據一項非正式調查，幾乎所有參與民防演習的人都支持民進黨。國民黨的支持者指責他們是為了政治利益而刻意挑起與中國的衝突，民進黨的支持者則反過來指責國民黨在為北京做傳聲筒，鸚鵡學舌般重複中國的宣傳。
One of China’s main weapons is cognitive warfare: the systematic targeting of an adversary’s perception of reality, identity and political will. “No place in the world is more bombarded by cognitive warfare than Taiwan,” says Chu, the Kuma Academy chief executive. China’s propaganda and disinformation, along with the military maneuvers off Taiwan’s coast, are all meant to weaken Taiwan’s resolve to defend itself, he says.
中國的一大武器是認知戰，即系統性地攻擊對手對現實、身份認同和政治意願的認知。「世界上沒有任何地方比台灣遭受更多認知戰的轟炸，」黑熊學院執行長朱福銘表示。他指出，中國的宣傳和虛假信息、台灣沿岸的軍事演習都是為了削弱台灣自我防衛的決心。
Kuma Academy and other groups hold workshops designed to help people identify and counter disinformation, a recognition that one of the key battlegrounds in modern conflict is not land or sea but a society’s collective psyche. The ultimate goal for Taiwan’s civil defense is what some call epistemic sovereignty, the ability to define one’s reality and history without being manipulated by disinformation.
黑熊學院及其他團體舉辦工作坊，幫助人們識別和反擊虛假信息。這源於人們的一種共識，即現代衝突的關鍵戰場不再是陸地或海洋，而是整個社會的集體心理。台灣民防的終極目標是一些人所說的「認知主權」，即在不受虛假信息操縱的情況下，定義自身現實與歷史的能力。
廣告
That ideal is still a long way off. Even President Lai’s office, which elevated the idea of civil defense, feels the need to frame it as disaster relief, not as preparation for war — a phrasing designed to appeal to a greater number of people. Catastrophic flooding on the island’s east coast last year gave the Lai administration a chance to demonstrate, free of politics or actual war, the utility of civil-defense training. More than 40,000 people a day went to help the affected area, but only the dozens of civil-defense teams on the scene had the know-how to set up mobile clinics, temporary shelters and donation sites.
然而，這一理想依然遙不可及。即便是將民防理念提升至新高度的賴清德總統辦公室，也覺得有必要將其包裝為「災難救援」，而非「備戰」——這種表述旨在吸引更多民眾。去年台灣東海岸發生洪災，這讓賴清德政府有機會在不受政治或實際戰爭干擾的情況下展示民防訓練的實用價值。當時每天有超過4萬人前往受災地區提供協助，但現場只有幾十支民防團隊懂得如何設立流動診所、臨時收容所和捐贈點的專業技能。
“It changed perceptions of civil defense,” says Liu Yu-hsi, an economics professor who serves as a presidential adviser. “Most ordinary people had the impression that civil defense was all about guns or militias. The ‘warrior’ perception is not good, but if you’re ‘disaster relief,’ that’s very positive.”
「這改變了人們對民防的看法，」擔任總統顧問的經濟學教授劉玉皙說道。「大多數普通人過去總認為民防就是動槍或民兵。這種『戰士』式的印象並不好，但如果是『災難救援』，那就非常正面了。」
Perceptions, though, can be resistant to change. Huang, the student, lives in a neighborhood dominated by the KMT, mostly older descendants of the Chinese who came in 1949. Recently, he tried to share with his neighbors information about how to protect themselves in a crisis. They refused to listen. “I find it incredibly difficult to talk about raising the public’s preparedness without further polarizing the people,” Huang says.
然而，觀念的改變並非易事。還在讀書的黃先生住在一個國民黨支持者占多數的社區，居民大多是1949年來台的外省人後代。最近，他試圖與鄰居分享如何在危機中自我保護的信息，但他們根本不聽。「我發現，要在不進一步加劇社會對立的情況下談論提高公眾的備災意識，實在太難了，」黃先生說。
Back in Hsinchu, Albee Lee is scrubbing the bathrooms and kitchens of the wealthy. Most of her clients support the KMT or exist complacently within their affluent tech bubble, she says. Most know nothing about her double life.
在新竹，阿爾比·李為富人打掃浴室和廚房。她說，她的絕大多數客戶要麼支持國民黨，要麼安於他們富裕的科技圈泡沫之中。大多數人對她的雙重生活一無所知。
When she and a friend talked to clients about the need to be prepared, she says, “we were either met with ridicule or sarcasm. People simply didn’t want to talk to us.” Lee is undeterred. “I still worry about a Chinese invasion. I hope to awaken the Taiwanese people — even if it’s just one by one.”
她說，當她和一個朋友向客戶談到做防備的必要性時，「我們要麼遭到嘲笑，要麼遭到諷刺，大家根本不想理我們。」但她並未氣餒。「我依然擔心中國的進攻。我希望喚醒台灣民眾——哪怕一次只能喚醒一個人。」
