How Obama and Trump Made Their Cases in Dueling Midterm Speeches

TIM BALK • 2026-10-26
Former President Barack Obama returned to the campaign trail on Saturday for midterm rallies in Cleveland and Detroit, setting up a rare split screen with President Trump, who staged a rally in the evening in a small city in central Georgia.
上週六，前總統歐巴馬重返競選活動，在克利夫蘭和底特律參加期中選舉集會，與當晚在喬治亞州中部一座小城舉辦造勢集會的川普總統形成了罕見的同期對比。
Their pitches offered a contrast in style and substance. Mr. Obama prosecuted his case against the Trump administration in two tightly scripted, roughly 40-minute bursts on Saturday.
兩人的演講在風格和內容上呈現鮮明對比。週六，通過兩段精心編排、時長約40分鐘的發言，歐巴馬對川普政府展開了抨擊。
Mr. Trump, who has been crisscrossing the country for weeks in a midterm push to save his party’s congressional majorities, defended his administration in a rambling and at times profane hour-and-a-half speech Saturday night.
川普數週來一直在全國各地奔波，為保住本黨國會多數席位展開期中選舉衝刺。週六晚間，他在長達一個半小時的演講中為自己的政府辯護，言辭漫無邊際，時而夾雜粗俗之語。
Here’s how Mr. Trump and Mr. Obama made their arguments 10 days before the election.
以下是川普和歐巴馬在選舉前10天各自闡述觀點的情況。
廣告
On the Iran war
關於伊朗戰爭
Mr. Obama assailed the Iran war, which has driven up prices, describing it as an “unnecessary war” that “nobody asked for.”
歐巴馬抨擊伊朗戰爭推高了物價，稱這是一場「不必要的戰爭」，「沒有人要求打這場戰爭」。
“Our allies and our bases have been attacked,” Mr. Obama said in Cleveland. “Americans have been killed. Hundreds of Americans have been wounded. We have run down our stock of missiles. And Iran now has veto power over the Strait of Hormuz, where oil goes through to the world market.”
「我們的盟友和基地遭到攻擊，」歐巴馬在克利夫蘭說。「美國人被殺。數百名美國人受傷。我們的導彈庫存已經耗盡。而伊朗現在對霍爾木茲海峽擁有否決權，石油經那裡進入世界市場。」
He said a central goal of the war — preventing Iran from acquiring a nuclear weapon — had been better addressed through the nuclear deal he reached with Iran in 2015. Mr. Trump pulled out of that agreement during his first term.
他說，這場戰爭的核心目標之一——阻止伊朗獲得核武器——本已通過他2015年與伊朗達成的核協議得到了更好的解決。川普在其第一任期內退出了該協議。
Mr. Trump said the unpopular war had been a success and had cowed Iran. He said Mr. Obama’s deal had been a “road to a nuclear weapon for Iran.”
川普稱這場不受歡迎的戰爭取得了成功，並震懾了伊朗。他說歐巴馬的協議是「通往伊朗核武器的道路」。
“We defeated them militarily, financially and otherwise,” Mr. Trump said of Iran, though the war continues and administration officials are concerned that Iran is in the advanced stages of plans for attacks against U.S. officials.
「我們在軍事上、財政上和其他方面擊敗了他們，」川普談到伊朗時說，儘管戰爭仍在繼續，而且政府官員擔心伊朗針對美國官員的襲擊計劃已進入後期階段。
廣告
“We have to make sure that they never, ever have a nuclear weapon, and we’ll do that very easily,” Mr. Trump said, adding, “Our country is getting stronger and stronger all the time.”
「我們必須確保他們永遠、永遠無法擁有核武器，我們做到這一點將輕而易舉，」川普說，他還說，「我們的國家每時每刻都在變得越來越強大。」
On the economy
關於經濟
Mr. Trump spent much of his speech warning about a risk he said Democrats would pose to the economy. A vote for Republicans, Mr. Trump said, was a vote for “more money in your pockets,” lower taxes and a “booming” economy.
川普在演講中花費了大量篇幅警告民主黨將給經濟帶來的風險。川普說，投票給共和黨就是投票支持「你口袋裡有更多的錢」、更低的稅收和「蓬勃發展」的經濟。
If the Democrats win back Congress, he claimed, the country will fall into a “1929-style economic depression the likes of which you have never seen before.”
他聲稱，如果民主黨贏回國會，國家將陷入「你從未見過的1929年式經濟蕭條」。
“They want all the things financially bad,” Mr. Trump said, citing transgender athletes in women’s sports, without explaining how that could affect the economy.
「他們想要所有經濟上糟糕的東西，」川普說，並提到女子體育中的跨性別運動員，但沒有解釋這如何影響經濟。
Mr. Trump also reminded voters of his promise to send $5,000 to every American adult if Republicans win in November. He argued that his sweeping tariff policies had “made us rich” and led to companies “pouring in” to the United States.
川普還提醒選民，他承諾如果共和黨在11月獲勝，將向每個美國成年人發放5000美元。他辯稱，他實施的全面關稅政策「讓我們富了起來」，並導致公司「湧入」美國。
Mr. Obama said that presidents did not have “control over the entire economy.” But in this case the “administration’s actions are directly responsible for rising costs,” he said, citing the Iran war.
歐巴馬說，總統並不「控制整個經濟」。但他表示，在這種情況下，「政府的行動直接導致了成本上升」，並提到伊朗戰爭。
“This is the reason why the price of oil is up — nobody disputes that,” Mr. Obama said. “This is the reason the price of diesel is up. This is the reason why the price of just about everything is up.”
「這就是油價上漲的原因——沒有人否認這一點，」歐巴馬說。「這就是柴油價格上漲的原因。這就是幾乎所有東西價格上漲的原因。」
廣告
Mr. Obama ridiculed Mr. Trump’s tariff regimen, saying it, too, had contributed to rising prices. He mocked Mr. Trump for picking a trade fight with “that well-known evil empire Canada.” And he suggested that Republicans should have passed legislation responding to inflation.
歐巴馬嘲笑川普的關稅制度，稱它也導致了物價上漲。他嘲諷川普與「那個眾所周知的邪惡帝國加拿大」打貿易戰。他還表示，共和黨本應通過立法應對通膨。
“The only bill they passed of any significance in the last two years was a trillion-dollar tax break that mostly benefited the richest Americans,” Mr. Obama said.
「過去兩年裡，他們通過的唯一一項有意義的法案，是一項主要惠及最富有美國人的萬億美元減稅政策，」歐巴馬說。
On what’s ‘radical’
關於何為「激進」
Mr. Obama pushed back on a core Republican argument going into November: that Democratic candidates are extreme.
歐巴馬反駁了共和黨在11月大選前提出的一項核心論點：民主黨候選人是極端的。
Mr. Obama, chuckling, said in Detroit that Republicans’ ads were aimed at convincing voters that his party was made up of “radicals or communists or jihadists or the woke police that will come arrest you and force-feed you tofu.”
歐巴馬在底特律笑著表示，共和黨的廣告旨在讓選民相信，民主黨由「激進分子、共產主義者、聖戰分子，或者會來逮捕你並強迫你吃豆腐的覺醒警察」組成。
“We are not going to fall for that okey-doke,” Mr. Obama said.
「我們不會上那個當，」歐巴馬說。
Mr. Obama was joining Dr. Abdul El-Sayed, the progressive Democratic Senate nominee in Michigan, who is in a close race and whose platform calls for universal health care. Mr. Obama objected to Republican efforts to cast Dr. El-Sayed as extreme.
歐巴馬當時正與密西根州進步派民主党參議員候選人阿卜杜勒·埃爾-賽義德博士一同出席活動。埃爾-賽義德目前選情膠著，其競選綱領呼籲全民醫保。歐巴馬反對共和黨試圖把埃爾-賽義德描繪成極端分子。
“Wanting everybody to have health care — how is that radical?” Mr. Obama said. “Wanting everybody to have a place to live that they can afford — how is that radical?”
「希望每個人都有醫保——這怎麼能算激進？」歐巴馬說。「希望每個人都有負擔得起的住處——這怎麼能算激進？」
Around the same time, Mr. Trump was arguing in Perry, Ga., that his party was running against “radical-left” candidates. He claimed that Democrats wanted to “loot your wealth” and “annihilate your borders.”
大約同一時間，川普在喬治亞州佩里辯稱，他的政黨正在與「激進左翼」候選人競爭。他聲稱民主黨想「掠奪你們的財富」並「摧毀你們的邊界」。
廣告
He charged that Democrats were running as communists, though no Democrat in any midterm race of note identifies as a communist. (Some Democratic candidates, mostly concentrated in deep-blue districts, identify as democratic socialists.)
他指控民主黨人以共產主義者身份競選，儘管期中選舉中，沒有任何值得注意的民主黨候選人自認是共產主義者。（一些民主黨候選人自認是民主社會主義者，主要集中在深藍選區。）
“Less than two years ago, the radical-left Democrats handed us the dark age — it was a dark age — of America,” Mr. Trump said in Georgia, where he described the incumbent Democratic senator on the ballot, Jon Ossoff, as a “radical-left senator.”
「不到兩年前，激進左翼民主黨人把美國帶入了黑暗時代——那確實是美國的黑暗時代，」川普在喬治亞州說。他把此次參選的現任民主党參議員喬恩·奧索夫描述為「激進左翼參議員」。
Mr. Ossoff, a mainstream Democrat, has held a durable lead in the polls over his Republican challenger, Representative Mike Collins.
奧索夫是主流民主黨人，在民調中對共和黨挑戰者、眾議員邁克·柯林斯一直保持領先。
On challenges facing America
關於美國面臨的挑戰
Mr. Trump and Mr. Obama offered different takes on the most pressing issues facing the country.
川普和歐巴馬對美國面臨的最緊迫問題提出了不同看法。
Mr. Trump said the “hardest job” was addressing the threat posed by immigration. He cast economic challenges as comparatively minor, and said deporting immigrants was “nasty work.”
川普說，「最艱難的工作」是應對移民構成的威脅。他把經濟挑戰說成相對次要，並稱驅逐移民是「麻煩的工作」。
“Some of them are so dangerous, Rick, we don’t want to get them out, we have to put them in jail,” Mr. Trump said, speaking from the stage to Rick Jackson, the Republican nominee for Georgia governor.
「他們中有些人太危險了，裡克，我們不能把他們趕出去，我們必須把他們關進監獄，」川普在台上對喬治亞州州長共和黨候選人裡克·傑克遜說。
Mr. Obama said in Detroit that “we do need to make sure our border is orderly and secure,” but suggested that Mr. Trump had reached beyond his 2024 campaign promises by “sending masked agents running through our streets.”
歐巴馬在底特律說，「我們確實需要確保邊境有序和安全」， 但他表示，川普「派遣蒙面特工在街頭巡邏」的做法已經超出了其2024年競選承諾的範疇。
And he questioned whether Mr. Trump was “thinking deeply” about the challenges facing Americans. As he closed his speech, he reflected on artificial intelligence.
他質疑川普是否在「深入思考」美國人面臨的挑戰。在結束演講時，他談到人工智慧。
廣告
“I know it’s been hyped, but there is a real technology that’s coming,” Mr. Obama said. “It is going to change a lot.”
「我知道它被炒作了，但一項真正的技術正在到來，」歐巴馬說。「它將改變很多。」
He added: “These are serious times. This is not the time for reality TV.”
他補充說：「這是嚴肅的時代。現在不是搞真人秀的時候。」
(Both presidents are set to return to the trail before the Nov. 3 election.)
（兩位總統都將在11月3日選舉前重返競選活動。）
